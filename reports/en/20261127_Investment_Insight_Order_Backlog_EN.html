"Years of order backlog" has been a common justification for defense, power equipment and component stocks this year. Yet in November alone, one company cut revenue guidance even as its backlog grew, while another took a loss on advance reservations. Three questions stand between backlog and revenue. How firm is the contract, how profitable is the order, and when can it be delivered?

"The order backlog covers two to three years of revenue." It has become a familiar explanation for the performance of defense, power equipment and component stocks this year. The logic is that even if demand weakens, companies can keep fulfilling existing orders and sustain earnings.1

November brought a series of challenges to that logic. On Nov. 5, German defense contractor Rheinmetall reported a 38% YoY increase in backlog while cutting its full-year revenue guidance again.2 On Nov. 27, it also cut its 2030 revenue target by roughly 30%.52 SoftBank Group, the Japanese investment group that had reserved power equipment for US data centers, recognized a JPY 597.2bn loss after revising its procurement plans.3

That does not make backlog a flawed metric. Few measures are as useful for assessing future revenue. But backlog is not homogeneous. Reservations and contracts can sit alongside one another, conversion rates vary, and some amounts remain uncertain until the contract ends.

01From order to revenue

Winning an order does not
immediately generate revenue

Order backlog is the portion of orders received that has not yet been recognized as revenue. Revenue is recognized when, or as, control of goods or services transfers to the customer. Some contracts recognize all revenue upon delivery; others recognize it during production based on progress.4 A large backlog means more commitments have yet to become revenue, not that revenue is assured.

Backlog is not a line item on the balance sheet or income statement. Companies compile and report it separately, and definitions differ. Korean companies in order-driven industries disclose backlog by product in the "Sales and Order Status" section of their quarterly and annual reports. Some companies report reservations separately from backlog; others include signed framework agreements even before individual orders are placed. The two companies in the next section illustrate this distinction.

02Contract certainty

Not all backlog is alike:
firm orders are not reservations

A slot reservation is an agreement to secure a place in a factory's production schedule. It precedes a firm order and generally involves a less definitive commitment. When supply is tight, customers get in line before their projects are finalized. A large volume of reservations signals strong demand, but also a large volume of commitments still awaiting finalization.

Source: GE Vernova earnings releases filed with the SEC5, 6, 7 / End-Q4 2025 figures are opening balances in the Q1 release.

US power equipment maker GE Vernova reports these figures separately. Its backlog stood at 64 GW at the end of Q3, up from 53 GW in Q2: 13 GW of reservations converted to orders, 3 GW of new firm orders and 5 GW of shipments. New reservations totaled 8 GW in Q3.7 Reservations continue to convert to orders, and orders to shipments. Group-wide Q3 orders across Gas Power, Electrification and Wind totaled $29.3bn, more than twice revenue of $12.6bn (a book-to-bill ratio of about 2.3x). Gas equipment volumes and prices both rose.7

Growth has slowed, however. Orders plus reservations rose by 6 GW in Q3, below the double-digit GW increases in each of the first two quarters. The company expects contracted volume to keep growing in 2027, with quarterly increases similar to those in the second half of 2026.8 The interpretation depends on whether demand is cooling or earlier production slots are already booked. The company expects most slots for deliveries through 2030 to be sold by year-end.9 It expects a combined total of at least 130 GW by then.7

At a March investor conference, management said customers reserving slots pay deposits of 20-25% of the equipment price.10 Even reservations with deposits are excluded from formal backlog.11 Data center exposure also depends on the measure. In April's Q1 earnings release, data centers represented about 20% of contracted volume, including firm orders and reservations. In March, management put the share at 10-15% of firm backlog and about one-third of reservations.11, 12, 13 Data center-related uncertainty is therefore more concentrated in reservations than in firm orders.

What happens to upfront payments when reservations are withdrawn?

On Nov. 10, SoftBank Group recognized a JPY 597.2bn (approximately $3.73bn) loss after changing its power equipment procurement plans for US AI data centers. The loss covered the broader set of agreements to secure power generation equipment, not just gas turbines. It comprised JPY 457bn in forfeited prepayments and JPY 140.2bn in contract modification and settlement costs.3, 14, 15

Sources: SoftBank Group earnings presentation, Reuters, Nikkei (as cited by Agora)3, 15, 16 / Original loss figures in yen; dollar amounts are presentation estimates. Recognition of a loss and the cash outflow need not occur at the same time.

The remaining $6.6bn will be used to purchase equipment for projects including the US PORTS-Pike power project. In a revised Nov. 11 presentation, the company clarified that the loss was unrelated to this project.16 In a further update on Nov. 12, it said it planned to recover $5.6bn of the $6.6bn through this project by end-2027, and $1bn over a longer period. These are plans, not completed recoveries.17 SK Securities said SoftBank had reserved production slots with several manufacturers in May for 9.2 GW of gas turbines. The portion affected by the revised contracts has not been disclosed. Gas turbine lead times run four to five years from order to delivery.1

GE Vernova shares fell nearly 10% intraday on the announcement before closing down about 4%.18, 19 The company said the announcement had neither a positive nor a negative impact on its business.20 It also said deposits on orders and slot reservations were non-refundable.19 A $3.73bn loss for the customer does not imply an equivalent reduction in supplier backlog. The amounts committed to each manufacturer were not disclosed.

The risk SK Securities identified was not the size of the loss but the sequence of events: production slots were reserved before the location and use of the data centers had been determined. In the brokerage's view, cancellation fees may cushion suppliers' losses but cannot guarantee deliveries.1 This also explains why stocks react so quickly. Prepayments and cancellation fees are one-off receipts. Equity valuations reflect the expectation that these slots will keep factories running for years and generate service revenue after delivery.

SK Securities offered four tests to distinguish an isolated incident from the start of a structural risk.

Defense: MOUs and framework contracts

Defense export deals progress from an MOU and a term sheet through a framework agreement to an implementing contract. SK Securities sees much of today's pipeline in these preliminary documents and uncalled volumes under framework agreements. The framework agreement for Spain's K9 self-propelled howitzers was disclosed at more than KRW 660bn, but the value of the implementing contract has not been disclosed. Major export opportunities expected to be signed from Q4 2026 through 2027 total KRW 60-70tn, two-thirds of which are concentrated in four deals.21 What moves share prices is not the number of prospects, but whether signatures turn them into backlog, in SK Securities' view.

03Timing risk

Firm-order backlog rose 66%,
but revenue guidance fell twice

Rheinmetall's end-September backlog reached EUR 78bn, up 38% YoY. Firm orders grew 66%, from EUR 32.7bn to EUR 54.3bn, improving backlog quality.22 Yet in the same announcement, the company cut full-year revenue guidance to about EUR 13.2bn, the second cut since August.2, 23 Firmer contracts do not necessarily mean earlier revenue.

Sources: Rheinmetall, Hanwha Investment & Securities2, 22, 23 / About 18% of the EUR 21.6bn rise in firm orders came from EUR 3.9bn in naval backlog acquired in Q1 (excluding it, growth was 54%). Separate acquired backlog from newly won orders. Margin guidance was also cut from 19.0% to 18.5%.

The two cuts had different causes. The cancellation of Germany's F126 frigate program led Rheinmetall to remove EUR 2.15bn in subcontract backlog in July and cut full-year revenue guidance by roughly EUR 300m in August.22, 23 Timing was the issue in November. Negotiations for the German military's large Boxer armored vehicle procurement (Arminius) are in their final stages, with orders possible only next year. Batch-level acceptance testing for 155mm ammunition was delayed.2 According to Hanwha Investment & Securities, shortages of inspection staff and facilities deferred several hundred million euros in revenue.23

The final quarter also carries a heavy load. Revenue through September was EUR 8.38bn, leaving around EUR 4.8bn to be recognized in Q4 to meet guidance. That is 53% above Q3 (EUR 3.15bn) and 24% above Q4 2025 (EUR 3.88bn).22, 24 Defense revenue is typically concentrated at year-end: last year's Q4 revenue exceeded Q3 by 68%. A 53% sequential increase is not necessarily unrealistic, but this year brings the added ammunition acceptance bottleneck. Of the EUR 78bn backlog, it is the EUR 4.8bn that must convert to revenue within three months that will determine this year's results.

The contrast is clearer in profit terms. Multiplying revenue guidance by margin guidance brings the implied full-year operating result (the company's measure excluding special items) down from EUR 2.66-2.76bn at the start of the year to about EUR 2.44bn.2, 24 Firm-order backlog grew 66%, yet expected operating profit for the year is about 10% lower than implied by initial guidance. That would still be around 33% above last year's actual operating result (EUR 1.84bn). Profit is not shrinking; it is growing less than initially expected.24

Shares fell more than 17% in early trading on the day of the announcement.25 Operating free cash flow through September was negative EUR 1.7bn. Cash went into inventory to support higher sales, while customer advances fell short of last year's unusually high inflows. The outflow narrowed to EUR 80m in Q3 alone.22

Backlog can have opposite effects on cash flow. GE Vernova's contract liabilities and deferred revenue rose $19.8bn in nine months. The company attributed the increase to payments received exceeding revenue recognized, as well as the acquisition of Prolec GE.26 For some companies, backlog brings cash in upfront; for others, it ties cash up before delivery. GE Vernova's deposits, however, come with obligations to deliver. They are not profit in their own right.

There is a more optimistic interpretation. Hanwha Investment & Securities sees the issue as one of contract and revenue-recognition timing, and expects it is likely to be resolved. Of Rheinmetall's EUR 80.5bn backlog at end-June, EUR 33.6bn is scheduled for delivery within two and a half years, with 90% already under contract.23

Source: Hanwha Investment & Securities23 / Backlogs dominated by ammunition convert to revenue faster than those dominated by tanks.

Longer lead times also increase the risk of a gap between the agreed price and actual production costs. Fixed-price contracts without raw material or inflation escalation clauses can erode margins even with a large backlog. If the unavoidable costs of meeting contractual obligations exceed expected benefits, a provision for future losses must be recognized (an onerous contract).27

Korean defense backlog: more years does not mean the same quality

Sources: Hanwha Investment & Securities, Rheinmetall22, 23 / LIG D&A stands for LIG Defense & Aerospace. Hanwha Investment & Securities is affiliated with Hanwha Aerospace and Hanwha Systems. Rheinmetall's multiple uses the company's full-year revenue guidance. As backlog and revenue definitions differ, these are indicative multiples, not the time required to fulfill all orders.

Hanwha Investment & Securities believes these backlogs provide visibility into medium-term results.23 According to SK Securities, the combined Q3 backlog of five Korean defense companies stood at about KRW 136tn, down 18% from end-2025 (KRW 166tn) and 11% YoY. Export orders this year amount to $10.1bn, down 35% from $15.4bn last year.21 The major export prospects discussed in section 02 need to become signed contracts to replenish the shrinking backlog.

Years of backlog can be misleading. Backlog divided by revenue rises when the denominator falls. Dividing Rheinmetall's EUR 78bn backlog by the midpoint of its initial revenue guidance (EUR 14.25bn) gives 5.5 years; using November's EUR 13.2bn guidance gives 5.9 years.22, 24 Cutting revenue guidance actually increases the backlog-to-sales multiple.

The multiple shows how much remains, not how quickly it will convert into revenue. Excluding framework agreements, Rheinmetall's 4.1 years is comparable to the Korean companies.

Some companies disclose a more useful measure. GE Vernova's quarterly filing shows when contracted revenue is expected to be recognized, by time horizon. This is the remaining performance obligation (RPO) required by accounting standards, which does not necessarily match the company's separately reported backlog. Across Gas Power, Wind and Electrification, 33% of the $104bn equipment RPO at end-September is expected to be recognized within one year, and 60% within two. For the $92.7bn service RPO, just 16% is expected within one year. The expected one-year revenue conversion share is roughly twice as high for equipment as for services.26 Of the combined $196.7bn, around $49.2bn, or one-quarter, is expected to be recognized within one year. Changes to contracts can alter both timing and value, so this is not guaranteed revenue either. Still, this disclosure lets investors ask how much revenue is expected over the next 12 months, rather than how many years of backlog exist.

CUTNov. 27: Rheinmetall CMD lowers 2030 targetsThe 2030 revenue target fell from around EUR 50bn to EUR 33-37bn, and the operating margin target from "at least 20%" to "around 20%." Full-year 2026 guidance remains unchanged at about EUR 13.2bn of revenue and an operating margin of about 18.5%. The analyst consensus before the announcement was EUR 36.8bn, suggesting the market had already anticipated the downgrade.52 Hanwha Investment & Securities argued that keeping 2027 guidance while reducing 2030 targets could help restore credibility. The 2030 downgrade is now confirmed; the outstanding question is the 2027 figure, which we could not verify at the time of writing.23
04Contract value

A KRW 3.8347tn contract
ended at KRW 9.73m

The headline value disclosed for a long-term supply contract often reflects estimated volumes multiplied by estimated unit prices. If customer demand weakens, the actual value delivered may be much smaller or disappear entirely. The face value of a contract and the amount ultimately fulfilled are different numbers.

Sources: Financial News, SBS Biz, The Korea Economic Daily, Kuki News28, 29, 30, 31 / KRW 9.73m is just 2.5 parts per million of KRW 3.8347tn, too small to appear on the chart.

In February 2023, L&F disclosed a KRW 3.8347tn contract to supply high-nickel cathode materials to Tesla in 2024-25.28 The contract was about four times its 2021 revenue (395%).32 After the close on Dec. 29, 2025, two days before the contract expired, L&F cited a change in supply volumes and filed an amendment reducing the contract value to KRW 9,730,316. The company said this reflected deliveries completed to date.29 Shares fell more than 11% in early trading the next day.33

The warning was already in quarterly filings

L&F's quarterly filings showed cumulative deliveries under the contract hovering around KRW 10m from the first half of 2024 through September 2025. The company said it had already mentioned delays in supply during its Q2 and Q3 2024 earnings announcements.34 By end-September 2025, 87.5% of the 24-month contract term had elapsed.

Comparing the elapsed contract term with cumulative deliveries would have made it difficult to count the full KRW 3.8tn as future revenue. Some contracts are back-loaded, however, so this alone could not predict termination. L&F said it had the production capacity to fulfill the contract and that weak revenue reflected lower demand.34

On Dec. 17, 2025, LG Energy Solution disclosed that Ford had notified it of a contract termination as the automaker discontinued some EV production.36 The agreement covered 75 GWh for delivery in 2027-32. The KRW 9.603tn terminated value was an estimate based on contracted volume and battery prices as of Oct. 14, 2024. The 28.5% share of revenue used 2023 revenue, the reference year when the contract was signed.30, 36 Shares fell 8.9% the next day.37 On Dec. 26, LG Energy Solution also terminated its contract with US battery pack maker FBPS. The terminated balance was KRW 3.9217tn, representing the original $2.795bn less $110m already supplied.31 In just over ten days, contracts with an estimated value of around KRW 13.5tn were terminated.38

This figure measures future contract value lost, not an accounting loss. LG Energy Solution said the FBPS termination would have limited financial impact because the agreement covered standard products made on existing lines, not a dedicated line.39 That is different from SoftBank Group's JPY 597.2bn recognized loss. But the burden has not vanished. The Polish plant that was to produce all of the Ford volumes is slated for investment to convert production to energy storage systems (ESS).40 Conversion costs remain, along with utilization pressure until replacement demand fills the 75 GWh gap.

Long-term agreements did not provide a safety net either

SK Securities noted that past LTAs in smartphone components, substrates and batteries were revised or terminated when demand weakened. Compensation covered only part of the impact, while the factories remained. What suppliers were left with was not the contract itself, but the non-refundable cash they had already received.41

A similar pattern is emerging. Q3 smartphone shipments fell about 20% YoY, and some Greater China manufacturers saw declines of more than 30%.42 SK Securities estimates Samsung Electronics is cutting Q4 orders for entry-level smartphone components by 20-30% from plan. Next year, the brokerage will focus on three things rather than backlog size: whether customer advances actually arrive, whether suppliers can raise prices, and whether aggregate industry capacity additions outstrip demand.41

How to assess backlog quality using regulatory filings

  1. Open the contract disclosure Find the company on DART, Korea's electronic disclosure system, and look for the "Conclusion of Single Sales or Supply Contract" filing (in Korean filings, the single sales/supply contract disclosure). Note contract value, its ratio to revenue, the term and any deposits or prepayments in the payment terms. Check whether firm and contingent amounts are disclosed separately. If counterparties or terms are withheld as commercially sensitive, the strength of the commitment is harder to assess.43
  2. Check contract progress Even before a contract expires, its progress can be tracked in quarterly and annual reports. Korea's Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) checks whether annual reports disclose progress after the supply contract announcement, reasons for non-payment if applicable, and future plans.44 Compare the elapsed contract term with cumulative fulfillment.
  3. Review amendments and terminations Check whether an amendment has been filed (labeled as a correction of disclosed information) or a separate termination filing has appeared.
  4. Compare backlog with cash flow Read backlog in "Sales and Order Status" alongside contract liabilities (customer advances) in the financial statement notes and operating cash flow. Contract liabilities are period-end balances: new advances may be offset by revenue recognition. This test is particularly useful in shipbuilding, defense and power equipment, where advances are common; component and material suppliers are often paid after delivery.

Mandatory disclosure thresholds: KOSPI-listed companies must disclose contracts worth at least 5% of latest annual revenue (2.5% for large companies with assets of KRW 2tn or more). For KOSDAQ, the threshold is 10%, with a contract value of at least KRW 300m.43, 45 Framework agreements without fixed values or quantities, and weaker contracts whose orders depend on customer sales or that contain clauses allowing non-performance, are outside the disclosure requirement. No disclosure does not mean no contract; a disclosure does not guarantee a firm commitment.43 A company may be designated as an unfaithful disclosure corporation for terminating a contract or reducing its value by 50% or more. On KOSDAQ, this may also apply if less than 50% is fulfilled after twice the original contract term (or one year if that period is shorter). Detailed rules and exceptions are governed by the exchange's disclosure regulations.43, 46

05Backlog quality

Supply shortages raise backlog value,
but do not guarantee revenue

The evidence on the other side deserves equal weight. Where supply cannot meet demand, customers pay upfront to secure production slots and accept higher prices. But shortages can also encourage premature orders, as the gas turbine example in section 02 shows. Scarcity increases the value of backlog; it does not guarantee that all of it will become revenue.

  • Substrate and MLCC lead times exceed nine months Hana Securities estimates ABF substrates and MLCCs for AI servers now have lead times of 48-56 and 40 weeks, respectively, versus about 12 weeks each in a balanced market. Major MLCC manufacturers operated at above 95% utilization in Q3.47
  • PCB backlog: value outgrew volume at three of four suppliers Q3 backlog at five major PCB suppliers rose 22-115% QoQ. For the four that reported volume growth, implied backlog value per unit ranged from -2% to +25%, rising at three. The figures reflect both price increases and a shift toward higher-value products, so the pure pricing effect cannot be isolated. Hana Securities believes suppliers are in a position to pass through higher raw material costs.48
  • Power equipment indicators also remain strong In the same report warning about gas turbine reservations, SK Securities saw little evidence of weakening power equipment demand. Hyperscalers are placing orders directly, and high-value products such as 765 kV transmission equipment are accumulating in backlog.1 We covered power bottlenecks in NO.27.

MONITOR Customer commitments are not firm supplier backlog

NVIDIA's commitments to suppliers for components and capacity rose from $279bn to $390bn in one quarter. Most of the increase came from multiyear memory purchases.49 This is a demand signal that could support suppliers' future revenue, not evidence that the entire amount is firm supplier backlog. Minimum purchase volumes and termination or modification terms vary by contract. We discussed memory suppliers' long-term contract terms in NO.34.

Commitments also run in the opposite direction. NVIDIA's commitments of up to $32bn to neocloud providers take effect only once the customers secure financing, install and commission equipment, and obtain NVIDIA's approval. They could shrink or fall away if schedules slip.49, 50 The Q3 Form 10-Q, which should contain more detail, was not yet listed among filings as of Nov. 27. NVIDIA said it would file within a few weeks; the filing deadline is Dec. 4 US time. We examined how these commitments translate into cash flows in Research No. 12.

Read the table across. Gas turbine reservations featured attractive cash and pricing terms, but less firm commitments. The battery contracts were signed, yet volumes depended on the customer's vehicle sales. When the customer's plans changed, the contracts disappeared. What ultimately protects a backlog is less the strength of the paperwork than the customer's intention to use the product. AI components remain on track for now, with strong demand and tight supply. But neither contract terms nor the extent of duplicate orders that might disappear when supply improves can be established from public disclosures. Hence no positive confirmation on those points.

CONCLUSIONConclusion

Backlog measures commitments,
not a revenue schedule

In November's examples, the numbers themselves were rarely wrong. Reservations were identified as reservations, framework agreements as framework agreements, and L&F's delivery figures were in its quarterly filings. Different kinds of commitments were simply read as one homogeneous backlog. Whether companies made timely and sufficient disclosures is a separate question. Readers, too, can do something about it.

The value of a backlog depends less on its size than on the certainty, speed and profitability with which it becomes revenue and cash. Ask three separate questions when looking at backlog.

  1. How firm is the contract? Is it a firm order, a reservation, an MOU, a framework agreement or an initial agreement? Check whether the company distinguishes them and separates firm from contingent amounts in disclosures.
  2. Is the order profitable and cash-generative? Is there a non-refundable customer advance? Was the order priced at a profitable level? A KRW 1tn order generates KRW 50bn of operating profit at a 5% margin, or KRW 200bn at 20%. If costs exceed contract value, even an uncanceled backlog can produce losses.
  3. When will delivery take place? Are final hurdles such as acceptance testing, approvals and customer site readiness on schedule? Rather than years of backlog, examine the share expected to become revenue within one year and the amount converting each quarter.

Glossary
Order backlog
Orders received but not yet recognized as revenue. As goods are transferred or work progresses, amounts leave backlog and become revenue. Korean companies disclose this in the "Sales and Order Status" section of quarterly and annual reports.
Slot reservation
An agreement reserving a factory production slot before the exact equipment or purchase price is finalized. A reservation deposit is paid; the reservation may or may not convert into a firm order.
Customer advances
Payments received from customers before delivery. A major source of contract liabilities in financial statements; recognized as revenue when the relevant performance obligations are satisfied. Whether the payment is refundable matters.
Acceptance testing
The customer checks whether delivered goods meet contractual specifications and accepts them. In defense, revenue recognition often depends on passing this test.
Guidance
A company's own financial outlook, typically expressed as a range or target for revenue or margins and revised as circumstances change.
MOU
Memorandum of understanding. A document recording intent to enter into an agreement, generally with limited legal enforceability.
Term Sheet
An agreement on key terms such as price and quantity, ahead of a separate definitive contract.
Framework agreement
An umbrella agreement allowing orders up to a specified limit over a set period. Actual quantities and values are determined by individual call-off orders.
LTA
Long-term agreement (LTA). A multiyear supply contract for agreed quantities. Minimum purchase obligations, pricing mechanisms and remedies for breach vary by contract.
Amended disclosure
A filing revising previously disclosed information. Supply contract disclosures are often amended to reflect the actual value when the contract expires.
Lead time
Time from placing an order to receiving the goods. It generally lengthens as supply becomes tighter.
Book-to-bill ratio
Orders received in a period divided by revenue in the same period. A ratio above 1 implies backlog growth, absent other changes such as cancellations or exchange-rate movements.
CMD
Capital Markets Day. An investor event at which management presents medium- and long-term targets and strategy.
Revenue recognition over time
For long-cycle products such as ships or large equipment, where control transfers to the customer during production, revenue is recognized over time based on progress. This differs from point-in-time recognition at delivery.
ABF substrates
Advanced substrates for high-performance semiconductors such as AI chips. Long manufacturing lead times constrain supply.
MLCC
Multilayer ceramic capacitor. A small component that stores and releases electrical energy. AI servers use far more of them than conventional servers.
Neocloud providers
Emerging cloud companies that purchase NVIDIA GPUs in bulk and rent out AI computing capacity.
Onerous contract
A contract whose unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations exceed expected economic benefits. IAS 37 requires a provision for the resulting loss.
10-Q
Quarterly report filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) by listed US reporting companies. It includes details such as commitments and risks not covered in earnings announcements.
