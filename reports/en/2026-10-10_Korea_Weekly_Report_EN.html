WHAT KIND OF CORRECTION WAS THIS?

Selling pressure met high discount rates,
dragging down large caps and market breadth.

Samsung beat lowered expectations. The slide began with flows, not an earnings miss.
  • Discount-rate pressure: The U.S. 10-year yield touched 5.36% intraday Oct. 7; the Oct. 9 Treasury reading was 5.24%. Rates remained elevated ahead of Oct. 14 CPI.
  • The changing marginal buyer: Foreigners sold KRW 6.337tn and institutions KRW 2.341tn over three days, while retail absorbed KRW 6.204tn. Samsung Electronics' buyback had ended and SK hynix's was nearing completion. Retail appears to have replaced buybacks as an important source of demand (our interpretation).
  • Narrowing participation: The KOSDAQ lost only 0.11% and the Secondary Battery TOP 10 gained 5.11%, so this was no indiscriminate selloff. Yet KOSPI breadth fell from 46.3% to 36.5% to 27.7%, and KOSDAQ breadth was in the 30s for the last two sessions.
  • A different reading: Eugene Investment & Securities argues that continuing inflows into equity funds mean the end of corporate buybacks need not create an immediate gap in demand.

Q.01

Will Foreign Buying Last?

  • The won strengthened nonetheless: USD/KRW fell from 1,350.6 to 1,338.5 at the 3:30 p.m. Korean close. This does not resemble an exit accompanied by a currency selloff (our interpretation).

  • Eugene Investment & Securities attributes renewed foreign selling since September to the speed of the rise in yields. It expects the selling to ease only once yields stop climbing (Oct. 6 note).

Q.02

After September U.S. CPI,
Will the 10-Year Fall Below 5.2%?

  • U.S. Treasury 10-year yield readings: Oct. 6, 5.27%; Oct. 7, 5.28%; Oct. 8, 5.22%; Oct. 9, 5.24%. The yield reached 5.36% intraday Oct. 7, its highest since 2002.
  • September CPI is due Oct. 14 at 9:30 p.m. KST. Major forecasts: headline +0.5%-0.6% month on month and core +0.2%-0.3%, depending on the survey. Our key core-CPI reference is +0.2% month on month.In August, headline CPI was +0.4% month on month and +3.4% year on year; core was +0.3% and +2.4%, respectively.
  • The Fed raised its policy-rate range to 3.75%-4.00% in September. Minutes showed most participants considered one further increase by year-end likely appropriate. CME FedWatch priced a 19.4% chance of an October hike (Kiwoom Securities, Oct. 7).
  • Daishin Securities sees a decline in yields requiring both stabilizing oil prices as U.S.-Iran relations improve and softer September inflation. Kiwoom instead expects yields to remain high and range-bound: monetary policy limits the upside, while fiscal pressures and Treasury supply support the floor.

OpenAI's disclosed annualized revenue of roughly $50bn appeared below the approximately $70bn figure previously circulated ($68bn in some Korean reports), reviving concerns about returns on AI investment and financial transparency.

It does not mean end demand for AI suddenly fell by that amount. Higher financing costs from major fundraising by Broadcom and Oracle were also cited.
