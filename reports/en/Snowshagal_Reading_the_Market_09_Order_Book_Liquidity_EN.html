If KRW 1.8tn Changed Hands,
Why Did Prices Jump?

Trading value and trading volume add up completed trades. They cannot tell you how many buyers are waiting to take your shares at this moment. For that, you need the order book. Stock B in our quiz had plenty of trades earlier in the day but little bid depth now.

And while KRW 1.8tn sounds substantial, it was only about 6.6% of total KRX trading value that day.

That money was spread across more than 2,400 stocks and four hours. At the level of a single stock at a particular moment, the available orders could still have been thin.

Volume tells you how many shares have already changed hands. Resting bids in the order book tell you what you can sell for now.

02HOW THE MARKET WORKS

Why Selling Gets Harder:
Inside the Order Book

Open the order book and the answer becomes clearer.

Every Share Traded Has a Buyer and a Seller

Trading volumeThe number of shares exchanged in completed trades. Every share sold is also a share bought. is the number of shares that changed hands. If someone sells 100 shares, someone else has bought those same 100 shares.

That number does not tell us who was more eager to trade. High volume only means that a large number of shares changed hands between buyers and sellers.

How to Read an Order Book

Unfilled orders sit in the order bookAn order offering to buy or sell at a stated price. It has not yet been executed.. Sell orders (asks) appear above the market, and buy orders (bids) below it. The number of shares waiting at each price level is known as order-book depthThe quantity of shares resting at each price level. It shrinks when orders execute or are canceled..

The gap between those prices is the bid–ask spreadThe difference between the best ask and best bid. A narrower spread generally makes trading easier.. Prices move in set increments called tick sizesThe minimum permitted price increment. Since a 2023 rule change, it is KRW 10 for shares priced KRW 10,000–20,000 and KRW 100 for KRW 100,000–200,000.. For shares priced around KRW 10,000, each tick is KRW 10. In actively traded stocks, the spread may be only one or two ticks. Stock B's spread is ten ticks wide.

A market orderAn order to trade against available orders without setting a price. It may execute quickly, but the price is not guaranteed. does not set a minimum selling price. It matches against available bids, starting with the highest and working its way down. If there is not enough depth at the top level, it moves to the next. And if total buying interest is insufficient—or trading is halted—even a market order might not fill in full.

The difference between the price you saw when placing an order and your actual average fill is called slippageThe gap between the price seen when an order is placed and the actual average fill. It generally grows with order size and thinner bids.. It can arise when a large order sweeps multiple price levels or when quotes change while the order is being placed. The ability to buy or sell promptly without moving the price much is called liquidityThe ability to buy or sell quickly without substantially moving the price. Volume alone cannot measure it.. Deeper resting orders generally mean better immediate liquidity.

When You Cannot Sell—even for a Moment

If prices move too quickly, an exchange may slow or suspend trading. Such safeguards are intended to limit the speed of disorderly moves, not to prevent declines. Prices may fall further after trading resumes. While a halt or call auction is in effect, you cannot necessarily sell at the moment you want. The 1,637 VI activations on the first after-hours day are one example.

An index decline exceeding 8% for one minute triggers a 20-minute market-wide halt, followed by a 10-minute reopening auction. There are also 15% and 20% stages; at 20%, the session ends for the day

A VI activation puts the affected stock into a two-minute call auctionA system that collects orders for a set period and matches them at one price. Used at the open, at the close, and during volatility interruptions., with orders collected and executed together at a single price. You cannot obtain an immediate execution during those two minutes. From September 14, 2026, Nextrade (NXT) likewise switched to two-minute call auctions when VI was triggered.

According to KRX figures, that was the highest total for any half-year period. On October 15, the KOSPI fell more than 8% intraday, triggering a circuit breaker and a 20-minute trading halt. It was the tenth such KOSPI event of 2026.

A stock at its daily lower price limit cannot trade at a lower price that day. Sell orders accumulate at the limit, but they execute only when buy orders arrive. After heavy selling through a foreign securities firm's brokerage accounts in April 2023, Seoul City Gas, Daesung Holdings and Sunkwang hit the lower limit four sessions in a row. Trading remained sparse even on the morning of the fourth day. When there are sellers but no willing buyers, reported volume can actually be very low.

FURTHER READINGWho Helps Keep Quotes on the Screen?

Market makers are securities firms contracted by the exchange to post bids and asks in less actively traded stocks. The after-hours market assigned market makers to 190 stocks. Between September 14 and 29, the average value of trades they actually executed was KRW 256,000 per stock per day; 43 stocks recorded zero. These are executed-trade figures: a low total does not mean market makers posted no quotes. KRX described the period through the end of September as a trial phase.

Sidecar trading curb is triggered when KOSPI 200 futures move more than 5%, or—on KOSDAQ—KOSDAQ 150 futures move more than 6% while the KOSDAQ 150 index moves more than 3% in the same direction, with the conditions lasting one minute. Only program-trading orders are suspended for five minutes; ordinary orders from individual investors remain permitted.

Two Trading Venues — Since March 2025, many Korean stocks have traded on both the Korea Exchange (KRX) and Nextrade (NXT). Orders for the same stock may sit on two separate books. Brokers use smart order routing (SOR) to direct orders to the venue offering better execution terms.

Tick size is the minimum increment in which a price can be quoted. Following the 2023 changes, it is KRW 10 for shares trading at KRW 10,000–20,000 and KRW 100 for KRW 100,000–200,000. A bid–ask spread must be at least one tick.

For Past Activity, Look at Trading Value

Volume can mislead even when comparing how actively different stocks trade. A lower-priced stock can report far more shares traded despite the same amount of money changing hands. That is why trading valueThe total monetary value of completed trades: the sum of shares traded multiplied by their execution prices. is often the better comparison. But trading value is still a record of past trades. To estimate how much your own sell order might move the price right now, look at the bids.

Limit Orders: Choose Your Price

A limit orderAn instruction to trade at a specified price or better. A sell limit cannot execute below the stated minimum price. sets your price. When selling, it can execute only at your limit or higher. What happens if you place a sell limit order for 3,000 shares of B at KRW 9,950?

With the order book unchanged, the limit sell immediately executes 300 shares at KRW 10,000 and 700 at KRW 9,950. The remaining 2,000 shares wait as an ask at KRW 9,950. They cannot execute below that price, but they may remain unsold unless new buyers are willing to pay at least KRW 9,950. A market order, by contrast, executes against existing bids while accepting the risk of a lower price.

Four Checks Before Selling

01 How large is my order relative to the best bid?

If your sell order exceeds the shares available at the best bid, it will start filling at lower prices. A 3,000-share sale of B is ten timesthe 300 shares at the best bid, and reaches the fourth bid level.

02 How many ticks wide is the spread?

A spread of one or two ticks between the best ask and best bid is relatively tight. A ten-tick spread is a sign of a thin market.

03 Should I use a market order or a limit order?

A market sell may execute immediately, but its price can slip. If there are too few bids, even a market order may not fill completely. A limit sell protects your minimum price but may leave part of your order unfilled..

04 What kind of trading session is this?

During lightly attended sessions such as after hours, during a two-minute VI auction, or when a stock is pinned at its lower price limit, an immediate sale at your desired price may be impossible.KRX after-hours trading accepts limit orders only.
COMMON MISREADINGSPlausible, but wrong
MISREADING 01
"Volume exploded, so I can sell quickly at a fair price."
B had four times A's daily volume, but only 300 shares were bid at KRW 10,000. Selling 3,000 shares at market swept down to KRW 9,800, reducing proceeds by KRW 335,000. Check the order book before the volume rankings.
MISREADING 02
"There are plenty of bids, so the stock must be headed higher."
Resting orders can be canceled or changed at any time before execution. A deep bid book may reduce the price impact of selling right now. It does not predict where the stock will trade next. Depth is not a directional forecast.
MISREADING 03
"Hardly any shares traded, so nobody wants to sell."
Sell orders can pile up at the daily lower price limit, yet not execute because no one is bidding. Reported volume will then be low. A low-volume day can be a day when sellers could not get out.
EPISODE 09 IN ONE SENTENCE

Volume and trading value describe past trades; bid depth and the spread describe current orders. Before selling, check what is available now.

If your order is larger than the available shares at the best bid, it can push through to lower prices. A volume ranking cannot tell you that.

Figures Not Used—and How to Interpret the Data

Real-stock order-book depth Quotes change moment by moment and are not preserved in a public historical order-book record here. That is why our order books and simulator use fictional stocks.

After-hours bid–ask spreads We could not find a comparable after-hours versus regular-session spread dataset. This article therefore uses VI counts and reported high–low price moves only.

Comparing VI activation counts The 1,637 and 400 totals count cases in which static and dynamic VI were triggered together twice—roughly a fourfold difference. Counting overlaps once gives 1,112 and 391, about 2.8 times. VI activations are not a direct measure of liquidity. After-hours trading lasts four hours versus six and a half hours for the regular session; stock mix and repeated activations in the same stock also matter. A difference in counts alone cannot establish the cause.

Stock-level high–low volatility Reports from the same day cite 2.9% for KOSPI (4.1% regular session) and 4.6% for KOSDAQ (5.8% regular session). Because sources describe the measure inconsistently—as an index move or an average across stocks—we do not use those figures in the main analysis.

Stock B's daily volume At 12m shares and KRW 10,000 per share, the notional trading value would be KRW 120bn. As a fictional stock, B is assumed to have traded heavily on morning news and then quieted down.
