About 10 minutes · 1 quiz · 1 interactive exercise

The essentialsIn three points
  • The same stock trades on both the Korea Exchange (KRX) and Nextrade (NXT). If you don't choose a venue, your broker's smart order router (SOR) decides where to send your order.
  • An order that can trade immediately is compared on total execution cost, including fees. At the same share price, Nextrade's lower venue fee may give it the edge.
  • An order that must wait in the order book is routed based on its likelihood of execution. Brokers measure that differently. A resting order waits for a counterparty on the single venue chosen by SOR. That venue also determines how long the order remains active.

01The question

One stock,
two markets

Suppose you buy 100 shares of Gana Electronics, a fictional company. You never chose a trading venue, yet your tradeA trade takes place when a buy order matches a sell order at an agreed price. confirmation says “NXT.”

When Nextrade launched in spring 2025, some investors complained that their orders remained unfilled. Both the “NXT” confirmation and the unfilled orders make more sense once you know that the two venues maintain separate order books.

Since March 2025, Korean stocks have traded on two venues: the established Korea Exchange (KRX)KRX operates the KOSPI and KOSDAQ markets. It is the primary venue for trading listed Korean shares.and a new alternative trading system (ATS),A licensed venue for trading listed shares outside the Korea Exchange. Its formal Korean legal category is a multilateral trading company. Nextrade (NXT)Korea's first ATS, launched in March 2025. It offers trading in roughly 600 KRX-listed stocks.. You own the same shares in the same brokerage account whichever venue you use. But the two order books may show different prices and quantities.

If you leave the venue unspecified, your broker's SORSmart Order Routing (SOR) is a broker-operated system that automatically directs an order to the venue it judges more favorable under its published execution policy.chooses between KRX and NXT. SOR is the default on most Korean stock-trading apps.

Nearly 60% of routed shares went to Nextrade

Regulatory data made public through Korea's National Assembly in late October put a number on that choice. From January through August 2026, the SOR systems of 10 major brokerages routed 64.2 billion shares during regular trading hoursThe KRX's standard trading session, from 9:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Korea time. , of which 59.9%went to Nextrade.

So who decides
where my order goes?

The short answer
If you don't specify a venue, SOR follows the broker's published routing policy. The priorities differ depending on whether your order can execute immediately or must wait in the book.

02The mechanics

How SOR chooses

SOR faces two main situations: your order can trade immediately, or it has to sit at your chosen price until someone takes the other side.

Buy nowSomeone is offering shares at ₩10,000. If you place a buy order at ₩10,000, it can match immediately. This is a taker orderAn order that immediately trades against existing orders in the book—for example, a market order or a buy limit order priced at or above the best ask..
Wait to buyYou want to buy at ₩9,990, but nobody is selling at that price. Your order rests in the order bookA live list of outstanding buy and sell orders, organized by price.until a seller appears. This is a maker orderAn order that adds liquidity to the book by waiting for an incoming order to match against it..

A single order can behave as both: some shares may fill immediately while the remainder rests in the book. “Maker” and “taker” describe how an order interacts with available liquidity, not which button you press. SOR evaluates them differently.

Not all orders can be split between venues.

In Scenario 3, the 300-share buy is split between the two venues. Immediately executable quantities can be combined this way, but resting orders are generally placed on one venue. In Scenario 4, choosing which queue to join is the key decision.

A single order may mix both types. In an example from Korea Investment & Securities, a 150-share buy immediately fills 50 shares on NXT and 30 on KRX, while the remaining 70 are posted on NXT, where execution is judged more likely.

Is the shorter queue always faster?

In Scenario 4, NXT had a shorter buy queue—600 shares ahead of you—but traded only 8,000 shares that day. KRX had 3,000 shares ahead, but daily volume of 80,000. Think of two shops: a short queue can move slowly, while a long one can clear quickly.

But orders do not arrive at a steady pace. Even if a stock trades heavily, your bid may never fill unless someone agrees to sell at your price. Dividing queue size by daily volume is only a rough indicator of execution prospects—not an actual probability of getting filled.

The other venue traded at my price. Why didn't I get filled?

Your resting order sits in only one venue's order book. Even if a trade occurs at the same price elsewhere, your order needs a matching seller on the venue where it was posted.

This also helps explain the complaints about unfilled orders raised in Chapter 1.

Who sets the rules?

Brokers set their own SOR policies because they have a best-execution obligationA broker's duty to seek the most favorable reasonably available terms when handling a client's order. under Article 68 of Korea's Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act. The duty became especially relevant when the market expanded to two venues in March 2025. A broker has three responsibilities.

Set and publish a policyThe broker must publish how it weighs price, fees, likelihood of execution, and other considerations. This is its best-execution policyThe broker's published rules explaining how it meets its best-execution duty, including which factors govern venue selection and their priority..
Explain it before tradingClients must receive a description of the policy before placing orders.
Review it every three monthsBrokers must periodically review the policy and publish changes.

SOR compares quotes in a consolidated order-book viewA display combining quotations from KRX and NXT for comparison. The books remain separate; orders are sent to each venue individually.and follows these rules. Most Korean brokers use either K-SOR from KoscomA Korean securities-market technology provider whose largest shareholder is the Korea Exchange.or NextSOR from Nextrade. Kiwoom Securities operates its own system.

When does SOR compare venues?

SOR can compare venues when both support the same kind of trading at the same time. After the KRX launched after-market tradingTrading after the regular session: KRX runs 4–8 p.m.; Nextrade runs 3:40–8 p.m.on September 14, 2026, that overlap extended to 8 p.m. Outside these windows, only one venue may be open, or the two may use different matching methods (seePart 18). Your broker then routes orders according to its preset arrangements.

Nextrade offers roughly 600 stocks; the others trade only on KRX. KRX trading can also be restricted by session or by an individual stock's status. ETFs and ETNs are excluded from its after-market session.

How long does an unfilled order remain open?

That depends on the venue. An order resting on KRX during regular trading expires if it is still unfilled at 3:30 p.m.; it does not carry over into the after-market. On Nextrade, a standard limit order can remain active through the after-market until 8:00 p.m., unless you cancel it. Even with the same stock, price and quantity, two orders can therefore have different lifespans depending on where SOR sends them.

Some brokerages can transfer the unfilled balance of a KRX order to NXT upon request. Availability depends on the brokerage’s settings and the order type.

You can choose the venue yourself

SOR is only the default. If you explicitly select KRX or NXT on the order screen, the broker follows your specific instructionA customer's explicit choice of trading venue. It takes precedence over the broker's standard best-execution routing policy.rather than its usual routing preference.

LEARN MOREWhen SOR fails or you amend an order

If SOR goes down—Procedures vary by brokerage. Some redirect orders to a preset venue, usually KRX; others suspend SOR orders and require you to choose a venue manually. During the September 14, 2026 outage, Samsung Securities redirected orders to KRX but asked customers to cancel outstanding orders that had not been cancelled automatically. When an outage is announced, check your order status, cancellations and executions yourself.

If you amend an order, SOR may decide again —Some brokers cancel the unfilled balance and treat an amendment as a new order, with a fresh venue decision. An existing order cannot simply be amended across venues.

03How to use this

What to check
before and after trading

So what should you look for in a stock-trading app?

Four things worth checking

01Before trading: the venue field

Check whether the order screen says SOR, KRX, or NXT. If you choose a venue, the order goes only there .

02Before trading: is the stock available on NXT?

Stocks not supported by NXT trade on KRX only. Check your broker’s app or Nextrade’s eligible-stock list to see where a stock can trade. Some stocks are excluded to comply with volume limits (see “Learn More” below).

03After trading: where did the fill occur?

Check the execution details. If SOR split your order, the fills may appear on both venues. Also check which venue is still holding any unfilled shares (see Chapter 2).

04If an execution looks unusual

If an order is slow to fill or remains unfilled unexpectedly, read your broker's best-execution policy, especially how it evaluates resting orders . You can also request supporting records to see whether the order followed that policy.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONSSounds plausible—but isn't
MYTH 01
“SOR always gets me the lowest price.”
SOR assesses the order books at the time of routing. Brokers must establish, monitor, and follow reasonable best-execution procedures, but quotes and available shares can change afterward. SOR cannot guarantee the eventual fill price or even a fill.
MYTH 02
“If my price traded on another venue, my order should have filled.”
A resting order can match only against orders on the venue where SOR posted it. A trade at the same price elsewhere is separate.
MYTH 03
“SOR compares the two venues all day.”
It compares them only while both are open for the same trading method: principally during the regular session and the 4–8 p.m. after-market overlap.

DEEPER DIVEA brief history of SOR—and two points of contention

SOR has been in the news regularly since Nextrade opened.

  1. 24.6

    The FSS issued best-execution guidance, establishing a framework for broker policies.

  2. 25.3.4

    Nextrade opened, bringing SOR-based routing into use.

  3. 25.9

    After criticism that SOR favored Nextrade, financial regulators announced a review of order allocation practices.

  4. 25.12.15

    KRX temporarily lowered its fees to NXT levels for two months. The cut covered trades through February 13; normal rates returned February 19 after the Lunar New Year holiday.

  5. 26.9.14

    KRX launched its after-market. Some brokers experienced SOR problems that morning.

  6. 26.10

    Data on how 10 major brokers allocated SOR orders was disclosed through the National Assembly (Chapter 1). According to the FSS’s National Assembly audit response, inspections repeatedly identified failures to split market orders across venues. The regulator also reported 63 best-execution-related complaints from Nextrade’s launch through August 2026.

Two debates stand out.

Immediately executable ordersShould Nextrade always get priority at identical quoted prices because its fees are lower? The industry view is that the comparison should be based on total value, including fees.
Resting ordersWhich venue offers a better chance of a fill: the one with a shorter queue or the one with more trading activity? That is the trade-off illustrated by the two shop queues in Chapter 2.
DEEPER DIVENextrade's volume caps versus the 59.9% figure

Two separate caps —Nextrade is subject to two trading-volume capsLimits on Nextrade trading activity. Broadly, its total traded share volume must be no more than 15% of KRX's total, while an individual stock's main-market volume is normally capped at 30% of that stock's KRX regular-market volume. The per-stock limit is temporarily deferred., calculated using the previous six months' volume.

Why some stocks disappear from NXT —To stay within these caps, Nextrade removes heavily traded stocks from its eligible list. The list grew to 796 by late March 2025, then shrank to 603 by late October 2026. Quick glossary
SOR
Smart Order Routing: software that sends orders to KRX or NXT according to a broker's execution policy.
Best-execution duty
A broker's legal duty to seek favorable execution terms for client orders (Article 68 of Korea's Capital Markets Act).
Best-execution policy
A broker's published method for fulfilling that duty, reviewed every three months.
Taker order
An order that immediately matches an existing order in the book.
Maker order
An order that rests in the book, waiting for a counterparty.
Consolidated quote view
Quotes from both venues displayed together for comparison; the underlying order books remain separate.
K-SOR / NextSOR
SOR platforms supplied to brokers by Koscom and Nextrade.
Specific instruction
An investor's explicit choice of venue, which takes priority over a broker's usual routing policy.
Venue trading fee
An exchange fee based on transaction value. It contributes to investors' overall trading costs.
Alternative trading system
A regulated venue for trading listed shares outside KRX. Nextrade is Korea's first; its legal category is a multilateral trading company.
Trading-volume cap
Restrictions on Nextrade's trading volume: a market-wide 15% ratio versus KRX and a 30% per-stock regular-market ratio (the latter deferred until September 2027, subject meanwhile to a below-100% threshold).
Tick size
The smallest permitted price increment; ₩10 for Korean shares trading between ₩5,000 and ₩20,000.
Real-world cases
Apr 2025 · “I placed an order, but it didn't fill”

Complaints emerged that orders routed by the default SOR settings on brokerage apps had gone unfilled. A petition calling for the closure of the new trading venue even appeared on the National Assembly's online petition platform.

Dec 2025–Feb 2026 · Two months of matching fees

A report said Nextrade's trading value dropped about 37% on the first day KRX cut its fees to NXT's level. Other data showed NXT's regular-session trading value relative to KRX rising from 27% in November 2025 to 29% in January 2026. A single day's trading value and monthly market-share figures use different measures, so neither alone proves what the fee cut caused.

Sep 14, 2026 · SOR disruption on the first after-market day

At Mirae Asset Securities and Samsung Securities, both using NextSOR, Nextrade pre-market orders experienced lookup and cancellation problems. Both brokers redirected orders to KRX. The disruption lasted until about 8:50 a.m. and was resolved as the regular market opened.

Reading the numbers carefully

59.9% —The 59.9% is based on the number of shares in routed orders, NOT the number of individual orders. Of the 64,214,220,000 shares allocated by the SOR systems of 10 major brokerages in the regular session from January to August 2026, 38,442,220,000 were routed to NXT. These are not executed shares. The data also predate the KRX after-market introduced in September.

By brokerage —Mirae Asset 67.7%; Korea Investment 64.7%; Daishin 62.3%; Kiwoom 59.9%; Samsung 59.3%; NH 58.1%; Shinhan 53.1%; Hana 51.2%; KB 48.2%; Meritz 45.1%. Differences may reflect clients' preferred stocks, trading times and order mix, so these shares do not rank execution quality.

How brokers assess resting orders (as reported) —Samsung, Korea Investment, KB and Kiwoom favor the market with fewer same-side shares queued at the order price. Meritz compares depth across multiple price levels. Mirae Asset uses outstanding and cumulative order depth. Daishin, NH and Shinhan compare queue size with trading volume. Our two simulator rules are simplified illustrations.

Fee rates —KRX charges 0.0022763% of transaction value, often rounded to 0.0023% in news reports; this article and simulator use the exact rate. NXT charges 0.00182% for taker orders and 0.00134% for maker orders. Brokerage fee schedules may include additional settlement charges. Korea Investment & Securities says its SOR uses standard routing fees, rather than client-negotiated commissions, when evaluating markets.

“About ₩5” —Compares venue fees only on an immediate ₩1 million purchase (₩22.763 − ₩18.2 = about ₩4.6).

796 → 603 stocks —Nextrade launched with 10 stocks on March 4, 2025, expanded to 796 by month-end, and had 603 as of late October 2026 reporting. While the per-stock cap is deferred, a separate condition limits NXT's eligible-stock list to no more than 700.

How the volume caps are calculated —Based on the previous six months of continuous-trading volume as of month-end. The market-wide 15% ratio compares overall NXT and KRX volume. The per-stock 30% ratio compares NXT main-market volume with KRX regular-market volume for the same stock (9 a.m.–3:20 p.m.).

February 19 —KRX's temporary fee rate last applied to trades on February 13, 2026. The original rate returned February 19, the first trading day after the Lunar New Year holiday, according to brokerage notices.

When SOR compares markets —Primarily during the regular session and the 4–8 p.m. overlapping after-market. Handling around the opening and closing auctions varies somewhat by brokerage policy.

We leave unverified numbers out rather than fill the gaps.
