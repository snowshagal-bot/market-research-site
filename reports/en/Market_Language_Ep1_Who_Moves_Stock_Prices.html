02
STRUCTURE & TERMS

The Biggest Buyer Is Not Always
the Side Moving the Market

Market flows are a record of who bought and who sold today. In Korea, the exchange publishes these flows each day by grouping investors into retail · foreign · institutionalThe three investor groups KRX reports each day. “Retail” means individual investors, “foreign” means overseas capital, and “institutions” aggregate pension funds, asset managers, insurers, and others..

01
Net Buying · Net SellingThe basic unit of flow reporting

The amount bought minus the amount sold.

Foreign-investor trading on a sample day
BUY₩8tn
SELL₩11tn
NET BUYING₩8tn − ₩11tn = −₩3tn

A news report would call this ₩3tn of net selling. The same line hides the fact that foreign investors also bought ₩8tn worth that day.

COMMON MISREAD

“Retail investors net-bought ₩5tn, so retail drove the market higher.”

BETTER READING

They may simply have absorbed stock being dumped during a sharp decline. Buying the most and leading the market are not the same thing.

02
The Last Trade Sets the PriceWhat matters more than net buying

Price is set not by the total amount traded, but by the level at which the latest trade occurs.

Every completed trade pairs a buyer and a seller for the same value.

Across the market as a whole, total purchases and total sales are always equal.

But once trades are split by investor type, the picture changes: foreign investors can be net sellers while retail investors are net buyers.

Order-book ladder (example)
69,800SELLSeller’s first offer. No buyer takes it.
69,500SELLThe seller lowers the offer. The urgency is still on the sell side.
69,200TRADEThe trade occurs only after the seller comes down to the waiting buyer’s bid. This is where price is set in the example.

When sellers move down while buyers hold their bids, as in this example, the price in that interval is effectively being set by the sell side.

A real trading day is far more complex. Timing, order aggressiveness, and stock-level concentration are all compressed into a single net-buying figure. A single day’s net flow cannot tell you with certainty which investor group drove prices lower.

Even after buying trillions of won,
you may still have been following the market.

Because price is determined by where trades clear, not by how much one side bought.

Same Net Buying, Two Different Faces

Buying that absorbs falling supply is different from buying that chases offers and pushes price higher. But one day’s aggregate number cannot cleanly separate the two.

Absorbing supply

Buying stock as sellers lower their prices.

The amount can be large without meaning the buyer itself pushed prices higher.

Driving the market

Buying aggressively by lifting higher offers.

Even a smaller amount can have a greater chance of moving the index.

There is no absolute dividing line. Put net buying next to the day’s price direction and intraday timing and you begin to see clues.

When heavy net buying coincides with a decline, the buying may have been absorbing supply into weakness. Intraday timing and stock-level concentration are needed to judge further.

If net buying persists alongside gains in the largest-cap stocks, it is a clue that the buyer may be driving the market. Persistence, trading value, and broader participation are still needed before calling it leadership.

03
Market Cap · Index Concentration · Market BreadthWhy the index can differ from investors’ experience

The KOSPI is a weighted average that uses market capitalizationShare price × shares outstanding. It is the market value of the entire listed company. The larger it is, the more influence the stock has on the index. as its weight.

The heaviest stocks can effectively become the index. Many smaller stocks can fall without pulling the index down.

The result created by those weights is index concentration; counting advancers and decliners gives you market breadth. This is how the index can look healthy while an investor’s own portfolio feels weak.

In the real KOSPI, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix occupy this heavyweight role. That is why the index should be read alongside the number of advancing stocksThe number of stocks that rose that day. Read together with decliners, it shows how broadly gains were distributed..

One-Line Glossary
Cash equities
The market where the shares themselves are bought and sold, as opposed to futures or options.
Large-cap stock
A stock with a large market capitalization and therefore greater index weight.
KOSPI 200
An index of 200 leading KOSPI stocks; a benchmark for futures, options, and many funds.
Change of hands
A transfer of ownership in the same stock from one investor group to another.
Trading value
The total value of trades executed that day; a gauge of how much money is circulating through the market.
market breadth
The balance between the number of advancing and declining stocks.
Price priority · Time priority
KRX’s order-matching rules: better prices take priority; at the same price, earlier orders are filled first.

That is the structure.

03
HOW TO READ IT & REVIEW

When You See This Headline,
Check Three Things

A COMMON HEADLINE

“Foreign investors net-buy for a third straight day; institutions turn net sellers after one day”

1
Who bought?

Net buying tells you only the difference between purchases and sales. Stop there, and you have read only half the story.

2
Where did trades clear?

If prices fell sharply the same day, aggressive sell orders likely consumed bids, or waiting buyers moved their bids lower. But one day’s net flows cannot identify with certainty which investor group drove the decline.

3
What happened to the largest-cap stocks?

If buying was concentrated in a few heavyweight stocks, the index move may largely reflect what happened in those names.

CAUTION
Easy-to-Misread HeadlinesHeadline traps
TRAP 01
“Retail investors bought ₩5tn and defended the market.”
Calling it “defense” requires another piece of evidence: did the decline narrow, or did prices turn when retail buying intensified? Absorbing supply and stopping a decline are not the same.
TRAP 02
“Foreign investors net-buy for three straight days: trend reversal.”
One day’s flows reflect one day’s conditions. It is common to buy for three days and sell it back in one. Only after checking persistence and the breadth of the buying can you talk about a genuine change in stance.
SCENARIO
KOSPI−4%
Retail+₩5tn
Foreign−₩3tn
Institutions−₩2tn

Did retail investors drive the market?

CHECK THE READING
CANNOT CONCLUDE Retail investors did absorb the stock being sold. But a −4% decline is more consistent with aggressive sell orders consuming bids, or waiting buyers retreating to lower prices.
You still cannot determine which investor group drove price from one day’s net-flow totals alone. You need to know who kept lowering offers and how the largest-cap stocks moved.

EPISODE 2 When Cash and Futures Diverge
Actual Market Cases Used in This Episode
2026.07.16 · Retail +₩3.66tn net buying, KOSPI −6.37%

Retail investors absorbed stock sold by foreign investors (−₩1.38tn) and institutions (−₩2.37tn). The index gave up the 7,000 level in a single day—an example of heavy net buying and a sharp market decline occurring together.

2026.07.22 · Foreign investors +₩2.62tn; index gives back 368pt from the high

SK hynix alone accounted for 48% of foreign net buying. Stocks that institutions had bought the previous day changed hands as institutions sold and foreign investors absorbed them. On the KOSPI, 469 of 915 stocks advanced—roughly half the market held up.
