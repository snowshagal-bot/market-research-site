SUMMARY · FIVE TAKEAWAYS

  1. EV demand has stalled. U.S. EV sales fell 42% in September. Europe grew, but the three Korean cell makers' combined share dropped from 55% in 2023 to 29% in January–September 2026.
  2. ESS is moving to the center of the revenue mix. Samsung SDI's Q4 ESS revenue is projected to overtake automotive batteries for the first time (Hana and iM Securities). Hana expects LG Energy Solution's ESS revenue to reach ₩25.1 trillion by 2028.
  3. The driver is a shift away from Chinese supply. Under Yuanta Securities' scenario, if tariffs apply and tax-credit benefits are passed through to buyers, the effective cost of Korean cells made in North America could be 40–55% lower than importing Chinese cells directly.
  4. But tax credits still account for much of the profit. Sell-side estimates put U.S. advanced manufacturing production credits (AMPC) at 50–80% of LG Energy Solution's 2028 operating profit (iM, Yuanta and Hana), and 87% of Samsung SDI's (Hana).
  5. Three things to watch. How quickly orders convert into revenue; earnings excluding tax credits and one-offs; and enforcement of restrictions on Chinese-linked suppliers.

The KOSPI fell 11% over Nov. 5–6, with semiconductors and batteries selling off together. Samsung SDI lost 8.1% on Nov. 6 alone.1

Over a longer horizon, the picture changes. From the KOSPI's June 22 peak, the index shed 44.5%, while LG Energy Solution (LGES) and Samsung SDI declined only about 12% each. L&F fell 40%, and EcoPro BM lost 50%.1

U.S. EV sales fell by more than 40% in September. Yet cell makers held up relatively well, partly on expectations for battery energy storage systems (BESS) serving the U.S. electricity grid.

The earnings breakdown tells a more complicated story. Of LG Energy Solution's ₩756.0 billion in Q3 operating profit, ₩416.9 billion came from U.S. advanced manufacturing production credits (AMPC). iM Securities estimates another roughly ₩320 billion consisted of one-off gains, including compensation tied to the termination of a joint venture. After non-operating losses, the company posted a ₩296.9 billion net loss. Winning more orders and generating sustainable profit from those orders remain two different things.2, 3, 4

01EV Demand Has Stalled

U.S. EV sales plunged more than 40% year on year;
Korean cells lost ground to Chinese rivals in Europe

EV batteries face headwinds in both markets: weak demand in the United States and shrinking market share in Europe. The U.S. decline also reflects an unusually strong year-earlier comparison ahead of the consumer tax credit's expiration.

What matters

  • Korean suppliers' EV battery shipments in the U.S. also plunged. In September, U.S. EV battery shipments fell 69.4% year on year for LG Energy Solution, 76.0% for SK On and 88.1% for Samsung SDI.5
  • Chinese cells captured more of Europe's growth. In September, European shipments from the three Korean makers rose 15%, versus 58% for CATL. The Korean trio's market share was 29%, down 8 percentage points from a year earlier.7
  • That does not mean all EV demand has stopped. LG Energy Solution said its shipments of both EV pouch cells and cylindrical cells increased in Q3.8
  • It is too early to price in an EV recovery. Hana Securities argued on Nov. 3 that EV subsidies were not a focal issue in the U.S. midterms, and that earnings forecasts should not assume a rebound in American EV demand.9

03Why Korean-Made Cells?

Tariffs and tax-credit requirements are shutting out Chinese cells;
Korean cells made in North America are filling the gap

The U.S. ESS market was already substantial. What has changed is who supplies the cells.

Five barriers facing Chinese-made cells

The first three affect cell economics and tax-credit eligibility; the last two apply only to certain equipment or purchasers.

Cell costs and tax-credit eligibility
Tariffs2026.07.24~Chinese ESS batteries face a combined 40.9% tariff: 3.4% base duty + an existing 25% Section 301 tariff + a 12.5% forced-labor-related Section 301 surcharge.14
Prohibited foreign entities (PFEs)Phased in during 2026–27Ownership and control, not simply country of origin, matter. Tax credits may be unavailable if a Chinese parent owns more than 50%; a single covered entity owns at least 25% (or covered entities together at least 40%); covered-entity debt exceeds 15%; or contractual rights confer effective control. The factory's location does not remove those restrictions.6
Material Assistance Cost Ratio (MACR)2026 → 2028Tax credits require a minimum share of costs not attributable to PFE involvement. The thresholds rise from 60% to 65% to 70% for Section 45X, and from 55% to 60% to 65% for Section 48E. With cells accounting for roughly 52% of ESS costs, sourcing cells from a Chinese supplier could make the 2027 threshold of 60% difficult to meet.6
Equipment- and buyer-specific restrictions
Grid security2026.07~08Under Executive Order 14421, the U.S. Department of Energy can prohibit or impose conditions on individual transactions involving power-grid equipment linked to countries of concern, including BESS and inverters. It is not a blanket ban. The FCC narrowed its inverter certification restrictions in August, exempting inverters meeting Section 45X eligibility guidance and previously certified models.15, 16
Defense procurement ban2027.10.01The Defense Department is barred from buying batteries from six companies—CATL, BYD, Envision, EVE, Gotion and Hithium. The restriction does not apply to civilian grid-scale ESS purchases.17

Sources: Yuanta Securities; tariff analyses (including Holland & Knight); U.S. DOE executive order and FCC notice commentary; Reuters reporting

Moving away from China does not automatically mean buying from Korea. The same July measures imposed tariffs of up to 12.5%, including baseline duties, on certain Korean imports (subject to product exemptions). A Section 301 investigation into structural excess capacity, due for a decision by March 2027, also covers 16 economies, including China and South Korea. This is why cells made in North American factories have an advantage.14, 18

Cells made in Ulsan or Seosan may help an ESS project satisfy Section 48E's non-PFE requirements, but their Korean manufacturer does not qualify for Section 45X on that production. Even with Korean-made cells, PFE-linked cathode materials or other inputs may affect the MACR calculation. LG Energy Solution said in its Q3 results that it had expanded non-PFE cathode sourcing and signed long-term North American lithium supply agreements.6, 8

The existing power grid—not data centers

Hana Securities (Nov. 6)
  • Rising Korean ESS orders do not primarily reflect expectations for on-site data-center generation. That segment remains small.
  • Instead, utilities in the established U.S. grid-storage market—roughly 70 GWh a year—are shifting orders away from Chinese cell suppliers toward Korean manufacturers.
  • Hana sees diversification away from China as bipartisan, supporting a durable benefit.

Tax-credit provisions remain in law through 2029

Yuanta Securities (Nov. 5)
  • Section 45X credits at full value through 2029 and Section 48E credits through 2033 are established in statute. Changes would require legislation passed by both houses of Congress and signed by the president.
  • Yuanta judges that the new Congress taking office in January 2027 following the midterms significantly reduces the risk of early cuts through budget reconciliation, which allows qualifying fiscal legislation to pass the Senate by a simple majority.
  • The outgoing Congress remains in session through year-end. Tariff enforcement and implementation of PFE rules remain executive-branch powers, so Yuanta expects limited changes before January 2029.
04How Tight Will Supply Be?

A potential shortage of qualified cells in the second half of 2027;
analysts disagree on how much data centers matter

Under Yuanta Securities' base case, North American demand for non-Chinese qualifying cells reaches 85 GWh in 2027 against 78 GWh of supply—a roughly 7 GWh shortfall. The firm expects an approximately 21 GWh exemption pool still eligible to use Chinese cells to be largely exhausted in the first half, concentrating the shortage in the second half.

Shortage or overcapacity?

Qualified cells will be in short supply in 2027

Yuanta Securities (Nov. 5)
  • Exemptions for Chinese cells are expected to be exhausted in H1 2027, and Korean manufacturers' North American lines are booked through 2028.
  • The base case shows a shortfall of about 7 GWh, but demand and supply ranges overlap: this is a conditional forecast, not a certainty.

Capacity could grow faster than demand

LS Securities (Nov. 4)
  • The combined ESS manufacturing capacity of the three Korean cell makers may increase faster than demand.
  • LS doubts that U.S. ESS demand alone can lift utilization beyond 80–90%.

A temporary shortage of eligible cells in 2027 and subsequent capacity oversupply can coexist. Nor does a shortage automatically guarantee higher prices or margins: long-term contract pricing, raw-material indexation and tax-credit-sharing terms matter.6, 19

Still marginal

Hana Securities (Nov. 6)
  • Some long-term contracts cover on-site power for data centers, but volumes remain small.
  • Even if some new data-center construction stalls, Hana expects a limited impact on Korea's ESS industry.

  • Of 106 GWh in expected North American cell demand for 2027, Yuanta assigns 18 GWh to behind-the-meter (BTM) storage at customer sites, including data centers, and server backup batteries.
  • The argument: if data centers have to bear grid-upgrade costs, they may add on-site storage rather than wait for interconnection.

What matters

  • The phase-down begins in 2030. For the same output, the 2030 AMPC amount is 75% of its 2029 value, falling to 25% by 2032. Credits are also unavailable for cells or modules that fail PFE or MACR rules even while the statute remains in force. The key is how much profit manufacturers can build without the credit before then.6
  • Hana Securities' view. Hana believes ESS and AMPC could support 2028 net profit of ₩3.15 trillion for LGES and ₩2.26 trillion for Samsung SDI even if EV revenue stalls and EV losses persist.9
  • Estimates differ on when ESS becomes profitable without AMPC. Excluding AMPC, Hana forecasts a ₩0.30 trillion operating profit for LGES's ESS business in 2027, while Yuanta expects a ₩0.20 trillion loss (turning to a ₩0.26 trillion profit in 2028). Both Hana and Yuanta project continued EV segment losses through 2028.6, 9, 13
  • Not every order is worth taking. On Nov. 5, LG Energy Solution said it would not participate in Korea's third centralized ESS capacity procurement auction (1,180 MW, about 7 GWh), citing the need to fulfill existing commitments first. Industry observers point to clearing bids falling from the ₩30/kWh range in the first auction to the ₩20/kWh range in the second. Domestic auction economics differ from the U.S. market.20
06Lithium's 41% Decline: Two Interpretations

Lithium prices dropped 41% in two months;
is demand being pulled forward—or are costs finally easing?

Spot lithium carbonate traded around $14,000 per metric ton in early November. The same decline has led brokerages to opposing interpretations.21

Demand was pulled forward from next year

Eugene Investment & Securities (Nov. 4)
  • Chinese battery manufacturers accelerated ESS-cell production and shipments ahead of a 2% consumption tax in September and the planned elimination of export tax rebates next January.
  • Payback could come in 2027 through weaker cell production and reduced lithium purchases.
  • Restarting idled Australian mines and the release of speculative inventories added pressure, although Eugene considers the decline excessive relative to fundamentals.

Lower input costs should widen margins

Yuanta Securities (Nov. 5)
  • Lithium carbonate May futures exceeded CNY 200,000 per metric ton, before spot prices fell to CNY 96,900–98,300 by late October.
  • Yuanta estimates this cuts cell costs by about CNY 0.06 per Wh, or roughly 15% of the cell price. Allowing for procurement lags, it expects ESS margins to improve starting in H1 2027.
  • U.S. utility-scale ESS system prices have fallen only about 2% over the past year.

What matters

  • Hana views CATL's margin decline—from 20% to 12%—as largely a China-specific story. It attributes the squeeze to the reinstatement of a 2% battery consumption tax in September (rising to 4% in September 2027) and a larger share of less-profitable domestic ESS sales. With export rebates also scheduled to end in 2027, Hana sees limited read-through to Korean manufacturers.7
  • Watch the gap between shipments and installations. The question is whether Chinese producers cut lithium purchases after shipping batteries ahead of demand (Eugene Investment & Securities).21

These three risks primarily concern supply and profitability rather than demand. None of the brokerage reports reviewed here cut its U.S. ESS demand forecast.

CONCLUSION

The earnings engine is shifting from EVs to grid-scale storage—
but its fuel is still government policy

U.S. grid storage is filling the gap left by weaker American EV demand. Tariffs and tax-credit eligibility rules restricting Chinese cells have directed orders toward Korean makers with North American factories. Yet analysts estimate that Section 45X credits will account for 50–80% of LG Energy Solution's 2028 operating profit and 87% of Samsung SDI's (Hana). In Q3, little profit remained after excluding tax credits and one-off gains, and LGES posted a net loss. The earnings recovery becomes convincing not when the backlog grows, but when companies earn money without tax credits or exceptional items. Three tests matter.

  1. Are orders becoming revenue?Watch whether LG Energy Solution announces the remaining 40–60 GWh of its approximately 90 GWh new-order target (NH and iM estimates) in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027. NH believes some orders could slip into next year. Also watch whether Samsung SDI reaches its target of more than 120 GWh.
  2. What profit remains after credits and one-offs?When will ESS operating profit turn positive excluding AMPC and one-time gains? Hana expects a ₩0.30 trillion ESS operating profit at LGES in 2027; Yuanta forecasts a ₩0.20 trillion loss.
  3. How will restrictions on Chinese supply be enforced?Watch the expected Nov. 10 notice extending China's export-control suspension, the Dec. 31 safe-harbor table, higher MACR thresholds in 2027 and the October defense procurement ban. Also monitor any action affecting South Korea under the Section 301 excess-capacity investigation.

Glossary
ESS·BESS
A system that stores electricity for later release. When batteries are the storage medium, it is called a battery energy storage system (BESS).
Front-of-meter (FTM) vs. behind-the-meter (BTM)
FTM systems sit on the utility or grid side of the meter, near power generation and transmission. BTM systems sit on customers' premises, such as factories and data centers.
GW vs. GWh
GW measures the power delivered at a given moment; GWh measures the energy stored. A 1 GW system running for four hours delivers 4 GWh.
AMPC(45X)
The U.S. advanced manufacturing production credit, awarded for qualifying cells ($35/kWh) and modules ($10/kWh) manufactured in the United States.
48E
An investment tax credit for ESS project owners. The base rate is 6%, rising to 30% with prevailing-wage and apprenticeship requirements, and potentially 50% with domestic-content or energy-community bonuses. ESS projects can qualify if construction begins through 2033, with credit levels stepping down to 75% in 2034 and 50% in 2035. The project as a whole must satisfy MACR thresholds.
MACR
Material Assistance Cost Ratio: the share of production or project costs not attributable to a prohibited foreign entity (PFE). Tax-credit eligibility depends on meeting the applicable yearly threshold.
PFE
Prohibited foreign entity: an entity subject to specified ownership or control by a country of concern, including China, that can be disqualified from certain tax credits.
EU battery passport
A digital product record for EV, industrial (above 2 kWh) and light-means-of-transport batteries placed on the EU market beginning Feb. 18, 2027. Detailed obligations, including carbon footprint and recycled-content reporting, phase in over time.
LFP
Lithium iron phosphate batteries. Their lower cost and favorable safety profile make LFP widely used in stationary storage.
Safe-harbor cost table
A U.S. Treasury table specifying default cost shares for components when calculating MACR. Yuanta estimates cells account for roughly 52% of grid-scale BESS costs.
