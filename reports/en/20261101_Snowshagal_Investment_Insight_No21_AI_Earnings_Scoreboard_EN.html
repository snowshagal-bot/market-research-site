In the final week of October, three U.S. Big Tech companies reported third-quarter results within two days. Alphabet's cloud revenue doubled, yet its shares plunged 12.6% the following day—their worst one-day close on record. Amazon's AWS posted its fastest growth in nearly five years, but earnings per share missed estimates. The stock opened down roughly 5% the next day before recovering to a 1.4% loss. One interpretation gaining ground: investors are looking beyond revenue growth to capital expenditure and cash flow.3, 4, 5, 6 This scoreboard brings together Q3 results from 12 companies along the AI supply chain—from the hyperscalers funding the build-out to chip designers, foundries, memory makers, equipment suppliers and Korean power and component producers. AMD and NVIDIA have yet to report; their slots remain open until their results arrive.

ALL01At a glance

The highest operating margins were in memory and foundry,
while Big Tech diverged on cash flow

Of the 10 companies that have reported, five beat earnings estimates, one was in line and four missed. Litigation settlement costs reduced Meta's reported profit, while a mark-to-market loss on its SpaceX stake weighed on Alphabet. Even after excluding the equity losses, Alphabet's EPS missed estimates by 11.6%. Amazon posted a record operating margin, but EPS fell about 4% short of consensus.1, 4, 7, 8, 9

THE READ · The quarter in one paragraph

Memory and foundry companies posted the quarter's highest operating margins, retaining 55–80 cents in operating income for every dollar of revenue amid tight supply. At Big Tech, revenue grew 12–26% while CapEx rose 30–120%. Microsoft alone generated positive quarterly free cash flow.

  • The four Big Tech companies are not in the same position. Microsoft generated approximately $18.6B in Q3 free cash flow despite increasing its investment. Alphabet (−$9.07B), Meta (−$2.12B) and Amazon (approximately −$33.5B) each spent more on capital investment than they generated in operating cash flow over the same quarter. All three are funding continued investment with available cash and new financing. Alphabet raised equity and debt; Amazon increased long-term borrowing. Meta completed a preferred-stock offering, but proceeds from its common-stock forward sale are not payable until settlement.1, 7, 9, 10, 11
  • Suppliers say capacity is running short. SK hynix said that DRAM and HBM output is effectively sold through 2029 when contracts still under negotiation are included. Signed long-term agreements already cover more than half of its 2027–28 capacity. Samsung Electronics said next year's HBM supply is sold out. ASML said its planned 2027–28 capacity would not satisfy demand.12, 13, 14, 15
  • Korean component makers saw different outcomes by product. Samsung Electro-Mechanics beat profit estimates by 7.6%, helped by MLCCs and substrates for AI servers. HD Hyundai Electric missed by 6.7%, while third-quarter orders fell 19%.16, 17, 18, 19
L102Big Tech · Funding the build-out

Capital spending outpaced revenue growth,
but the cash-flow picture varied widely

Combined Q3 capital expenditure at Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Amazon reached $208.8B under each company's reporting definition, up 84% year over year. Their respective revenue growth rates were 12–26%.1, 7, 9, 20, 21 Cloud revenue is growing alongside that spending. Yet a comparison of cash flow over the same quarter shows how different their financial positions have become.

  • Microsoft · “Demand continues to exceed supply,” CFO Amy Hood said. Revenue guidance for the next quarter was $96.0–97.1B, with Azure growth of 49–50%, ahead of market expectations. However, delayed delivery of third-party data centers led the company to trim its calendar-year CapEx outlook to about $170B, from roughly $175B in July.21, 24
  • Alphabet · Cloud revenue doubled and backlog reached $561B, with TPU system sales contributing significantly to growth. But Search (+12%) and YouTube advertising (+9%) fell short of expectations. Mark-to-market losses of approximately $8.66B on equity investments including SpaceX reduced EPS by about $0.55. Excluding that impact, EPS of $2.67 still missed the $3.02 estimate by 11.6%. The quarter's negative FCF reflected CapEx and TPU inventory build-up, but also a $5.2B payment in July for the EU's 2018 Android fine and interest. That payment reduced operating cash flow and was separate from CapEx. Alphabet maintained its 2026 CapEx outlook of $195–205B and expects a “significant increase” next year. During the quarter it raised $12.4B through share issuance and approximately $28.7B through bonds.7, 8, 22, 23
  • Meta · Revenue exceeded both consensus and the top of Meta's own $64.0B guidance. Q4 guidance of $73–76B had a $74.5B midpoint, above the market's roughly $73.75B estimate. Operating income fell 37% after a $9.89B charge to settle youth mental-health litigation. Even excluding that cost, the operating margin of 35.4% was below 40.1% a year earlier. Its financing arrangements are at different stages: approximately $12.5B of mandatory convertible preferred stock was issued on October 29; proceeds from a $25B common-stock forward sale will be received on settlement, no later than November 19, 2027. Once non-voting Class C shares begin trading (effective January 19, 2027), Meta plans to pursue a roughly $60B at-the-market offering program, but neither the program's establishment nor its eventual sales volume is assured.1, 11, 25
  • Amazon · AWS revenue rose 39%, its fastest growth in nearly five years, while Amazon's consolidated operating margin hit a record 13.9%. Operating income exceeded the top of company guidance ($26.5B) by 6%. EPS nevertheless declined year over year because of below-the-line items: last year's net income included a $9.5B pretax gain on its Anthropic investment, while Q3 2026 interest expense rose to $1.69B from $0.54B and other income swung to a $0.70B loss, leaving a $1.21B non-operating deficit. EPS of $1.89 also missed the $1.97 consensus. CapEx more than doubled year over year, and Amazon raised $48.1B in long-term debt during the quarter. It maintained its 2026 cash CapEx outlook near $220B and said it expects to spend “meaningfully more” next year. The midpoint of Q4 operating-income guidance ($28.5–33.5B) was below consensus of approximately $33B.4, 9, 26, 27

MKT06Post-earnings share prices

Earnings surprises and stock reactions
did not always point in the same direction

Of the 10 companies that reported, four stocks rose and six fell by the close of their first regular session following earnings. All four U.S. Big Tech stocks declined, though Microsoft and Amazon outperformed their respective benchmarks on those days.

  • Not all declines told the same story. Alphabet fell 12.6% on October 29, its worst one-day close on record, while the Nasdaq declined 1.4%. Microsoft gave up an after-hours gain of roughly 4% but closed down only 0.2%, outperforming the Nasdaq by 1.2 percentage points. Amazon opened down about 5% on October 30 before finishing 1.4% lower, a smaller decline than the S&P 500's 1.8% drop.3, 5, 6, 24, 46
  • Against the wider market · The KOSPI gained 6.6% on October 28, the day SK hynix reported. On October 29, when Samsung Electronics released detailed results (having issued preliminary figures on October 8), the KOSPI fell 1.2% and Samsung shares lost 4.1%, underperforming the benchmark. Many commentators cited the absence of a specific new buyback-and-cancellation commitment that investors had hoped to see.38, 43, 48
  • TSMC beat earnings estimates, but shares fell 0.8% in the next session. The stock was already weak ahead of the results. After U.S. consumer inflation came in above expectations on October 14, chip stocks retreated; TSMC had closed down 2.0% in Taiwan's regular session on the report date, October 15.15, 47
  • Meta beat revenue estimates, yet shares fell 7.0% the next day. News outlets and brokerages cited the move into negative free cash flow and next year's investment burden. Still, a Hana Securities report noting the same risks concluded that upward revisions to semiconductor earnings forecasts were likely after the results season.2, 49, 50
  • HD Hyundai Electricrose 0.7% on the announcement day and 2.8% the following day, before dropping 4.8% on October 29.45

CLOSING · The takeawayMemory and foundry companies reported the quarter's highest operating margins. Big Tech, meanwhile, showed how differently companies can absorb a surge in capital spending. Microsoft kept quarterly free cash flow in positive territory even as it invested more; Alphabet, Meta and Amazon spent more on capital investment than they generated in operating cash flow. The strong profits at memory and foundry companies are being powered by spending from these very customers. That makes the next test more demanding than chip sales growth alone: are hyperscalers' investment plans translating into firm orders—and ultimately into cash? AMD's November 4 report and NVIDIA's November 18 release are the first checkpoints.

Terms · GLOSSARY
Analyst consensus
The average of analysts' earnings estimates—the number investors broadly expected before the release.
Capital expenditure (CapEx)
Spending on long-lived assets such as data centers, servers and factories. For hyperscalers, AI infrastructure accounts for much of the investment.
Free cash flow (FCF)
Operating cash flow less capital spending. Negative FCF means operating cash did not cover capital investment over the period; the gap can be funded from existing cash or external financing.
Remaining performance obligations (RPO) / backlog
Contracted amounts not yet recognized as revenue. They help assess revenue visibility, but timing of recognition and actual cash collection must be considered separately.
HBM
High-bandwidth memory built by stacking DRAM dies. It sits alongside AI accelerators; HBM4 is the newest generation discussed here.
MLCC
Multilayer ceramic capacitors store and release tiny electrical charges; tens of thousands can be used in a single AI server.
FC-BGA
Advanced packaging substrates that connect high-performance chips to a circuit board, used in server CPUs and AI accelerators.
Thermocompression (TC) bonder
Equipment that bonds stacked HBM dies using controlled heat and pressure.
After-hours trading
Trading outside regular market hours. U.S. Big Tech companies typically release earnings after the closing bell, so their stocks react first in after-hours trading.
Mandatory convertible preferred stock
Preferred shares that must convert into common shares on a specified date; they pay a preferred dividend until conversion.
Common-stock forward sale
An arrangement fixing the sale price today while the shares and cash are exchanged at a future settlement date.
