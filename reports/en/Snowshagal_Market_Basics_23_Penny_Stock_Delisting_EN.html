Why Korea's penny stocks face delisting

A new rule can force companies off the exchange
simply because their shares trade below ₩1,000.

~11 min read · 1 quiz · 1 interactive exercise

The essentialsIn three points
  • Since July 2026, common shares listed on KOSPI and KOSDAQ have been placed on the delisting watchlist if they close below ₩1,000 for 30 consecutive trading days. They face delisting if they fail to close at or above ₩1,000 for 45 consecutive trading days within the following 90 trading days.
  • Because those 45 days must be consecutive, a decision can come before the 90-day window ends. On October 22, five KOSDAQ companies became the first to face delisting under the rule.
  • A reverse stock split raises the price per share but leaves the company's market capitalization unchanged by itself. A separate market cap requirement applies, and safeguards limit repeated or excessively large reverse splits used to avoid delisting.

For individual investorsIf a stock you hold is near ₩1,000, watch the closing price rather than the intraday price. The article ends with four checks for your holdings, including disclosures and reverse split schedules.

01THE CASE

The first five
delisting cases

On October 22, five KOSDAQ-listed companies faced delistingRemoval of a stock from the exchange on which it is listed.. The trigger was neither a loss nor an auditor's opinion. Their shares had simply closed below ₩1,000 for too long.

TKG Aikang, Nuintek, Hyungji Global, Barunson E&A and i-Scream Edu. These were the first cases under the July rule targeting penny stocksIn this Korean market context, the term refers to shares priced below ₩1,000, not to a universal definition of penny stocks.. On August 13, all five were placed on the delisting watchlistA stock flagged by the exchange for a risk of delisting. KRX also uses the English term “administrative issue.” for failing the minimum share price requirement. For companies such as i-Scream Edu, already flagged for low market capitalization, the price deficiency was added as another ground for designation.

Answer: ③

The 45 days must be consecutive. On October 22, day 46, all five still closed below ₩1,000.

Why are penny stocks
being delisted?

THE SHORT ANSWER
Korean regulators and the Korea Exchange (KRX) regard penny stocks as generally more volatile and more vulnerable to price manipulation. Since July 2026, both KOSPI and KOSDAQ have therefore imposed a ₩1,000 minimum share price as a continued listing requirement. Stocks that close below that level for too long face removal from the market.

The five companies, however, have not yet left the exchange. Legal challenges have put the delisting process on hold.

02HOW THE RULE WORKS

How to count days
under the ₩1,000 rule

Each day's closing priceThe final official price set at the end of that day's trading session. is compared with ₩1,000. The counting rules differ before and after watchlist designation. All days are counted as trading daysDays on which that particular stock is eligible to trade. Weekends, market holidays and trading suspensions, including those for reverse splits, are excluded..

  • Even one won short counts as below the threshold. A stock that trades above ₩1,000 intraday but closes at ₩999 fails the test for that day. A close of exactly ₩1,000 qualifies.
  • The price-related grounds for watchlist designation are removed the day after the 45th qualifying session. If other grounds apply, such as insufficient market capitalization, those must also be resolved before the stock can leave the watchlist entirely.
  • The new price requirement did not count days before July 1. The first 30-session streak was completed at the August 12 close, and the first price-related designations followed on August 13. Including market cap deficiencies, 30 companies were newly placed on the KOSPI and KOSDAQ watchlists that day.

Can a reverse split lift the price above ₩1,000?

The quickest way to raise the price per share is a reverse stock splitCombining several shares into one. Also called a share consolidation.. Consolidating shares raises the reference price when trading resumes by the same factor.

Limits on repeated or excessive reverse splits

Companies on the watchlist for a share price deficiency face special restrictions on reverse splits and capital reductionsA reduction in a company's stated share capital. Many reduce the share count, raising the price per share mechanically as in a reverse split. A capital reduction to absorb accumulated losses can signal financial distress.. If they complete either transaction during the 90-trading-day compliance window, the consequences are as follows.

Reverse splits and capital reductions during the compliance periodWatchlist stocks with a price deficiency

Completion is measured by the date the consolidated shares are listed. Reverse splits and capital reductions completed before July 1, 2026 do not count toward the prior history.

03PUTTING IT TO USE

What if your stock
is affected?

Penny-stock delisting does not happen overnight. A sequence of exchange notices comes first.

1Warning · Day 25Notice of possible watchlist designation
2DesignationNext trading day after 30 straight closes below ₩1,000
3Decision · Days 46–90Failure to meet the rule triggers grounds for delisting
  • An advance warning means there is still time. In August, some companies recovered to ₩1,000 before designation and avoided being placed on the watchlist.
  • Delisting for a minimum share price deficiency is a form of rules-based delistingAlso known as formal delisting: removal based on prescribed quantitative requirements rather than a separate substantive review of the company.. Failure to meet the numerical requirement is enough, and there is no exchange appeal procedure for this ground.

The final seven days: liquidation trading

A delisting decision does not mean the shares leave the exchange immediately. Trading is first suspended. Several days later, shareholders are normally given a final chance to sell during liquidation tradingThe last period in which shares designated for delisting can be traded on the exchange..

Decision→Trading halt→Liquidation trading→Delisting
DurationUsually 7 trading days
ExecutionOrders are collected and matched at a single price every 30 minutes (call auctions).
Price limitsNone. The ordinary daily price limitsThe permitted daily price range. For ordinary Korean stocks, it is generally 30% above or below the previous close. of ±30% are lifted.
OrdersOnly limit orders are accepted; investors must specify a price.

With far more sellers than buyers, prices can fall sharply. Even if you place a sell order, it will not execute without a buyer. Wonpoong Mulsan, delisted in September under the market cap rule, fell 45% on its first day of liquidation trading.

There is one exception. Since September 2026, companies facing delisting solely for insufficient market capitalization may transfer to KONEX at their existing share price without liquidation trading if they meet certain conditions, including profitability. KONEX is a small market with daily trading value below ₩1bn, however, and this route is unavailable to companies delisted for a share price deficiency.

Postponing liquidation trading does not mean trading resumes. Shareholders in these companies remain unable to sell on the exchange until the matter is resolved.

The dispute centers on two questions: the ₩1,000 threshold itself and the fairness of the delisting procedure.

The exchangeThe purpose is investor protection. The exchange says it announced the rule months before implementation, leaving time for measures such as reverse splits.
The companiesA ₩1,000 share price is not a measure of financial distress. The risk of manipulation depends on free float, trading value and corporate governance. The companies argue that they were denied an opportunity to be heard and that less severe alternatives exist.

South Korea allows less time to regain compliance but requires a much longer consecutive streak.

  • Nasdaq issues a deficiency notice after 30 consecutive business days below $1. A company that has carried out a reverse split in the previous year, or reverse splits with a cumulative ratio of at least 250-to-1 over the previous two years, faces delisting proceedings without a compliance period if it falls below the $1 requirement.

  • Companies may request a Nasdaq hearing to contest a delisting determination, although trading may remain suspended in the meantime.

Four things to check in your brokerage account

01Two exchange notices

When you see a notice of possible watchlist designation or an actual designation, count the remaining trading days.
  • Check the exchange's disclosure system, KIND (kind.krx.co.kr), or the stock disclosures in your brokerage app.
  • The designation notice specifies the conditions and deadline for removal from the watchlist.

02Reverse split schedule

The announcement specifies the trading suspension period and the listing date for the consolidated shares. You cannot sell on the exchange during the suspension.

03Check market cap too

Check the company's market cap in your app. From July 2027, the thresholds rise to ₩30bn for KOSDAQ and ₩50bn for KOSPI.

04Once delisting is decided

Check the liquidation trading notice for the first and last trading dates. For stocks whose procedures are on hold, refer to new exchange notices following the court ruling.
COMMON MISREADINGSPlausible, but wrong
MISREADING 01
“My watchlist stock closed above ₩1,000 today, so it's in the clear.”
One day is not enough. It must close at or above ₩1,000 for 45 consecutive trading days. One close below the threshold resets the streak.
MISREADING 02
“The moment delisting is decided, my shares become worthless.”
They do not disappear that day. Usually, there is a final seven-trading-day liquidation period. Even afterward, what disappears is not necessarily the company but the exchange as a place to sell your shares.
MISREADING 03
“A reverse split that raises the price above ₩1,000 makes the company more valuable.”
The number of shares falls in the same proportion, so market cap is unchanged. In one study, more stocks fell than rose after their reverse splits.
PART 23 IN ONE LINE

The penny-stock rule is not a test of earnings. It counts consecutive trading days with a closing price of at least ₩1,000.

But a low share price does not make a company cheap. A reverse split changes the price per share, not the size of the company.

A quick glossary of related terms
Delisting
Removal of a security from an exchange. Not the same as corporate bankruptcy.
Penny stock
In this Korean rule, a stock priced below ₩1,000.
Delisting watchlist
Stocks flagged by the exchange because they are at risk of delisting. Also called administrative issues by KRX.
Closing price
The final official price set at the end of the trading session.
Trading day
A day on which the stock is eligible to trade; suspension periods are excluded.
Market capitalization
Share price × shares outstanding; the market value of the company's equity.
Reverse stock split
Combines several shares into one. The price per share rises mechanically, but market cap does not.
Capital reduction
A reduction in stated share capital, often involving a lower share count.
Rules-based delisting
Formal delisting based on quantitative criteria, rather than a substantive review.
Liquidation trading
The final trading period before delisting, usually seven trading days.
Daily price limits
The permitted daily trading range, normally ±30%; lifted during liquidation trading.
Injunction to suspend a decision
An application to temporarily suspend a decision pending a ruling on the merits.
Margin purchase
A purchase made with money borrowed from a broker.
Unfunded purchase
Buying shares without enough cash upfront, with the shortfall due at settlement.
