The essentialsThree key points
  • For individual South Korean tax residents holding shares of Korean-listed companies with a December fiscal year-end, at least KRW 5 billion in a single stock at year-end qualifies the holder as a major shareholder for tax purposes (ownership-percentage and related-party aggregation rules also apply). Capital gains on sales of that stock the following year are generally taxed at 22–27.5%, including local income tax.
  • The test uses the settlement date, not the trade date, so the selling deadline falls several days before year-end.Under exchange rules, December 31, 2026 (Thursday) is a market holiday. Shares sold by Monday, December 28, will therefore be excluded from year-end holdings.
  • Most retail investors are nowhere near the threshold. Yet selling by large holders adds to retail net selling in the final week of December. Historically, the indices still rose on average in December despite heavy retail selling.

For individual investorsFor Korean tax residents who do not qualify as major shareholders, on-exchange sales of Korean-listed shares are generally exempt from capital gains tax. This particular tax rule gives them no reason to rush to sell at year-end.

01THE EVENT AND THE QUESTION

₩3 Trillion in Net Sales
in a Single Day

On December 28, 2021, retail investors across the KOSPI and KOSDAQ recorded KRW 3.1586 trillion in net sellingThe value of sales minus purchases. A positive figure means sales exceeded purchases.. It was a daily record at the time.

That was effectively the final day to sell shares before the year-end test for major shareholder statusA tax classification based on the value of holdings or the percentage of shares owned in a single company.. Investors who qualify face capital gains taxA tax on the profit realized from selling assets such as shares. when they sell that stock the following year. Here, “major shareholder” is a tax-law category, not a person who controls the company. The value threshold was KRW 1 billion per stock at the time; it is now KRW 5 billion.

WARM-UP QUIZThink it through

Major shareholder status under the value test is assessed using holdings on Thursday, December 31, 2026, a year-end market holiday. What is the latest day to sell enough shares to fall below the threshold?

Answer: ③

The test uses the settlement date, when ownership actually changes hands, rather than the trade date. A sale on December 29 (option ②) settles in the new year because of market holidays.

Why do large retail investors
sell at year-end?

THE ANSWER IN BRIEF
Under the value test, holdings on a single day, December 31, determine tax status for the following year. A position worth KRW 5 billion or more in one stock qualifies its holder as a major shareholder; gains on sales of that stock the following year become taxable. Reducing the position below the threshold before year-end can avoid that tax, provided the other major shareholder tests are not met.
02HOW IT WORKS

December 31:
One Day of Holdings

The value test is applied once a year, and the resulting tax status applies throughout the following year.

The table covers the value test alone. Separate rules apply to ownership percentages and the largest shareholder group.

  • Each stock is assessed separately. Positions in different companies are not added together.
  • Holdings are valued at the December 31 closing price, or the preceding trading day's closing price if the market is closed.
  • Ownership percentageThe proportion of a company's total issued shares that you own. is another test. Holding at least 1% of a KOSPI-listed company, 2% of a KOSDAQ-listed company or 4% of a KONEX-listed company qualifies you as a major shareholder regardless of the position's value. This test does not wait until year-end: if additional purchases take you to the threshold during the year, you qualify from that point onward.
  • Normally, only your own holdings count. However, members of the largest shareholderThe shareholder with the largest combined stake when holdings of related parties, such as family members, are included. group must also include holdings of related parties, such as relatives. In an additional tax assessment disclosed by Korea's National Tax Service (NTS) in August 2026, an investor whose own holdings fell below the threshold was taxed as a major shareholder because the investor was related to a member of the largest shareholder group and their holdings were aggregated.

What major shareholder status means

Taxable salesEvery sale of that stock the following year is subject to tax, even if only one share is sold.
Tax ratesTax baseThe amount on which tax is calculated. For shares, it is the gain less applicable deductions.: 20% on the first KRW 300 million and 25% on the excess. Local income taxAn additional tax paid to local government, equal to 10% of the national income tax amount. brings the combined rates to 22% and 27.5%. Shares of non-SMEs held for less than a year are subject to a 30% national rate (33% including local income tax).
FilingFile and pay the tax yourself within two months after the end of the half-year in which the sale occurs.
Already a major shareholder?If you already qualified at the end of 2025, your 2026 sales remain taxable even if you reduce your holdings at year-end. The reduced position affects only the value test for 2027.

Selling before the 31st is not enough

For tax purposes, the date of a share sale is the settlement dateThe date on which shares and cash are exchanged. Korean equities settle two trading days after execution., not the date the order executes. Korean equities settle two trading days after the trade (T+2). For an explanation of the two-day gap, see Part 22 (Korean edition).

1Mon., Dec. 28Sale executed
2Wed., Dec. 30Settlement; last trading day
3Thu., Dec. 31Holdings assessed; shares excluded
  • Sell on Tuesday, December 29, and settlement moves to January 4, after the December 31 market holidayA day when the securities market is closed. It does not count toward settlement either. and New Year's Day. The shares still count toward your December 31 holdings.
  • There have been reported cases of investors relying on the trade date, only to be classified as major shareholders under the settlement-date rule and charged additional tax penalties.
  • December 28 is also the last day to buy shares and qualify for dividends at companies that use a December year-end dividend record dateThe date that determines which registered shareholders qualify for a dividend. Under T+2 settlement, buy at least two trading days before the record-date cutoff..

Retail net selling includes other motives, such as profit-taking and dividend-related trades. On the same day in 2025, foreign investors bought a net KRW 1.7777 trillion on the KOSPI market. No official data isolate sales by large holders motivated by the tax rule.

  • Shinhan Securities found that from 2017 through 2022, retail investors' share of total selling rose from below 50% five days before the cutoff to above 65% on the day itself.
  • Such selling tends to be concentrated in mid-cap stocks and the KOSDAQ market, where retail participation is high.

The value threshold has changed repeatedly

  1. 2000

    KOSPI: KRW 10 billion per stock or a 3% ownership stake.

  2. 2013

    The KOSPI value threshold fell to KRW 5 billion.

  3. 2016–20

    The KOSPI threshold fell in stages: KRW 2.5 billion, then KRW 1.5 billion, then KRW 1 billion.

  4. Dec. 2023

    The value threshold rose to KRW 5 billion across all three markets to reduce volatility associated with year-end selling.

  5. Jul. 2025

    The government proposed restoring the KRW 1 billion threshold in its tax reform plan.

  6. Sep. 15, 2025

    Following strong opposition, the KRW 5 billion threshold was retained. It also applies to the 2026 year-end assessment.

For a higher thresholdYear-end selling and subsequent buying in January can disrupt the market. A higher threshold reduces that disruption.
For a lower thresholdRaising the threshold cuts taxes only for a small group of wealthy investors. It also runs against the principle of taxing gains where they arise.

Four checks for your brokerage account

01Value your positions

For each stock, calculate the December 30 closing price × number of shares held.
  • Combine holdings of the same stock across all brokerage accounts.
  • Check the ownership-percentage test even if the position is below KRW 5 billion.

02Check the date

The 2026 selling deadline falls on Monday, December 28 under the exchange's rules.
  • Confirm it against KRX's year-end trading schedule when announced in mid-December.
  • Companies with a non-December fiscal year-end have a different assessment date.

03Read the flows

Reports of retail net selling in the final week of December partly reflect the tax calendar. Look at index performance and institutional and foreign flows that week, too.

04If you qualify

When you sell that stock the following year, file a preliminary capital gains tax return for the relevant half-year. If your status is unclear, consult a tax professional or the NTS.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONSPlausible, but wrong
MYTH 01
“Any sale before December 31 avoids major shareholder status.”
The test uses the settlement date. In 2026, a sale by Monday, December 28, settles by December 30 and the shares are excluded from year-end holdings.
MYTH 02
“Only the part of my holdings above ₩5 billion is subject to tax.”
Once you qualify as a major shareholder, the entire capital gain on sales of that stock the following year is subject to tax, not just gains attributable to holdings above the threshold. Applicable deductions still apply.
MYTH 03
“Holding ₩5 billion across several stocks makes me a major shareholder.”
If you own two positions worth KRW 3 billion each, neither meets the value threshold, even though the combined portfolio is worth KRW 6 billion.
MYTH 04
“A position below ₩5 billion means I needn't worry about major shareholder status.”
That considers only the value test. You may still qualify if your ownership percentage is at least 1% on KOSPI or 2% on KOSDAQ (4% on KONEX), or if you belong to the largest shareholder group and aggregated holdings with related parties meet a threshold, even when your individual position is small.
EPISODE 24 IN ONE SENTENCE

The major shareholder value test does not track your holdings throughout the year. It looks at your holdings on December 31 alone.

Selling around that one date adds to retail net sales in the last week of December. But heavier net selling does not necessarily mean falling stock prices.

A quick glossary of related terms
Major shareholder
A shareholder classified as subject to capital gains tax under these rules; not necessarily someone who controls the company.
Non-major shareholder
A shareholder who does not meet the major shareholder tests. On-exchange gains on Korean-listed shares are generally exempt from capital gains tax for resident individuals in this category.
Capital gains tax
Tax on the profit realized from selling an asset.
Tax base
The amount to which the tax rate is applied.
Local income tax
An additional local tax equal to 10% of the national income tax amount.
Securities transaction tax
Tax on the gross sale value, regardless of profit or loss.
Settlement date
The date shares and cash are exchanged, two trading days after execution for Korean equities.
Market holiday
A day when the market is closed; excluded from settlement-day calculations.
Net selling
The value of sales exceeds the value of purchases.
Largest shareholder group
The largest shareholder and related parties. Their holdings are aggregated for the major shareholder test.
Preliminary tax return
A capital gains tax filing for the relevant half-year, ahead of any required final annual return.
Notes on interpreting the figures

As of · Based on announcements and reports through November 27, 2026. The government can change the major shareholder thresholds by presidential decree. In 2025, it proposed restoring the KRW 1 billion threshold but later withdrew the proposal.

Market holidays · Under exchange rules, December 31 is a market holiday; if it falls on a public holiday or Saturday, the preceding trading day is closed instead. In 2023, when December 31 fell on a Sunday, the year-end market holiday was Friday, December 29.

Scope · This article concerns individual tax residents of South Korea holding shares of Korean-listed companies with a December fiscal year-end. The test uses holdings at the end of the company's own fiscal year if it ends in another month. Nonresident investors may be subject to different rules and tax treaties.

Tax rates · Adding local income tax (10% of the national tax) to national rates of 20%, 25% and 30% produces combined rates of 22%, 27.5% and 33%. The KRW 2.5 million basic deduction applies once per year to taxable domestic and foreign share gains combined, not separately to each stock.

Statistics · The 2021 figure is based on reports of the exchange's tally the following day (preliminary same-day figure: KRW 3.0903 trillion). That daily retail net-selling record was subsequently broken; on July 31, 2026, net selling on KOSPI alone reached KRW 8.2543 trillion. The 2025 figure covers KOSPI only, so it is not directly comparable to 2021. The 2010–2019 figures come from the original KCMI report; the Shinhan Securities analysis is cited from media reporting.
