Same KRX category, very different capital
Samsung’s KRW 15tn is for employee stock compensation; SK hynix’s KRW 40tn is to be fully cancelled Both show up as buying by 'other corporations' in KRX flow data, but they are different forms of capital allocation1 · In Korea Capital Market Institute’s study of 282 KOSPI buyback decisions from 2024 through September 2026 (280 analyzed), cumulative abnormal returns over the three trading days around the announcement were 3.42% for cancellation-purpose buybacks and 0.59% for employee-compensation buybacks · Those figures measure the announcement effect, not what happens when the buying ends10
HOW TO READ IT
Other-corporation net buying and the two buyback programs are of the same order of magnitude But net buying is a net flow, while buyback value is gross executed purchase value, so the two cannot be subtracted from each other. The data support only the narrower statement that they are similar in scale—the same standard used in Note No.04. Daishin Securities and press reports identify the two buybacks as the main driver of other-corporation buying3, 11, 12 · Investor-category net flows do not identify the actual counterparty to each trade. They only show that, over the same period, one group was selling while other corporations were buying

What ended, what remains

The Finish Line Was Set by
Value, Not Shares

Samsung ended Oct. 6 · SK hynix 84.2% complete by value · Note No.04 estimate vs. actual

By share count, Samsung Electronics had already exceeded its disclosed target on Oct. 1, but purchases continued through Oct. 6 · The reason is simple: Samsung disclosed a KRW 15tn purchase budget, not a hard share-count ceiling2, 14, 16

Note No.04 Estimate vs. What Actually Happened

Samsung ElectronicsNOTE NO.04: OCT. 1 → ACTUAL: OCT. 6
ESTIMATE
Note No.04 divided the remaining disclosed share count by the average daily purchase pace and arrived at Oct. 111
ACTUAL
The share-count estimate was right in one sense: Samsung reached 100.96% of the disclosed share count on Oct. 1. But because the program was budget-based, the company kept buying for two more trading days—Oct. 2 and Oct. 6—and finished on Oct. 62, 14
WHY
The average purchase price, KRW 263,823, was 6.3% below the KRW 281,500 reference price used at the board resolution. That let Samsung buy 6.7% more shares with the same KRW 15tn budget2, 5
SK hynixNOTE NO.04: OCT. 16 → MEDIA ESTIMATE: AROUND OCT. 20
ESTIMATE
Using the same method, Note No.04 estimated Oct. 1611
PRICE EFFECT
SK hynix’s average purchase price is about KRW 1.75m by this note’s calculation, above the KRW 1.662m reference price. That reduces the number of shares needed to spend the budget to roughly 22.9m. On price alone, the program would have ended sooner1, 2, 5
WHAT DELAYED IT
The company slowed the daily purchase request from 600,000–650,000 shares to 500,000 and then 400,000 in October · KRW 6.3149tn remains2, 6

The binding constraint was the amount of money, not the number of shares. When the market price is below the reference price, the same budget buys more shares—as with Samsung. When it is above the reference price, it buys fewer—as with SK hynix. Note No.04 therefore called Samsung’s finish too early on a share-count basis; for SK hynix, the October slowdown mattered more than the price effect

What Does Not Immediately Become Supply When the Buying Stops

KNOWN

Confirmed by filings and executions

  • All shares acquired by SK hynix are scheduled to be cancelled · Once cancelled, they do not return to the tradable float1, 3
  • Samsung’s buyback is intended to fund employee stock compensation, including performance incentives and special management-performance bonuses1
  • Samsung plans a roughly KRW 30tn 3Q cash dividend, including the regular quarterly dividend · The board is expected to finalize it at the end of October (Aug. 21 disclosure)17
  • On Oct. 7, the first day after Samsung’s buyback stopped, other corporations were net sellers of KRW 7bn in Samsung, versus average daily net buying of KRW 507.3bn during the purchase period2
ESTIMATE

Numbers built on assumptions

  • SK hynix could finish around Oct. 20 if the current pace and share price hold, according to press estimates2
  • Estimated daily buying while the program remains: about KRW 0.7tn · 400,000-share purchase request × Oct. 7 close of KRW 1.723m5, 6, 18
  • A possible additional SK hynix buyback in 4Q appears as an unattributed line in an article discussing an IBK Investment & Securities report · It is not a company announcement19
UNKNOWN

Still unknown

  • When Samsung’s compensation shares will be granted or sold, and whether they will carry transfer restrictions · Restricted stock awarded in January this year carried a one-year lockup20
  • Samsung’s remaining shareholder-return plan · The company estimates roughly KRW 90–110tn of 2026 shareholder-return capacity remains. About KRW 30tn of 3Q cash dividends is due to be finalized at the end of October; the size and form of the remainder are scheduled for a board decision at the end of January 202717
  • When foreign selling will ease

On Samsung’s capital-allocation calendar, the sequence is: KRW 15tn compensation buyback completed Oct. 6 → roughly KRW 30tn dividend finalized at end-October → remaining shareholder return decided at end-January 2027. A dividend is not open-market buying, so it does not directly replace the bid that just disappeared2, 17

A buyback ending is not the same thing as new sell supply appearing. Shares earmarked for cancellation disappear; compensation shares cannot be sold before they are granted, and any transfer restriction pushes potential selling out further. Whether the shares from this KRW 15tn program will carry such restrictions has not been confirmed3, 20

  1. Samsung finished later than the share-count estimate because the program was defined by value; SK hynix is likely to finish later than Note No.04 projected because it slowed the pace
  2. Samsung’s buying is finished. SK hynix still has roughly two weeks of demand left at about KRW 0.7tn per day, based on the current requested volume
  3. A buyback ending does not instantly create new sell supply · What disappears first is buy demand

Average vs median

The Average Is +6.4%,
but the Distribution Is All Over the Map

Daishin Securities · Buyback cases since 2020 · 33 periods with the same flow mix · 60 trading days after completion

Daishin Securities found that in 33 periods when financial investment firms were net buyers while other institutions were net sellers—the flow mix now seen in Samsung—the average return over the 60 trading days after a buyback ended was +6.4%, better than the +0.1% average in 34 periods when both groups were net sellers. But the same chart shows the median only as a bar, without a printed figure, and it appears to sit around 0% by visual read3, 5

HOW TO READ IT
When the average is far above the median, a small number of large winners may be doing much of the work Daishin’s original PDF shows the median only graphically and does not make clear whether that chart covers all 285 observations or the 105 non-financial direct-buyback observations. This note therefore uses the median only as a directional check, not as a precise statistic
  • In the seven periods when other institutions accounted for at least 50% of selling and financial investment firms were buyers, the average return was +22.5% · Four of those seven cases were auto-related, and dispersion was wide3
  • The only flow mix with a clearly positive median was financial-investment selling plus buying by other institutions: mean about +5.0%, median about +2.6% by visual read · Neither Samsung nor SK hynix currently matches that combination3, 5

What the Historical Cases Can—and Cannot—Tell Us

The evidence that completion itself determines direction is weak. In the 34 periods when both groups sold, the mean was +0.1% and the median about −0.6%—both essentially flat. SK hynix currently fits that mix, with financial investment firms at −KRW 0.4tn and other institutions at −KRW 2.0tn. Even Samsung’s apparently stronger setup has a median near zero3, 5 · Daishin itself makes the outlook conditional not on the fact that the buyback has ended, but on whether financial-investment buying persists and whether earnings and shareholder-return policy become more concrete3

COUNTERPOINT · The Flow Gap May Be Smaller Than It Looks

Daishin argues that institutional sellers may already have cleared much of their inventory during the buyback, leaving a lighter overhang afterward3 · Eugene Investment & Securities also argues that fears of a post-buyback flow vacuum are unlikely to be realized21 · Shinhan Investment Corp. described Samsung’s preliminary 3Q operating profit of KRW 107.4tn as slightly above consensus, while an FnGuide consensus reported on Oct. 5 was KRW 110.2803tn—so the verdict depends on which benchmark is used7, 8, 22 · Samsung’s foreign ownership at 46.41%, below its 10-year average of 52.92%, can be read as suggesting less room for incremental foreign selling than in the past. It does not mean the selling is over22 · Foreign KOSPI net selling on Oct. 6, the completion day, and Oct. 7 (including NXT) was larger than the September daily average. Foreign investors also sold roughly KRW 2tn on Oct. 8, though that session was distorted by expiry and rebalancing. So far, the flow data have not confirmed the counterargument9, 18, 23

PAGE 03 · TAKEAWAYS
  1. The historical flow mix that resembles Samsung averaged +6.4% after completion, but the median appears close to 0%
  2. The historical cases do not make a strong case for either a post-buyback drop or a post-buyback rebound
  3. The question is not simply that the buyback ended. It is who buys next

EVENT FILTER · A DAY TO READ WITH CAUTION

On Oct. 8, regularly scheduled rebalancing across 24 domestic equity ETFs worth KRW 23.2tn was expected to concentrate in one session because of the holiday calendar; KRW 18.1tn of that total was semiconductor-related. The flows are still recorded, but the signal is checked again using a two- to three-session average after the event has passed—for Oct. 8, that means Oct. 12–14

CLOSING NOTE
One Bid Has Stepped Away.
The Next One Is Still Unclear
  • Since late August, the core of other-corporation buying that absorbed heavy net selling—+KRW 43.4tn from Aug. 24 to Oct. 2—was the two companies’ buybacks · From Sep. 7, average daily purchases were roughly 12% of Samsung’s median daily volume and 21% of SK hynix’s
  • The two programs belonged to the same KRX investor category but served different purposes: Samsung’s KRW 15tn funded compensation; SK hynix’s KRW 40tn is for share cancellation · Samsung’s next shareholder-return steps are roughly KRW 30tn of dividends to be finalized at end-October and a decision on the remainder at end-January 2027
  • The historical average is +6.4%, but the median appears close to 0% · Completion by itself is a weak directional signal
  • Even with preliminary operating profit of KRW 107.4tn, the KOSPI closed −2.62% on Oct. 8 · Because options expiry and ETF rebalancing hit the same session, the decline is poor evidence that the buyback ending, by itself, drove the move

The buybacks were undeniably large sources of demand, so their disappearance changes the flow structure. But price direction is not determined by one missing buyer; it is the net result of foreign, individual, institutional and passive flows arriving on the same day

DECISION RULES
  1. Does daily foreign KOSPI net selling fall below the September average of roughly KRW 1.1tn?
  2. After roughly KRW 0.7tn a day of SK hynix buying disappears, do individuals and financial investment firms repeatedly fill the gap?

GLOSSARYQuick Glossary
Other corporations
A KRX investor category for ordinary corporations that are not financial institutions, pension funds, foreign investors or individuals · Listed companies buying back their own shares are recorded here
Share buyback
A company purchasing its own shares · After board approval, the company discloses the size and purchase window and files a completion report when the program ends · Both programs in this note were defined by purchase value, so the final share count changes with the market price
Share cancellation
Retiring repurchased shares so they are removed from shares outstanding · Cancelled shares do not return to the tradable float
Restricted Stock Award (RSA)
Equity compensation granted to employees with restrictions that prevent sale for a specified period
Financial investment firms
A KRX institutional-investor subcategory that includes securities firms’ proprietary trading · Daishin Securities interprets net buying by this group as a sign of market interest and cumulative selling by other institutions as evidence that their inventory overhang has eased
Average vs. Median
The mean adds all observations and divides by the count; the median is the middle observation when values are ranked · A few large outcomes can pull the mean far above the median
Liquid bank deposits
Demand and savings deposits that are readily available for use · In Korean market commentary, this measure is often watched for signs that money is moving back into bank deposits rather than risk assets
Brokerage customer deposits
Cash held in securities accounts and available for stock purchases · A commonly watched gauge of retail buying capacity
Cumulative abnormal return (CAR)
A stock’s return in excess of the market return, accumulated across several days around an event · Used to gauge how the market reacted to an announcement
ETF rebalancing and options expiry
Rebalancing days generate trades to reset index and ETF weights; options-expiry days can concentrate arbitrage unwinds · Because these are mechanical flows that need not express a directional view, this note filters financial-investment and institutional flows on those sessions
NXT
Korea’s alternative trading system, Nextrade · Investor-flow figures can differ slightly depending on whether NXT trades are included
