After the Selloff, Is the KOSPI Really Cheap?

What a low P/E says—and what it doesn't—about earnings

The KOSPI fell 7.57% on October 15 to close at 5,554.22. From 7,003.74 on October 2, it has lost 20.7% in seven trading sessions. Brokerage estimates from October 8–13 put its 12-month forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio at roughly 5 times. Keep those earnings forecasts unchanged and update only the price to the October 15 close, and the ratio falls to the mid-4s (Snowshagal calculation). After a decline this steep, is the market finally cheap? The denominator in a forward P/E is not earnings already made, but earnings expected over the coming year. If those forecasts are too optimistic, a low P/E may be an illusion. If the earnings hold up, today's discount may be excessive. This note does not try to call the bottom. It asks how far expected earnings would have to be cut to justify the current price, how much that answer depends on the P/E multiple assumed to be reasonable, and why prices can keep falling even while earnings remain strong. We examined the earnings side in Investment Note No. 11.

THE SHORT ANSWER

  1. A forward P/E in the mid-4s is not proof that stocks are cheap. Updating brokerage P/E figures from October 8–13 for the October 15 closing price, with earnings estimates held constant, gives 4.2–4.6×. At that same price, a 10× reference P/E corresponds to earnings 55% below forecasts; a 7× multiple corresponds to a 36% haircut (Snowshagal calculations). Price alone cannot tell us how much is attributable to lower earnings and how much to multiple compression.
  2. A cut to forecasts is not the same as a fall in actual profits. Halving the ₩907 trillion 12-month operating-profit forecast for Samsung Electronics and SK hynix (the Big Two) gives ₩453.5 trillion—just 3% below their combined ₩468 trillion over the latest four quarters, a figure that includes preliminary and estimated third-quarter results (Snowshagal calculation).
  3. Brokerage back-solves start with assumptions, too. Plugging the October 15 close into the Daishin and Eugene frameworks yields implied earnings cuts ranging from 45% to 67% for the KOSPI and 69% to 94% for the Big Two, depending on the assumptions (Snowshagal calculations). The methods do not provide independent corroboration.
  4. Three forces can push prices lower despite strong reported earnings. Doubts about future profits following CXMT's announcement; high borrowing costs and strains on AI infrastructure financing; and forced selling, including margin liquidations and position unwinds. Heavy foreign selling continued. The funding question for AI infrastructure matters not just for valuation multiples, but also for future memory-chip orders and earnings estimates.
  5. There is a serious case against the bearish view. TSMC's third-quarter net profit rose 69%, and ASML said customers continue to raise their equipment-demand forecasts. Yet strong demand does not settle questions about returns on investment or intensifying supply competition. Both can weaken confidence in how long today's earnings will last.

Q1
THE PREMISE

Does a P/E in the Mid-4s Mean “Cheap”?

A P/E in the mid-4s is a question to investigate, not a verdict of undervaluation. The same price can be consistent with earnings 55% below forecasts at a 10× reference P/E—or no cut at all at 4.5×.

Imagine a stock expected to earn ₩100 per share over the next 12 months, trading at ₩450. Its forward P/E is 4.5×. What that price implies depends on the multiple we think those earnings would normally deserve: the assumed reference P/E.

One ₩450 share price, three explanationsIllustrative example · Forward EPS ₩100; share price ₩450
Assumed reference P/EEarnings consistent with priceVersus ₩100 forecast
10× reference P/E₩4555% lower
7× reference P/EAbout ₩6436% lower
4.5× reference P/E₩100No haircut

All three scenarios produce the same price. So it is misleading to say, “A 4.5× P/E prices in a 55% earnings decline.” The precise statement is: “If the reference P/E remains 10×, a 55% reduction in forecast earnings would reconcile that multiple with today's price.” Price alone cannot separate earnings weakness from multiple compression. P/E ratios also reflect growth, risk and differences in cash distributions such as dividends.

Cutting forecasts is not the same as reporting lower profits

A downward revision may mean a company earns less than it does today—or that it earns more, just not as much as analysts had expected. The Big Two, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, have a combined 12-month operating-profit forecast of ₩907 trillion (October 12).16 Cut that forecast in half and it becomes ₩453.5 trillion, only 3.1% below the ₩468 trillion earned or estimated over the latest four quarters (Snowshagal calculation). Here, a 50% cut to forecasts is a very different proposition from profits actually halving.

The ₩468 trillion is not entirely confirmed, either. Samsung's third-quarter operating profit of ₩107.4 trillion is a preliminary estimate issued before its books are closed17, while SK hynix's third-quarter contribution comes from a consensus of brokerage forecasts.16

Different brokerages, different P/Es—even before updating the price

Brokerage forward P/Es dated October 8–13 ranged from 4.6× to 5.5× for the same index. Providers differ (Refinitiv, FnGuide and Quantiwise), as do coverage rules—whether only ordinary shares are included, how companies without earnings forecasts are treated, and when estimates are updated. Such differences produced gaps of 0.3–0.4 P/E points even on the same day: 5.5× versus 5.1× on October 8, and 4.9× versus 4.6× on October 13.6, 16, 18, 19 Holding each source's earnings estimates fixed and updating only the price to October 15 gives a range of 4.2–4.6× (Snowshagal calculations). These are not newly compiled October 15 consensus P/Es.

Eugene's distribution since 2005 puts the bottom-decile P/E range at 5.3–8.5×. Both its October 13 estimate of 4.9× and the roughly 4.5× repriced for October 15 are below that historical range.16 In its October 13 note, Eugene called the situation “difficult to compare with historical precedents.”

The KOSPI's earnings forecasts are heavily concentrated in two companies. Eugene estimated that the Big Two would account for 68% of the index's Q3 operating profit and 76% of net profit attributable to shareholders19. On a 12-month forward operating-profit basis, the two account for ₩907 trillion—or 71%—of the KOSPI's ₩1,280 trillion total (October 12; Snowshagal calculation).16 These operating-profit shares should not be confused with the net-earnings denominator used to calculate forward P/E.

How much does the answer change with the assumed P/E?

Even using the same P/E of roughly 4.5× (4.51×), a 5.3× reference multiple corresponds to a 15% earnings haircut, while a 12× multiple implies 62% (Snowshagal calculations). As prices fell between October 13 and 15, the implied haircut increased by 3–7 percentage points at each unchanged reference multiple.

The right reference multiple depends on industry mix and where earnings sit in the cycle. Daishin notes that cyclical shares tend to command low P/Es when profits are high and higher P/Es when profits are depressed. In its analysis, the KOSPI's cyclical group saw earnings forecasts fall 42% between late 2021 and September 2023, but market capitalization fell only 9%; the group's P/E therefore rose. Since late 2025, the relationship has reversed: forecast earnings have multiplied 4.8 times, while market capitalization has increased only 2.1 times.18

Valuation theory points to the same problem. NYU's Aswath Damodaran cautions that extrapolating boom-year profits for cyclical firms overstates value. Normalizing earnings also requires normalizing the reinvestment and financing costs needed to sustain them. Earnings can be inflated at a cyclical peak, making the P/E look unusually low; at a trough, the reverse applies.21 His work does not establish an appropriate P/E for the Korean market.

Under Daishin's classification, cyclical sectors—those whose earnings move sharply with business conditions—accounted for 68% of the KOSPI's market capitalization as of October 8.18 With cyclical companies carrying such weight, part of the 55% haircut implied by a 10× reference P/E may be attributable not to lower earnings but to the low multiples these businesses commonly trade on near peak profitability (Snowshagal assessment). We have not established that 10×, roughly the historical median, is the appropriate benchmark for today's index.

Q2
THREE PATHS

Why Do Prices Keep Falling When Earnings Are Strong?

Three channels matter: doubts about future earnings; a lower multiple even if profits hold up; and forced selling that moves prices before fundamentals change. Financing concerns for AI infrastructure companies on October 14–15 raised a further question: could the first two channels reinforce each other?

A · Earnings doubts

  • China's CXMT and its next-generation DRAM roadmap; risks to memory prices and margins; pace of AI infrastructure spending
  • Watch: Commercial yields, customer qualifications, memory prices, orders and forecast revisions

B · Multiple compression

  • High borrowing costs; financing strain among AI infrastructure companies
  • Watch: Long-dated U.S. yields, corporate credit, like-for-like forward P/E and cash flow

C · Forced selling

  • Margin calls and crowded-position unwinds · Heavy foreign selling (whether forced is unconfirmed)
  • Watch: Verified flows, positions and liquidation data · Do not assign sellers or causal weight without evidence

A · Doubts about future earningsCXMT

One name recurred in coverage of the October 13 plunge: Chinese DRAM manufacturer CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies). At an industry conference in Shanghai on October 10, its president, Cao Kanyu, said the company had made “key progress” on 4F² DRAM architecture, which reduces cell area, and planned to introduce server-grade DDR5 RDIMMs based on that architecture by year-end. International reports carried the remarks on October 12.1 Server DRAM has largely been supplied by Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and Micron. The prospect of another competitor raised concerns about pricing and margins. CLSA expects CXMT's DRAM shipment share to increase from 12% this year to 14% next year.1 Concerns about more supply from CXMT's expansion were again cited in October 15 closing reports.9

There is contrary evidence. Asked on October 14 whether Samsung was also developing 4F², Samsung Electronics CTO Song Jai-hyuk replied, “Yes.”3 Samsung has previously said it aims to mass-produce sub-10nm DRAM using new technology by 2028. SK hynix also disclosed a 4F² roadmap last year. CXMT has not released detailed product specifications or a precise launch date. According to TechInsights data cited in coverage, the three established memory makers are producing server products on the 1c (sixth-generation 10nm-class) process, while Chinese manufacturers remain at least two generations behind.1

Memory-related discounting is not confined to Korea. As of October 12, four U.S. memory and storage names—Micron, Sandisk, Seagate and Western Digital—were down an average 33.5% from their peaks. GPU designers Nvidia and AMD, together with foundry TSMC, were an average 7.5% below their 52-week highs. DS Investment & Securities suggested the gap could reflect investor attention shifting toward compute rather than outright weakening memory demand.22

B · A lower multiple, even without an earnings cutRates · AI financing

The price investors will pay for a given stream of earnings can fall even if forecasts do not change. From October 1 to 14, the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield held between 5.19% and 5.31%; the 30-year yield ranged from 5.52% to 5.67%.23 Higher-than-expected September consumer inflation raised fears of rate hikes, but9the U.S. 10-year yield closed lower on October 14 than the day before,23 and Korea's 10-year government-bond yield fell 6.8 basis points to 4.193% on October 15.11 After the Korean close on October 15, U.S. September producer prices also came in well above forecasts, rising 1.3% month-on-month.14, 15 The question here is not whether yields rose or fell on any single day, but whether financing will remain expensive. On BlackRock's October 14 earnings call, CEO Larry Fink pointed to historically large government deficits and a generational AI infrastructure buildout that companies must finance. He expects structurally higher interest rates to persist for longer, particularly at the long end of the curve.24 Broadcom CEO Hock Tan was also reported as saying at an October 12 event in Singapore that, as chip shortages ease, access to capital could become the biggest constraint on AI infrastructure expansion.25

On October 14, those concerns showed up in one company's securities. Shares of U.S. data-center lessor CoreWeave dropped 15.5%. The yield on its 9.625%-coupon bonds due 2032 rose to 14.8%, as the price fell below 78 cents on the dollar, according to a report citing Bloomberg data. CEO Michael Intrator told employees that markets can misread a company and behave irrationally; he maintained that its backlog more than covered all obligations.7 This is a signal from a single company and a single bond maturity—not a measure of borrowing costs or default risk across the AI industry.

Financing pressure can compress multiples, but it also links back to Path A. If funding constraints force data-center operators to slow capital spending, expectations for GPU, HBM and server-DRAM orders and prices may change. No such shift has yet been established. Higher interest rates can raise the return investors demand on future earnings, but rates also embed growth expectations. The “normal” P/E does not fall mechanically with yields.

C · Forced selling before fundamentals changeMarket flows

Direct observations. On October 13, forced sales against unsettled stock-purchase obligations reached ₩59.35 billion, the highest since July 31 (₩122.01bn), according to Korea Financial Investment Association figures reported by SBS Biz.26 Foreign investors remained net sellers for an eighth session on October 15, with estimates ranging from roughly ₩3.6 trillion to ₩4.8 trillion, depending on the reported tally.9 These figures do not capture all derivatives liquidations. Nor can they establish how much of the index's decline was caused by forced selling.

Positioning before the crash. BofA's October Global Fund Manager Survey (161 respondents overseeing $453bn; fielded October 2–8) found the largest overweight in technology since August 2020 and a cash allocation of 3.9%. “Long global semiconductors” was named the most crowded trade by 45% of respondents.27 Because the survey preceded the selloff, it does not show how much of that positioning was subsequently unwound.

Brokerage interpretation and the regulator. Yuanta Securities suggested that reductions in total-return-swap (TRS) and contract-for-difference (CFD) exposures may have contributed to the October 12–13 slump. It expected selling pressure to ease quickly once those positions had been sharply reduced.28 That easing had not been demonstrated by October 15. Nor is there direct evidence separating those liquidations from the reported foreign net-selling totals. Since September 2023, Korean CFD transactions have been classified by the underlying investor—retail, institutional or foreign. Routing an order through an overseas broker alone does not make it a foreign investor's sale.29 FSC Chairman Lee Eog-weon's October 15 warning that investments relying on excessive debt and margin financing are risky is also relevant to this channel.13

Still unverified. The actual size of liquidations, who initiated the sales, and their contribution to the index decline. Position unwinds can drive prices down without establishing that the underlying businesses are worth less.

Q3
BROKERAGE MODELS

What Do Brokerage Models Imply at Today's Price?

Daishin emphasizes doubts about cyclical earnings. Eugene presents scenarios involving 60–70% cuts to the Big Two's profits. Hanwha reads the price as implying lower profitability. Each result depends on a chosen reference multiple or historical relationship; agreement between them is not independent validation.

1 · Discounting peak-cycle earningsDaishin Securities

Daishin decomposed an 8.95-percentage-point rise in the KOSPI's earnings yield (E/P, the inverse of P/E), from 9.3% at end-2025 to 18.2% on October 8. It allocated 1.21 points to sector-weight changes, 1.44 to interest rates, 6.81 to doubts about cyclical earnings and −0.52 to other factors.18 In that model, the cyclical-earnings discount accounts for approximately 76% of the increase in earnings yield over the period (Snowshagal calculation). That is a model classification, not an observed attribution of the last few days' losses.

Daishin also estimated that the P/E would have been 8.4×, rather than 5.5×, if sector weights and interest rates were the only influences. Treating 8.4× as the benchmark translates into a 35% implied earnings haircut for the KOSPI: 1 − 5.5 ÷ 8.4. Keep that October 8 benchmark fixed and update only the price to October 15, and the implied cut is about 45% (conditional Snowshagal calculation). Cyclical sectors—memory, equipment, chemicals, steel and shipbuilding, among others—traded at 4.5× earnings, or 0.44 times the 10.3× P/E of noncyclicals, versus an average relative multiple of 0.99 since 2015. Daishin interpreted this as the market valuing only 44% of current cyclical earnings as sustainable (October 8 basis).18 Those numbers do not isolate differences in growth, risk or cash distributions—other factors that also determine multiples.

2 · Regressing index levels on operating profitEugene · Illustrative scenario

Eugene regressed the KOSPI's annual average level in 2000–2025 against each year's aggregate KOSPI operating profit. The fitted equation is “Index = 9.0008 × operating profit (₩tn) + 697.05,” with an R-squared of 0.87.16 Eugene reported that cutting only the Big Two's 12-month forward operating-profit forecasts—Samsung ₩527tn and SK hynix ₩380tn as of October 12—by 60%, while leaving the other companies' ₩373tn unchanged, would give ₩730tn of KOSPI operating profit and an index level of 7,270. A 70% cut gave ₩650tn and 6,550. Taking the lower edge of its ±1,000-point scenario band (6,270 and 5,550), Eugene characterized KOSPI levels of 5,500–6,200 as consistent with a 60–70% cut in Big Two earnings. Recalculating from the stated inputs produces somewhat different figures.

3 · Market-implied ROEHanwha Investment & Securities · Illustrative

Using valuations as of October 13, Hanwha estimated a “market-implied ROE” in the 12% range, below both the 12-month forward ROE of 25.5% and trailing ROE of 15.7%. It interpreted the price as discounting lower profitability.30 Hanwha did not publish the full reverse-calculation formula, so we could not independently reproduce the model.

Expressing the implied cut in ROE terms

Assuming equity remains unchanged, an ROE in the 12% range is 47–51% of the 25.5% forward estimate—equivalent to a 49–53% haircut. Divide the same report's 12-month forward P/B of 1.17× (October 13) by ROE to obtain P/E. At 25.5% ROE that gives a P/E of about 4.6×; at ROE in the 12% range it gives 9.1–9.8×. The result is therefore algebraically equivalent to assuming a reference P/E of 9.1–9.8× and calculating 1 − 4.6 ÷ 9.1–9.8. Similar results do not constitute independent confirmation (all Snowshagal calculations).30

Q4
HISTORY · PREVIOUS CYCLES

What Happened in Earlier Memory Downturns?

In the last two downturns, the trailing four-quarter operating profit of each of the Big Two fell 56–90% from its own peak; one cycle ended in an operating loss. Those are different benchmarks from today's back-solved scenarios, so they do not tell us whether the next downturn will be shallower or deeper.

Samsung's trailing four-quarter operating profit fell from ₩63tn in Q3 2018 to ₩28tn in Q4 2019, and from ₩58tn in Q2 2022 to ₩6.6tn in Q4 2023. SK hynix fell from ₩21tn in Q3 2018 to ₩2.1tn in Q1 2020, and from ₩15tn in Q2 2022 to a loss of ₩10tn in Q3 2023.16 Using the displayed amounts, the declines work out to 56%, 89% and 90%, respectively (Snowshagal calculations).

Direct comparison would be misleading. The historical figures are realized peaks and troughs for each company; today's figures are hypothetical outcomes for both combined. In Eugene's published scenarios, the Big Two's earnings after 60% and 70% cuts (₩362tn and ₩270tn, respectively) are 23% and 42% below their ₩468tn trailing four-quarter total, which includes preliminary and estimated Q3 figures. The October 15 regression center-line estimate (₩167tn) is 64% below that base; its lower-band and upper-band assumptions (₩278tn and ₩56tn) imply 41% and 88% declines (all Snowshagal calculations). The published scenarios' 23% and 42% gaps are smaller than the historical 56–90% declines; the center-line and upper-band figures fall within that range. But all of these comparisons mix company-specific realized cycle extremes with combined-company hypothetical figures. They cannot establish the likely depth of the next downturn.

Q5
COUNTERARGUMENTS

Could the Market Be Too Pessimistic?

TSMC and ASML offered counterevidence that demand for semiconductors and equipment remains strong. Even so, doubts about returns on investment and tougher supply competition could lead investors to discount the durability of profits. Historical rebound statistics after crashes also come from samples concentrated in a few crises.

COUNTERPOINT · TSMC RESULTS · OCT 15

Q3 beats estimates; Q4 guidance rises further

TSMC reported third-quarter net income of NT$763.1bn, up 69% year-on-year and ahead of LSEG's NT$746.3bn estimate. Revenue of US$46.72bn topped the company's guidance of US$44.6–45.8bn; fourth-quarter revenue guidance was US$52.3–54.3bn.12

Foundry profits cannot be mapped directly onto the margins of the Big Two, which have much greater exposure to memory. Strong earnings are also a separate question from whether high equity prices can be sustained.

COUNTERPOINT · ASML RESULTS · OCT 14

Customers keep raising equipment-demand forecasts

ASML reported third-quarter revenue of €11.662bn and net income of €4.96bn. CEO Christophe Fouquet said customers continued to lift short- and medium-term equipment-demand projections, with some even interested in placing orders for 2029 or 2030. As currently planned, ASML's 2027 and 2028 capacity would not fully meet customer demand.4

This argues against the claim that AI-related demand has already rolled over. Alongside CXMT's announcement, it also shows that robust demand for future equipment and increasingly intense future supply competition can coexist. Equipment orders are no guarantee of the Big Two's memory-chip prices or margins.

DATA POINT · ANTHROPIC (FT REPORT)

Growth slows; adjusted profit turns positive

The Financial Times reported that Anthropic presented third-quarter revenue of approximately $16.8bn to investors, with slower growth than in previous quarters this year. The company also cited positive adjusted earnings for the second consecutive quarter and positive cash flow in Q3.8

This is not evidence that AI demand has vanished. But rapid growth that falls short of expectations can still trigger a valuation reset. Anthropic is privately held; its earnings and cash-flow numbers are company-reported, and adjusted profit excludes certain expenses. They should not be treated as audited final results.

COUNTERPOINT AND CAVEAT · KIWOOM / DAISHIN

High forecasts, but mixed revision trends

Kiwoom argued that the consensus for 2026 KOSPI operating profit (₩970tn) has been revised down during October, while upward revisions have resumed for 2027 (₩1,344tn) and 2028 (₩1,475tn). It did not provide date-by-date revisions.6 Daishin's semiconductor net-income consensus for 2027 is ₩836tn, about 37.5% above the ₩608tn forecast for 2026. But the 2027 estimate has itself fallen 0.6% from ₩841tn at end-September (Snowshagal calculation).18

Forecast levels are still high, but the recent direction of revisions differs by source.

COUNTERPOINT · HANWHA

“6,000 is deep value,” argues Hanwha

Hanwha argued that periods when market-implied ROE falls below trailing ROE have tended to coincide with maximum index drawdowns. It called KOSPI 6,000 a deep-value zone and recommended aggressive buying, citing potential ROE improvement after long-term supply agreements (LTAs) and 2027 HBM price negotiations.30

The supporting evidence was presented in a chart beginning in 2015, without sample counts or an explicit decision rule. The October 15 close of 5,554.22 was already 7.4% below 6,000 (Snowshagal calculation).

COUNTERPOINT AND CAVEAT · EUGENE

Low P/Es and post-crash rebound statistics

Since 2005, buying in Eugene's bottom-decile P/E range of 5.3–8.5× produced an average one-year KOSPI return of +28%, excluding dividends; 94% of those observations were higher one year later. Eugene also reported a 93% one-year positive-return rate after days when the KOSPI 200 fell more than 5% and VKOSPI exceeded 40.16

Today's forward P/E is even below that range. Yet among 36 qualifying crash days, excluding October 13, fully 22 occurred in 2026. The reported 93% positive-return rate reflects 13 of 14 pre-2026 cases, seven of them in 2008–09. Sharp down days within one crisis are not independent events, and their subsequent one-year return windows overlap. VKOSPI closed at 55.38 on October 15, above the sample's threshold of 40, but10that only tells us volatility was elevated. It does not give us the probability of a rebound over the next 12 months.

WHAT TO WATCH

Which Numbers Would Change the View?

Strong semiconductor demand is not, by itself, enough to validate today's earnings forecasts. The companies generating that demand must be able to keep funding investment, and memory manufacturers must retain sufficient profits even as competition grows.

Conversely, a sharp selloff in the bonds of a few AI companies does not establish that the entire chip upcycle is over. TSMC's and ASML's latest disclosures are evidence that semiconductor and equipment demand remains strong. Assume a 10× reference P/E, and today's mid-4× valuation can be reconciled with a 55% cut to projected earnings. But the equation does not reveal what earnings decline the market actually expects.

The questions now are less about how large the forecasts look on paper than how long those earnings can last, how much cash they produce, whether AI investment remains financeable, and whether the discount investors apply to earnings narrows. A low multiple can persist even if earnings estimates hold steady. The table below therefore looks first at price changes alongside like-for-like forward earnings revisions; the P/E is the outcome of that comparison.

GLOSSARY
Reference multiple
The P/E investors might ordinarily assign to a given level of earnings. Here, the term refers to the 12-month forward P/E. It varies with sector mix and the stage of the earnings cycle.
Implied earnings haircut
How far projected earnings must be lowered to reconcile the current price with an assumed benchmark: 1 − price-consistent earnings ÷ 12-month earnings forecast. A reference multiple or regression model must be specified first.
Consensus
An aggregation of earnings estimates from analysts. Figures vary by data provider (FnGuide, Quantiwise, Refinitiv, etc.) and update date.
12-month forward P/E
Share price (or market capitalization) divided by projected earnings over the next 12 months.
Earnings yield (E/P)
The inverse of P/E: expected earnings relative to price, not itself a discount rate. Daishin decomposes changes in this measure into sector composition, interest rates and doubts about cyclical profits.
ROE · TTM · 12MF
Return on equity. TTM uses the most recent four quarters of results; 12MF uses forecasts for the coming 12 months.
Market-implied ROE
An assumed profitability level reverse-engineered from current valuations (Hanwha; formula undisclosed).
Preliminary results
Estimated earnings a company reports before closing its books. Finalized figures follow in the full earnings release.
4F²
A DRAM cell design with area equal to four times the square of the minimum feature size (F). That is roughly 33% smaller than the conventional 6F² cell.
Global Fund Manager Survey
BofA's monthly survey of asset allocations and crowded trades among fund managers worldwide.
Cyclical stocks
Daishin's definition: stocks in the top third of forward-earnings volatility in 2015–20, whose profits depend strongly on product pricing, utilization or order cycles (memory, equipment, chemicals, steel, shipbuilding, etc.).
The Big Two
In this note, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.
Regression and R-squared
A fitted linear relationship between two variables and a measure of in-sample fit. Extrapolating beyond the sample increases uncertainty.
Circuit breaker
A mechanism that pauses trading for 20 minutes if the index remains at least 8% below the previous close for more than one minute (first-stage threshold).
VKOSPI
An index of expected volatility derived from KOSPI 200 option prices.
Forced liquidation
A sale triggered by insufficient collateral, regardless of the investor's wishes. Liquidation of unsettled stock purchases is only one category.
TRS · CFD
Derivatives that transfer gains and losses without direct share ownership. Positions may be closed out forcibly when margin is insufficient.
