What is the difference between
operating profit and net income?

Both are measures of profit. The difference is where you stop the calculation.

THE ANSWER IN ONE SENTENCE
Operating profit is earned from the core business. Net income is the bottom line after adding non-operating gains or losses and subtracting income tax expense.
In SK hynix's Q3, a roughly KRW 32.6tn non-operating loss and KRW 10.9tn in income tax expense left KRW 39.8tn in net income.

Two sources of non-operating losses

Most of the roughly KRW 32.6tn Q3 non-operating loss came from currencies and changes in the value of investment assetsEquity stakes in other companies or financial instruments held for investment rather than for use in core operations. . Let's illustrate the mechanics with hypothetical numbers first.

Here are SK hynix's actual Q3 figures.

①Foreign-exchange losses−KRW 11.6tn
  • At quarter-end, dollar-denominated cash, receivables and payables are remeasured in Korean won using the closing exchange rate.
  • In Q3, a a stronger wonAn appreciation of the Korean won. When the KRW/USD exchange rate falls, each U.S. dollar converts into fewer won. contributed to an FX loss. In Q2, the company instead recorded a gain of about KRW 1.1tn.
②Valuation losses on investments−KRW 22.9tn

  • The company did not identify which assets drove the loss. In Q2, gains related to Kioxia—from a stake sale and rising valuations—totaled about KRW 63tn.
COMMON CONFUSIONA valuation loss ≠ a cash outflow
  • A valuation loss is a decline in an asset's carrying value. It does not mean cash has left the company's bank account.
  • At the end of Q3, SK hynix's cash and liquid financial assetsCash, cash equivalents and assets readily convertible into cash. Company earnings releases may also include short-term financial products in a broader cash-assets figure. totaled KRW 114.5tn, up KRW 26.5tn in three months
  • . Nor is a valuation gain the same as cash received. It differs from a gain on disposalA gain realized when an asset is sold for more than its carrying amount. from actually selling an asset.

Changes in cash reflect operating cash generation as well as uses such as dividends and share buybacks. A valuation loss does not, by itself, explain why cash rose or fell.

Net income halved—yet a special dividend?

On the earnings date, SK hynix declared a dividend of KRW 20,000 per share. Half of it—KRW 10,000 per share—was a special dividendA one-off dividend paid in addition to the regular dividend, often following an exceptional gain or event. funded by gains from the sale of a Kioxia stake.

Net income measures profit recorded for the current quarter. Dividends are paid from available cash, supported by accumulated profits and distributable reserves. A decline in one quarter's net income does not mean an equivalent amount of cash has vanished.

P/E ratios also depend on net income

Divide earnings attributable to shareholders by the relevant share count to get EPSEarnings per share. Broadly, profit attributable to ordinary shareholders divided by the weighted-average number of ordinary shares. Trailing P/E commonly uses earnings from the last four quarters., or earnings per share. Divide the share price by EPS to get the P/E ratioPrice-to-earnings ratio: share price divided by earnings per share. It shows how much investors pay for each unit of annual earnings per share. (seePart 4). The earnings figure in a trailing P/E generally comes from the latest four quarters combined. Large non-operating gains or losses can therefore move the ratio substantially.

The underlying business has not changed, yet the P/E moves from 5× to 20×. When non-operating items are unusually large, a P/E ratio alone may say little about whether a stock is cheap or expensive.

GO DEEPEROther terms you will see beside net income

Net income attributable to owners of the parent · The portion of consolidated net income attributable to parent-company shareholders. Where subsidiaries have outside investors, their share—called non-controlling interests (NCI)Ownership interests in a subsidiary held by investors other than its parent company.—is excluded. Korean filings call this “profit attributable to owners of the parent.” EPS generally uses earnings attributable to ordinary shareholders, rather than total consolidated net income. See Part 14 for the role of outside shareholders in a subsidiary.

Valuation gains/losses vs. disposal gains/losses · Valuation gains and losses arise when an asset's carrying or fair value changes while it is still held. Gains and losses on disposal arise when it is sold. SK hynix's Q2 results included both a one-off gain from selling a Kioxia-related stake and a valuation gain from a rise in the value of the remaining investment; the latter did not itself bring in cash.

FX translation vs. transaction gains/losses · Foreign-currency translation gains and losses arise when dollar-denominated assets or liabilities are remeasured at period-end exchange rates. FX transaction gains and losses arise when transactions are settled at different exchange rates, such as when dollar receivables are collected or converted into won.

Exchange rates also affect operating profit · Semiconductor sales invoiced in dollars must be reported in won. Samsung Electronics said a stronger won reduced its Q3 operating profit by about KRW 10tn quarter on quarter. That is different from non-operating FX gains or losses arising from remeasuring foreign-currency assets and liabilities or settling currency transactions.

How operating profit is defined · Under the financial reporting standards used by Korean listed companies (K-IFRSKorean International Financial Reporting Standards: the accounting framework followed by Korean listed companies.), operating profit has been defined as revenue less cost of sales and selling and administrative expenses. This provides a consistent subtotal for comparing companies.

Why the two losses exceed the total non-operating loss · SK hynix's Q3 FX-related loss (about KRW 11.6tn) and investment valuation loss (about KRW 22.9tn) add up to roughly KRW 34.5tn—more than its total net non-operating loss of about KRW 32.6tn. Other non-operating items therefore offset around KRW 1.9tn, but the company did not provide a detailed breakdown.

Preliminary earnings · Results released on earnings day are preliminary and have not yet undergone the external auditor's review. Figures can change when the quarterly report is filed.

Operating profit changes in 2027 · Under the new accounting standard, K-IFRS 1118, operating profit will become the residual category after items classified as investing, financing and other categories are excluded. The current operating-profit calculation will need to be disclosed separately in the notes. A company's reported operating-profit subtotal may therefore encompass different items under the new standard.

THE SECOND SECTION'S ANSWER
Non-operating results can swing dramatically from one quarter to the next. Following net income alone may conceal what is happening in the core business.
SK hynix's non-operating results swung from a KRW 62.2tn gain in Q2 to a KRW 32.6tn loss in Q3. Meanwhile, operating profit rose from KRW 60.5tn to KRW 83.3tn. Much of the Q3 loss reflected changes in reported asset values rather than cash paid out.
03PUTTING IT TO WORK

How to read
an earnings report

Now apply that income-statement staircase to SK hynix's Q3.

Three questions about SK hynix Q3

Did the core business deteriorate?No

Operating profit rose 38% quarter on quarter to KRW 83.3338tn, a record for the sixth consecutive quarter.

Did non-operating items drive the fall in net income?Yes

Net non-operating results swung from a gain of more than KRW 62tn in Q2 to a loss of roughly KRW 32.6tn in Q3.

Will it happen again next quarter?Unknown

That depends on exchange rates and investment values. We also need the quarterly reportThe formal report filed by a company each quarter. The financial statement notes provide details about individual income and expense items. to know which assets were written down.

Use the same approach with other companies. Korean preliminary earnings announcementsPreliminary earnings are published ahead of the external auditor's review and may be revised later. typically provide five key figures.

Item ③ is the formal line for pre-tax profit from continuing operations; item ⑤ is the portion of net income belonging to the parent company's shareholders. The difference between item ② and item ③ reflects non-operating income and expenses.

Four checks for any earnings headline

01Which kind of profit?

First establish whether the headline refers to operating profit or net income. In a single quarter, the two can move in opposite directions .

02Operating vs. pre-tax profit

The gap reflects non-operating results. If it is large, look in the company's earnings materials for the explanation.

03Cash flow or change in value?

Valuation gains/losses and FX remeasurement effects do not themselves move cash. Distinguish them from gains and losses realized on disposal.

04Will it recur?

A gain from selling a stake is one-off. A P/E ratio based on trailing earnings that include such gains can be distorted.
COMMON MISREADINGSPlausible—but wrong
MISREADING 01
“Net income halved, so business must have collapsed.”
SK hynix delivered record operating profit in Q3. Non-operating results drove the net-income decline. Always ask which profit measure fell first.
MISREADING 02
“The KRW 22tn valuation loss was cash paid out.”
The carrying value of an investment declined. Cash and near-cash financial assets actually increased during the same quarter. A valuation loss is not an expense paid in cash.
MISREADING 03
“If net income exceeds operating profit, it was a better quarter.”
SK hynix's Q2 net income of nearly KRW 93.9tn included gains from selling a stake and marking investments higher. Non-operating gains may not recur.
PART 15 · THE TAKEAWAY

Operating profit measures the core business. Net income is the bottom line after non-operating results and income tax expense.

When net income is above or below operating profit, the question is not which figure is correct. Ask what was added or subtracted between the two, whether any cash actually moved, and whether the event is likely to recur.

Quick glossary of related terms
Income statement
A statement showing a company's revenue, expenses and profit over a reporting period
Core business
The company's main commercial activity; for SK hynix, manufacturing and selling memory chips
Revenue
Income recognized from sales of goods or services; revenue may be recorded before cash is collected
Cost of goods sold (COGS)
The direct cost of producing the goods sold
SG&A expenses
Selling, general and administrative costs of marketing, selling and running the business
Operating profit
Revenue less COGS and SG&A expenses; profit from core operations under the current Korean presentation
Operating margin
Operating profit divided by revenue
Non-operating gains/losses
Gains and losses outside core operations, including FX, investments, financial instruments and interest
FX gains/losses
Gains or losses arising from exchange-rate changes, whether from remeasurement of currency positions or settlement
Valuation gains/losses
Changes recognized in the value of investments still held
Disposal gains/losses
Gains or losses realized on the sale of an asset
Profit before tax
Profit before recognizing income tax expense; here, operating profit plus net non-operating results
Income tax expense
Tax cost recognized in the income statement; not necessarily the amount of tax actually paid
Net income
The final profit figure after income tax expense
Profit attributable to parent owners
The parent shareholders' portion of consolidated net income
EPS
Earnings per share: earnings divided by the relevant share count; trailing EPS typically covers four quarters
P/E ratio
Price-to-earnings ratio: share price divided by EPS
Special dividend
An additional, usually one-off dividend beyond the regular payout
Consolidated basis
Financial figures combining a parent company with its subsidiaries
Disclosure
Information a company is required to publish for investors
Cash and near-cash assets
Cash, cash equivalents and other assets readily convertible into cash
Preliminary earnings
Results released before the external auditor's review

Important notes on the numbers

58% · The decline from KRW 93.9226tn in Q2 to KRW 39.7756tn in Q3 is roughly 57.7% (57.6% in some reports). Company materials and several news articles round it to 58%, which is the figure used here.

One decimal place in KRW tn · To make the waterfall and tables add up consistently, figures are rounded to one decimal place in KRW trillions. Calculations are based on company disclosures. Q3 profit before tax was KRW 50.7085tn. Q2 non-operating results—calculated as disclosed profit before tax less operating profit—were about KRW 62.1657tn. Some articles reported different values, such as KRW 50.7080tn and KRW 62.2166tn. Q1 non-operating results (about KRW 14tn) and income tax expense (about KRW 11.3tn) were calculated using reported pre-tax profit of KRW 51.61tn.

Investment valuation loss · The assets behind the KRW 22.92tn valuation loss were not identified. The fact that Q2 gains were Kioxia-related does not establish that the entire Q3 loss came from Kioxia.

Share price on earnings day · SK hynix closed at KRW 1,487,000 on October 28, up 10.89% from KRW 1,341,000 the previous day. One breaking report gave a different rise of 11.41%. Multiple market-moving developments occurred that day, so the share-price response cannot be isolated to any single earnings figure.

Income tax expense · This is an income-statement expense and may differ from taxes actually paid that quarter, including because of deferred tax. The simulator's 22% and the bakery's 20% are illustrative assumptions. SK hynix's Q3 income tax expense was about 21.6% of pre-tax profit (calculated for this article).
