About 10 min · 1 quiz · 1 interactive calculator

The essentialsIn three points
  • When stocks are bought with broker loans, the collateral coverage ratio at the close (account collateral value ÷ loan balance) must meet the broker's minimum. Otherwise, the broker asks for more collateral. The usual minimum is 140%.
  • If you miss the deadline, the broker generally sends a market sell order into the opening call auction two business days after the collateral shortfall. A failed cash settlement follows a different clock: the third business day after purchase.
  • The number of shares sold comes from a formula. The broker uses a discounted price to calculate how many shares must be sold to restore the required coverage. The sale can be several times larger than the shortfall.

01THE CASE

The 140% threshold

In March 2026, South Korea's Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) published an investor dispute: an account was about ₩2 million short of required collateral. Yet forced liquidationWhen pledged shares no longer provide enough collateral—or a repayment deadline is missed—the broker sells shares without the investor's approval. of the investor's shares totaled roughly ₩30 million.

In Part 5, we saw how borrowing can magnify stock-market losses. Now we follow the numbers and deadlines inside the account: when does the broker sell, and why can the sale dwarf the shortfall?

The timing matters. In November 2026, South Korea temporarily changed the rules governing stock purchases financed by brokers.

  2. 11.6

  3. 11.9

What does the 140% actually measure?

Buying shares with money borrowed from a brokerage is called a margin loanA brokerage loan used to buy shares. In South Korea, this is formally a credit-transaction loan, and the purchased shares serve as collateral.. The purchased shares secure that loan. Each day, the broker checks whether the collateral is worth enough relative to the debt.

Collateral coverageAccount collateral value ÷ loan balance. If you invest ₩4 million of your own money and borrow ₩6 million to buy 1,000 shares of fictional Gana Electronics (₩10 million in total), the calculation is 10 ÷ 6 = about 167%.
Required minimumMaintenance collateral ratioThe lowest collateral coverage ratio allowed before the broker requests more collateral. It varies by firm and stock; 140% is common. sets the minimum; 140% is common. On a ₩6 million loan, the collateral must stay at or above ₩8.4 million.
Trigger priceLoan × 1.4 ÷ number of shares. For 1,000 shares backed by a ₩6 million loan: ₩6 million × 1.4 ÷ 1,000 = ₩8,400. That is a 16% drop from the purchase price.

What can change the calculation?

Higher thresholdsSome stocks require 150% or 160%. During the current relief period, some brokerages have reduced their minimum to 130%.
Cash in the accountAdded at full value to the collateral total.
Other sharesIncluded if pledged, sometimes at only a percentage of market value.

Does crossing the line trigger an immediate sale?

No. Falling below the trigger price does not mean your shares are sold on the spot. There is a sequence.

QUICK QUIZTest yourself

Fictional Gana Electronics closes Monday at ₩8,100. With ₩6 million borrowed, the account's collateral coverage is 135%—below its 140% minimum. If the investor does nothing on Tuesday, when will the broker normally place its forced-sale order?

Answer: ③

The key date is the day collateral fell short (Monday). The broker checks Monday's closing values and requests more collateral. Tuesday is the deadline. If the account still falls short, a sell order is entered before Wednesday's opening auction. Timing can differ by a day depending on the brokerage and coverage level. Some firms currently allow eligible clients to request a one-day postponement.

How many shares could be sold on Wednesday morning? At Monday's closing price, the collateral shortfall is just ₩300,000 (₩8.4m − ₩8.1m). But the broker does not simply sell ₩300,000 worth. Depending on its formula, the sale can exceed ₩1.5 million—or consume all 1,000 shares.

when, and at what price?

THE SHORT ANSWER
When closing collateral coverage falls below the requirement and the shortfall remains past the deadline, shares are normally sold at the opening two business days later. The quantity is set by a brokerage formula that discounts the previous closing price.
02THE RULES

the formula decides

Two kinds of borrowing can lead to a forced sale.

Brokerage margin loanMoney borrowed from a broker to buy stocks. The loan has a term—often 90 or 180 days, sometimes extendable—and accrues interest. The collateral-coverage rule above applies here.
Unpaid settlement balanceA debt arising when an investor pays only part of the purchase price upfront and fails to pay the remainder by the settlement date. A securities-backed loanA loan secured by shares you already own. Its collateral requirements and forced-sale rules resemble those for a brokerage margin loan.—borrowing against shares already held—also follows similar collateral rules.

The broker normally makes this determination using values finalized after the close, not intraday readings. A brief dip below 140% during trading is not a shortfall if the ratio recovers by the close.

A steeper drop can bring the deadline forward. IBK Investment & Securities requires payment on the same day the shortfall occurs if coverage falls below 130%. Check your broker's own terms for its deadlines.

The morning of the forced sale

Here is how the morning after the payment deadline typically unfolds.

  1. 8:00

    Nextrade's pre-market sessionTrading before the regular session. Nextrade currently operates it from 8:00 to 8:50 a.m. opens (see Part 18). Some brokers tell clients they can sell there themselves to repay the loan.

  2. 8:30

    The Korea Exchange (KRX) opening call auctionOrders are collected before the regular session and matched at one opening price. At KRX, this auction runs from 8:30 to 9:00 a.m. See Part 18. begins. The forced-sale order is normally entered as a market orderAn order to buy or sell at the best price available, without specifying a limit price. to sell. Some firms use a limit order at the daily price floor instead.

  3. 8:50

    Automated cancellation cutoff. If the investor adds funds or sells shares to repay the debt before this deadline, the automated order is withdrawn. This is the usual cutoff at most large brokerages. Mirae Asset Securities moved its cutoff from 8:30 to 8:50 a.m. on October 19, 2026.

  4. 9:00

    The auction orders match at the day's opening priceThe first traded price of the session. KRX determines it by matching accumulated opening auction orders at 9:00 a.m.. If the stock opens below the previous close, the forced sale occurs at that lower price.

If you have paid in or sold shares, confirm that the forced-liquidation order has actually been canceled.

Why can the sale far exceed the shortfall?

The quantity is not based simply on the missing cash. Shares are sold to pay down the loan until the collateral left in the account meets the minimum ratio against the remaining debt. The broker also assumes the stock may open lower, so it calculates the required quantity using a discounted share price.

Selling one share reduces the debt and therefore the collateral required—but it also removes one pledged share. The gap closes by only the difference between those amounts. Consider the quiz account: ₩6 million borrowed against 1,000 Gana Electronics shares, with a previous close of ₩8,100.

Unpaid settlement works differently: there is no need to restore a collateral ratio. This does not multiply the quantity in the same way, but if the opening price is higher than the floor, shares worth more than the amount owed may still be sold.

The loan also comes with a term and interest

Margin-loan rates vary by borrowing period, brokerage, customer tier and whether the account was opened in person or online. In May 2026, the average rate for the longest loan period at ten major brokerages was 9.38%. Two methods of charging interest can produce different bills.

If the sale does not repay the loan · If a steep decline means selling all the shares cannot cover the loan, the difference remains payable. Overdue interest may accrue, and unpaid debt may be reported to the Korea Credit Information Services, restricting future margin trading (FSS). Korea Investment & Securities' November 2026 guide gives an example: with ₩5 million borrowed and a prior close of ₩5,230, all 1,000 shares are subject to liquidation. Selling at ₩4,500 raises ₩4.5 million, leaving another ₩500,000 to pay. The total loss is ₩5.5 million—more than the investor's original ₩5 million cash investment.

Three months without the mandatory 140% rule

During the exemption, brokerages are not required by regulation to maintain the 140% floor. Instead, they set thresholds based on market conditions. Their responses have broadly followed two approaches.

What the exemption covers—and what it doesn't

CoveredThe regulatory collateral requirement for broker credit exposure (credit extensionA brokerage's lending of money or securities to investors, including margin loans and loans secured by existing stock holdings.). How much relief applies to margin loans or securities-backed loans depends on the firm.
UnchangedForced selling when a purchase remains unpaid by its settlement deadline.
How much breathing room?Lowering a threshold from 140% to 130% allows only about another 7% decline (130 ÷ 140 ≈ 0.93). A further drop beyond that can still trigger a sale under the reduced requirement.

The exemption expires on Sunday, January 31, 2027. There are concerns that when the normal minimum returns, accounts still below the restored requirement could face forced sales at the same time.

Four checks for your own account

01Before you borrow: the trigger price

Loan × your broker's required coverage ÷ share count. If you borrowed ₩6 million against 1,000 shares and your broker's minimum was 130%, the trigger price would be ₩7,800. Compare it with today's closing price to see how close you are.

02Three items in the agreement

Check the current collateral minimum; the price the broker uses to calculate the forced-sale quantity (15% below the previous close or the daily price floor); and whether margin interest is charged retroactively or tier by tier.

03If you receive a warning

Check the amount due and deadline first. There are three ways to meet the requirement:
  • Deposit cash
  • Sell some shares to reduce the debt
  • Pledge other eligible securities (substitute collateralStocks or other securities accepted instead of cash as collateral. Only a specified percentage of market value is counted.)
Watch the clock, too.
  • On the morning of liquidation, check the 8:50 a.m. automated cancellation cutoff;
  • the application window for postponement (for example, 4–5 p.m. the previous day at Korea Investment and 5–10 p.m. at Kiwoom);
  • and, if you hold several stocks, the deadline to request a change to which security is sold.

04How you'll be notified

Collateral-shortfall warnings go to the phone number or contact method agreed when opening the account. Even if messages are blocked or go unread, forced selling still proceeds.

Reported forced sales tend to lag the market sell-off by several days. Forced selling tied to unpaid settlement rose from ₩7 billion on November 6 to ₩37.9 billion on November 9 and ₩63 billion on November 10.

A fall in outstanding brokerage margin loans does not necessarily mean all borrowing used to buy stocks has declined. In November, personal unsecured loans and overdraft balances at some commercial banks increased. That does not establish that the money went into shares. But stock investments funded with bank loans do not appear in either brokerage margin-loan balances or the reported forced-liquidation series. They may not trigger a broker's forced sale, but the losses and debt still belong to the investor.

COMMON MISREADINGSPlausible—but wrong
WRONG 01
"Coverage fell below 140% during trading, so my shares get sold today."
The intraday figure keeps changing. A shortfall is determined by closing values, and the broker normally gives you time to add collateral before selling.
WRONG 02
"If the broker delays the forced sale, my loss gets smaller."
A postponement changes the sale date, not the amount borrowed. The debt and interest remain.If the stock falls further in the meantime, it may sell for even less.
WRONG 03
"The stock bounced after liquidation, so the broker was wrong to sell."
If the broker sold at the time and in the amount specified by the agreement, a later rebound does not prove wrongdoing. The FSS has called that a matter of hindsight. The real question is whether the broker followed the agreed procedure. In one case, it held the broker responsible for mishandling an investor's request to change which security was sold.
PART 20 · ONE TAKEAWAY

Forced liquidation does not strike the instant a stock falls. It arrives on a morning determined by the closing numbers and the payment deadline.

A ₩2 million collateral shortfall turning into a ₩30 million forced sale was not random. It came from a calculation written into the brokerage agreement.

Key terms in one line
Forced liquidation
A broker sells shares without the investor's approval after a collateral shortfall or missed repayment. Also called a compulsory sale.
Brokerage margin loan
Money borrowed from a broker to buy shares; the purchased shares secure the loan.
Collateral coverage ratio
Account collateral value ÷ loan balance. Usually assessed on finalized closing values.
Minimum coverage ratio
The level below which the broker requests more collateral, often 140%.
Additional collateral
A margin call to make up a shortfall, satisfied by cash, selling shares or pledging eligible securities.
Unpaid settlement balance
Debt arising when the full purchase price is not paid by settlement day. Forced selling generally takes place three business days after purchase.
Restricted cash-only account
A 30-day restriction after an unpaid settlement: new purchases must be fully paid in cash at any brokerage.
Securities-backed loan
Borrowing against shares already in your account.
Substitute collateral
Eligible securities accepted instead of cash, counted at a specified percentage of their market value.
Retroactive vs tier-by-tier interest
Two ways to calculate interest: apply the final rate tier to the full loan term or charge each tier only for its own days.
Broker credit extension
A broker's lending of cash or securities, including margin financing and securities-backed loans.
Outstanding margin loans
The total amount borrowed to buy stocks that has not yet been repaid.
How to interpret these figures

Terms for new loans · Brokerages are changing their terms. For loans newly entered into on or after November 9, 2026, Korea Investment & Securities raised its initial deposit requirement from 45% to 50% (60% for Samsung Electronics and SK hynix) and shortened the standard loan term from 180 to 90 days (renewable in 90-day periods). Earlier loans keep their original terms.

"Two days later" · This means business days. Korea Investment & Securities' agreement specifies the liquidation date as the collateral-shortfall date plus two days.

8:50 a.m. · As reported in October 2026, this was the usual automated-cancellation cutoff at ten major brokerages. It is a brokerage operational deadline, not a legally prescribed time. Check your own firm's notice.

195 shares, ₩1,546 · These figures appeared in Korea Investment & Securities' margin-trading guides revised on August 8, 2025 and February 7, 2026. Its November 9, 2026 guide uses a revised example with a ₩5 million loan: for a ₩200,000 shortfall, 155 shares are sold, raising about ₩930,000 at ₩6,000 per share, or 4.7 times the shortfall. The formula is unchanged: shares to liquidate = (collateral value − loan × minimum coverage ratio) ÷ (previous closing price − calculation price × minimum coverage ratio). The calculation price is 15% below the previous close. Other brokerages may use different formulas and prices.

Forced-sale statistics · In June 2026, the FSS reported that average daily forced sales for margin loans and unpaid settlement combined at ten leading brokerages rose from about ₩10 billion in 2025 to ₩37.3 billion in May 2026. That covers more than KOFIA's unpaid-settlement-only series.

Interest rate · The 9.38% rate is the May 2026 average for online accounts at ten major brokerages in the tier beyond 90 days. Rates may have changed after the July 2026 policy-rate increase.
