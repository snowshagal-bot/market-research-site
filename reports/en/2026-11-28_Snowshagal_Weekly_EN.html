WHAT TO WATCH
  • Sun., Nov. 29 Investment management committee of the stabilization fund's contributing institutions.
  • Nov. 30 Emergency financial-sector market stability review chaired by the head of the Financial Services Commission (FSC); heads of the five largest financial holding groups and Korea Development Bank (KDB) to attend.
  • From Nov. 30 Whether the timing of capital calls under existing commitments or the amount of any expansion is announced.

Current size

  • : Existing capital commitments of ₩10.76tn (2020), less than 0.3% of KOSPI market cap.
  • Reports suggest an expansion to ₩20tn or more; as of Nov. 27, the finance ministry said expansion had not yet been discussed.
  • KDB is sounding out contributors on whether existing commitments can be drawn and whether they would provide additional capital.

Historical precedent

  • Of six interventions since 1990, actual share purchases occurred in just three: 1990, 2003 and 2008 (Hanwha Investment & Securities).
  • All three were followed by further declines after the announcement; worst returns within one year: -24.6% · -5.8% · -27.5%
  • The KOSPI rose after one week in just two of six cases; more cases were positive one year later.
  • The latest peak-to-trough drawdown before the announcement, -46.6%, was the deepest of the six.

Other policy tools

  • Taiwan combined stabilization fund deployment with short-selling bans (Kiwoom Securities).
  • A short-selling ban would require weighing its costs and benefits against Korea's bid for MSCI developed-market status.
SIGNS OF RESILIENCE
  • Specific timing or amounts for deployment are announced.
  • Trading value returns to its previous five-session average.
DOWNSIDE SIGNALS
  • The meeting merely reiterates readiness.
  • KOSPI falls below 4,700.

TOP PRIORITY · 01

Semiconductors & Memory

The question is not how big the rebound is, but whether it spreads to the chip giants.

Why Nvidia's calendar matters

  • The hearing concerns oversight of AI chip export controls, the same risk behind iM Securities' worst-case assumption of lost sales through indirect channels to China.
  • The quarterly report may disclose litigation or risks not included in the earnings release; that is what matters.

The China factor

  • U.S. remote-access rules took effect on Nov. 19; legislation bringing remote access under export controls passed the House (Eugene Investment & Securities).
  • Other export-control bills are due to be settled in December through negotiations on the final National Defense Authorization Act.
VIEWS FROM THE STREET
THE VALUATION CASE

iM Securities Even in its worst-case scenario (10% of revenue via indirect China sales disappears and 12 GW of U.S. data center capacity is delayed to 2029), the 2028 DRAM supply-demand balance is +1%, close to equilibrium with supply 1% above demand. A 1x P/B multiple (on next year’s estimated book value per share) implies the following discounts to consensus 2028 earnings: Samsung Electronics -79% · SK hynix -66%.

Eugene Investment & Securities Estimated KOSPI operating profit this year is ₩997tn, 2.3 times the initial forecast; the 12-month forward P/E of 3.3x is at a historical low.

Hanwha Investment & Securities A major order cycle for materials, components and equipment as four megafabs procure in sequence. Equipment orders for the first megafab, Y1, have begun.

THE CASE FOR WAITING

iM Securities Korean chip inventory growth has turned positive. Drawdowns from June highs: Samsung Electronics -49% · SK hynix -62% ; both deeper than historical average downcycle declines (−41% and −51%, respectively).

Hana Securities If November memory exports fall m/m, fundamentals come into question; a standalone memory-stock strategy would make little sense.

Hanwha Investment & Securities The 2027 SK hynix operating profit estimate has fallen below the low end of the estimate range.

The premise The valuation case assumes no major cuts to 2027–28 earnings. If inventories and exports deteriorate first, the same P/E multiple may no longer look cheap.

MAY BEAR THE COST OF POLICY

Banks & Financial Holding CompaniesKB Financial -4.86%

This week

KB Financial -4.86% · Hana Financial Group -4.60% · Shinhan Financial Group -2.67% · Woori Financial Group -1.74% · KOSPI Financials +1.26%, supported by strong brokerage shares.

Why

Banks account for nearly half of stabilization fund commitments. Capital calls or an expansion would increase risk-weighted assets, putting pressure on capital ratios.

The numbers

The BOK raised its policy rate to 3.25% on Nov. 26 (5–2 vote). Its conditional six-month rate projections (three dots from each of seven members) had a median of 3.50%; seven of 21 dots were at 3.75%. Kyobo, SK, Eugene, Hanwha, Daishin and Kiwoom all expect a February hike and a terminal rate of 3.50%.

Watch

Whether additional bank capital commitments are determined at the Nov. 30 meeting; lending rates ahead of the February BOK meeting.

Risk

Rate hikes support interest income but put pressure on funding costs and loan demand. Large fund contributions could constrain shareholder returns.
