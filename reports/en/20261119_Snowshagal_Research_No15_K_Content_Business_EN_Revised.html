How K-Content
Makes Money
Now

Why record revenue failed to lift entertainment stocks.
Concerts, merchandise, new acts—and tourist spending.

Combined revenue at Korea's four major entertainment agencies hit a record in January–September. Yet by Nov. 17, their shares were down 36–53% from the start of the year. Most of the revenue growth came from one company, while both the business model and its biggest earners are changing. Foreign arrivals also topped 20 million for the first time—but visitors now spend their money differently.

OPENINGIN2026.11.18
Introduction

Shares Surged.
Earnings Hadn't Changed.

Hover over underlined terms for definitions, or tap on mobile. Red dotted lines flag key concepts. Select a + panel to expand the analysis.

On Nov. 18, at a summit in Shenzhen, South Korea and China announced the substantial conclusion of follow-up FTA talks on services and investmentafter eight years and eight months of negotiations.1 Entertainment stocks moved twice that day.

  • Regular session Shares rose on reports that the two countries were discussing cultural exchanges. SM closed up 14%.2
  • After the close Details of the FTA outcome were reported only after the regular session ended. In the subsequent after-hours marketSM hit its daily price limit.
  • Concerts Were Not Included The announcement covered proposed market access for films, games, television co-productions and travel agencies. It did not authorize K-pop concerts in mainland China. Lifting theunofficial Chinese restrictions on Korean entertainment remains a market hope, not a confirmed policy change. See Section 05 for details.1, 4
  • A Second Headline After the bell, HYBE also disclosed plans to acquire 50.1% of Japanese entertainment company KeyHolder for about ₩105.7bn.5 The after-hours move in HYBE also reflected this news. Its contribution cannot be separated from the FTA announcement.
  • What Matters Nothing changed in these companies' earnings overnight. Expectations for future earnings changed.

The session captured the contradiction in K-pop equities: record revenue, but shares down an average 43% year-to-date as of the previous close. HYBE had lost more than half its value. In this sector, investors price the next act, not just the latest quarter.

The rally continued Nov. 19. HYBE gained another 14% and SM 8% in the regular session, which ended at 4:30 p.m. because of the college entrance exam.3 Reports that day said the government had opened further channels for discussing concert permits with China (Section 05).

Six days earlier, on Nov. 12, South Korea welcomed its 20 millionth foreign visitor of the year—a first.6 K-content was the most-cited reason for considering a trip to Korea.7 While markets fixated on semiconductors, K-content was producing both records and steep share-price losses.

What This Report Covers

  • Sections 01–02 How much did revenue rise? How much became profit? Why did stocks fall?
  • Sections 03–05 How the revenue model shifts from albums to live shows and merchandise, how newer acts replace established ones, and how export markets shift from Japan toward the U.S. and China.
  • Sections 06–07 · Extended Analysis The same principle applies to tourism: rising visitor numbers and sales do not guarantee profits for every business. Where does tourist spending land, and how does regulation affect what companies retain?

This is an analysis of the numbers, not a recommendation to buy or sell a stock.

TRACK01RECORD YEAR
2026 BY THE NUMBERS

Record Revenue and Exports.
Album Sales Rebound.

Start with the good news. Combined revenue at the four agencies hit a record, as did physical-album exports. Domestic album sales also rebounded after two straight years of declines.

  • Four-Agency Revenue HYBE, SM, JYP and YG generated a record ₩5.662tn in revenue in January–September, up 50.9% y/y. Operating profit rose 32.6% to ₩446.3bn (Kiwoom Securities).8 Revenue for the third quarter alone reached ₩2.241tn.9
  • Operating Profit Still Below 2023 The four agencies earned ₩446.3bn in operating profit through September, 16% below the ₩528.7bn reported for the same period in 2023.10 Exclude HYBE's roughly ₩255bn one-off share-based compensation charge, and the figure approaches ₩700bn—above 2023 (our calculation). Revenue hit a record; reported operating profit did not.
  • HYBE's Record Quarter HYBE reported its highest quarterly revenue, ₩1.526tn in Q3. Nine-month sales reached ₩3.674tn, putting ₩4tn in annual revenue within reach.11
  • Physical Album Sales Domestic album sales reached 85.1m units in January–October, up 11.7%. After two consecutive annual declines, sales are on track to top 100m for the first time in three years (based onCircle Chart data, as cited by Kiwoom Securities).8, 12
  • Album Exports Exports grew 57.5% to more than $400m in the first ten months, already exceeding the full-year 2025 figure (Korea Customs Service data via Kiwoom).8
The combined top line looks exceptional. At the company level, however, only two agencies grew Q3 revenue, and only two improved operating profit.
TRACK02DE-RATING
THE GAP BETWEEN EARNINGS AND SHARES

Revenue Soared.
Why Did Stocks Fall?

By Nov. 17, all four stocks were down 36–53% from the start of the year, while the KOSPI had gained 18%. Why the disconnect?

  • How Far Did They Fall? As of Nov. 17: HYBE −53%, SM −43%, JYP −41%, YG −36%.3 Their combined market value shrank from ₩24.1tn on Feb. 19 to ₩10.9tn by mid-November (Kiwoom Securities).8
  • The Valuation Multiple Fell, Too Share prices reflect earnings multiplied by what investors will pay for them. The four agencies' average 12-month forward P/Efell from 24.3× at the start of the year to 15× in November—more than one-third below their five-year average of 23.4× (Kiwoom Securities).8 A falling multiple despite stable or rising earnings is called de-rating.
  • Trading Dried Up Average daily trading value across the four stocks fell from ₩270bn in January to ₩51bn in September. Market attention and capital shifted to semiconductors (Kiwoom Securities).8

REASON 1 · THE GROUP-TOTAL ILLUSION

The four agencies did post record revenue. But splitting the increase by company tells a different story.

  • Other three agencies Excluding HYBE, nine-month revenue was ₩1.988tn (+9.3%) and operating profit ₩283.3bn (−2.5%). Kiwoom argues that merchandise growth (+20%) merely filled the gap left by established acts finishing their tours.8
  • One Company's Record, One Tour BTS's world tour drove HYBE's revenue surge, drawing roughly 70,000 people per show (Kiwoom Securities).8 Next year's results will be compared against quarters that included that tour.

ONE MORE NUMBERRecord Operating Profit, Minimal Earnings for Shareholders

  • Third quarter HYBE reported record operating profit of ₩188.5bn in Q3, but net income was just ₩11.3bn—down 90% q/q. Non-operating items produced a net loss of ₩148.6bn, which the company said was largely a paper loss from revaluing foreign-currency cash as the won strengthened.15 Net income attributable to parent shareholders was a loss of ₩6.8bn.16
  • January–September Operating profit was ₩162.9bn, alongside a ₩35.6bn net loss and a ₩63bn loss attributable to parent shareholders. The results include about ₩255bn in one-off share-based compensation expense. The controlling shareholder transferred existing shares to the company, which held them as treasury shares before distributing them to employees. Their value was recognized as an expense.17, 18 No cash left the company, and no new shares were issued. HYBE separately reported adjusted nine-month operating profit of ₩417.9bn, excluding that expense.16 Reported profit and profit excluding one-offs tell different stories.
  • Share Price The day after the results, Nov. 4, HYBE shares fell 11.5% to a 52-week low.3, 19 The net-income figure alone cannot explain the full decline.

An FX revaluation loss does not mean operations deteriorated; it could reverse if exchange rates turn. But equity holders also care about the profit attributable to them. Focusing on revenue and operating profit misses that distinction.

REASON 2 · GROWTH SLOWED

Fast-growing companies command higher multiples. That helped entertainment stocks trade above 20× earnings. When growth slows, the multiple is often the first thing to fall.

  • Profit Growth at the Other Three Operating-profit growth at SM, JYP and YG slowed from more than 40% annually during 2020–2023 to low single digits from 2024 onward (Yuanta Securities).13
  • The Seven-Year Clock The standard exclusive artist contract lasts seven years. Afterward, the agency's share of revenue may shrink, or renewal may fail. Investors assign less lasting value to profits concentrated in veteran groups (Kiwoom Securities).8

REASON 3 · HITS CANNOT BE FORECAST

  • An Industry Without an Order Backlog Shipbuilders and defense contractors can estimate next year's revenue from signed orders. Music agencies cannot count concertgoers until tour schedules are announced.
  • Earnings Forecasts Follow Tour Announcements Kiwoom describes entertainment as an industry where hit-making cannot be assumed. It argues that a world-tour announcement—not quarterly earnings—restored the sector's multiple after it fell to 15× in 2024.8
  • The Same Pattern on Nov. 18–19 News, not new earnings figures, moved the stocks.

The Outlook Varies Widely by Company

The question investors asked this year was not how much revenue these companies generated, but whether they could repeat it—and keep it as profit for shareholders. Expectations alone can lift stocks, as on Nov. 18–19. Sustained gains, however, need evidence that concerts and merchandise can generate recurring profits. The next three sections examine where that evidence might emerge.
TRACK03LIVE & MERCH
HOW THE BUSINESS MAKES MONEY

From Album Sales
to Concerts and Merch

Entertainment earnings once followed the album-release calendar. Concerts and merchandise now drive much of the top line.

  • Live Shows and Merchandise Combined concert revenue across the four agencies reached ₩1.8095tn in January–September (+95%). Revenue from merchandise and licensingrose to ₩1.2227tn (+54%), versus growth of 32% for albums and recorded music (Kiwoom Securities).8
  • Merch Grew Beyond Album Releases In Q3, merchandise revenue rose 23% at SM, 44% at YG and 47% at JYP.8, 20
  • Every Concert Is a Storefront HYBE sold ₩107bn in merchandise on its North American tour in Q3 alone (Kiwoom Securities).8

A Larger Concert Mix Can Compress Margins

An individual concert may become more profitable as attendance rises. Yet a larger concert share can reduce an agency's overall profit margin. Here's a simple example using ₩100 in ticket revenue.

  • In the Forecasts HYBE's gross marginis projected to fall from 47.4% in 2022 to 34.1% in 2026. Kiwoom cites concerts' rising share of revenue, from 14.5% to 39.7%, as the main driver.8
  • Why Merchandise Matters Merchandise generally carries better margins than concerts. Combined merchandise and licensing revenue at the four agencies tripled from ₩351.4bn in 2020 to ₩1.0882tn in 2025 (Kiwoom Securities).8
  • Sales Beyond Concert Venues Of the 426 pop-up stores opened by the four agencies over the past year, 77% were overseas (Kiwoom Securities).8

Albums Are Shifting From Listening to Collecting

  • U.S. U.S. physical-music revenue rose 25.9% in the first half, far outpacing overall music-market growth of 6.9% (RIAA data cited by Kyobo Securities).21
  • Albums as Collectibles Albums sold as keychains, cards and other merchandise-style formats made up 17.8% of sales among Circle Chart's top 400 albums, up from 11.3% in 2023.22
Concerts have lifted revenue while thinning overall margins. Merchandise could offset part of that pressure. That makes the merchandise line in an agency's earnings report worth watching.
TRACK04NEXT ACTS
THE NEXT REVENUE GENERATORS

A New Generation
Takes the Stage

Established groups have generated most of the profits so far. Brokers expect younger acts to begin taking on a bigger role from 2027.

Entertainment agencies treat each act as an IP asset. Why does year four matter? Groups often make their first tours in years two and three, then graduate to venues holding more than 10,000 people in year four—a step-change in scale (Kiwoom Securities).8

  • New Groups, Bigger Stages Roughly 19 groups debuting in 2024–2026 are set to reach the large-venue phase over the next three years. The 13 acts from 2024–2025 hit year four in 2027–2028; the 2026 cohort follows in 2029 (Kiwoom Securities).8
  • Starting From a Bigger Base Groups debuting in 2024 averaged 293,000 concertgoers by year three, 2.5× the 119,000 for the previous generation (Kiwoom Securities).8
  • Already a Major Share of Albums This year, newer actsaccount for roughly 40% of album sales (Eugene Investment & Securities).23
  • Streaming Audiences Are Growing Aggregate Spotify monthly listenersacross major acts at the four agencies total 248.95m, up 4.5%. Excluding the three largest acts, growth rises to 8.1% (Yuanta Securities).13

All Four Agencies Plan New Debuts in 2027

  • An Unusual Year All four major agencies plan to launch new groups in 2027, which Hana Securities describes as highly unusual.24
  • New Acts Cost Money First Development costs come before revenue. If a debut succeeds, album sales, merchandise and touring revenue follow in stages (Kiwoom Securities).8
  • The Margin Opportunity Agencies generally retain a larger share early in an artist's contract. Kiwoom expects margins to improve as newer groups contribute more revenue.8, 13

Not Everyone Agrees on the Scale

  • The Concern DS Investment & Securities sums up this year's selloff as concern over what comes after the biggest groups.25
  • More Acts Do Not Guarantee More Profit More newer groups are entering the pipeline. But their future earnings depend on concerts not yet staged. Three tests connect the two: (1) do venues get larger, (2) how many seats sell, and (3) how much profit remains per show? New-act development costs and the gap left by veteran groups must also be covered.
If newer acts become the main earners, agencies may retain a greater share of revenue. Tour dates and venue capacity are early signals. Ticket sales and reported earnings will ultimately show whether that translates into hits and profits.
TRACK05WORLD TOUR
WHERE THE MONEY COMES FROM

From Japan to China and the U.S.:
K-Pop Album Exports Shift

For years, Japan was the biggest overseas buyer of Korean physical albums. This year, China and the United States overtook it in export value.

  • U.S. Eight of the top ten CDs sold in the U.S. in the first half were K-pop albums (Kiwoom Securities).8 U.S. CD unit sales rose 16.0% in the first half; excluding K-pop, growth was just 6.7% (Kyobo Securities).21
  • Europe Album exports to Germany rose 181% and to the U.K. 111% in January–September (Kyobo Securities).21
  • Concert Audiences Are Expanding, Too Western markets' share of concert attendance at the four agencies rose from 20% in 2019 to 33% in 2026 (Kiwoom Securities).8
  • Locally Cast Groups One localized group —formed with members recruited overseas and debuted in its home market—attracted 597,000 concertgoers in its third year. Two more such acts are expected to debut in the U.S. next year (Kiwoom Securities).8
  • More Markets, More Room to Grow New acts can target different regions instead of competing for a fixed pool of fans, Kiwoom argues.8

Records and Merch Sell. The Concert Door Remains Shut.

China has effectively restricted the distribution of South Korean popular culture since around 2016. Business never stopped entirely: China now leads album exports, and SM opened a 335-square-meter official merchandise store in Shanghai on April 29.27 What remained restricted was large-scale concerts on the mainland. The key is what the Nov. 18 announcement promised—and what it did not.

  • South Korean Government's Position The presidential office said Nov. 18, local time, that the agreement does not require either country to approve the other's artists for concerts. But it said more channels were now open for discussions that could lead to permits. Beijing treats large gatherings differently from films and games, so smaller shows might be an easier starting point.30
  • China's Position On Nov. 19, China's Ministry of Commerce confirmed the substantial conclusion of negotiations, naming culture and internet services among the areas of cooperation. It made no mention of concerts. Remaining steps include technical finalization, translation, legal review and signature.31
  • An Official “Lifting” May Never Be Declared China has never formally acknowledged the existence of the restrictions, making an official repeal announcement unlikely, according to SK Securities.28
  • Concerts Would Still Take Time Tours are normally planned at least six months ahead. Even if permits are agreed next year, performances in China may not resume until 2028 or later, SK Securities forecasts.28 Daol Investment & Securities expects any earnings impact no earlier than the second half of 2027.32
  • What a Reopening Would Mean There are two effects to separate. Existing album and merchandise sales could rise. More importantly, concerts would create ticket and on-site merchandise revenue that does not currently exist. The first effect is already reflected in reported sales; the second has yet to generate revenue from mainland-China shows. Investors are betting on the latter.
New markets buying K-pop are a confirmed trend. Concert access to mainland China is still only a possibility—the government has spoken of new channels for discussions—and any earnings impact would lag. These are not equivalent developments.
EXTENDED ANALYSISWhere Does K-Content-Driven Spending End Up?

That covers the entertainment agencies. Sections 06–07 apply the same test to tourism. More visitors and higher revenue do not guarantee that every business keeps more profit.

TRACK0620 MILLION
INBOUND TOURISM

20 Million Visitors.
A Shift in Spending.

K-pop agencies earn money when fans buy tickets and merchandise. Tourism works the same way: which companies benefit depends on where visitors actually spend.

  • K-Content Tops Travel Motivations K-content was cited by 34.5% of respondents considering a visit to Korea, followed by traditional culture (32.5%) and proximity (26.5%), according to a Korea Culture & Tourism Institute survey.7 Respondents could select multiple reasons. The findings do not mean all 20 million visitors came because of K-content.
  • Spending Outpaced Visitor Growth Foreign visitors spent ₩15.9416tn on cards in January–September, up 47.7%—more than twice the growth in arrivals (+21.5%).6 Average card spending per visitor rose from ₩767,000 to ₩932,000.33
  • Concertgoers Spent More Foreign fans attending a major group's March concert at Gwanghwamun spent an average ₩3.53m each and stayed 8.7 days, according to a government survey. For the April Goyang show, the figures were ₩2.91m and 7.4 days. The average inbound visitor in Q1 spent ₩2.45m and stayed 6.1 days.34 Those figures come from specific large concerts, not a representative average for concert tourists.
  • China Chinese arrivals reached 5.47m in the first nine months, up 29%.8 Women in their twenties and thirties accounted for 47% of the increase. A 2025 survey found that 95.3% of Chinese women in that age range traveled independently, not in organized tour groups (Kiwoom Securities).35
  • Japan Moved the Other Way Amid China–Japan tensions, Chinese arrivals in Japan fell 55% to 3.34m in January–September (Kiwoom Securities).8 Some demand may have shifted to Korea, but the scale is unknown.
  • Beyond Seoul Regional airports handled 3.24m foreign arrivals through September, up 37.4% and accounting for 19% of the total.6

Winners and Losers

A major explanation is the changing customer mix. Duty-free stores once relied heavily on Chinese bulk resellers known asdaigou. Today's independent travelers make smaller, more frequent purchases at beauty chains, convenience stores and dermatology clinics.33

  • When the Won Strengthens Kiwoom argues that exchange rates affect tourist spending more than visitor counts. As travel becomes more expensive, visitors cut shopping and experiences first; accommodation spending is stickier (Korea Tourism Organization survey).8
  • The Money Flows Both Ways As the won strengthened, South Koreans spent more in Japan. Their shopping expenditure there rose 66% to ¥78.3bn in Q3 (Hanwha Investment & Securities).37
  • Cosmetics Exports to China Rebound Exports to China rose for five straight months from June, jumping 31.6% in October. Yet January–October exports were still only 35% of the 2021 level (Kiwoom Securities).35
  • Related Research Cosmetics exports: see Investment Insight No.23; domestic department-store spending: see NO.38.

TRACK07HOUSE RULES
Regulation

REGULATORY WILDCARD
Casino Contributions to the Tourism Fund

Policy determines how much profit businesses retain from the same revenue. Casino contributions to Korea's tourism fund illustrate the risk. Here are the proposals and dates—not a prediction.

Foreigner-only casinos pay a share of revenue into the Tourism Promotion and Development Fund. The levy uses progressive revenue brackets. In July, the government outlined a plan to raise the statutory ceiling.42, 43

Government View · Culture Ministry
  • Since the levy was introduced in 1995, foreigner-only casino revenue has increased 10.3×, while the contribution structure has remained largely unchanged for almost 30 years.
  • The levy is not a tax, the ministry says. It funds tourism facilities, promotion and visitor acquisition.
  • The proposed 15% would not apply across all revenue.44
Industry and Broker Concerns
  • Operators have called the proposal a punitive tax.45
  • In a worst-case scenario where 15% applies across high-revenue operations, combined operating profit at three casino companies could fall 24% (Kiwoom estimate).8
  • After July reports, their shares dropped 17–25% by Oct. 23 (iM Securities).42
The impact depends heavily on the assumption. If 15% applies only to annual revenue above ₩300bn per casino property, operating profit at two companies could fall 8–9% (iM Securities).42 Introduction of the amendment was deferred until after the National Assembly audit,46 and Kiwoom expects a bill around year-end or early next year.8 No introduction had been confirmed by this report's publication date.

The government is also discussing raising the departure levyembedded in airfares from ₩7,000 per person to ₩15,000–20,000 (Kiwoom Securities).8

The policy direction has been announced, but the thresholds and rates will be set later. Until then, both the industry's and brokers' impact figures are scenarios.

CLOSING

Record Revenue. Falling Stocks.
What Happened in Between?

The disconnect is clear. One company drove most of the revenue increase. Concerts became a bigger revenue stream but brought thinner margins. The next generation of profitable acts has yet to fill major venues. Tourism tells a parallel story: record visitor numbers, but a different mix of businesses capturing the spending.

The Nov. 18–19 rally again showed how quickly news can move this sector ahead of earnings. It has happened before. Around the 2017 South Korea–China summit and presidential visit, entertainment-agency market caps rose by as much as 41%, but tours in China never reopened (Hana Securities).29 Expectations also reversed after last November's APEC meeting and a visit to China this January (SK Securities).28 The Tests for 2027 Tour schedules and venue sizes for newer acts; merchandise revenue; whether operating profits reach shareholders; and concrete follow-up announcements from China.

Glossary
IP (intellectual property)
In entertainment, “IP” often refers to an artist or group itself. Albums, tours and merchandise all earn revenue under that identity.
MD (merchandise)
Fan goods such as light sticks, clothing, dolls and photocards, sold at venues, pop-up stores and online.
newer acts
An artist or group early in its career—generally no more than seven years since debut in this report. Veteran or established acts are the opposite.
12-month forward P/E
Share price divided by estimated earnings per share over the coming 12 months. Measures how many times expected earnings investors pay.
de-rating
A decline in the valuation multiple (P/E) even when earnings hold steady or rise. The reverse is re-rating.
gross margin
Gross profit divided by revenue, after direct costs. Concerts often have lower gross margins because production and artist payouts are substantial.
unofficial Chinese restrictions on Korean entertainment
The unofficial restrictions China has effectively imposed on Korean dramas, films and concerts since around 2016. Beijing has never formally announced such a policy.
Casino drop
The amount casino customers spend buying chips. Net gaming revenue reflects the amount retained by the casino, determined in part by the hold rate.
Average daily rate (ADR)
Average room revenue per occupied room-night at a hotel.
licensing
Fees paid for using an act's name, likeness or characters on a third party's products.
Circle Chart
Korea's official music chart, operated by the Korea Music Content Association. Physical-album counts are based on shipments.
pop-up stores
A temporary retail location selling merchandise, often tied to an album release or tour.
Spotify monthly listeners
The number of people who listened to an artist on Spotify during a month.
localized group
A group recruited and debuted outside Korea, using a Korean agency's planning and training model.
daigou
Chinese resellers who buy goods in bulk from Korean duty-free stores to resell in China.
Tourism Promotion and Development Fund
A government fund used for tourism infrastructure, promotion and attracting visitors. Casino and departure levies are major funding sources.
departure levy
A charge included in airfare for travelers departing South Korea; proceeds go to the Tourism Promotion and Development Fund.
after-hours market
Trading on an alternative exchange after the regular market closes, through 8 p.m. Thin liquidity can produce large price moves.
follow-up FTA talks on services and investment
The 2015 South Korea–China FTA focused on goods. Separate talks to further open services and investment markets began in 2018.

Methodology All dates are Korea time. Share-price moves use closing prices; “YTD” means versus the Dec. 30, 2025 close. The four agencies are HYBE, SM Entertainment, JYP Entertainment and YG Entertainment. Growth is y/y unless otherwise stated.

Differences Between Sources

  • “96% of the increase” Kiwoom's wording. Recalculating from the same report's company figures gives 91% for revenue and more than 100% for operating profit. See the expandable calculations in Section 02.
  • Share-Price Declines Kiwoom gives −37% to −53% (apparently as of Nov. 13), and Yuanta −37% to −56% (Nov. 12). This report recalculates from Nov. 17 and Nov. 18 closes. The KOSPI YTD return also varies by date, from +18% to +26%.
  • Nov. 18 Price Moves Regular-session closes and after-hours quotes differ sharply. Figure IN-1 separates them. After-hours quotes are as of 7:30 p.m.
  • Timeline on Nov. 18 Immediately after the close, market reports attributed the rally to news of cultural-exchange talks. Every report on the substantial conclusion of the FTA negotiations reviewed here appeared after the 3:30 p.m. market close; the earliest was at 3:44 p.m. The regular-session gains are therefore not attributed to the later announcement.
  • “Record” Operating Profit Kiwoom also calls the four agencies' nine-month ₩446.3bn operating profit a record. This report uses “record” only for revenue because it did not independently compare the operating-profit figure with prior years. Nor does it call combined Q3 revenue a quarterly record, since the cited reporting did not.
  • 2027 Operating-Profit Forecasts Kiwoom: ₩1.0125tn (+51.8%); Yuanta: ₩916.2bn (+32.6%). The brokers start from different 2026 estimates.
  • Physical Album Sales Kiwoom reports 85.1m albums sold in January–October; media reports cite about 79m among Circle Chart's top 400 for January–September. Periods and universes differ. Figures for full-year 2023 also vary: 114.05m (Kiwoom) versus 115.77m (Korea Herald).
  • Leading Destination for Album Exports The U.S. led in Q1 (reports citing the Customs Service). China led over January–October (Customs data reproduced by Kiwoom).
  • Foreign-Visitor Spending The Culture Ministry's ₩15.9416tn (+47.7%) covers card spending in January–September. Kiwoom's tourism receipts estimate of ₩31.5tn (+36.7%) and ₩1.84m per visitor covers a broader scope.
  • Visitor Growth +22% in January–August (Eugene Investment & Securities) versus +21.5% in January–September (Culture Ministry): different periods.
  • Casino-Levy Impact Kiwoom's −24% assumes the 15% ceiling applies broadly to high-revenue operators. iM's −8% to −9% applies it only to revenue above ₩300bn annually.
  • SM's Q3 Operating Profit Reports differ between ₩35.5bn and ₩35.7bn. This report uses ₩35.5bn.
  • P/E Discount for the Four Agencies Kiwoom calls a 15× November P/E a 33% discount to the five-year average of 23.4×. The two figures imply about 36%, so this report says “more than one-third.”
  • 2028 Attendance for Newer Acts Kiwoom's summary gives 5.49m for acts debuting after 2020; the detailed table gives 5.44m for 2028. This report uses the detailed annual series.
  • Comparison With 2023 The ₩528.7bn operating-profit figure for January–September 2023 comes from press coverage that does not specify the consolidation basis. The ₩446.3bn for 2026 is Kiwoom's combined figure. The roughly ₩700bn excluding one-offs adds HYBE's approximate ₩255bn charge to ₩446.3bn.
  • HYBE Operating-Profit Growth Kiwoom's table shows +254.3%; HYBE's filings imply roughly +260% because the nine-month 2025 base differs by several hundred million won. Figure 02-3 and the expandable calculations use Kiwoom's figures.
  • Nov. 19 Share Prices The regular market closed at 4:30 p.m. for the national college entrance exam. Figure IN-2 uses those regular closes, not after-hours prices. Nov. 17–18 prices remain as the reference for the events.
  • Age of Chinese Visitors Kiwoom describes 47% of additional Chinese arrivals as women aged 21–30, while the figure legend reads 21–40. This report uses the broader phrase “women in their twenties and thirties.”
  • HYBE's Q3 Non-Operating Loss The ₩148.6bn figure is the difference between operating and pretax profit. The explanation that most came from revaluing foreign-currency cash is the company's.
  • 2025 Album Exports The Korea Customs Service reported $301.74m (+3.4%); Kiwoom's series shows $322.46m. Figure 05-1 displays both.
  • Yuanta's 2027 Forecast Its summary table totals ₩916.2bn for four agencies (JYP +3.7%), while summing its company-level data yields ₩927.4bn (JYP +4.9%). Figure 02-4 uses the company-level figures.
  • Duty-Free Spending Per Buyer Foreign revenue divided by number of buyers. The January-to-September −28.9% compares months within the year; August's −22.4% is versus a year earlier.
  • Concert-Tourist Spending The ₩3.53m comes from a government survey of foreign guests at a March Gwanghwamun concert. Kiwoom cites the Korea Tourism Organization. It should not be read as the average for all concert tourists.
