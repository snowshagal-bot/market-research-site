Investment Insight No. 34 · Nov. 14, 2026

The market is right to focus on 2028 as the year new supply accelerates. But whether those chips create a glut depends on demand; how much earnings decline depends on long-term supply agreements.

SUMMARY · FIVE TAKEAWAYS
  1. 2028 is the year the market fears. Analysts see operating profit at the two leading Korean memory makers rising roughly 56–68% in 2027 from 2026 levels, yet their shares trade at 12-month forward P/E ratios of roughly 3x.
  2. New fabs begin production in 2027, but the capacity wave comes in 2028. The first wafers arrive in 2027, but installing and qualifying the full toolset can take more than a year. DS Securities expects capacity growth to peak in 2028.
  3. More capacity does not automatically mean a memory glut. The three leading memory manufacturers expect supply to remain tight in 2028. Whether there is a surplus depends on demand, which the available data cannot yet establish.
  4. Long-term agreements cushion the downturn; they do not eliminate it. Even contracted volumes can reprice within agreed bands. In bearish brokerage scenarios, operating profit falls to a little over half its 2027 level in 2028, and roughly 40% by 2029.
  5. Watch order reductions and contract coverage—not just new construction. The counterargument comes from demand: PC makers have started trimming memory procurement, while some data-center projects have slipped. Server-side order cuts have not yet been confirmed.

On Nov. 12 (U.S. time), NVIDIA announced a CEO transition, naming CFO Colette Kress interim CEO. Its fiscal third-quarter results remain scheduled for Nov. 18 at 6:20 a.m. Korea time, followed by a 7 a.m. earnings call.1 On Nov. 13, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 3.4%, while Micron declined 2.5%.2 It was another week in which investors asked how long the AI investment cycle could last.

Korean memory makers face that question more directly than most. SK hynix reported third-quarter revenue of ₩104 trillion and operating profit of ₩83 trillion.3 Yet Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are trading 40–60% below their June peaks.

There is no single explanation for those declines. Credit concerns among the companies financing AI infrastructure have also weighed on sentiment (No. 29). This report isolates the 2028 supply question: the year widely expected to see the sharpest increase in memory capacity. We examine whether that timing is right—and how severe the impact might be—through fab schedules, supply and demand, and contract terms.

What to watch

  • The market's reference year has shifted to 2028. Daishin Securities argues that the question by year-end will be how much of 2028 earnings can be sustained. Its base case calls for flat earnings in 2028.8
  • Some analysts have raised earnings estimates while cutting price targets. On Nov. 9, Morgan Stanley raised its 2027 and 2028 operating-profit forecasts for both companies but lowered its share-price targets, expecting margins to revert toward historical averages from 2029 onward.9
  • No confirmed server order cuts yet. According to SK Securities, past downcycles followed a sequence: a share-price peak, then order cuts about three months later, and price declines another three months after that (see No. 29). The firm reported no such order-cut signal after this June's peak as of Nov. 9.10 Some PC vendors, however, have begun reducing planned memory purchases (Section 06).

A 3x P/E is not automatically a bargain. If the earnings denominator reflects a cyclical peak, a fall in profit will push the multiple higher even if the share price stays flat. Under Eugene Securities' bear case, the same share price would imply a P/E rising from the low 2x range in 2027 to the low 4x range in 2028.

02NO.34Fab Ramp-Up Timeline

Production starts in 2027;
the capacity surge follows in 2028

Hana Securities expects equipment move-in at SK hynix Yongin Fab 1, Micron Idaho Fab 1 (ID1), and Samsung's Pyeongtaek P5 to begin during the first three quarters of 2027. The equipment move-in marks the start of bringing production tools into completed cleanrooms. First wafers can come out that year, but if tool installation and qualification takes roughly a year, meaningful output ramps in 2028.

Why does the process take more than a year?

  • More tools are needed. New fabs use 1c-node DRAM processes, which require more equipment. SK hynix began moving tools into its Cheongju M15X fab in Q4 2025 and started wafer processing in Q1 2026, according to company filings.12 Hana Securities expects tool setup to finish in Q4 2026—roughly a year after move-in began—and assumes a similar timetable for fabs starting in 2027.11
  • Fab construction itself takes time. DS Securities estimates about three and a half years from construction to wafer output. HBM requires stacking and testing after wafer fabrication before it can be shipped.4
  • Industry researchers see a similar timeline. TrendForce expects new fabs to make a meaningful contribution to supply from 2028 onward.16
  • But 2027 will not be a zero-output year. Yongin Fab 1's first cleanroom is targeted for February 2027, and Micron ID1's first wafer is slated for mid-2027.13, 14 Hana Securities projects both fabs starting at about 10,000 wafers per month in Q3 2027, reaching only about 40,000 and 15,000 wafers per month, respectively, by year-end.11

That is why capacity growth is expected to peak in 2028. The figures below refer to DRAM, including HBM. NAND needs to be analyzed separately: TrendForce expects its supply balance to ease from the second half of 2027.16

DS Securities estimates that China's CXMT accounts for about one-quarter of the four firms' capacity increase. Of Samsung's 150,000-wafer-per-month addition in nominal capacity, 90,000 (60%) is expected to come from Pyeongtaek P4, which has been expanding since 2026. P5 would contribute only about 20,000 wafers per month even at the end of 2028.4 Samsung itself has said it would be difficult for incremental capital spending in 2027 to make a meaningful contribution to bit output in 2028.17

The market has good reason to focus on 2028. But the bulk of that year's added capacity is expected to come from fabs already running and from China—not solely from the fabs making their debut in 2027.

Nominal capacity (monthly wafer-start capacity) and effective capacity are not interchangeable. Hana Securities' 2027 forecasts of +15% globally and +14% for the top three are nominal measures; DS Securities' +14.7% and +20.6% are effective-capacity measures. Both are year-end monthly run-rates—not annual shipment growth or estimates of excess supply.

03NO.34Will Supply Outstrip Demand?

A 20% capacity increase
does not mean a 20% DRAM surplus

Whether supply becomes excessive depends on demand. Manufacturers and brokerage analysts generally expect the market to remain tight in 2028.

What to watch

  • Can prices fall even in a tight market? Yes. Prices that have reached cyclical highs can decline simply because shortages become less acute. DS Securities' call for a price correction is not the same as a forecast of oversupply.
  • Demand estimates also offer a counterweight. DS Securities forecasts global AI server shipments rising roughly 30% to 5.03 million units in 2028.4 But server unit shipments and DRAM production capacity are different measures. Comparing their growth rates directly cannot establish whether there will be a surplus.
  • Oversupply therefore cannot yet be treated as a certainty. The timing of the supply build-out is comparatively visible; whether demand can keep pace remains contested (Section 06).

What long-term agreements can—and cannot—lock in

  • They help secure volumes and a price floor. Customers reserve capacity in advance, while suppliers use floors and prepayments to limit the downside. Micron says its margins would exceed those of any previous cycle peak even at the contracted floor prices.14
  • Contract coverage is still expanding. SK Securities reports discussions to extend three-year-plus-two-year arrangements into firm five-year agreements.6 IBK Securities believes 2028 production may already be largely committed, potentially at levels approaching full utilization.20
  • They do not freeze prices within the permitted range. SK hynix incorporates market prices within agreed bands. More than half of Micron's contracts with established pricing mechanisms also include ceilings, which cap upside when prices rise.
  • A substantial share of capacity remains outside long-term agreements. Without granular disclosures, investors can still track whether the share covered by long-term supply agreements is growing.

Why would more profit survive this downcycle?

  • HBM prices respond later—and less severely. DS Securities expects HBM contract prices to lag conventional DRAM market movements, while a higher HBM mix cushions the blended ASP decline.4
  • Contract price floors provide a backstop. Eugene Securities estimates that prices in its bear case are close to the LTA floor. Even there, it projects DRAM margins comparable to the 2017–18 peak.5
  • Both brokerages argue that much of this downside is already reflected in share prices. Eugene Securities notes that its bear-case 2028 BVPS is close to the current share price; DS Securities argues that current valuations reflect something like its bearish 2029 earnings scenario.4, 5 However, a P/B of 1x is a valuation reference point, not a guaranteed floor. If projected book value per share falls, that reference point falls too.

The percentage decline depends on the 2027 starting point. DS Securities forecasts Samsung's 2027 operating profit at ₩632tn, while Eugene Securities uses ₩563tn in its bear case.

06NO.34The Demand-Side Counterargument

The counterargument: demand could
turn before the fabs ramp up

If demand weakens first, the risk arrives earlier than 2028. PC inventories are building and procurement plans are being scaled back. Server-related order cancellations have not yet been confirmed.

Early warning signs
  • PC manufacturers have begun cutting memory purchases. TrendForce says notebook shipments fell 15.8% quarter-on-quarter in Q3, while PC makers' DRAM inventories have increased since that quarter. Some vendors are revising procurement plans for Q4 2026 and Q1 2027. A number had ordered more than needed as prices surged.21
  • Apple forecasts a gross margin of 45–46% for its December quarter, with memory costs among the headwinds. Qualcomm's smartphone chip revenue fell 26% year-on-year. Hana Securities interprets this as demand destruction caused by passing higher component costs on to customers.22
  • Eugene Securities also identifies smartphones, PCs and China's largest cloud providers as weak links, although it expects North American cloud providers to absorb the shortfall.5
  • Data centers that would consume the memory are being delayed. Morgan Stanley estimates that about 5 GW of capacity originally due online in 2028 has slipped to 2029. It cut its forecast for new data-center capacity growth in 2028 from 21% to 9%, with 20% growth now projected for 2029.9 It nevertheless maintained its outlook for capital spending by major cloud providers. Its conclusion is a delay in bringing facilities online, rather than a broad cancellation of investment.23
Evidence that demand is holding up
  • In the same announcement, Apple projected December-quarter revenue growth of 7–10%, which would be a quarterly record.22 Qualcomm's decline also reflects a reduction in Apple-related revenue.24 That points more to component costs squeezing margins than to a collapse in end demand.
  • South Korean semiconductor exports totaled $16.6bn in Nov. 1–10, up 331% year-on-year.25 For DRAM, value per unit of weight increased 18% and estimated shipment volume rose 7.5% against the same period of the previous month. The per-weight price also changes with product mix.26
  • PC DRAM prices are still rising. TrendForce expects Q4 contract prices to increase 13–18% quarter-on-quarter, though at a slower pace than in Q3. Memory manufacturers are tentatively planning to reduce PC DRAM allocations in 2027.21
  • DRAM lead times have shortened from 19 to 18 weeks, while DDR5 32GB spot prices have also declined. Daishin Securities attributes the moves to sales from stockpiled inventory and corrections in lower-density products, rather than clear evidence that the shortage has ended.27

Customer inventories are the first place to look

  • In 2019, buyers stopped purchasing despite having the cash. Free cash flow at four major cloud service providers (CSP) increased 29%, yet server DRAM prices fell almost 50% over the year. In January 2019, North American data-center operators held at least five to six weeks of server memory inventory—more than twice normal levels.4
  • What is piling up at server vendors today is not memory—at least not yet. DS Securities believes the inventory at Dell and HPE consists of other components waiting for scarce DRAM. In that view, DRAM is the bottleneck, not the component accumulating on shelves.4
  • Watch whether weakness spreads from PCs to servers. In DS Securities' estimated DRAM demand mix, PCs account for 7% and servers for 57% (including HBM ).4 PCs are too small a segment to determine the direction of the entire market. Servers matter more. The next warning would be server customers beginning to talk about rising memory inventories.

What could delay or change the conclusion?

11. If new capacity arrives ahead of schedule

Some reports suggest Samsung P5 Phase 1 could begin operating as early as 2027.28 equipment move-in and initial production moving earlier would bring forward the supply increase. The effect on 2028 output would depend on the subsequent ramp-up pace.

22. If HBM demand rolls over

HBM accounts for 31% of DRAM wafer usage in 2027, but just 13% of bit shipments output, according to DS Securities.4 If HBM demand weakens, more capacity could eventually be redirected toward conventional DRAM . The switch would not happen overnight: process changes, equipment operations and customer qualification take time.

33. If contracts are renegotiated

Weaker demand could prompt customers to seek revised volumes or prices. SK Securities notes that breaching a supply agreement could cost buyers more than their prepayments: it may also impair access to future capacity and contract terms.6

END · THREE THINGS TO TRACK

The market is right about 2028;
demand and contracts determine the damage

The sharpest capacity increase is expected in 2028. But demand will determine whether that turns into oversupply, and contract terms will determine how much earnings withstand a price decline. Three indicators matter more than another fab announcement:

  1. Orders and customer inventoriesWhether PC procurement cuts spread to servers; what customers say about server-memory stocks; South Korea's 10-day DRAM export price and volume data
  2. Long-term contract coverageOn the late-January Q4 earnings calls, track long-term supply agreements customer counts, the percentage of capacity covered, contract duration and any disclosed pricing structure.
  3. The pace after the first waferHow quickly Yongin Fab 1 and Micron ID1 ramp after first wafers, and whether Samsung P5's equipment move-in is brought forward (which would pull supply growth earlier).

Glossary
Long-Term Supply Agreement (LTA)
A multi-year agreement setting out volumes and how prices will be determined. Micron calls its versions strategic customer agreements (SCAs).
Nominal versus effective capacity
Nominal capacity measures wafers that can enter production each month. Effective capacity adjusts for the share allocated to HBM and changes in bits per wafer (as estimated by DS Securities).
Equipment move-in · tool setup · first wafer
The start of tool deliveries, completion of tool installation and calibration for normal production, and the first wafer produced. Qualification, yields and production ramp-up can still take time after the first wafer.
Conventional DRAM · blended ASP
Conventional DRAM excludes HBM. Blended ASP averages pricing across HBM and conventional DRAM.
order cuts
Customers reducing or deferring orders already placed.
take-or-pay
A contract requiring the customer to pay for committed volumes even if it does not take delivery. Micron says all its strategic customer agreements contain some take-or-pay volume.
12-month forward P/E · P/B · BVPS
Share price ÷ expected earnings per share over the next 12 months · share price ÷ book value per share · book value per share.
