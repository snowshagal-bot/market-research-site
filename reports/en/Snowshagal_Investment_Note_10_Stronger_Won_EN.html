FX · Q3 Earnings

The Cost of a Stronger Won

The won strengthened. So why are earnings still holding up?

Research & graphics by Snowshagal · October 9, 2026 · Information through the October 8 market close · Q3 figures are brokerage estimates except LG Electronics preliminary results

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • The benchmark matters. The Q3 average USD/KRW rate was 1,422—2.7% higher than a year earlier, but below Daishin Securities’ previous 1,520 assumption. That is why the same quarter can be described as both FX-supported and FX-diluted, depending on the benchmark.1
  • Hyundai Motor’s +KRW 980bn FX contribution is a year-on-year improvement. It is our sum of two Daishin Securities year-on-year FX estimates for Hyundai Motor: KRW 230bn from the average rate and KRW 750bn from the quarter-end rate. It is neither translation profit generated this year nor a company-reported figure.1, 2
  • Electronics estimates diverged under the same FX move. Yuanta Securities raised its Q3 operating-profit forecast for Samsung Electro-Mechanics from KRW 602bn to KRW 683bn, while cutting LG Innotek from KRW 320bn to KRW 79bn.3
  • In Q4, FX could turn into an earnings headwind. Under Daishin Securities’ 1,360 assumption, the Q4 average rate would be 6.1% lower year on year, while the quarter-end translation effect would become much smaller.1

1 · BENCHMARKS

One exchange rate, three earnings channels

How can a stronger won still help automaker earnings? The answer is the benchmark you choose.

Q3 average USD/KRW fell 5.3% from Q2, but its Q3 average was still 2.7% higher than a year earlier. The rate did not move below 1,400 until after August 21, so the stronger won was only partly reflected in the quarterly average (Figure 1).1

Against brokerage assumptions, the comparison flips. Daishin Securities had previously assumed 1,520, versus an actual Q3 average of 1,422. The same $100 of revenue therefore translates into more won than a year ago, but less than the broker had modeled (Table 1).1, 2

Three ways FX reaches earnings

There are three: the Average rate used to translate dollar revenue into won; the Quarter-end rate used to remeasure foreign-currency liabilities; and the gap between the exchange rate when raw materials were purchased and when the finished product was shipped. The three channels hit earnings at different times (Table 2).

For the quarter-end channel, this note focuses on the translation of automakers’ foreign-currency warranty provisions. Where FX translation gains or losses appear in the accounts depends on the company and the item.

Part of the “miss” is an FX-assumption issue

Some earnings misses reflect an exchange-rate assumption that proved too high. Hana Securities cut its Samsung Electronics forecast after revising its FX assumption and said a downgrade to SK hynix—whose Q3 model used 1,500—was also unavoidable.

The estimate cut is entirely FX-driven, not a change in fundamentals.

Hana Securities Semiconductor & Components Weekly · October 67

In Q4, the year-on-year comparison reverses. Under Daishin Securities’ 1,360 assumption, the quarterly average would be 6.1% below Q4 2025’s 1,448. In Daishin’s words, it would be the first year-on-year decline in the average rate in 12 quarters, since Q4 2023 (Figure 2).1

2 · AUTOS

What the +KRW 980bn FX contribution actually means

Daishin Securities estimates Hyundai Motor’s Q3 operating profit at KRW 2.54tn, almost unchanged from KRW 2.537tn a year earlier. The headline is flat, but the bridge suggests FX gains offset much of the pressure from volume and costs.1, 5

In Daishin Securities’ year-on-year bridge for the automotive division, FX is the largest positive contributor. The average-rate effect is +KRW 230bn and the quarter-end effect +KRW 750bn, for a combined +KRW 980bn by our calculation. Lower tariff burden adds +KRW 570bn, while volume/mix (−KRW 1.0tn) and costs (−KRW 400bn) offset those gains (Figure 3).1, 2

The tariff item appears to reflect the lower burden from a rate of 15%, down from 25% in Q3 last year; the tariff rates are from Hanwha Investment & Securities.8 The five factors sum to +KRW 150bn, about KRW 109bn more than the automotive division’s +KRW 41.3bn change in operating profit. Daishin’s report does not explain the gap, so we leave it unreconciled (Snowshagal calculation; Appendix 1).1, 2

COMMON MISREAD

Year-on-year improvement is not this year’s profit

This does not mean Hyundai Motor generated KRW 750bn of translation profit in Q3 this year. The quarter-end contribution of +KRW 750bn is best read as the year-on-year swing between last year’s translation loss and this year’s translation gain. Quarter-end USD/KRW rose 3.4% in Q3 2025 but fell 11.9% in Q3 2026 (Snowshagal calculation).1, 2

So subtracting KRW 750bn from this year’s profit would not reveal “core” earnings. If last year carried a KRW 150bn translation loss and this year a KRW 600bn translation gain, the year-on-year improvement is KRW 750bn—but the amount embedded in this year’s profit is KRW 600bn. These are illustrative numbers only (Table 3).

Broker estimates also differ materially. Hanwha Investment & Securities expects the FX-translation impact on overseas warranty provisions to improve year on year by 0.4 percentage point for Hyundai Motor and 1.6 points for Kia. If the 0.4-point figure is mechanically applied to Hyundai Motor’s estimated revenue, it implies about KRW 180bn—roughly one quarter of Daishin’s KRW 750bn quarter-end contribution. The methodologies and coverage are not necessarily comparable.2, 8

Still below expectations

Against consensus, the picture is weaker. Daishin Securities’ KRW 2.54tn estimate is 13.2% below the KRW 2.925tn consensus. FX is only part of the explanation: Daishin also cites weak volume from strike disruptions and higher raw-material costs, while Hana Securities adds a roughly 5% quarter-on-quarter decline in the average exchange rate, higher material costs and lower utilization.1, 9 The year-on-year operating-profit estimates range from +0.1% to −12% (Table 4).

Hanwha Investment & Securities cites strike-related production disruption—estimated at about 55,000 vehicles—along with higher raw-material prices and an unfavorable mix. Hana Securities is more positive on mix, arguing that a higher hybrid share supported average selling prices.8, 9

Under the assumption, the average rate turns into a Q4 earnings headwind

The two FX channels would move in opposite directions. The average rate would be 6.1% lower year on year, reducing the won translation of overseas revenue. The quarter-end rate would be 1,360, almost unchanged from the Q3 close, leaving little new revaluation gain. Because the quarter-end rate rose 2.3% in Q4 last year and generated a translation loss, the year-on-year comparison would still be slightly positive. But the negative average-rate effect is larger (Snowshagal calculation; Table 5).1, 2

Daishin Securities sees greater exposure for automakers with a large domestic won-cost base, and less for parts suppliers that produce and deliver locally. Offsets include normalized production, new models and lower material costs in Hana Securities’ view, and new models plus expanded U.S. local production that reduces tariff burden in Hanwha’s.1, 8, 9

3 · ELECTRONICS

Same FX move, different earnings outlooks

Yuanta Securities raised its Q3 operating-profit estimate for Samsung Electro-Mechanics from KRW 602bn to KRW 683bn, while cutting LG Innotek from KRW 320bn to KRW 79bn.3

LG Innotek’s revenue forecast fell by only about KRW 100bn, but its operating-profit forecast was cut by KRW 241bn. Samsung Electro-Mechanics’ revenue estimate was unchanged while its profit estimate rose (Table 6).2, 3 Yuanta attributes the divergence to pricing, product mix and the timing lag in raw-material costs.3 The two companies differ in products, customers, launch schedules and cost structures, so this is not a clean test of FX resilience alone.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics: pricing and mix

Yuanta expects component ASPs to rise about 10% quarter on quarter on a richer high-value product mix. The component business is negotiating price increases with direct customers after raising channel prices, while package substrates (FC-BGA) are already seeing higher pricing. Yuanta expects a broader pricing cycle in 2027 and also points to active hedging and conservative FX guidance as reasons for the limited currency impact.3

LG Innotek: the raw-material timing lag

Yuanta says Q3 absorbed two timing effects at once: inventory built when USD/KRW was high in the first half, and the lag between raw-material purchases and shipments for a major customer’s new model. It expects cost pressure to ease from Q4 as materials bought after the won strengthened begin flowing through.3

LG Electronics: below expectations, but profit still grew

LG Electronics is the only company in this group to have released preliminary results. Q3 operating profit of KRW 781.8bn was 25.4% below Yuanta’s one-month consensus of KRW 1.0479tn, but still 13.5% higher year on year.2, 3, 6 Yuanta attributes the revenue shortfall to FX and deferred Vehicle Solution (VS) revenue, and the profit miss to AI data-center and robotics investment plus some voluntary-retirement costs.3

Three questions that determine FX exposure

Do you earn in dollars but spend in won? Automakers have greater exposure, while locally producing parts suppliers have less, according to Daishin.1 Is inventory purchased at a weak-won rate still on hand? If so, margins can fall after the won strengthens.3 Can the company raise prices or shift its mix? Yuanta sees more room in MLCCs and substrates, and less in camera modules.3

4 · WHAT TO WATCH IN EARNINGS

Read the earnings release with the FX effect separated

An earnings miss means something different if the underlying business is holding up once the currency effect is separated out. The key misreading also differs by company (Table 7).

Q4 FX assumptions

The actual rate in early October was around 1,340, below Daishin Securities’ Q4 assumption of 1,360.1, 4, 8 Hanwha Investment & Securities says lowering the Q4 applied exchange rate from 1,450 to around 1,350 would reduce Hyundai Motor and Kia operating profit by KRW 450–620bn. The report does not specify whether that range is combined or per company.8

Hanwha’s FX report puts October USD/KRW in a 1,320–1,400 range. It argues that further won appreciation may be limited as National Pension Service hedging stops and the conversion effect from the SK hynix ADR fades. If the won weakens again, the Q4 earnings headwind would ease accordingly—our interpretation.10

Counterpoint · A stronger won is not bad for everyone

Banks are expected to record FX translation gains in Q3. Hana Securities estimates KRW 550bn across IBK, Hana and Woori, but classifies the gain as non-recurring.14 The timing differs too. In Yuanta’s view, LG Innotek absorbed the FX-related cost burden in Q3 first, and that burden should ease in Q4.3

A cut to earnings estimates is not automatically negative for the share price. This note examines the earnings transmission mechanism, not how much of it is already priced in.

CONCLUSION

The FX support behind Q3 estimates may not carry into Q4

Q3 USD/KRW was higher than a year earlier but lower than brokers had expected. For autos, that gap appears in Daishin’s year-on-year FX contribution, which may have masked pressure from volume and costs. In electronics, the same currency move coincided with sharply divergent estimate revisions.

Under Daishin Securities’ assumptions, the quarter-end benefit fades in Q4 and the average USD/KRW rate falls below last year’s level. Companies such as LG Innotek, which absorbed the cost lag earlier, could move the other way. The question for this earnings season is not whether management cites FX, but whether the underlying business holds up once the currency effect is separated.

GLOSSARY

Average rate
The average exchange rate over a quarter · Used as an approximation for translating foreign-currency transactions such as revenue and costs into won
Quarter-end rate
The exchange rate on the final day of the quarter · Used to remeasure foreign-currency monetary assets and liabilities at period end
Warranty provision
A liability accrued in advance for future warranty repairs on vehicles already sold · Overseas obligations are often denominated in foreign currency, mainly dollars, so their won value falls when USD/KRW declines
Current-period effect vs. YoY contribution
Current-period effect is the amount generated in this quarter; year-on-year contribution is the change versus the same quarter last year · The KRW 750bn discussed in this note is the latter
FX translation gain/loss
Accounting gain or loss created when foreign-currency items are remeasured · Depending on the item and accounting treatment, it may appear in operating profit, non-operating profit/loss, or other comprehensive income
Consensus
Average of brokerage forecasts · The figure varies somewhat by data provider and lookback period
Preliminary results
Results released by a company before the full accounts are finalized · Final figures may differ
ADR
A depositary receipt listed in an overseas market · Converting issuance proceeds into won can create demand for the currency
