SNOWSHAGAL INVESTMENT INSIGHT · NO. 23

October cosmetics exports set a record for the month, and the January–October total has already surpassed all of last year. But selling more does not necessarily mean earning as much as investors expect. The question now is which companies can turn the export boom into profits.

FIVE POINTS FOR BUSY READERS

  1. October cosmetics exports set a new record for October. The January–October total has already exceeded the full-year 2025 figure. September, however, remains the biggest single month on record.
  2. Growth is clearly strong, but it is too soon to call it an acceleration. Combining September and October to smooth out the Chuseok holiday effect produces a lower growth rate.
  3. Q3 exports to Europe under HS 3304 grew roughly three times as fast as those to the US. But 85% of Europe's incremental exports went to the UK, Netherlands and Estonia. Whether those shipments became local sales is a separate question.
  4. The export boom is not translating evenly into corporate profits. APR doubled revenue but missed profit expectations. LG Household & Health Care increased group profit, while its beauty division remained loss-making.
  5. The sector's valuation multiple has fallen sharply, but lower does not automatically mean cheap. Fourth-quarter earnings must show whether the lower multiple is justified.

On Nov. 1, the government reported a record October for cosmetics exports1. Yet the combined market capitalization of nine cosmetics companies tracked by Hanwha Investment & Securities fell 5.5% in the preceding week, Oct. 26–302.

That looks contradictory until you examine the earnings. APR's third-quarter revenue doubled year over year, but operating profit came in 11.5% below market expectations2, 3. Profits did not fall. They grew—just not as much as investors had expected.

The international spread of K-beauty is real. During L'Oréal's Oct. 22 earnings presentation, the CEO described the K-beauty boom as real and estimated that Korean skincare brands were growing at an average rate of roughly 30%4, 5. Selling more and retaining more profit are two different things. This report follows the export boom through to the companies—and earnings—that stand to benefit.

WHAT THE DATA TELLS US

  • Why KRW growth is lower The average won/dollar exchange rate in October was 5.3% lower than a year earlier. The same dollar revenue translates into fewer won2.
  • Strong growth, but acceleration is unproven October's growth in won terms (+48.1%) exceeded Q3's (+42.1%). But exports were depressed by the Chuseok holiday in October 2025. Combining September and October gives +34.7% in won terms and +41.7% in dollars2, 8, 9. We need more evidence before concluding that growth is speeding up.
  • Year-to-date exports have exceeded last year January–October exports reached US$12.57bn, surpassing US$11.42bn for full-year 20251.

  • Volumes also grew By 5.6% under the Bank of Korea index, or 23.1% by Customs shipment weight9, 10.

Higher volumes alongside higher average export values are encouraging. But the average can rise simply because exporters ship more small, high-priced serums, even if no individual product becomes more expensive. This is not, by itself, proof of price hikes on like-for-like products or stronger brand pricing power. Yuanta argues that brand pricing limits the impact of won appreciation10.

The two methods define volume differently, so they assign different shares of growth to price. The claim that unit values account for most growth applies specifically to the BOK-index calculation.

STEP 02Europe

Europe's exports surged—
but were those shipments sold or warehoused?

Q3 HS 3304 exports to Europe grew roughly three times as fast as those to the US. Most of the increase, however, was concentrated in three countries.

Europe's Q3 export growth
was concentrated in three countries

Circle size represents Q3 HS 3304 export value; percentages show YoY growth · Darker circles: growth above 100% · Locations approximate

Source: Korea Customs Service9; hub locations: Hana Securities8

  • Growth In Q3, HS 3304 exports to Europe (excluding seven countries including Russia) rose 113% versus 35% to the US. For January–September, Europe grew 90% against 36% in the US—a roughly 2.5-to-1 ratio9. The ratio depends on product coverage and period.
  • Concentration The UK, Netherlands and Estonia accounted for 85% of Europe's incremental exports. Shipments to Germany fell 7%, while France grew 10%9.
  • Distribution hubs Evidence points to expanded distribution hubs in the UK and Netherlands. Hana Securities reports APR logistics centers operating in both countries8. Estonia also posted a surge, but the presence of a comparable hub has not been confirmed. Exports measure shipments, not whether goods are sitting in a local warehouse or have reached consumers.

What export statistics capture—
and what still needs verification

There are signs of broader growth. In Hana's October data, Germany rose 102% and Spain 123%. But at US$15m and US$5m, respectively, their export values were far below the Netherlands (US$52m) and the UK (US$51m)8.

As of Oct. 31, an average of 13.2 Korean-brand products ranked in Amazon Beauty's top 100 across five European countries, compared with 12 in the US2. That can serve as a secondary measure of brand visibility and channel penetration—not a direct comparison of revenue or market share.

Has Europe overtaken the US?

Yuanta Securities reported that Europe accounted for 23.2% of January–September exports, exceeding the US share of 21.0% for the first time11. Rebuilding the series from Customs data yields a figure near 23% only when seven countries including Russia, plus Türkiye, are included. Excluding those countries puts Europe at 17.6%, below the US.

Hanwha's figures, which exclude Russia and the CIS, likewise put Europe at 19.5% versus 20.6% for the US in Q3, and 18.6% versus 20.4% in October2, 5. The defensible conclusion is that the ranking depends on which countries count as Europe.

WHAT THE DATA TELLS US

  • Currency hurt profits through two channels Foreign-currency receivables generated losses, and overseas sales translated into fewer won. APR put the combined operating-profit impact at about ₩27.8bn, or 3.6 percentage points of margin14, 15.
  • The accounting effect is a separate number Under the previous presentation standard, before IFRS 18, operating profit would have been ₩176.7bn15. The ₩16.1bn gap reflects where income and expenses are classified, not the absence of FX exposure. Consensus comparisons must use the same accounting basis.
  • APR's business segments diverged Cosmetics and beauty revenue rose 150.6%, while beauty-device sales fell 17.9%. APR attributed the device decline to the absence of a major promotional event14.
  • Amorepacific grew by a different route Sales of flagship North American brand Laneige fell 10%, but gains at Aestura, Innisfree and other brands lifted Americas revenue 16.9%. Domestic operating profit climbed 42% to ₩84.6bn8, 11.

LG Household & Health Care's Nov. 3 results underscored the contrast. Q3 consolidated operating profit rose 52.4% YoY to ₩70.4bn, even as revenue slipped 0.2% to ₩1.5774tn16. The household-care division earned ₩31.0bn and beverages earned ₩50.3bn, while beauty posted a ₩10.9bn operating loss. The beauty division's loss narrowed year over year but did not turn into a profit17, 18. Higher group earnings do not mean the beauty business has recovered.

APR guided to full-year revenue of ₩3tn and operating profit of ₩730bn–₩750bn at its earnings presentation14. Hana Securities highlighted the ₩750bn figure8. Subtracting January–September operating profit of ₩503.5bn3leaves ₩226.5bn–₩246.5bn to earn in Q4. The lower end is roughly 3% above Q4 consensus of ₩220.1bn as of Nov. 1; the upper end is about 12% higher2. Nov. 2 brokerage forecasts span that range: Yuanta ₩224.2bn, Hanwha ₩229.7bn and Hana ₩247.1bn—some below the minimum needed, others above the upper end2, 8, 11. Whether estimates move up is the next test. These are the profits required to meet management's annual target, not thresholds that guarantee a higher share price. APR also cautioned that FX effects may prevent it from achieving its 24–25% operating-margin target14.

Preliminary results have not been externally audited. APR early-adopted IFRS 18 in 2026 and restated the prior-year comparatives on the new basis3.

STEP 04Valuations

The sector's multiple has fallen sharply.
That doesn't make it cheap.

The sector's forward P/E fell from 19.4x at the end of August to 12.5x on Oct. 30. Earnings must establish whether that lower multiple is warranted.

WHAT THE DATA TELLS US

  • Trading at 60% of the 10-year average does not imply a 40% discount to fair value A historical average is not a fair-value guarantee. If the index constituents, growth profile or earnings durability change, the same multiple may no longer be appropriate.
  • What brokerages cite Won appreciation (USD/KRW from ₩1,554.4 per dollar on July 2 to ₩1,335.3 on Oct. 30)11, freight costs and fears of slower consumer spending2. Timing alone cannot establish causation.
  • Freight costs In September, average sea-export transport costs per 40-foot container reached ₩9.51m to the US West Coast and ₩5.04m to the EU, up roughly 90% and 36–39%, respectively, from April19, 20. These are averages across sea exports, not evidence that cosmetics companies' individual logistics bills rose by the same percentages.

Yuanta argues that brands and distributors shipping directly overseas bear the freight and FX burden. ODM manufacturers and packaging suppliers with more domestic deliveries have been less exposed, in its view11. Daishin Securities similarly argues that local production gives Cosmax and Kolmar Korea an advantage when the won strengthens21. The next test is whether ODM earnings support that thesis.

COUNTERPOINTIs a stronger won necessarily bad for cosmetics stocks?

  • In Yuanta's data since 2013, the sector's average P/E was 24.9x during periods when USD/KRW was below ₩1,200, versus 16.8x when it was at or above ₩1,30011.
  • Eugene Investment & Securities listed cosmetics among sectors that held up well during the past year's period of won appreciation (Oct. 2025–Oct. 2026)22.

These observations show correlation, not proof that a stronger won lifts share prices. FX can also directly erode earnings, as APR's Q3 illustrates.

P/E estimates vary by universe and date: Yuanta puts it at 12.9x (Oct. 30; average 22.2x since 2013), while Daishin has 10.3x for 11 cosmetics/apparel companies (21st percentile over ten years)11, 21. They should not be conflated into a single supposedly fair sector P/E.

STEP 05What to Watch

What to check in the next earnings cycle—
and what would change the thesis

The companies and the evidence to watch

Company / indicatorWhat would matter
APR
Does Q4 operating profit approach guidance? Are FX and freight pressures easing? Do subsequent earnings estimates rise?
Amorepacific
Can gains at other brands keep offsetting weakness in one flagship brand? Are overseas growth and profitability improving together?
ODM
Do margins improve as well as revenue? Does the supposed relative advantage from a stronger won show up in reported results?
European exports
Do shipments into hub countries translate into local sell-through and reorders? Does distribution broaden across countries and channels without excess inventory?

These are tests to apply going forward, not conditions already confirmed.

01 · FX

Q4 exchange rates remain lower

Yuanta sees continued pressure because the quarter-to-date average USD/KRW rate is below Q3's. APR plans to shorten collection periods on overseas receivables rather than use a separate FX hedge11.

02 · BASE EFFECT

This year's growth sets a high bar

Daishin expects growth in the 30% range during 2026 to create a demanding comparison in 2027. It favors overweighting the sector in the first half, then moving to neutral in the second21.

03 · TIMING

Shipment dates distort monthly figures

Hana says some year-end promotional inventory shipped in September; October then benefited from deferred September shipments and more working days8. That can make monthly growth rates volatile.

These factors affect reported growth, but they do not eliminate demand risk. If we see slower reorders, deeper discounting or rising inventories,the interpretation of strong exports must change.

CLOSING VIEW

Strong exports do not make every K-beauty stock equally attractive. Even when overseas sales rise, exchange rates, freight and marketing costs leave very different amounts of profit at different companies. The next question is not simply how fast exports are growing, but whether earnings beat market expectations—and whether they can keep doing so. The industry's boom is established. Investors now need to identify the companies that can turn that boom into durable profits.

  1. 1Does APR deliver at least the ~₩226.5bn of Q4 operating profit needed for the low end of guidance, and approach the ~₩246.5bn required for the top end?
  2. 2Do the Nov. 6 ODM results show improving operating margins, not just sales?
  3. 3Do final October Customs figures show European growth spreading beyond the UK and Netherlands?

Calculations
  • Shares: Combined HS 3303/3304/3305/3307 for Jan.–Sep. 2026. Total exports US$10.606bn, including US$2.230bn to the US.
  • Q3 growth: HS 3304 totals for July–September. Europe excluding seven states: US$298m → US$634m; US: US$515m → US$697m.
  • Three-country contribution: Incremental exports to the UK, Netherlands and Estonia of US$286m ÷ Europe's US$336m increase = 85%.
  • Q3 by country (HS 3304): Japan US$211m → US$252m; Estonia US$14m → US$53m; France US$34m → US$37m; Germany US$24m → US$22m.
  • Combined Sep.–Oct. growth (USD): (September US$1.213bn + October US$1.182bn) ÷ (US$937m + US$753m) − 1 = +41.7%. September from Customs; October from Hana.
  • Weight-based: Q3 HS 3304 shipments 79,243 → 97,509 metric tonnes (+23.1%), export value +38.2%, and value per kg +12.3%.
  • BOK-index value: Using Yuanta's volume and unit-value figures, 1.056 × 1.321 ≈ 1.395, or about +40%. The underlying Bank of Korea index was not independently retrieved.
  • APR's required Q4 operating profit: ₩730bn − ₩503.5bn ≈ ₩226.5bn (2.9% above ₩220.1bn consensus); ₩750bn − ₩503.5bn ≈ ₩246.5bn (12.0% above consensus).
  • P/E compression: End-Aug. → Oct. 23: 13.3 ÷ 19.4 − 1 = −31%; Oct. 23 → Oct. 30: 12.5 ÷ 13.3 − 1 = −6%. These are changes in multiples, not share-price returns.
  • Freight: In September, ₩9.511m to the US West Coast and ₩5.04m to the EU per 40-foot container, including surcharges and fees. The April report gave ₩5.053m and ₩3.698m, while the subsequent May report revised the April US West Coast figure to ₩4.932m. Implied increases: US West Coast 88–93%, EU 36–38%. Yuanta reports +87.9% and +39.3%.
Differences across sources

  • European growth in Q3 and Jan.–Sep. (HS 3304; excluding seven states): Europe +112.6% in Q3; +89.9% in Jan.–Sep. (US$854m → US$1.622bn). US: +35.3% and +35.8%, respectively.

GLOSSARY
HS 3304
International tariff classification for skincare and makeup. HS 3303 covers fragrances; 3305 hair products; 3307 shaving, body and bath preparations.
Average export unit value
Export value divided by shipment volume. It can rise if higher-priced products gain share, even without any like-for-like price increases.
CIS
Commonwealth of Independent States, a grouping of several former Soviet republics. Hanwha excludes Russia and the CIS from its Europe calculation.
Seven states including Russia
This report groups Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan together.
ODM
Original design manufacturing: companies that develop and produce products for brands, such as Cosmax, Kolmar Korea and Cosmecca Korea.
12-month forward P/E
Share price divided by estimated earnings per share over the next twelve months. The ratio can fall if the share price drops or expected earnings rise.
IFRS 18
A new financial-statement presentation standard, scheduled to take effect in 2027. Early adoption can move items such as gains and losses on foreign-currency receivables into operating profit.
Guidance
A company's own target or forecast for financial performance.
Preliminary earnings
Financial results disclosed before external audit; final figures may differ.
Base effect
When a low comparison period, such as a weak month last year, makes the latest YoY growth rate look unusually high.
Consensus
The average of analysts' estimates. Here, the FnGuide consensus cited by Hanwha.
