Thursday, November 26. The Bank of Korea raised its policy rate and lifted its GDP growth forecasts to 3.9% for 2026 and 4.1% for 2027, up from 3.3% and 2.9% in August.1 Semiconductor exports drove the upgrade. On the same day, Korea's Ministry of Data and Statistics reported that Q3 real household income fell 2.1% year on year, its first decline in 10 quarters.2 The economy is on course to grow nearly 4%, yet households are worse off.

The quarterly data tell a similar story. Q3 GDP grew 1.0% q/q, well above the BOK's 0.3% forecast, but private consumption fell 0.2%, the first decline in three years and nine months, since Q4 2022. The BOK also cut its 2026 consumption growth forecast from 2.1% to 1.8%.3

That does not mean the income from the chip boom has vanished. The money is already here. It has accrued first as corporate profits, and most of the large bonuses and dividends that will pass a share of it on to households are expected next year. It could take more than a year for those gains to lift spending broadly. Meanwhile, higher interest costs and prices are already squeezing budgets. This report traces the pipes carrying the income and the valves restricting consumption, then examines how Korea differs from Taiwan, which entered the same cycle a year earlier.

01Two growth rates

GDP growth raised to around 4%,
consumption growth cut to 1.8%

The BOK's November outlook in one sentence: the economy is growing quickly, but households are slow to open their wallets. The gap between GDP growth of 3.9% and 4.1% and private consumption growth of 1.8% and 2.4% in 2026 and 2027, respectively, is where this report begins.

  • The BOK points to falling stock pricesThe BOK governor attributed the cut to this year's consumption forecast to the drag from falling equities. He added that it would take time for IT-sector growth to translate into private consumption.3
  • KDI reached the same forecastsThe Korea Development Institute (KDI) also projected private consumption growth of 1.8% in 2026 and 2.4% in 2027 in its second-half economic outlook.6 It estimates that higher oil prices have cut this year's consumption growth by 0.4 pp, while the chip boom has had little effect. Next year, it expects the chip boom to contribute 0.6 pp and oil prices to subtract 0.2 pp.7

National income and household income diverge

When chip prices rise, Korea can buy more imports with the same volume of exports. As a result, Q3 real gross domestic income (GDI) rose 18.8% y/y.8 Household finances moved in the opposite direction.

+18.8%Q3 real GDI, y/y · purchasing power of national income (BOK)
−2.1%Q3 real household income · nominal monthly income: KRW 5.48m, up 0.8% (Ministry of Data and Statistics)
−1.7%Q3 nominal disposable income, first decline in 13 quarters · non-consumption expenditure, including taxes and interest, +11.0%

The two measures cover different things. GDI covers the entire domestic economy, including corporations and government, while the Household Income and Expenditure Survey reports averages per household. The expiry of last year's Q3 consumption vouchers also created a substantial base effect in the household figures. Public transfer income fell 15.4%.9 Even so, all other income categories — business, transfers and property — declined, with only wage income rising (+3.6%).2 Income for the bottom 20% of households fell 2.7%, and disposable income fell 5.4%.9 The gap between the income Korea earns and the money households can spend is what this report traces.

The headline growth rate also reflects changing weights

In simplified terms, real GDP growth combines increases in output across industries using their previous-year nominal shares as weights. As chip prices surged, the nominal GDP share of the industry that includes semiconductors more than doubled, from 9.0% in 2025 to 20.7% in H1 2026. The larger share this year feeds into next year's growth weights. Yuanta Securities argues that this change in weights, rather than a broad acceleration in the economy, is a major reason for the higher 2027 growth forecast.10

  • Growth of 2.0% excluding semiconductorsQ2 GDP growth excluding the semiconductor industry was 2.0%, close to Korea's previous potential growth rate, according to Yuanta Securities.10
  • The BOK also estimates around 3.0% for next year on the old weightsThe BOK identified the same effect. IT manufacturing's share of nominal GDP more than doubled from 10.4% at the end of 2025 to 24.5% in Q2 2026. Holding that share at its earlier level would put next year's growth forecast at around 3.0% rather than 4.1%, the BOK estimates.11 Yuanta's 2.0% refers to actual Q2 growth in 2026, whereas the BOK's 3.0% is a forecast for 2027. They are not directly comparable.
  • Corporations account for 83% of the increaseYuanta Securities estimates that corporate operating surplus accounted for 83% of the increase in Q2 nominal GDP.10 Operating surplus in the national accounts is not the same as accounting profit or cash on company balance sheets. It indicates where the additional income accrued first.
  • Household consumption's GDP share at a record low of 39.4%Hana Securities estimates household consumption will account for 39.4% of GDP this year.12 Rather than an outright decline in spending, this largely reflects nominal GDP growing much faster as chip prices rose. It shows that household consumption is not keeping pace with the economy as a whole.

So growth of around 4% is not a false reading; it simply describes a different economy from the one most households experience. The additional income has accrued first to companies, not households.

The BOK still expects income from the chip boom to translate into consumption eventually. It even raised its 2027 consumption growth forecast from 2.3% to 2.4%. What has changed is its caution over how quickly and how broadly the gains will spread. Three publications from October to November show the shift in emphasis.

The three publications are not contradictory. The October report also noted that bonuses and dividends would chiefly strengthen the spending power of higher-income households. It identified mounting household debt, a weak construction sector and the equity-market correction as barriers to a broader recovery.13 The November report on Taiwan likewise envisaged consumption improving with a lag. Yonhap News Agency also noted that the November report differed in focus from the BOK's earlier work on trickle-down effects.16

The minutes of the October Monetary Policy Board meeting reveal the same concern. One member noted that the benefits of bonuses and dividends were concentrated among some households, whereas higher rates could raise interest expenses much more broadly.17 For the rate decision and the arithmetic behind the 4.1% forecast, see No.48 — Stocks Nearly Halved. 03Three pipes, two valves

Three narrow pipes carry the income.
Two valves restrict spending.

There are three routes from chipmakers' earnings to households: jobs, bonuses and the distribution of corporate profits through dividends, taxes and public spending. All three are narrow. Two additional valves are restricting consumption: stock prices and interest costs.

  1. PIPE 01Jobs
    1.9%IT manufacturing's share of employment at establishments in Korea (2025, 390,000 workers), vs. 10.6% in Taiwan (910,000)

    Semiconductor production is capital-intensive and employs relatively few people. With only one-fifth of Taiwan's share of employment in IT manufacturing, Korea has a correspondingly narrower direct channel for wages and bonuses.16 These figures exclude indirect jobs at equipment and materials suppliers, in plant construction and in logistics. Still, total employment rose just 0.5% y/y in October.12

  2. PIPE 02Bonuses
    ₩110kExtra spending per ₩1m in one-off income, such as a bonus (BOK estimate)

    Households spend less of a one-off windfall than they do of a regular paycheck.13 The gains are also concentrated among high-income workers, who have a lower propensity to consume.16 In an August BOK analysis cited by Hana Securities, 27% of bonuses available as cash went toward additional spending. The sample and denominator differ, so that estimate cannot be directly compared with the KRW 110,000 figure.12

  3. PIPE 03Corporate profits
    83%Corporate operating surplus as a share of the Q2 increase in nominal GDP (national accounts; Yuanta Securities)

    The additional income flows first into corporate profits and corporate tax revenue.10 To reach households, it must pass through dividends, bonuses or fiscal spending. Taking individual ownership shares into account, the BOK estimated that households would receive about KRW 11tn in dividends from the two chipmakers, adding around 0.05 pp to next year's consumption growth.18 Fiscal spending has a bigger impact. If higher corporate tax receipts help fund roughly KRW 20tn in additional transfers to households next year, consumption growth could rise by 0.20–0.25 pp. That would exceed the bonus effect and reach middle- and lower-income households.13

  4. VALVE 01Stocks
    −43.6%Retail investors' average daily stock trading value: KRW 65.9tn in Q2 KRW 37.2tn in Q3

    Trading value measures market activity, not the value of stocks households own. But it has a direct effect on consumption statistics. The BOK said Q3 private consumption fell in both goods, such as passenger cars and appliances, and services, including finance and dining. It said consumption of financial services would depend on where the stock-market correction ended.19 The BOK estimates that an auto industry strike and the drop in stock trading together shaved about 0.6 pp off Q3 consumption growth. Without them, consumption would have risen 0.4% instead of falling 0.2%.20 Falling stock prices also depress spending through the wealth effect.16 In the November consumer survey, the index of current household savings fell four points from the previous month.21

  5. VALVE 02Interest costs
    +75bpCumulative policy rate hikes since July

    Average monthly household interest expenses reached KRW 147,000 in Q3, up 11.0% y/y. The Ministry of Data and Statistics attributed this to the two policy rate hikes during the quarter.2 The burden rises first for households with floating-rate loans or those taking out or refinancing debt. Households with substantial deposits may earn more interest instead. The BOK attributed the November decline in consumer sentiment to rising lending rates as well as corrections in domestic and overseas equities.22

Money comes in slowly and goes out quickly

The pipes and valves also operate at different speeds. KDI's analysis of two decades of data found that consumption barely responded to rising chip prices in the first year. The response began after four quarters and peaked after seven to eight. Rising oil prices, by contrast, reduced consumption immediately.23

These different lags go a long way toward explaining why consumption has lagged GDP growth this year. Much of the income is expected next year, but the costs have already eaten into household budgets.

A BOK analysis found that even in regions where workers received bonuses, extra spending went first to expensive goods, such as cars and department-store purchases, with little change at supermarkets and convenience stores. We discussed this in No.38 — ₩22tn in Chip Bonuses: Department Stores First in Line. That pattern has yet to change.

Of the three pipes, employment has the greatest potential to widen. A bonus is a one-off payment; a job provides a monthly paycheck. KDI also argued that policy should improve employment and income conditions rather than focus on short-term consumption stimulus.23

04The retail picture

Offline sales fell 1.4% in October.
Was it just the holiday calendar?

The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources reported that sales at major retailers rose 10.9% online but fell 1.4% offline in October. It was the first decline in offline sales since January.24 The biggest reason is the calendar. Chuseok, Korea's autumn harvest holiday, fell in October last year but shifted to September this year. That makes it important to look at the two months together.

The simple average of the two monthly offline sales growth rates is below 2% (5.1% in September and −1.4% in October), about a third of the 6.2% recorded in H1. Overall sales growth averaged 5.1%, also below the first-half rate of 7.3%.26 This is not a statistical adjustment for holiday effects, but the combined picture is still weaker than in H1. There is evidence on the other side, too. Online sales rose 10.9% in October, including a 17.2% increase in food sales and 14.4% growth in services and other categories such as delivery.27 Buying less is not the same as shopping somewhere else. Offline sales alone are not enough to conclude that consumption as a whole has cooled. Brokerages point instead to weakness in specific categories.

  • Department-store and luxury growth slows to around 1%Kiwoom Securities notes that department-store sales growth slowed from 8.4% in September to 1.0% in October, while imported luxury goods decelerated from 15.6% in August 6.9% in September 1.0% in October. It cited the weaker wealth effect and a stronger won, concluding that Q4 retail sales forecasts would have to be cut.28
  • Spending is shifting toward essentialsIBK Securities notes that even online, where Chuseok has less impact, average September–October growth was weak for appliances (+1.6%) and fashion (−3.2%), while food (+12%) and services (+12.5%) remained strong.29
  • Shoppers are moving to cheaper alternativesHanwha Investment & Securities identified a pattern of trading down: luxury sales weakened while mainstream fashion held up. It said the income effect of bonuses had yet to appear.30

Consumer sentiment has fallen for two months

The November Composite Consumer Sentiment Index (CCSI) fell 2.3 points m/m to 103.1, its lowest reading since April. It remains above 100, indicating greater optimism than the long-term average, but all six component indices declined. The largest drop was in current economic conditions (78, −4). The BOK cited corrections in domestic and overseas equities, higher lending rates and uncertainty over the semiconductor cycle.22 Savings and employment expectations, which are directly relevant to spending power but are not CCSI components, also deteriorated.

CAUTIONHomeplus and the hypermarket data

Homeplus temporarily suspended operations at 67 hypermarkets nationwide from November 27 because it lacked funds to purchase inventory. Online deliveries stopped the previous day, and no reopening date has been set.31 Court-supervised rehabilitation reflects the company’s financial difficulties; the cash shortage of a single retailer is not evidence of weaker consumer demand.

But the disruption must be accounted for when reading the data. Both the trade ministry’s hypermarket figures and the hypermarket category of the Ministry of Data and Statistics’ retail sales index will reflect the Homeplus closures in November and December. Hypermarket sales alone could give a misleading picture of consumer demand. Hanwha Investment & Securities notes that October hypermarket sales per store fell only 1.2%, much less than the overall decline of 14.1%, and believes competitors are filling the gap left by Homeplus.30

05The Taiwan comparison

Taiwan's consumption recovered after a year.
The conditions were different.

Taiwan entered the chip boom a year before Korea, and it took about a year for consumption to catch up. That has raised hopes that Korea will follow suit in a year's time. The BOK's assessment: it could, but the recovery may be slower.

  • Taiwan's consumption was slow to catch up, tooTaiwan's GDP growth accelerated from 1.1% in 2023 to 5.3% in 2024 and 8.9% in 2025.16, 32 On November 27, Taiwan's statistics agency raised its forecast for 2026 GDP growth by 2.23 pp to 13.28%.32 Yet private consumption growth was just 1.4% in 2025 and did not pick up to 5.4% until the first three quarters of 2026.16
  • Stocks and fiscal support revived Taiwanese consumptionThe BOK found that consumption in Taiwan recovered from Q4 2025, helped by a strong equity market and government fiscal support.15 Taiwan's statistics agency attributed the 5.50% increase in Q3 private consumption (preliminary estimate, Nov. 27) to spending on communications, recreation, transport and overseas travel, as well as stock and fund transaction fees amid buoyant financial markets. It forecast full-year consumption growth of 4.74%, noting that recent financial-market volatility had partly offset the gains.32 Taiwan's consumption is sensitive to stock-market swings, too. In Korea, weaker stock trading had already reduced Q3 consumption growth.
  • Korea entered the same cycle about a year laterKorea's growth accelerated from H2 2025, roughly a year after Taiwan's. Private consumption growth in Korea over the same Q1–Q3 2026 period was reportedly 2.1%.16, 33

In Taiwan, fiscal support and equities have both pushed consumption in the same direction. In Korea, next year's fiscal spending will push consumption up while higher interest rates pull it down. The idea that Korea will automatically follow Taiwan in a year's time is therefore hard to justify.

Even if Korea follows Taiwan, the recovery may be uneven. The BOK noted that Taiwan's K-shaped divergence between IT and non-IT sectors had deepened. Its policy recommendations therefore prioritized improving middle-class spending power and easing living-cost pressures on lower-income households.15

The same data, two interpretations
The recovery will come, even if it is late
  • The BOK and KDI both forecast 2.4% private consumption growth in 2027. KDI estimates a larger chip-price contribution of +0.6 pp next year.6, 7
  • The BOK governor said several indicators point to strong consumption next year.3
  • Hana Securities sees significant potential spillovers to retail if bonuses in H1 and the subsequent transmission of capital expenditure into jobs and wages in H2 materialize.12
  • A stronger won lowers import prices and boosts purchasing power for middle- and lower-income households (BOK, October).13
The recovery will be slower and narrower
  • IBK Securities: Falling asset prices, limited spillovers and higher financing costs could widen the gap between headline economic indicators and actual spending in 2027.29
  • Daishin Securities: Given this year's downgrade, growth of 2.4% next year is hardly a meaningful improvement.5
  • Yuanta Securities: Even if chip prices hold steady, real GDI growth will slow from over 18% in Q3 2026 to 2.6% in Q3 2027. The slowdown will also show up first in corporate profits and corporate tax receipts.10
  • KDI identifies high market interest rates, weaker equities and slow wage growth outside the chip sector as downside risks.23
  • Two Monetary Policy Board members dissented in favor of holding rates. Press coverage interpreted this as reflecting concern over the limited spillover from semiconductors to employment and income across the economy.11

How to read Monday's (Nov. 30) October retail sales release

  1. Do not be alarmed by a monthly declineSeptember retail sales rose 3.6% m/m, helped by a recovery in auto sales and holiday demand. It was the largest increase in three years and seven months.35 October could easily see a reversal.
  2. October 2025 was also a strong monthLast October, retail sales jumped on Chuseok and consumption vouchers.36 A large base effect will distort the year-on-year comparison as well.
  3. Watch the categoriesRather than passenger cars and luxury goods, look for growth in non-durable goods such as food and beverages, and semi-durables such as clothing. It is better to look at September and October together and compare them with Q3.

The October Industrial Activity Trends release is due Monday, November 30 (Kiwoom Securities calendar).37

CONCLUSIONConclusion

The chip income is already here.
Reaching the checkout will take time.

Both the BOK and KDI expect consumption to improve next year. The question is how quickly and how broadly. Income from the chip boom has accrued first as corporate profits, and large bonuses and dividends are concentrated in 2027. Korea's channels to households are narrower than Taiwan's. The estimated consumption response to higher chip prices only starts after four quarters, while interest costs and higher prices are already eroding household budgets.The BOK calls it a matter of time. November's figures suggest Korea may need more time than Taiwan did.

Four signals will help show whether the wait is getting shorter.

  1. Do bonuses translate into everyday spending?After bonus payments in January–March, check retail sales and card-payment approvals. If gains are confined to cars and department stores, the recovery remains concentrated among higher-income households. Gains at convenience stores, in food and beverages and in dining out would signal a broader recovery.
  2. Does the boom create jobs?Watch whether Q3's 4.1% q/q increase in equipment investment (BOK) translates into factory and construction jobs.38 If employment growth climbs above its current sub-1% pace, recurring wages can replace one-off windfalls as the engine of spending.
  3. Does household purchasing power recover?Q3 disposable income fell 1.7% even in nominal terms. Purchasing power begins to recover when nominal disposable income growth exceeds inflation. The recovery in equities and the path of interest rates will influence that figure.
  4. Warning signsA simultaneous fall in semiconductor export prices and stock prices would weaken both next year's bonuses and the wealth effect. Less income would be coming in.

Glossary
Real income
Income adjusted for inflation. If nominal income rises 1% but prices rise 3%, real income falls. The Household Income and Expenditure Survey uses monthly averages per household.
Private consumption
Spending on goods and services by households and nonprofit institutions serving households. It is the largest component of GDP.
Real gross domestic income (GDI)
The purchasing power of the income earned by the domestic economy. It grows faster than real GDP when export prices rise more than import prices.
Base effect
An unusually high or low comparison-period figure that makes the measured growth rate look weaker or stronger.
Operating surplus
The portion of value added accruing to producers after labor compensation and taxes on production are accounted for. Broadly analogous to corporate profit, but not the same accounting measure.
Trickle-down effect
The idea that income gains accruing first to companies or high-income earners spread across the economy through jobs, wages, dividends and taxes. The BOK used the term in its October report.
One-off income
Non-recurring income such as a performance bonus or special bonus. It generally translates into less additional spending than recurring wages do.
Propensity to consume
The share of income spent on consumption. It tends to be lower at higher income levels and for one-off payments.
Wealth effect
The tendency for spending to rise when stocks or home prices increase and fall when asset prices decline.
bp
Basis point: one hundredth of a percentage point. 75 bp equals 0.75 percentage points.
Trading down
Shifting to cheaper brands or retail channels for similar goods.
Composite Consumer Sentiment Index (CCSI)
A monthly BOK survey-based index of households' views on the economy and their finances. Readings above 100 indicate sentiment more optimistic than the long-term average.
Court-supervised rehabilitation
A legal process in which a financially distressed company restructures its debts under court supervision in an effort to continue operating.
Retail sales
A monthly measure of goods consumption (the Retail Sales Index) published by Korea's Ministry of Data and Statistics. It excludes services such as dining out and travel.
K-shaped recovery
An uneven recovery in which some industries or income groups prosper while others stagnate or decline, tracing the two arms of a K.
Non-durable goods
Goods consumed quickly, such as food, beverages and cosmetics. Clothing and shoes are semi-durables, used for roughly a year or more; cars and appliances are durable goods.
Differences between sources
IT employment shareThe BOK's 1.9% uses total employment at establishments as the denominator. Some press reports called it a share of all employed persons, but dividing 390,000 by roughly 29 million gives 1.3%, not 1.9%. Other sources put semiconductor industry employment at 2.6%, using different industry definitions and denominators.
Size of bonus paymentsThe BOK's KRW 60–70tn is a pre-tax estimate of special bonuses at major semiconductor, auto and shipbuilding companies. Hana Securities' KRW 21.9tn figure, used in No.38, covers the immediately monetizable portion of bonuses to be paid by Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix next year. The two are not directly comparable.
Private consumption forecastsSome reports described the 2027 consumption forecast as 2.2% 1.8%, but the BOK cut its 2026 forecast (2.1% 1.8%). The 2027 forecast actually rose from 2.3% in August to 2.4%.
