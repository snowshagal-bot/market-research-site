01Measured in dollars, the decline was −7.6%

Translate the same three months into dollars and KOSPI's decline narrows to −7.6% · that is simply the arithmetic of a stronger won reducing the loss measured in dollars4 · it approximates the index-level return seen by an unhedged dollar-based investor (actual performance depends on entry point, hedging and dividends) · but this is a difference in measurement units, not proof that FX caused the equity decline

02Earnings expectations edged lower

  • Daishin's Q3 KOSPI operating-profit forecast fell from KRW 262tn to the KRW 257tn range (period not specified) · in the quant section of the same report, the aggregate moved from KRW 265.6tn on Aug 28 to KRW 260.7tn on Sep 25 (−1.8%) · both semiconductor and non-semiconductor estimates were cut3
  • FnGuide consensus (reported Sep 30) puts combined Q3 operating profit for Samsung Electronics and SK hynix at KRW 188.34tn13
  • DS Investment & Securities' combined estimate of KRW 174tn is about 7.6% lower · DS cites USD/KRW, down roughly 11% from end-Q2, as the biggest reason, alongside MX costs, temporary VD/CE expenses and the HBM shipment mix4, 13, 14

Earnings Up,
Multiple Down

Index = forward EPS × forward P/E · end-June (approx.) → Sep 23

Split the index into earnings and valuation and the three-month picture changes · KOSPI 12-month forward EPS rose from 1,089.5p on Jun 24 to 1,275.84p on Sep 23 · +17.1% · from Jun 30 to Sep 23, KOSPI fell from 8,476.48 to 7,080.92 (−16.5%) · the June-end P/E is only an approximation, pairing the Jun 30 index with Jun 24 EPS (7.78x), but even on that approximation the falling component is the multiple3, 4, 8, 10

7.8→4.74→5.55x · end-June (approx.) → low (Daishin tally · date not specified · first sub-5x reading since 2000) → Sep 23 · 2010–2025 median 9.82x3, 4

With EPS rising while the index fell 22% in July, the multiple compression also points to July · Daishin's assessment is that in September the index rose roughly in step with forward EPS, leaving P/E in a 5.3–5.6x range3 · Q3 earnings forecasts were being cut even as forward EPS rose partly because the 12-month window was rolling toward higher 2027 earnings (the share attributable to this effect cannot be verified) · the 2027 forecast was also nudged higher (KOSPI operating profit KRW 1,250tn → KRW 1,263tn)3

Why did the multiple compress? · what is known and what is not

KNOWN

Events during July's selloff

  • Jul 2 · Meta disclosed plans to sell excess AI computing capacity externally · KOSPI −7.89%, SK hynix −14.57%19
  • Jul 28–29 · circuit breakers on two consecutive days · intraday KOSPI in the 5,200s · cited factors included CXMT competition concerns, controversy over Nvidia circular transactions, SK hynix Q2 results falling short of expectations, oil above USD 80 and liquidation of leveraged products20
  • U.S. 10-year yield rose from 4.422% to 4.72% in July · the monthly-indicator article cited rising U.S. yields as a main backdrop5
ESTIMATE

Discounting the peak · this note's hypothesis

  • What the Meta, CXMT, Nvidia and SK hynix headlines all touched was the durability of AI demand · the hypothesis is that the market cut the value assigned to how long elevated earnings could last, rather than just the size of earnings
  • Counterpoint · Daishin says talk of a peak-out is premature3 · Hanwha also expects any slowdown in semiconductor export growth to be limited21
  • Rates alone are not enough either · the multiple collapsed during July's ~30bp rise, but over Aug 31 → Sep 25, when the 10-year rose another 43bp, P/E held roughly flat at ~5.50x (Aug 31, this note's calculation) → 5.55x (Sep 23)3, 4, 5, 6
UNKNOWN

How much of 2027 earnings will the market believe?

  • Consensus 2027 KOSPI operating-profit estimates are still rising3
  • The multiple has recovered only partway from the low · the market has not yet resolved which side is right
The earnings numbers grew. The price paid for them shrank in July."Discounting the peak" is a hypothesis · the multiple's resilience through September's rate surge weakens a rates-only explanation

Two different flows

Export Dollars and
Equity-Market Money

Foreigners sold roughly KRW 41.6tn net in Q3 · September's largest net buyer was Other Corporations

There is no evidence this time for a simple link in which more export dollars translate into equity buying · dollars earned by exporters and dollars moving with foreign equity trades are different flows · estimated Q3 foreign net selling combines KRW 9.9tn in July and KRW 10.2tn in August (Daishin) with September's KRW 21.5108tn(KRX), for roughly KRW 41.6tn in total (approximation combining sources)3, 4, 22

A substantial share of the KRW 134.9195tn sold net over five months occurred even during the index surge in May–June3, 23 · foreign selling and index direction did not always move together

01Who sold?

  • Of September's foreign net selling, SK hynix accounted for KRW 11.5805tn and Samsung Electronics KRW 4.8920tn · together the two stocks made up 76.6%22
  • Their combined share of KOSPI market capitalization rose from 48.81% to 50.85% during September · some of the selling therefore also reflects trimming weights after price gains22

02Who absorbed it?

Other Corporations were September's largest net buyers · Daishin attributes most of that to Samsung Electronics and SK hynix buybacks and argues those purchases helped firm the index floor · cumulative purchases: Samsung Electronics KRW 10.31tn (74.7% of plan), SK hynix KRW 24.39tn (58.8%, all to be cancelled) · net buying exceeded KRW 1tn a day on 20 of 22 trading days since Aug 20 in the buyback context3

03Where the export-side dollars went

  • Yuanta · semiconductor companies' cash and short-term investments rose sharply · companies need to retain dollars against future foreign-currency CAPEX · direct investment in KRW bonds and FX swaps increased, improving foreign-currency funding conditions and pushing basis-swap levels sharply higher in H216
  • IBK · corporate dollar deposits accelerated even as USD/KRW fell sharply after June · IBK, however, reads this as a response to wider rate differentials and dollar demand ahead of U.S. investment, making it a won-negative factor17
  • Overseas investment · CAPEX · IBK says the USD 20bn annual cap on direct investment in the U.S. stimulates corporate dollar demand17 · SK hynix's ADR proceeds also list capital expenditure among the uses of funds18
Link to NO.04 · when the supporting bid runs out

Daishin-estimated buyback completion · Samsung Electronics around Oct 8; SK hynix around Oct 163 · the same supporting bid discussed in NO.04, "The Supporting Bid Runs Out First" · Daishin expects foreigners and financial-investment firms to fill the gap if rates and FX stabilize3 · whether they actually do can be checked in daily flows after mid-October

Counterpoints · what this framework may miss

1

Would a weaker won be bullish?

A weaker won would support earnings translated back into KRW · but IBK says foreign equity flows have been the variable most closely linked with USD/KRW since last year17 · FX direction alone cannot determine the index direction

2

Q4 growth rates are partly a base-effect story

The comparison base drops to USD 59.52bn in Oct 2025 and USD 60.77bn in Nov 2025, while the Chuseok shift also creates a working-day reverse base effect2 · even if high growth rates persist, the new information is not the year-on-year base comparison but changes in earnings estimates

3

A peak is about the slope, not the level

September commodity DRAM export unit value −3.5% m/m, NAND −22.0% m/m (Korea Customs Service, weight-based)21 · exports can be at a record while the market focuses first on a rollover in unit-value momentum · Hanwha's main text, however, characterizes commodity DRAM as "unit prices steady, volumes up" and puts high-value memory (MCP) unit prices at +2.7% m/m21

4

Part of the gap is structural

In iM's chart, Korea's market-cap-to-exports ratio sits well below Taiwan's · iM also points to Korea's more diversified export mix24 · an underlying difference remains that Q3 events alone cannot explain

GLOSSARYQuick glossary
Forward EPS
Expected earnings per share over the next 12 months · consensus compiled from brokerage forecasts · as time passes, later months roll into the window, which can mechanically lift the figure during a growth phase
Forward P/E
Index level (price) ÷ forward EPS · the multiple showing how much the market is willing to pay for one unit of expected earnings
Index in USD terms
KOSPI divided by that day's USD/KRW rate · approximates the translated index return seen by an unhedged dollar-based investor
Average daily exports
Monthly exports ÷ working days · useful when comparing months with different numbers of working days, such as when holidays shift
Base effect
When a low or high comparison base makes a growth rate look unusually large or small
Other Corporations
Domestic corporations outside the individual, foreign and institutional categories · company share repurchases are recorded here
ADR
American Depositary Receipt · a security that allows shares of a non-U.S. company to trade in the U.S. market in dollars
FX swap
A transaction that exchanges currencies now and reverses the exchange later at a pre-agreed rate · a channel for borrowing or deploying dollar funding
Cross-currency basis swap
The spread embedded in swapping KRW and USD interest-rate cash flows · reflects conditions in Korea's dollar funding market
EBSI
Export Business Survey Index · a reading above 100 means more firms expect export conditions to improve in the next quarter than worsen
