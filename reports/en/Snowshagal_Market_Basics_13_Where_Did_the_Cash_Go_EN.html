These interpretations need not conflict. They could describe successive steps in the same transaction.

① A buys

A buys KRW 10tn of shares from B. A: −KRW 10tn; B: +KRW 10tn. Total: unchanged.

② B withdraws

B transfers the KRW 10tn received to a bank. Total: −KRW 10tn.

The point

Both "spent on shares" and "moved to banks" can be true. This is an illustration, not evidence tracing the actual KRW 51tn.

If deposits fall,
does that mean cash left the stock market?

Not necessarily. Deposits are a balance. A balance shows the outcome, not the path the money took.

THE SHORT ANSWER
Investor deposits are cash balances at brokerages. The balance alone cannot tell you where the money went.
Net buying, bank deposits, and borrowing over comparable periods can help narrow the possibilities.
02How the mechanics work

Your account is not
the entire market

Start with one brokerage account, then zoom out to the market as a whole.

Investor deposits are cash left in the account

  • Investor deposits · Of the money customers place with brokerages to trade financial products, the portion still held as cash
  • · Examples: cash deposited to buy shares, or proceeds from sales not yet withdrawn
  • · Brokerages cannot freely use this money. Under Korea's Capital Markets Act, it must be separately deposited with Korea Securities Finance.
  • · KOFIA publishes daily figures—the "cash on the sidelines" cited in headlines

In Korea, stock sales settle two business days after the trade date (Part 10). Keep estimated sale proceeds shown in your app separate from settled cash and the amount actually available to withdraw.

Across the market, the money does not simply disappear

When you buy shares, the cash in your account goes down. But the seller receives that money. The shares change hands; the cash moves between accounts.

  • If both accounts are in the reported deposit pool, the total is unchanged
  • . The aggregate balance falls when cash leaves the reported deposit pool —for example, through bank withdrawals, conversion to separately counted products such as broker-issued notes, or payment to a seller outside that pool
  • . Individual investors' net purchases are therefore a clue to who bought, not a measure of how far the deposit balance fell
A common mix-upPortfolio losses ≠ falling deposits
  • If A buys KRW 1m in shares and their value halves, A's shares are worth KRW 500k. But B still has the KRW 1m received.
  • "Trapped in a falling market" may mean investors still hold shares. It does not, by itself, explain why aggregate deposits declined.

EXTRA CONTEXTTerms that often appear alongside "sideline cash"

Deposit turnover ratio · If investor deposits are KRW 100tn and daily trading value is KRW 50tn, the ratio is 50%. That does not mean half of deposit cash was used to buy stocks. The same money can be traded repeatedly, and trading value includes transactions by many investors. In market commentary, readings above 40% are sometimes viewed as entering an overheated range.

Unpaid purchase settlements · Amounts owed on stock purchases that have not been paid by the settlement deadline. Failure to pay can trigger broker-initiated forced sales. The balance exceeded KRW 1tn for eight consecutive sessions, October 12–21.

CMA · A Korean cash-management account in which a brokerage places customer funds in instruments such as bonds and pays a return.

Broker-issued notes · Short-term promissory notes with maturities of up to one year, issued directly by eligible well-capitalized brokerages. Several products offering rates around 4% appeared in October.

THE ANSWER FROM PART 2
Buying shares reduces the cash in your account. Across the market, that cash passes to the seller.
For the aggregate to fall, cash must leave the accounts captured by the deposit statistic. The balance alone cannot tell you how it left.
03Put it into practice

How to read
"sideline cash" headlines

When you see a headline claiming that trillions "vanished" from investor deposits, look for four other pieces of information.

Four questions to ask about a cash-balance headline

01What is the reference point?

The same KRW 88.6841tn is down 36.5% from June's peak but barely changed from the year's low on January 13. Start with the dates and the benchmark.

02How much did individuals buy over the same period?

From June 4 to October 23, individuals were net buyers of more than KRW 47tn across Korea's KOSPI and KOSDAQ markets. The cash paid goes to the sellers. Net buying is a clue, not the amount by which investor deposits declined.

03Where else might cash be held?

Check whether bank term deposits, CMAs and broker-issued notes also increased. Growth in bank deposits does not prove the money came out of brokerage accounts. Compare the direction of the moves,not assume a direct transfer.

04What happened to borrowed money?

Falling margin-loan balances alongside rising forced sales may indicate some deleveraging was involuntary. But those figures measure different things. Use them to narrow the possible explanations, not to calculate a single cash-flow figure.
COMMON MISREADINGSPlausible, but wrong
MISREADING 01
"Deposits fell, so retail investors fled stocks."
Some cash may have been withdrawn; some sellers may have received proceeds outside the reported pool. Over the comparison period, both retail net buying and bank deposits increased. A balance does not trace the money.
MISREADING 02
"If cash builds up, share prices must rise soon."
Investor deposits peaked on June 4; the KOSPI reached its record closing level on June 22. Cash sitting in an account is not guaranteed to enter the stock market. Cash that could buy shares is not cash committed to buying them.
MISREADING 03
"Margin borrowing fell, so the market is now safer."
Borrowers may have repaid loans voluntarily—or positions may have been liquidated. Forced deleveraging means sell orders have already hit the market. Watch forced-selling data too, while remembering the coverage is different.
PART 13 · IN ONE SENTENCE

A KRW 51tn drop in investor deposits means that the cash balance covered by this statistic fell by KRW 51tn.

Cash spent buying shares passes to sellers. The proceeds may remain in brokerage accounts or move to other products or banks. This balance alone cannot tell us how much went where.

The total broke its prior record of KRW 137.4174tn set on May 12. It was almost 60% above the KRW 87.8290tn recorded at the end of 2025. May's daily average deposit turnover ratio was 50.29%.

June 18 & 22, 2026 · The KOSPI above 9,000

The KOSPI first closed above 9,000 on June 18, at 9,063.84, then posted a record close of 9,114.55 on June 22—18 days after the deposit peak.

Oct. 12–21, 2026 · Borrowing contracts

Outstanding margin loans fell from KRW 34.2887tn to KRW 30.2710tn. Unpaid purchase-settlement balances exceeded KRW 1tn for eight straight trading days, while associated forced sales totaled KRW 403.4bn.

October 2026 · Trading value also declines

The KOSPI Market's average daily trading value fell to KRW 20.5578tn in October, almost 60% below May's KRW 50.3471tn. Yuanta Securities analyst Lee Jae-won said that the end of share buybacks, together with declining trading value and investor deposits, had weakened the market's ability to absorb price shocks (see Part 12 on buybacks).

Oct. 22–26, 2026 · Deposits around KRW 88tn

After eight consecutive sessions of declines, deposits reached KRW 88.6841tn on October 22, their lowest since January 13 (KRW 88.0489tn). KOSPI Market trading value that day was KRW 14.9156tn, the year's lowest. Deposits rebounded to KRW 89.8626tn on October 23 before slipping to KRW 88.6825tn on October 26.

Oct. 23, 2026 · Five major banks' term deposits: KRW 1,016.8561tn

The balance rose KRW 10.7690tn from the end of September. Roughly 84% of the KRW 79.9831tn increase since the end of January was concentrated in the second half.

IMPORTANT DATA CAVEATS

Why deposits fell · Both "used to buy shares" and "moved into bank deposits" are interpretations reported in the press. This article does not assign portions of the decline to either explanation.

Retail net buying vs. investor deposits · The roughly KRW 47tn of retail net purchases should not be directly matched against the KRW 51tn decline in investor deposits. Net buying identifies buyers, but cash paid goes to sellers; the net-purchase figure does not show whether sellers' proceeds stayed in the deposit pool. Borrowed purchases, shifts to overseas equities, IPO subscriptions and settlement timing complicate the picture. The article did not establish whether KRW 28.2538tn of ETF net buying was included in KOSPI Market net buying, so that figure is shown separately.

Bank term deposits · There is no evidence in these figures that the five major banks' increase in term deposits came from brokerage accounts. Only the concurrent direction of the change can be established.

The deposit peak · KOFIA's underlying data confirms KRW 139.6947tn (KRW 139,694,762m) on June 4. Some news reports used other figures; this article consistently uses the primary data.

Different comparison windows · The KRW 51tn deposit decline runs from June 4 to October 22. Retail net buying of roughly KRW 47tn covers June 4 to October 23. The KRW 10.7690tn bank term-deposit increase runs from end-September to October 23. Because both coverage and dates differ, these figures cannot be added or subtracted to calculate where the KRW 51tn went.

Coverage of the deposit statistic · The legal definition of investor deposits is not assumed to match every component of KOFIA's published series. Here, "outside the reported pool" means outside KOFIA's investor-deposit statistics.

Deposit turnover ratio · This is trading value divided by investor deposits—not the percentage of deposited cash used to buy shares. Markets included in trading value can differ by source. The 40% "overheating" marker is a market convention, not an official threshold.

Margin loans vs. forced sales · The KRW 403.4bn forced-sale figure covers unpaid purchase settlements in KOFIA's statistics. It is not the portion of the margin-loan decline attributable to forced liquidation.

Same −KRW 10tn. Different stories. · The figures are hypothetical. Only two accounts appear in this simplified ledger, and scenario ④ assumes the seller's proceeds fall outside the reported deposit pool. This article does not independently identify which actual investors' balances are included in that pool.

Rounding of investor deposits · KOFIA's underlying series is recorded in millions of won. Values below KRW 100m have been truncated in the Korean figures.

Figures that cannot be verified are not filled in.
