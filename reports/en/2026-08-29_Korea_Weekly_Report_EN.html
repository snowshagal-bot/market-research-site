08.27 · Bank of Korea Monetary Policy Board

The Bigger Change Was Not the Hike,
but the Growth Outlook

The Event WHAT HAPPENED
  • Policy rate 2.75% → 3.00% · a second consecutive hike after July · Board member Hwang Kun-il dissented in favor of holding
  • Growth forecast: 2026 2.6% → 3.3% · 2027 2.1% → 2.9%
  • Core-inflation forecasts raised to 2.5% for both 2026 and 2027 · headline forecasts held at 2.7% and 2.3%
  • Rate-path distribution: 10 votes at 3.25% · 6 at 3.5% · 5 at 3.0%
  • The statement removed the phrase that it was 'necessary to continue the rate-hike stance'
What Changed WHY IT MATTERS
  • Brokerage forecasts had split exactly in half ahead of the meeting · three called for a hike, three for a hold
  • Market pricing leaned toward a hold · the 3-month Monetary Stabilization Bond traded at a 9bp spread
  • The bigger change was not the hike itself, but the revision that put growth above potential even in 2027 · leaving the BOK with a hawkish rationale after the meeting
  • Korea–U.S. policy-rate gap 1.00pp → 0.75pp · the current-account surplus and dollar selling by large semiconductor exporters added pressure for KRW appreciation
How the Market Read It MARKET RESPONSE
  • Market yields rose immediately after the announcement, then reversed lower after the dissent and rate-path distribution were digested
  • KOSPI on Aug. 27 +1.53% · the market did not treat the hike as a negative
  • The impact differed by sector · KRX Banks for the week +5.39% · KOSPI 200 Financials +3.80% · on the other side, KRX Automobiles -2.70%
What Carries Forward WHAT REMAINS
  • One more hike this year is effectively the consensus · most expect a hold in October followed by a November hike
  • More than the policy rate, the unresolved issue is the long end · expanded government spending means more government-bond issuance feeding into the term premium
Where Views Diverge

The terminal-rate call is split · Kiwoom raised its forecast from 3.25% to 3.50% · Hyundai Motor Securities assigns 60% to a 3.25% terminal rate and 40% to 3.50% · the split turns on whether analysts take the BOK's revised growth outlook at face value

Early in the Week · Shareholder Returns at the Two Semiconductor Mega Caps

The Announcements Are Done;
Execution Runs Through November

The Event WHAT HAPPENED
  • Samsung Electronics announced total shareholder returns of KRW 90–110tn · with roughly KRW 30tn in cash dividends planned during Q3
  • SK hynix KRW 40tn treasury-share repurchase and cancellation · purchase period Aug. 20–Nov. 19 · cancellation within one to two weeks after completion
  • SK hynix raised shareholder returns from 'up to 50%' of cumulative free cash flow to '50% or more'
Confirmed vs. Unconfirmed WHY IT MATTERS
  • Confirmed · SK hynix purchase period and cancellation · Samsung Electronics Q3 cash dividend
  • Unconfirmed · whether Samsung Electronics treasury shares will be cancelled · how the remaining resources will be split between dividends and buybacks
  • One reading is that Samsung Electronics' treasury shares are not slated for cancellation, so they support near-term flows rather than shareholder return
  • Another is that the 10% cap under Korea's Act on the Structural Improvement of the Financial Industry makes further common-share cancellation difficult, which could push the remaining resources toward dividends
How the Market Read It MARKET RESPONSE
  • KOSPI on Aug. 24 -3.12% · sold after the announcement was confirmed · assessed as KRW 30–50tn below the market's KRW 140tn expectation
  • Treasury-share purchases are real market buying, but they are not the same as investor-category flows
  • On Aug. 24–26, residual buying outside the three investor groups was +KRW 1.6483tn · +KRW 1.5919tn · +KRW 1.5982tn, respectively · treasury-share acquisition accounts are included here
  • Because a standalone 'other corporations' figure is unavailable on some days, we do not isolate a treasury-share amount
What Carries Forward WHAT REMAINS
  • Treasury-share buying remains in the market through Nov. 19 · it will account for part of buying outside the three main investor groups and may help support the index on the downside
  • When the repurchase ends, that demand disappears as well · today's floor should not be treated as permanent
  • The next checkpoint is disclosure of whether Samsung Electronics cancels shares and how it allocates the remaining resources
