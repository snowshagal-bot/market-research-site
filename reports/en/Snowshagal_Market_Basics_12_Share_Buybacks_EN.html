Who are "other corporations"?

The Korea Exchange (KRX) reports trading by investor category: retail, foreign, institutional and "other corporations." The last is a Korea-specific reporting category for domestic non-financial companies. When a listed company buys back its own shares, those trades generally appear here. For why the biggest buyer is not necessarily the force setting the price, see Part 1.

AUG 19-OCT 21, 2026 - ACTUAL MARKET EVENTSTwo companies announce KRW 55tn in buybacks

On August 19, SK hynix's board approved open-market purchases of 24.07 million common shares, worth about KRW 40 trillion - around 3.3% of its issued shares. The company said the shares would all be cancelledCancellation permanently reduces the number of issued shares. Unlike shares held in treasury, those shares cannot be reissued.. Two days later, Samsung Electronics announced a KRW 15 trillion buyback for employee share-based compensation. Together, the two announced programs totalled KRW 55 trillion.

Both companies finished ahead of schedule. Samsung completed a program originally due on November 21 by October 6. SK hynix finished on October 21, nearly a month before its November 19 deadline. It spent its KRW 40.0043 trillion authorization to acquire 23,693,272 shares - 376,728 fewer than the originally planned 24.07 million, because the value limit was reached first. Between August 20 and September 10, other corporations recorded more than KRW 1 trillion in net purchases on the KOSPI on every trading day except September 3.

Completion of the buyback does not mean the SK hynix shares have already been cancelled. The company plans to cancel all of them, but cancellation is a subsequent step that must be confirmed through a separate filing. Completing a repurchase and completing a cancellation are not the same event.

Samsung's repurchase spending was close to the net buying recorded under other corporations. In effect, the company absorbed shares sold by foreign and retail investors. Institutions were net buyers of KRW 881 billion during this period.

And the share prices? The KOSPI reached 7,080.92 on September 23 during the repurchase period, then fell to 5,552.74 by October 20. It rebounded 3.29% to close at 5,735.66 on October 21. SK hynix had closed at KRW 1,662,000 the day before its board decision. On October 21, when the buyback ended, the stock rose 5.81% on the day to KRW 1,294,000 - still 22% below that pre-announcement close. Samsung's October 21 close of KRW 223,500 was also below its average repurchase price of KRW 263,823.

As the programs approached completion, press reports warned of a potential gap in buying demand: a buyer spending roughly KRW 1 trillion a day was about to leave the market.

If the company kept buying,
why did its share price fall?

A share buyback adds purchase orders to the market. For two months, the company bought stock that other investors were selling. But prices reflect the interaction of all buy and sell orders, not just one participant's activity. Even with an additional buyer, prices can fall if sellers are more aggressive. No one can know the counterfactual price path without the buyback.

While buying

The company places purchase orders day after day, absorbing shares that others sell.

Once finished

Those orders stop. That is the gap in buying demand that headlines warned about.

After purchase

If the shares are cancelled, the issued share count falls permanently. If held in treasury, they may later go to employees or be sold.

Two different questions are being asked here. Does a buyback support the share price? That is a question about supply and demand while purchases are underway. Does a buyback benefit the shareholders who remain? That is a question about capital allocation - how much company cash is spent, and at what price. The first section dealt with market demand; the next works through shareholder value.

THE ANSWER IN ONE SENTENCE
Buybacks can provide buying support, but they do not guarantee a higher share price.
Whether they benefit continuing shareholders is another question. Examine the price paid and what the company does with the shares.
02MECHANICS AND TERMINOLOGY

Higher EPS Does Not
Always Mean More Shareholder Value

The first section asked whether buybacks support market prices. Here the question is whether continuing shareholders benefit. All else equal, repurchasing shares increases earnings per share (EPS). But does that automatically make each remaining share more valuable?

Treasury shares: stock a company owns in itself

Treasury shares (called treasury stock in US accounting) are shares a company has repurchased and still holds. While held in treasury, they carry neither voting rights nor dividend entitlements.

ON THE BOARD - TWO THINGS SHRINK IN A BUYBACK

Fewer shares in circulation,
less cash on the balance sheet

A company uses its own cash to buy back stock. That reduces the number of shares used in the EPS calculation and reduces its cash balance. The total number of issued shares falls only when the shares are cancelled. Whether each continuing shareholder is better off depends on how much value leaves the company relative to the reduction in shares.

Buying below intrinsic value benefits holders

Suppose Company A spends KRW 10 billion on buybacks. At the assumed fair value of KRW 100,000 per share, it could buy and cancel 100,000 shares. The company would then be worth KRW 90 billion, with 900,000 shares remaining. Fair value would still be KRW 100,000 per share.

If a company buys below intrinsic value, it retires more shares with the same cash, increasing the theoretical value of each remaining share. Paying above intrinsic value does the reverse. The excess goes to the selling shareholders.

Here, KRW 100,000 per share is an assumed intrinsic value for the exercise - not the stock's quoted market price. In practice, a company's fair value cannot be known precisely. That makes it difficult to declare with certainty whether a real buyback took place at a bargain or at an excessive price. Try changing the price and spending amount below.

The EPS calculation assumes the post-buyback share count lasts for a full year. In practice, spending every last won on buybacks could leave a company short of operating or investment capital. Those risks are excluded here. Actual stock prices do not follow this model mechanically.

EPS may rise while intrinsic value per share falls

In the simulator, earnings per shareNet income divided by shares outstanding. Treasury shares are excluded from the denominator; reported EPS generally uses the weighted-average share count for the period. (EPS) rose at every repurchase price because net income stayed constant while the denominator shrank. Yet intrinsic value per share rose only when the company bought below value. Higher EPS did not automatically make continuing investors wealthier: the company had spent cash. In reality, lower net income or added interest expense could prevent EPS from rising even after a reduction in shares. We examined the relationship between EPS and share prices in Part 4.

For accounting purposes, a company's own treasury shares are excluded when calculating EPS. The effect therefore begins when the shares are bought, not when they are cancelled. Actual quarterly or annual EPS, however, uses time-weighted average shares outstanding. Shares repurchased in December affect that year's EPS less than shares repurchased in January.

After the buyback: hold or cancel?

Repurchased shares can follow two paths. Cancellation reduces the issued share count permanently. Shares kept in treasury can later be sold or granted to employees, returning them to circulation.

The cash leaves when the company buys the shares. Cancelling shares already held in treasury does not trigger the same cash payment a second time.

Buybacks and dividends distribute cash differently

Companies generally have two principal ways of returning cash to shareholders. Dividends pay cash to all eligible shareholders in proportion to their holdings. In a buyback, only investors who sell shares receive cash. Those who do not sell receive no immediate payout, but their ownership percentage can rise as shares outstanding decline. Both are forms of shareholder returnsDistributing capital to shareholders through dividends or share repurchases and cancellations..

South Korea's amended Commercial Act: a one-year cancellation rule

South Korea's amended Commercial Act took effect on March 6, 2026, upon promulgation. As a rule, newly acquired treasury shares must be cancelled within one year of acquisition. For shares already held before the law took effect, companies were granted a six-month grace period followed by a one-year cancellation window. The amendment restricts the long-standing practice of keeping treasury shares indefinitely for later use.

Samsung's KRW 15 trillion repurchase was intended for employee share-based compensation. That purpose may qualify for an exception to the cancellation requirement, but retaining and subsequently distributing the shares is still subject to the legally prescribed shareholder-approval process.

DEEPER DIVEOther terms you may encounter in buyback filings

Funding and purchase methods - Under Korea's Commercial Act, buybacks generally must be funded within the amount of distributable profits. Shares must be purchased on an exchange or offered for purchase from shareholders on equal terms. Both Samsung and SK hynix used the exchange.

Daily purchase-order limit - A company buying shares directly discloses a daily ceiling on purchase orders. Samsung's disclosed limit was 7,321,653 shares per day.

Trust-based buyback - Instead of purchasing shares itself, a company can enter into a trust arrangement with a brokerage or bank to buy shares on its behalf. The reporting requirements and rules differ from direct purchases.

Cancellation without capital reduction - Cancelling shares purchased from distributable profits without reducing the company's registered share capital. The share count falls, but legal share capital remains unchanged.

Material Event Report (Treasury Share Acquisition Resolution) - The filing made when a company decides to repurchase its shares. It sets out the planned quantity and value, the period, purpose, method and daily order limit.

Treasury Share Acquisition Results Report - Filed after the repurchase is completed. It shows the actual number of shares acquired, total cost and average purchase price.

Exchangeable bond (EB) - A bond that gives its holder the right, under specified conditions, to exchange it for shares. The amended Act prohibits issuing bonds exchangeable for, or repayable with, a company's own treasury shares.

Higher EPS alone does not prove that continuing shareholders own more value.
Consider the cash spent and the price paid, then check whether the repurchased shares were cancelled or retained.

Four things to check in a buyback filing

0101 Will the shares be cancelled?

Look for the "Purpose of Acquisition" field. SK hynix planned to cancel the shares; Samsung planned employee stock awards. Shares reserved for compensation are not permanently eliminated; they may later be transferred to employees.

0202 How big is it relative to the company?

SK hynix planned to acquire 24.07 million shares, about 3.3% of shares issued; Samsung planned 53.29 million, or about 0.9%. Compare the repurchase amount with each company's market capitalization. Relative size matters when comparing buybacks across companies.

0303 By when, and how much was actually bought?

Check the acquisition period and the results filing. Samsung bought roughly 56.86 million shares, more than planned; SK hynix bought roughly 23.69 million, fewer than planned. Both programs targeted a cash amount, not a fixed final share count. The number initially disclosed was estimated using the share price around the board resolution and changed with actual execution prices. Once repurchases end, the recurring buy orders disappear as well.

0404 Has a separate cancellation filing appeared?

Buying shares and cancelling them are distinct disclosure events. On March 31, 2026, Samsung resolved to cancel treasury shares acquired in 2025, worth KRW 14.5806 trillion. SK hynix completed its October 21 repurchase, but the cancellation step remained outstanding. A "Resolution on Share Cancellation" filing is needed to confirm that the issued share count has actually been reduced.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONSStatements that sound right, but are not
MYTH 01
"A buyback means the share price will rise."
SK hynix spent roughly KRW 40 trillion buying stock over two months, yet its share price ended October 21 at 22% below the pre-announcement close. A buyback represents one source of buying demand. All buy and sell orders together determine the market price.
MYTH 02
"EPS rises only after shares are cancelled."
Treasury shares are excluded from the share count used for EPS. The effect begins as shares are repurchased. Cancellation prevents those shares from returning to circulation.
MYTH 03
"Every repurchased share gets cancelled."
Samsung's KRW 15 trillion program was intended for employee compensation. The amended Act permits exceptions subject to shareholder approval. Read the stated purpose before drawing conclusions.

QUICK GLOSSARY
Treasury shares (treasury stock)
Shares repurchased and held by the issuing company. They have no voting or dividend rights while held.
Acquire / hold / dispose / cancel
Buy the shares; keep them in treasury; sell or transfer them; permanently retire them.
Cancellation without capital reduction
Cancellation of shares purchased from distributable profits without reducing registered share capital.
Open-market repurchase
Buying shares on the stock exchange through regular market orders.
Trust-based repurchase
A buyback carried out through a brokerage or bank under a trust arrangement.
Other corporations (KRX category)
Domestic non-financial corporations in Korea's investor-flow statistics. Listed-company buybacks are included.
Net buying
Purchases minus sales when the net amount is positive; negative amounts indicate net selling.
Earnings per share (EPS)
Net income divided by the relevant share count, excluding treasury shares.
Shareholder returns
Cash or capital returned to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases/cancellations.
Treasury-share holding/disposal plan
A plan required to retain treasury shares under an exception to the cancellation rule, subject to shareholder approval.
Gap in buying demand
A situation in which a large, consistent buyer leaves the market, removing a recurring source of demand.
CASE FILES
Aug 19, 2026 - SK hynix's KRW 40tn buyback

The original plan covered 24.07 million common shares (about 3.3% of issued shares) and was sized at KRW 40 trillion based on the previous close of KRW 1,662,000. All shares were intended for cancellation after open-market purchases. The company also revised its shareholder-return policy from "up to 50%" to "at least 50%" of cumulative free cash flow over 2025-2027. The repurchase finished on October 21, with 23,693,272 shares acquired for KRW 40.0043 trillion (the approved limit). That was 376,728 shares below the initially disclosed quantity. Cancellation had not yet occurred.

Aug 21, 2026 - Samsung's KRW 15tn buyback

Samsung initially planned to acquire 53,285,968 common shares for employee stock-based compensation, including performance incentives and special performance awards. Its daily purchase-order limit was 7,321,653 shares. From August 24 to October 6, it bought 56,856,305 shares for KRW 14.9999 trillion at an average price of KRW 263,823. Afterward, treasury shares held by the company represented 2.34% of issued shares.

Mar 31, 2026 - Samsung resolves to cancel KRW 14.5806tn in shares

The decision covered 73,359,314 common shares and 13,603,461 preferred shares acquired following board resolutions on February 18 and July 8, 2025. Registered share capital was not reduced.

Mar 6, 2026 - Amended Commercial Act takes effect

Passed by the National Assembly on February 25 and promulgated on March 6, the amendment generally requires cancellation of newly acquired treasury shares within one year. Previously held shares receive a six-month grace period, followed by a one-year cancellation period.

Q1-Q3 2026 - KRW 61.7522tn in listed-company share cancellations

According to CEO Score, 391 companies cancelled more than 2.5 times the KRW 24.091 trillion cancelled during all of 2025. Samsung led with KRW 17.0722 trillion.

Jul 2022 - Three-month suspension of daily buyback limits

From July 7 through October 6, a temporary regulatory measure removed the usual daily purchase-order ceiling for treasury-share acquisitions. Under normal rules, daily orders are subject to a cap.

HOW TO READ THESE FIGURES

Other corporations versus company buybacks - The KRX "other corporations" category includes trading by non-financial companies other than the issuer itself. SK hynix's KRW 38.49 trillion in net buying under that category and its KRW 38.8154 trillion in gross repurchase spending are close, but not identical. The company's own purchases are already included in the category: adding the two would double-count them. And because one is a net flow while the other is gross purchases, their difference should not be treated as the net purchases of unrelated corporations.

Measurement periods - Reported investor flows can cover slightly different intervals. This article uses August 20-October 19 (39 trading sessions) for SK hynix and August 24-October 6 (29 sessions) for Samsung.

SK hynix final volume and expenditure - The final figures are based on reporting that cites the exchange filing of October 21. The investor-category data in the quiz and bar chart end on October 19, so the adjacent buyback figure also uses repurchases through that date (KRW 38.8154 trillion). The approximate average price of KRW 1,688,000 is calculated here as total spending divided by shares acquired.

Average repurchase price versus today's share price - A market price below the company's average buyback price does not create an accounting mark-to-market loss on treasury stock. Treasury shares are deducted from equity rather than recorded as assets, and gains or losses on dealings in the company's own shares are not recognized in profit or loss. Economically, however, paying too much may still harm continuing shareholders. That is a separate assessment.

Share price movements - This article does not attribute share price changes during or after the buybacks to the purchases or their completion. The wider market also moved substantially over the period.

Samsung's planned purchase: about 0.9% of issued shares - The ratio uses the planned 53,285,968 shares and an inferred issued share count. The denominator was derived from press reports stating that 136,727,099 treasury shares held after the buyback amounted to 2.34% of issued shares.

Company A simulator - All figures are hypothetical. Operating-business value is fixed, and interest earned on cash is assumed to be zero. The KRW 100,000 per-share intrinsic value is solely an illustrative assumption.
