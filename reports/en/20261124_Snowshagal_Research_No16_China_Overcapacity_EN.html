Where Does
China's Surplus Output Go?

Autos, steel and chemicals:
The world's trade barriers are closing in on South Korea, too.

Cars, steel and chemical feedstocks that China cannot sell at home are flowing into world markets. Importing countries are responding with tariffs and quotas. Some measures apply regardless of origin, leaving Korean-made goods facing the same barriers. This report follows three industries to see which side of the wall South Korea stands on.

Introduction

December 1:
The EU's New Gate

Hover over underlined terms (tap on mobile) for definitions. Red dashed underlines mark key concepts. Expand boxes marked + for more detail.

On Tuesday, November 24, the European Commission published a provisional measure in the Official Journal restricting imports of hybrid vehicles. From December 1, country-specific quotas will apply for 200 days. Imports above quota will not be permitted.⁠1

The measure was prompted by Chinese-made hybrids. The EU had already agreed with China on an annual cap of 350,000 vehicles. But the legal instrument is a safeguard. Unlike anti-dumping measures targeting particular countries, safeguards generally apply regardless of origin. Hybrids made in South Korea face the same gate.⁠2, 3

  • Meanwhile, in steel Japan's provisional hot-dip galvanized steel anti-dumping duties on imports from South Korea and China expire December 7; the investigation deadline is December 12.⁠5, 6 In October, the Korea Trade Commission recommended anti-dumping duties on Chinese galvanized cold-rolled steel.⁠7
  • Inside South Korea BYD rose to fourth in South Korea's imported-car market in September, behind Tesla, BMW and Mercedes-Benz.⁠8

These three developments reflect the same underlying trend. We begin inside China, then trace where its cars, steel and chemicals go and how import barriers affect Korean companies. With the US already imposing high tariffs on Chinese cars and steel, it is a less accessible destination for China's surplus output; this report covers it only in relation to barriers facing Korean steel (Chapter 03).

CHAPTER 01
Inside China

Weak Demand at Home,
More Exports Abroad

China's production capacity has not shrunk in line with domestic demand. Some output that cannot find buyers at home is exported instead.

  • Weak domestic demand China's retail sales grew just 0.4% YoY in October, while automotive retail sales fell 21.4%. Real estate investment fell 21.6% in Jan–Oct (National Bureau of Statistics, Hanwha Investment & Securities and Eugene Investment & Securities).⁠10, 11
  • Support is waning Under China's trade-in subsidy program, consumers receive subsidies for replacing old vehicles. Support for 2026 ends at year-end; an extension into 2027 has not yet been announced. Kiwoom Securities expects a domestic recovery to remain difficult in 2027, with surplus inventories finding their way into exports.⁠12
  • Export support is also falling China will cut its export VAT rebate for NEVs from 13% to 9% on April 1, 2027. Battery export rebates will be abolished on January 1. Because the export date determines eligibility, exporters have an incentive to bring shipments forward through March.⁠13, 14
  • Foreign brands are losing ground Chinese brands' share of the domestic passenger vehicle market rose from 51% in 2022 to 74% in Jan–Oct 2026 (Kiwoom Securities).⁠12 Hyundai Motor and Kia had a combined 0.6% share of the Chinese market in September (Hana Securities).⁠8
  • Not all exports are surplus Some cars are made specifically for overseas demand. Chinese manufacturers' pricing and product competitiveness also matter. A recovery in China's domestic market would not necessarily reduce export pressure proportionately.
A note on the numbers CPCA vs. CAAM; exports vs. overseas sales
  • The 8 million vehicles (+74%) figure is CPCA's passenger vehicle export total, covering complete vehicles and CKD kits. Some reports give 7.946 million (+74.6%); these refer to the same data. The China Association of Automobile Manufacturers (CAAM) uses a different scope; the two series are not mixed here.
  • The 2026 trade-in subsidies were not confirmed until December 31, 2025 (Xinhua).⁠17 No announcement before year-end does not necessarily mean the scheme is ending. Kiwoom Securities assumes it will end. NEV purchase tax relief ends in 2028.
  • Exports measure shipments leaving China, not necessarily actual retail sales abroad (discussed in Investment Insight No.33).
  • The three utilization figures refer to different periods and sources. They are not directly comparable in magnitude; they illustrate the same broad pattern of spare capacity.
CHAPTER 02
Autos

Where Do 8 Million Vehicles Go?
South Korea on the Receiving End

The EU is the largest destination. South Korea accounts for just over 3% of China's exports, yet more than one in three imported cars sold there came from Chinese factories.

Europe: A New Cap

The EU agreed with China to cap Chinese-made hybrid imports at 350,000 per year and set country-specific quotas on November 24 under its provisional measure. Countries accounting for more than 2% of EU imports in 2025 received an allocation about 15% above their 2025 volume.⁠1 The same rules affect the two countries differently.

  • China must cut volumes The quota is 28% below recent imports. Chinese-origin vehicles must have an export quota certificate issued by the China Chamber of Commerce for Import and Export of Machinery and Electronic Products (CCCME) to use China's allocation.⁠1
  • South Korea has less room to grow Current volumes can fit within the quota. South Korea's Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources (MOTIR) also expects limited near-term impact, but some opportunities to increase exports may be constrained (News1).⁠18 Any imports above quota are barred. A new model launch or concentrated shipments could exhaust a quarterly allocation.
  • Local production is outside the cap Hyundai Motor and Kia manufacture a substantial share of the hybrids they sell in Europe at European plants, including those in Czechia and Slovakia. The Commission noted that Chinese-origin vehicles had eroded the EU market shares of Japanese- and Korean-origin vehicles.⁠1 Restrictions on Chinese imports could ease competition for vehicles made at European plants. For a separate discussion of local production shares, see Investment Insight No.33.
  • A route around the quota Chinese automakers are also establishing local production in Europe. Leapmotor began production in Spain in October; Chery and BYD are preparing to operate plants in Spain and Hungary, respectively (SK Securities).⁠19 Surging exports have also strained pure car and truck carrier (PCTC) capacity. An estimated 25–30% of China's deep-sea vehicle exports are shipped in containers (iM Securities).⁠20

South Korea: Chinese-Made Cars Are Already Here

  • Made in China ≠ Chinese brand Tesla vehicles made in Shanghai still account for the bulk of Chinese-origin imports into South Korea. According to Reuters, South Korea received about 20% of Tesla Shanghai's vehicle exports in Jan–Sep.⁠24
  • BYD sells without subsidies BYD registered 20,137 vehicles in Jan–Sep and ranked fourth among imported brands in September.⁠8 In October it fell to fifth, with 1,594 registrations, down 39% MoM (The Korea Economic Daily).⁠22 It was excluded under the EV subsidy eligibility assessment introduced in July and has been ineligible for central government subsidies since the second half. Shanghai-made Teslas remain eligible (EToday and Kiwoom Securities).⁠23, 12
  • Domestic brands' position Hyundai Motor and Kia's combined domestic market share fell to 72.2% in Jan–Oct from about 76% in 2023–24. In EVs alone, their share was 54.1% (MTN).⁠21
  • 2027 subsidy threshold The government has announced plans to lower the vehicle price threshold for full EV subsidies from below KRW 53m to below KRW 50m in 2027.⁠25 Final guidelines have not yet been issued.⁠26 If the threshold falls, subsidies for vehicles priced between KRW 50m and KRW 53m would be halved, narrowing the effective purchase-price gap with ineligible Chinese brands.
  • The next entrants Kiwoom Securities estimates that if XPeng, Chery and others enter in 2027, Chinese brands could account for 10–15% of South Korea's EV market.⁠12
What barriers has South Korea erected against Chinese vehicles? Tariffs, subsidies, tax credits and trim prices
  • Tariffs Chinese-origin passenger cars face an 8% basic tariff. Passenger cars were excluded from tariff elimination under the Korea–China FTA (PIIE).⁠27 Japan imposes no import tariff on finished vehicles from China (Kiwoom Securities).⁠12
  • Subsidy eligibility Companies that fail the EV subsidy provider assessment are ineligible for both national and local subsidies. BYD and Zeekr are excluded (Kiwoom Securities).
  • Production tax credits Japan grants a per-vehicle tax credit for domestically produced EVs. In South Korea, a bill to include EVs under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act has been introduced in the National Assembly (Kiwoom Securities).
  • Subsidy threshold and trim prices The impact varies more by trim price than by brand. The base trims of the Ioniq 5 (range: KRW 47.35m–61.5m) and EV3 (KRW 39.95m–48.95m) are below the threshold, as are the base Shanghai-made Tesla Model 3 (KRW 46.99m) and Model Y (KRW 49.99m). Kiwoom Securities sees the threshold favoring Chinese-origin vehicles because many key Hyundai Motor Group EVs sell above KRW 50m.⁠12
  • These three measures do not exclusively target China-made vehicles. They support domestic production directly or determine subsidy eligibility through after-sales service and other criteria.
CHAPTER 03
Steel

The Wall Stands in
South Korea's Way, Too

South Korea is erecting barriers against Chinese steel while also facing barriers imposed by other countries.

  • China faces two to three years of restructuring The China Iron and Steel Association expects excess supply to push the steel industry into a two- to three-year restructuring period (Hana Securities).⁠16
  • Exports have barely fallen China exported 95.5 million tonnes of finished steel in Jan–Oct, down just 2.3% YoY. That is nearly 10 million tonnes a month.⁠28 The association said exports of semi-finished steel rose substantially this year. Finished steel data alone may understate overall export pressure (Hana Securities).⁠16
  • South Korea is a major destination In Jan–Oct 2025, South Korea was the second-largest destination for Chinese steel after Vietnam (6.51 million tonnes).⁠29

Barriers South Korea Has Erected

  • Support for domestic steelmakers At the time of the HRC ruling, the government expected domestic shipments to increase by more than 1 million tonnes annually and domestic market share by about 8.9 percentage points (Dailian).⁠34 Domestic HRC distribution prices reached KRW 920,000 per tonne in the second week of November, up 15% year to date (Hana Securities).⁠16 The contribution of anti-dumping measures to that price rise cannot be isolated.
  • Higher costs for steel buyers The same barrier puts upward pressure on input costs for domestic automakers, appliance manufacturers and shipbuilders that buy HRC and galvanized steel. Stronger steel prices benefit producers but can squeeze downstream industries.

Barriers Facing South Korea

  • The EU cites global overcapacity After its steel safeguard, in force since 2018, expired in June, the EU introduced a new steel regime in response to global overcapacity.⁠49 The EU-wide duty-free quota fell 46%, from 33.82m to 18.35m tonnes.⁠39 China has no FTA with the EU and cannot use the FTA-partner quota (Seoul Shinmun).⁠50
  • Exposure to Japan South Korea exports 300,000–400,000 tonnes of galvanized steel to Japan annually, worth about KRW 300bn (SBS Biz).⁠43
  • Only US-bound volumes rose South Korea's steel exports fell across all regions outside North America in Jan–Oct (Europe −9.2%, Southeast Asia −8.6%); shipments to the US alone rose 92% by volume. US HRC prices rose 64% year to date while Chinese HRC prices fell 3%, leaving US sales profitable even after a 50% tariff (Eugene Investment & Securities).⁠51
  • US producers want a higher barrier The US industry has requested a quota on Korean steel, with at least 50% tariffs retained even within quota. That is more restrictive than the EU steel regime's duty-free quota (Sep 28 letter from eight organizations).⁠47 According to Eugene Investment & Securities, some Korean steelmakers cut US-bound shipments after the first half.
Notes on the steel figures Duty ranges, destination rankings and domestic demand
  • Duty rates vary by exporter. Companies with price undertakings agree not to sell below a stipulated price in lieu of duties.
  • EU steel: South Korea can also access 1.475m tonnes of shared, first-come, first-served quotas beyond its dedicated allocation: 908,000 tonnes for FTA partners plus 567,000 tonnes including common WTO-member quotas. MOTIR calls the new regime the 'new steel measure'.⁠49, 40
  • Japan's 55.3% maximum includes Chinese producers. For Korean exporters, rates are 29.2% for POSCO, 30.6% for Dongkuk CM and SeAH CM, and 38.0% for most others (SBS Biz).⁠43
  • South Korea's second-place destination ranking refers to 2025. The sources reviewed do not give a 2026 ranking.
  • Domestic demand: Eugene Investment & Securities cited a Construction & Economy Research Institute of Korea forecast of 0–3% growth in construction rebar and bar demand and 2–4% in structural sections in 2027.⁠52
CHAPTER 04
Chemicals

Less Capacity,
Stronger Pricing

In chemicals, production capacity matters more to pricing than trade barriers. Products for which capacity additions have stopped are gaining pricing power; those facing continued expansion remain under pressure.

  • Another big year for capacity additions BloombergNEF (BNEF) projected global ethylene capacity additions of 14.6 million tonnes in 2026, about twice the five-year average, with China accounting for 56% (February forecast, SunSirs).⁠53
  • More capacity is coming Hana Securities expects ethylene and propylene chain capacity additions to continue in 2027–28, delaying a meaningful earnings recovery for naphtha cracking centers (NCCs).⁠15
  • A counterexample: petroleum products China is not exporting surplus supply across every product category. Its petroleum product exports fell 28% MoM in October. Shandong's independent refiners, cut off from discounted crude, saw their utilization rates fall to 54.7% by mid-November (Hana Securities).⁠54 When cheap feedstocks disappear, Chinese plants cut output too. For more on excess oil supply, see Investment Insight No.37.

South Korea Has Started Cutting Capacity

Under BNEF's forecast, China alone will add about 8.2 million tonnes of ethylene capacity this year.⁠53 South Korea's cuts are unlikely to lift global ethylene prices immediately. The aim is to bring costs down to sustainable levels, rather than engineer a market rebound.

  • Daesan No.1 H&L Advanced, formed by combining Lotte Chemical's Daesan NCC with HD Hyundai Chemical, launched on September 1. Ethylene capacity falls from 1.95m to 850,000 tonnes, while capacity utilization of the remaining facilities rises from about 80% to 100% (CEO Score Daily).⁠57 The goal is to idle overlapping capacity and run the remaining facilities at full utilization, lowering unit costs.
  • Yeosu No.1 The plan is to idle Yeochun NCC (YNCC) plants No.2 and No.3 for three years and combine Lotte Chemical's Yeosu plant with the remaining operation after a vertical demerger.⁠61 Government approval was granted July 20 (announced July 22). The target is to launch the integrated company in H1 2027.⁠59, 62 No disclosure of the demerger decision had been issued as of November 24.⁠63
  • Shaheen adds 1.8 million tonnes S-OIL's Shaheen project in Ulsan adds 1.8 million tonnes of annual ethylene capacity. Its completion ceremony is scheduled for November 25, with phased start-up from January 2027.⁠64, 65 Subtracting this from the 2.49 million tonnes of approved cuts leaves a net planned reduction of 690,000 tonnes.
  • Adding capacity is not necessarily a disadvantage If new plants have lower costs than existing NCCs, the expanding producer may be better positioned to survive. Hana Securities sees lower crude and naphtha prices improving Shaheen's cost competitiveness.⁠15 The government plans to determine the scale of Ulsan capacity cuts after observing Shaheen's start-up (The Public).
  • An example of low-priced Chinese imports South Korea's imports of Chinese ethyl acetate, a solvent used in coatings and adhesives, rose 83% from 30,500 tonnes in 2023 to 55,900 tonnes in 2025. The average import unit value fell 23%, from about $880 to $680 per tonne. China supplied 99.8% of South Korea's ethyl acetate imports in 2025 (World Bank WITS).⁠66, 67, 68
  • Chemicals face trade barriers too The Korea Trade Commission opened a new anti-dumping investigation into Chinese ethyl acetate in August. In September it began a review to determine whether to extend duties imposed since 2022 on Chinese polyester fully drawn yarn (FDY). Final rulings in both cases are due in H1 2027.⁠69 Conversely, the EU imposed definitive anti-dumping duties of 5.2–7.5% on South Korean ABS.⁠70
Notes on the chemical figures Net capacity cut, breakeven and import share
  • The 690,000-tonne net reduction is a simple comparison of approved capacity cuts with new nameplate capacity, not a change in actual output.
  • Shaheen's actual earnings contribution depends on initial utilization and market conditions (Hana Securities).
  • An NCC spread above the estimated breakeven is a signal of improving industry conditions, not proof that individual companies are profitable at the operating level. Costs differ by plant. In November, Dubai crude fell 20% while basic petrochemical product prices fell just 6% (Yuanta Securities).
  • The 99.8% ethyl acetate figure is a share of import volume, not of total domestic consumption. The EU's ABS anti-dumping duties reach 21.7% for Taiwan.
BOX A Fourth Industry: Shipbuilding
  • Competition for Orders, Not Import Barriers Shipbuilding faces few import barriers of this kind, so competition takes place in new orders. China accounted for 77% and South Korea 15% of global newbuild orders in Jan–Oct, measured in compensated gross tonnage (CGT, adjusted for shipbuilding complexity) (Clarksons Research, via Kiwoom Securities).⁠71
  • China is adding shipyard capacity Chinese shipyards' scheduled deliveries in 2027–28 are about 40% above 2026, while South Korea remains at a similar level (Kiwoom Securities). SK Securities estimates that China accounts for 75% of announced global shipyard expansions and reactivations since 2021. It sees this as a key determinant of merchant shipbuilding profits beyond 2028.⁠72
  • Fewer orders ahead Kiwoom Securities expects merchant ship orders to fall 20–30% in 2027 versus 2026. If orders shrink, price competition among expanded shipyards could intensify.

Which Side of the Wall
Does South Korea Stand On?

Whether China's overcapacity helps or hurts a Korean company comes down to where that company sells: inside a protected market, or across its trade barriers.

Inside Domestic steel. Anti-dumping duties curb Chinese imports and support prices.
Outside Autos and steel bound for the EU, and steel bound for Japan. Korean-origin products face barriers too.

The first two checkpoints are December 1, when EU hybrid quotas take effect, and December 12, Japan's galvanized steel investigation deadline.

Glossary
Safeguard
An emergency import restriction used when a surge in imports causes or threatens serious injury to a domestic industry. It generally applies regardless of origin, subject to exemptions, often through quotas or additional duties.
Anti-dumping duty
A duty imposed after an investigation finds that imports priced below normal value (often the exporter’s home-market price) cause injury to the importing country's industry. Rates are specific to the origins and exporters covered.
Quota
A limit on import volume over a specified period. Imports above quota may be prohibited or face a different tariff. In-quota imports are not always duty-free. EU steel is duty-free within quota and subject to 50% duty above it. The EU's provisional hybrid safeguard prohibits imports above quota.
NCC spread
The difference between prices of basic petrochemical products, such as ethylene and propylene, produced by a naphtha cracking center (NCC) and the cost of naphtha feedstock. An indicator of industry conditions, not actual operating profit: labor, electricity and depreciation are not deducted.
Provisional measure
A temporary measure imposed before an investigation concludes. EU provisional safeguard measures last no more than 200 days.
CPCA
China Passenger Car Association. Publishes monthly passenger car retail, wholesale and export data. Exports include complete vehicles and CKD kits.
CKD
Completely knocked down: export of unassembled vehicle kits for assembly at the destination.
Trade-in subsidies
A Chinese government program subsidizing replacement of old vehicles and appliances with new ones.
New energy vehicle (NEV)
China's classification covering battery electric vehicles, plug-in hybrids, range-extended EVs and hydrogen fuel cell vehicles. Conventional hybrids are excluded.
Penetration rate
The share of newly sold vehicles belonging to a particular category (here, NEVs).
Capacity utilization
Actual output as a share of maximum production capacity.
Crude steel
Steel at the initial production stage, before further processing into finished steel products. The benchmark measure of steel output.
Price undertaking
An exporter commits not to sell below an agreed price instead of paying an anti-dumping duty. Duties may apply if the undertaking is breached.
Hot-dip galvanized steel
Steel sheet or coil coated by immersion in molten zinc to resist corrosion. Widely used in construction materials and appliance components.
Ethylene
A basic petrochemical feedstock produced, among other methods, by cracking naphtha. Used to make plastics, including polyethylene. Plant size is often measured in ethylene capacity.
Vertical demerger
A Korean-style corporate division (muljeok bunhal) in which a business is transferred to a new company wholly owned by the original company, rather than distributing the new shares to its shareholders.
EV subsidy eligibility assessment
A screening system introduced in July 2026 to determine which companies qualify for EV subsidies. Criteria include contribution to domestic industry and after-sales service capability. Companies that fail are ineligible for both central and local subsidies.

Differences between sources

  • Chinese passenger vehicle exports CPCA's Jan–Oct exports appear in reports as either '8 million vehicles (+74%)' or '7.946 million (+74.6%)'. These are the same series; this report uses the former convention.
  • EU hybrid cap 350,000 is above last year's actual volume (about 300,000) but about 56% below this year's estimated 800,000. Whether the cap represents an increase or a reduction depends on the comparison base (see No.33).
  • EU quota for South Korea Some reports citing MOTIR give 67,942. The quarterly allocations sum to 67,924 (30,566 + 31,245 + 6,113), which is the figure used here.
  • Vehicles in transit The implementing regulation defines imports by the customs declaration for release for free circulation (recital 115). It includes no explicit exemption for vehicles in transit. This report does not presume how individual customs cases will be handled.
  • South Korean export volume and value The 107,104 vehicles in 2025 used as the quota base come from EU data; the $2.42bn value is from the Korea International Trade Association.
  • EU steel and hybrid vehicles Both quota systems generally apply regardless of origin, but the EU steel regime is a new tariff-rate quota measure replacing the safeguard that expired in June 2026, whereas the hybrid measure is part of a WTO safeguard investigation. Steel above quota faces a 50% duty; hybrids above quota cannot be imported.
  • Quotas versus recent imports The −28% for China and +25% for South Korea compare the 200-day quotas annualized over 365 days with trailing 12-month imports (Table 2 of the implementing regulation). Against actual 2025 imports of 107,104, South Korea's increase is 15.7%. China's −56% compares the cap with estimated 2026 imports of 800,000. Annualization is for comparison, not an allocation under any definitive measure after 200 days.
  • Tariffs within the EU hybrid quota The previously applicable customs duties still apply within quota (Article 1(4)). Korean-origin vehicles benefit from the Korea–EU FTA rate, which is why Korean news reports describe it as a 'duty-free quota'.
  • US industry quota request A minimum 50% duty within quota is a proposal contained in the September 28 letter from AISI and seven other organizations. The letter does not specify a rate for imports above quota. This is not a US government decision.
  • Chinese export destinations The Kiwoom Securities chart uses China Customs data for Q1–Q3, whose coverage differs from the CPCA export series.
  • 2027 EV subsidy threshold Kiwoom Securities describes the KRW 50m threshold as if finalized. The government announced a plan to lower it starting in 2027 earlier this year; the industry considers it not yet final. This report describes it as a proposed threshold.
  • EU steel quota South Korea's dedicated allocation totals 2.073m tonnes across 30 product categories (2.057m tonnes in 14 categories with a market share of at least 5%, plus 16,000 tonnes in the other 16 categories). Some articles report only 2.057m. The previous 2.581m tonnes is the eighth-year allocation under the expired safeguard; the comparison spans two regimes.
  • Start date of provisional steel bar duties Eugene Investment & Securities states that duties apply 'from November'. The Ministry of Finance and Economy administrative notice of Nov 13 proposed duties for Dec 1–Mar 31, 2027, with comments due Nov 23 (Mysteel). This report follows the notice. No final notice was located as of Nov 24.
  • Steel bars and special steel bars The Korea Trade Commission's official product name is 'steel bars' (carbon and alloy steel bars excluding stainless steel). News and industry sources call the same case 'special steel bars'. Import statistics through Nov 15 may not match the investigated product scope exactly.
  • US quota proposal Eugene Investment & Securities attributes the proposal to AISI. The late-September letter was co-signed by AISI and other steel industry groups and unions.
  • Japan's galvanized steel duty rates Japan's Ministry of Finance published a range of 29.2–55.3%. The range for Korean exporters alone is 29.2–38.0%.
  • NCC spread Yuanta Securities gives $204 for Q3 in the report text and $202 in its table. The figure uses the text value. November's $337 is through Nov 13, not the full-month average.
  • Total ethylene capacity cuts Daesan (1.10m tonnes) + Yeosu No.1 (1.39m tonnes) = 2.49m tonnes. Kuki News reports 'about 2.5–2.6m tonnes' for the two deals.
  • Period of idling The government-approved Daesan (1.10m tonnes) and Yeosu No.1 (1.39m tonnes) plans both provide for three years of idling (Newsis and New Daily). References to idled capacity in this report have that meaning.
  • Ethyl acetate import unit value WITS import value divided by volume ($26.84m ÷ 30,500 tonnes in 2023; $38.05m ÷ 55,900 tonnes in 2025). The unit value can vary with the product mix and freight costs.
  • Trade-in subsidies Kiwoom Securities assumes the program ends on Jan 1, 2027. As China did not confirm its extension last year until year-end, this report says '2026 support ends; extension into 2027 remains undecided'.
  • Chinese economic indicators Hanwha and Eugene Investment & Securities report Jan–Oct manufacturing investment at −2.9%, while Kiwoom Securities gives −7.3%. This report excludes the manufacturing investment figure.
