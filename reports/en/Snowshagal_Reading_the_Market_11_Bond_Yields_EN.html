AUG 27, 2026 · ACTUAL MARKETWhat happened on the day of the hike?

On August 27, the Monetary Policy Board (MPB) raised the Base Rate to 3.00%, its second consecutive monthly hike. The vote was 6–1. Board member Hwang Kunil wanted the rate to remain at 2.75% and cast a dissenting voteAn opinion or vote against the majority decision. The BOK's policy statement identifies the dissenting member and the rate they preferred. against the increase.

Judging by the survey alone, the decision was a surprise. The three-year yield rose immediately after it was announced. What came next changed the direction of trading.

The 79% figure is the share of survey respondents who expected a hold. It is not a 79% probability of no change implied by bond prices.

The Monetary Policy Decision statementThe document published alongside the BOK's rate decision. It explains the rationale and outlines the intended direction of policy.issued that day no longer said it was necessary to maintain the rate-hiking stance. Instead, the Board said it would decide the timing and pace of further increases as new economic data came in.

At the press conference, Governor Hyun Song Shin said the median of Board members' six-month-ahead Base Rate forecasts was 3.25%. He stressed the need to assess the effects of two consecutive hikes and indicated that any subsequent increases were likely to be gradual.

The BOK was not the only force pushing yields down. Government bond yields in the United States, Australia and New Zealand also fell, as did oil prices. Still, multiple news accounts described the same sequence: an immediate jump after the rate decision, followed by a reversal once the statement and press conference were digested.

If the Base Rate went up,
why didn't market yields rise with it?

The Base Rate takes effect now. A three-year KTB yield reflects expectations about short-term interest rates over the next three years. Those expectations, however, are already embedded in bond prices before the announcement. What moves yields on decision day is the gap between what investors had priced in and what the central bank actually delivers.

BEFORE THE ANNOUNCEMENT

Most survey respondents expected a hold. But traders were also wary of a second consecutive hike.

THE DECISION

The BOK delivered a surprise hike. Three-year yields jumped first.

STATEMENT, DOT PLOT, PRESS CONFERENCE

Further hikes remained possible, but fears of an especially rapid or aggressive tightening cycle eased. Three-year yields reversed direction.

Financial News attributed the subsequent buying to a perception that the risk of further tightening was less severe than feared. Hyundai Motor Securities analyst Lee Hwa-jin said bonds turned higher as the governor's remarks were seen as dovishA stance that places relatively more weight on economic growth and favors smaller rate increases, holding rates steady or easing. The opposite is hawkish..

THE SHORT ANSWER
Bond yields reflect expectations about the future path of rates. Markets react when that expected path changes.
That's why the wording of the statement and the governor's explanation can matter as much as the rate decision itself.
02HOW POLICY WORKS

What the BOK
Actually Publishes

Policy day brings more than one number. Alongside the decision come a statement, any dissenting votes and an explanation from the governor. Four times a year, the BOK also publishes its own rate-projection dot plot.

The picture changes as maturities get longer. A rate covering three years must account for expected interest rates throughout that period, not just today's rate.

ON THE BOARD · DIFFERENT TIME HORIZONS

The Base Rate is set for today.
The 3-year KTB yield reflects the next three years.

The three-year KTB yield reflects the expected path of short-term rates and a term premiumThe extra yield investors may demand for holding longer-maturity debt and bearing the risks that arise over time. When yields fall, bond prices rise

An existing fixed-rate bond has a set coupon. If market yields fall, that coupon becomes more attractive relative to newly issued bonds, so the bond's price rises. If yields rise, the reverse happens. That is why bond-market reports often use 'yields fell' and 'bonds rallied' to describe the same move. It explains why bond prices were described as stronger on August 27.

The dot plot: looking six months ahead

dot plotA chart of Monetary Policy Board members' six-month-ahead Base Rate projections. First published in February 2026, it represents conditional assessments, not policy commitments.The BOK introduced its dot plot in February 2026. Each of the seven members submits three dots, for a total of 21. Members may place all three at the same rate or distribute them among different rates to express their views of the range of possible outcomes. The BOK publishes it in February, May, August and November, when it also releases its economic outlook.

In August, 16 of the 21 dots sat above the prevailing 3.00% Base Rate. That does not mean 16 policymakers wanted another hike, or that the probability of a hike was 76%. The 21 dots were submitted by just seven Board members, three apiece.

Why did a higher dot plot look less hawkish?

The August dot plot was higher than May's. But the BOK had also lifted the Base Rate from 2.50% to 3.00% in the meantime. Compare the median projection with the rate prevailing at each meeting, and the picture changes.

In May, the median dot stood 0.50 percentage point above the then-current Base Rate. In August, the gap was 0.25 percentage point. But the two projections concern different future dates, and the BOK had hiked twice between them. The smaller gap alone does not prove that fewer hikes were coming. Contemporary reporting instead interpreted the dot plot and the governor's remarks as less aggressive than investors had feared. Financial News reported growing expectations for a more gradual pace after the median came in at 3.25%. The market's question shifted from whether the BOK would deliver two consecutive hikes to how much further it would go, and how quickly. To understand why bond yields fell despite an upward shift in the dots, you have to compare the announcement with expectations beforehand.

Hawks and doves

Two labels appear frequently in central-bank coverage. A policymaker who puts greater weight on controlling inflation and favors higher interest rates is described as hawkishA stance that gives relatively more weight to inflation risks and favors raising interest rates or keeping them elevated.; someone more concerned about growth who favors fewer hikes or cuts is dovish. The decision itself and its forward guidance can point in different directions. A hold accompanied by warnings of future hikes is a 'hawkish hold'; a hike accompanied by signals of a slower path can be called a 'dovish hike'. The August 27 press conference was widely interpreted as dovish.

GO DEEPERWays to gauge what markets have priced in

OIS: An overnight index swap exchanges payments linked to an overnight rate for fixed-rate payments over a specified period. A six-month OIS rate can serve as a rough gauge of the expected average overnight policy-rate path, and therefore of expectations around upcoming decisions.

3-MONTH MSB: The yield on a three-month Monetary Stabilization Bond issued by the BOK. It embeds substantial expectations about policy rates over the next three months.

MONETARY POLICY BOARD MEMBERS: Seven members, including the governor and senior deputy governor, with the governor serving as chair. The other five are appointed by the president upon recommendations from designated bodies.

MEETING MINUTES: The published record of what Board members said and the arguments they made. Minutes come out later, not on decision day, and provide more detail about the reasoning behind the vote.

THE ANSWER FROM PART TWO
A BOK decision comes with a statement, dissenting votes and the governor's explanation.
Markets respond to whichever parts change what investors previously expected.
03PUTTING IT TO WORK

How to Read
Decision Day

The Monetary Policy Board meets eight times a year. After the October 22 meeting, the next is scheduled for November 26. What should you check first in the headlines? These four questions apply just as well to the October 22 decision.

Four checks for any BOK decision story

01Did the decision match expectations?

Compare pre-meeting surveys and brokerage previews with the outcome. In August, 79% of surveyed bond-market professionals expected a hold, but the BOK hiked. The bigger the surprise, the more likely yields are to react sharply immediately after the announcement.

02How many members dissented?

A dissent favoring a hike at a hold meeting can be taken as a signal of a possible increase next time. In its October preview, Kiwoom Securities called a hike dissent a 'policy signal pointing to renewed tightening in November.' If one dissent was already expected, it conveys relatively little new information.

03Did the last paragraph of the statement change?

In August, the wording about needing to maintain a rate-hiking stance disappeared, replaced by language about deciding the timing and pace of additional hikes. What was taken out can matter just as much as what was added.

04How did the initial reaction compare with the close?

On August 27, the three-year KTB yield initially rose by about 8bp after the decision but finished the day 6.1bp lower. If you look only at the close, you may miss what the market was reacting to during the session.
COMMON MISREADINGSPlausible, but wrong
MISREADING 01
'When the central bank hikes, all market yields rise.'
On August 27, the Base Rate rose, but three-, five- and ten-year KTB yields all fell. Government bond yields reflect expectations for the future path of short-term rates as well as a term premium, not just today's policy setting. So yields can fall even on the day of a hike.
MISREADING 02
'When rates rise, stocks fall.'
The KOSPI fell 6.37% on the July 16 hike day, but the selloff had begun in the morning before the decision. On August 27, when the BOK raised rates again, the index rose 1.53%, supported by semiconductor stocks following Nvidia's earnings news. A rate hike alone cannot explain the day's stock-market direction.
MISREADING 03
'The dot plot is a promise about future rates.'
Each member's three dots express a conditional distribution of rate projections. If inflation or growth deviates from forecasts, the dots can change. They are not commitments to hike at a particular meeting.
EPISODE 11, IN ONE SENTENCE

The Base Rate is the rate the BOK sets today. Bond yields also reflect expectations for the future path of interest rates.

Markets therefore respond not simply to the rate decision, but to what it changes about expectations already priced in.

Quick glossary of related terms
Base Rate
The policy interest rate set by the BOK and used as a benchmark in its transactions with financial institutions.
Monetary Policy Board (MPB)
The BOK's highest monetary-policy decision-making body; seven members, eight scheduled rate-setting meetings a year.
Call rate
The interest rate on very short-term interbank borrowing, usually overnight; generally trades near the Base Rate.
Korean Treasury bonds (KTBs)
Government-issued bonds. The three-year KTB yield is a benchmark market interest rate.
bp
0.01 percentage point. A 6.1bp move equals 0.061 percentage point.
Monetary Policy Decision statement
The document released with a rate decision, explaining its rationale and the intended policy direction.
Dissenting vote
A vote against the majority; the policy statement identifies the member and the rate they preferred.
Dot plot
A chart containing three six-month-ahead Base Rate projections from each of seven Board members; published in February, May, August and November.
Hawkish / dovish
Hawkish: prioritizing inflation and tighter policy. Dovish: placing relatively more weight on growth and less tightening.
Hawkish hold
Leaving rates unchanged while signaling that hikes may still be coming.
Bond rally
A rise in bond prices, equivalent to a fall in yields.
Term premium
Extra yield required for holding longer-maturity debt; one component of longer-term bond yields alongside expected short-term rates.
Dovish hike
Raising rates while signaling a less aggressive future path.
OIS
An interest-rate swap exchanging overnight-index-linked payments for fixed payments over a specified period; often used to gauge rate expectations.
Real-world examples
AUG 27, 2026 · SECOND STRAIGHT HIKE

Base Rate: 2.75% → 3.00%. Vote: 6–1 (Hwang Kunil favored keeping it at 2.75%). In a Korea Financial Investment Association survey two days before the meeting, 79% expected a hold and 20% a hike. The three-year KTB yield jumped about 8bp immediately after the decision before closing at 3.755% (−6.1bp); the ten-year closed at 4.238% (−5.0bp). The statement dropped language about maintaining the hiking stance. The August dot-plot median was 3.25%. KOSPI closed at 6,912.37 (+1.53%).

JUL 16, 2026 · A HIKE AMID A SELLOFF

Base Rate: 2.50% → 2.75%, the first increase since January 2023. The KOSPI opened down 4.45% at 6,960.50 before the rate decision, triggering a sell-side trading curb (sidecar) at around 9:10 a.m. It closed at 6,820.60 (−6.37%). The governor said he did not entirely agree with attributing the stock-market drop to the rate hike.

FEBRUARY 2026 · BOK DOT PLOT DEBUTS

The BOK extended its rate-outlook horizon from three months to six months and introduced three dots per member, 21 in total. The chart is released alongside economic forecasts in February, May, August and November.

Important qualifications when reading the data

WHY YIELDS FELL ON AUGUST 27: Reports confirm that yields initially jumped after the decision, then reversed after the policy statement and press conference. Overseas government bond yields and oil prices also fell that day. We do not apportion the 6.1bp decline among individual causes.

AUGUST 26 YIELDS: Calculated backward using the August 27 close and the reported daily changes.

GOVERNOR'S REMARKS: Reports vary slightly in their wording, so the substance is paraphrased rather than presented as a direct quotation.

79% AND 93%: Shares of respondents in Korea Financial Investment Association surveys, not market-implied probabilities derived from bond prices.

MATCH THE SURPRISE TO THE OUTCOME: The exercise shows the pressures from the rate decision and subsequent guidance separately. It does not add them to calculate a net direction or assign probabilities or magnitudes to actual moves.

MAY VS. AUGUST DOT PLOTS: Each chart projects six months ahead, so their target dates differ. The gap between the median and the then-current Base Rate is presented for comparison; it is not a forecast of the total amount of further tightening.

We leave unverified figures blank rather than fill them in.
