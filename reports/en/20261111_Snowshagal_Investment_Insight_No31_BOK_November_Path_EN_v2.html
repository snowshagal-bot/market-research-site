INVESTMENT INSIGHT NO. 31 · NOV 11, 2026

The KOSPI is rebounding after falling nearly 45% from its peak. But the indicators that matter to the BOK— exports and inflation—still point toward tighter policy. A November hike remains the base case; the real question is where rates peak. The bond market has already priced in an early end to the cycle. Getting the terminal rate right does not guarantee a bond-market profit.

SUMMARY · FIVE KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Stocks plunged, yet the data driving the BOK's decisions moved the other way. The dollar value of semiconductor exports jumped 259.4% year on year in October, and the BOK expects growth and inflation next year to exceed its August forecasts.
  2. A November hike is the consensus; the path afterward is not. iM, Daishin and SK Securities forecast a 3.50% peak in early 2027. KB Securities expects the BOK to stop at 3.25% after a November hike.
  3. The bond market has already priced in an approaching end to the cycle. KTB yields fell despite the prospect of more BOK hikes. As reported on November 8, interest-rate swap pricing suggested roughly one further hike after November.
  4. Three rate measures tell three different stories. The BOK's six-month rate-path dot plot(a conditional projection), the terminal policy rate(broker forecasts), and the three-year KTB yield(a traded market yield) are three different things.
  5. Getting the policy-rate path right is not the same as making money on bonds. Yields have already fallen, making bonds more expensive to buy. If the three-year KTB yield rises from 3.73% on November 11 to 4.00%, the bond price would fall roughly 0.8% in an illustrative calculation.

On November 6, a circuit breaker halted trading on the KOSPI. The closing index stood 44.5% below its June 22 peak.1 Our analysis of the selloff appears in NO.29. The index fell another 0.81% on November 9 to close at 5,018.85—the lowest close of the decline, 44.9% below its peak.2 Stocks then rebounded for two sessions, rising 4.39% on November 10 and 2.60% on November 11. The November 11 close of 5,375.36 was still 41.0% below the peak.3, 4

The bond-market picture looked very different. three-year KTB yield fell from 4.02% at the end of September to 3.633% on November 10.5, 6 But on November 11 it jumped 9.6bp in a single day to close at 3.729%. U.S. October inflation had come in above expectations, reviving talk that the Federal Reserve might raise rates by half a percentage point in December.7, 8 While stocks bounced, bond traders began repricing U.S. inflation and the risk of further rate hikes.

After the crash, one question dominated bond-market conversations: “Surely this is enough to make the BOK pause?”

A month ago, NO.13 asked whether rates would rise in October. The answer was a unanimous hold. Now there are two questions: At what level does the BOK stop tightening? And how much of that expectation is already embedded in bond prices.

What matters

  • The October hold was not a declaration that tightening was over. Minutes released on November 10 show most board members favored assessing the effects of the previous two hikes before deciding when and how quickly to tighten further. Some also warned that semiconductor-led growth could keep core inflation elevated for longer.18
  • Nor did the BOK precommit to a November hike. One member stressed the need to balance the case for tightening against the sectors left behind by the boom. Domestic-demand recovery was also described as gradual.18
  • The previous tightening cycle also peaked at 3.50%, in January 2023. SK Securities expects the current cycle to end at or below 3.50%, noting that the economy is less overheated and rates have already risen in advance.14

Stocks matter to the BOK.
But the selloff alone will not change its outlook.

The BOK is not ignoring the stock market. In October, the governor acknowledged a temporary, modest pullback in consumption caused by falling share prices. But he urged investors to focus on semiconductor prices and contracts, rather than share prices. Those underlying indicators have yet to turn.19

The numbers driving the BOK's decision

  • Exports. October exports rose 93.7% year on year to $115.28 billion; semiconductor exports surged 259.4% to $56.44 billion. These figures are measured by value, so higher memory-chip prices account for a substantial share. The timing of last year's Chuseok holiday, which fell in October, also inflated the comparison: average daily exports rose a smaller 76.0%.20 Exports in November 1–10 increased 111.9% to $33.46 billion. With half an additional working day, daily average exports were up 97.7%. Chip exports climbed 331.2% to $16.6 billion, or 49.7% of the total. Ten-day readings can be volatile.21
  • Memory prices and contracts. The DDR5 16Gb contract price rose from $40 in June to $50 in October, according to figures cited by iM Securities. SK Hynix said long-term agreements with 14 major customers covered more than half of its 2027–28 production capacity.12
  • Inflation. Headline CPI inflation slowed from 3.2% in June to 2.8% in October. iM Securities attributes much of that decline to supply-side factors such as oil prices. Yet core inflation(excluding agricultural products and petroleum) rose from 2.7% in September to 2.8% in October.12, 22, 23
  • Projections. At its October meeting, the BOK expected 2027 growth, headline inflation and core inflation to exceed their respective August forecasts of 2.9%, 2.3% and 2.5%. The governor projected that inflation would approach the 2% target in 2028.10, 24, 25 IBK Securities argues that tightening remains defensible as long as inflation stays above target, even if it moderates.26

Consumption has indeed weakened: retail sales fell 1.0% quarter on quarter in Q3, while private consumption declined 0.2%. But June's spending rise had been boosted by temporary factors, including purchases brought forward ahead of an automobile excise-tax change; the subsequent reversal also mattered. iM Securities says the slowdown cannot be attributed solely to falling stocks.12 KB Securities, by contrast, worries about a negative wealth effect: lower equity valuations could erode household financial assets and spending.17 October employment data released November 11 showed a net gain of 139,000 jobs, yet the youth employment rate fell 1.7 percentage points year on year to 42.9%.27 More evidence is needed before concluding that the export boom is spreading broadly to domestic demand and employment.

iM Securities' core-services inflation series tracks demand-sensitive service categories (2.13% in June; 2.18% in October). It suggests underlying pressure has not eased; it does not prove overheating has intensified, nor is it directly comparable with the 2% headline inflation target.

Bond prices, however, tell a different story. KTB yields fell even as analysts maintained that the BOK could hike again. That raises the question that matters to investors: how much of an approaching end to the hiking cycle is already priced into bonds?

The bond market has already
bet on an early end to hikes.

Bond prices rise when yields fall. To see how far investors have brought forward the expected end of rate hikes, look at swaps, spreads among one-, two- and three-year KTB yields, and the implied average short-term rate.

How far ahead did the market move?

  • Swaps implied roughly one more hike (as reported Nov 8). Interest-rate swap pricing had previously reflected another 100bp of tightening. But KB Securities analysis reported on November 8 interpreted prices as implying just 27.5bp over six months and 31.5bp over nine months.17 That implied little tightening beyond a single November hike. U.S. inflation data and the subsequent November 11 yield jump may have changed the pricing. Swap rates also reflect term and liquidity factors, not just policy-rate expectations.
  • One-, two- and three-year yields converged. By November 6, the gap between one- and three-year yields had narrowed from 15.5bp to 2.5bp in just one week (see chart below). iM Securities interprets the convergence as the market already pricing the end of the hiking cycle.5
  • Vulnerable to U.S. surprises. When Korean yields spiked on November 11, the ten-year KTB yield also climbed to 3.950%. On the same day, the U.S. ten-year Treasury yield jumped 11.9bp to 4.98%.7, 8 Even a market priced for an imminent end to hikes can be pushed around by U.S. developments.
  • Working backward implies a 3.2–3.4% average short-term rate. iM Securities models the three-year KTB's fair-value yield as the expected average short-term rate over three years plus a 30–50bp premium.12 Applying that assumption to the November 11 yield of 3.73% implies an average short-term rate of roughly 3.2–3.4% over the next three years—below the 3.50% terminal rate forecast by several brokers. That could indicate a lower peak, but it could also reflect expectations of subsequent cuts or a narrower risk premium.

SK Securities illustrates the distinction: it forecasts the BOK holding the base rate at 3.50% through end-2027, yet projects an average three-year KTB yield of 3.45% in Q4 2027.14

Why did yields fall so quickly? SK Securities attributes much of the fourth-quarter rally to supply and demand. In October, foreign investors bought a net 129,000 three-year and 46,000 ten-year KTB futures contracts, while the government cut planned November KTB issuance by KRW 5 trillion. The KOSPI fell on four of the five days with the largest October yield declines.5, 14 If risk appetite returns, yields could retrace much of that move.

Foreign buying of cash bonds was less sustained. Of October's KRW 6.45 trillion in net purchases, 87% had three years or less remaining to maturity, and a single day, October 30, accounted for 38%. Investors then sold a net KRW 820 billion in November 2–6.5 They bought 11,000 three-year KTB futures contracts on November 10, then sold 8,684 on November 11.6, 7

Short-term funding sends a different signal

CP The 91-day A1-rated commercial paper yield stands at 3.82%, 82bp above the BOK base rate. One-month paper trades near the policy rate, leaving a spread of as much as 53bp to three-month paper. iM Securities interprets this not as a credit problem, but as reluctance to lock into maturities crossing the November policy meeting and year-end. MMF assets reached a record KRW 273 trillion.28 Short-term funding markets are still pricing in a November hike. iM Securities expects some of the extra yield premium to unwind after year-end, although expectations of a February hike should limit that reversal. Retail bank deposit rates are set separately from these wholesale-market benchmarks.

What would change the brokers' forecasts?

For a 3.25% terminal rate

iM Securities says further evidence would be needed: cancelled orders, cuts to customers' capital spending, declining export volumes, weaker consumption or employment, or a credit crunch.12

For a 3.75% terminal rate

iM Securities says export strength would have to spread to household consumption, employment and wages, with persistent pressure in services inflation and inflation expectations.12

Neither set of conditions is clearly in place yet. Major producers have not publicly disclosed meaningful order cancellations; long-term supply agreements have instead expanded. Nor is there clear evidence of a sustained spillover to wages and service prices.

SK Securities argues that since 1999, Korea has closely followed an aggressive U.S. tightening cycle only once, in 2022–23. The Fed raised rates by more than 500bp and USD/KRW climbed 10% during that cycle. This time, the won has moved in the opposite direction.14

Getting the terminal rate right
doesn't guarantee a return on bonds.

Three-year KTB yields need only rise to the midpoint of iM Securities' fair-value range (3.85%) for a newly purchased three-year bond to lose roughly 0.3% of its price. For loans, the first step is to check your contract.

Bonds

SK Securities favors holding three-year KTBs for income in the first half of 2027 (carry strategy), then adding longer-maturity bonds in H2 (extending duration). iM Securities prefers not to chase yields below its 3.70% fair-value floor, instead buying in stages if yields rebound.12, 14 By November 11, the three-year yield had risen back to 3.73%, just above that lower bound.7

Floating-rate loans

First, check which benchmark your loan uses—COFIX, CD rates or bank bond yields—and the next reset date. An increase in the BOK base rate does not lift every loan rate by the same amount on the same day. CD The 91-day CD rate is 34bp above the policy rate, compared with a historical average spread of 19bp. Loans linked to CDs already reflect some expected tightening.28

What could delay or overturn the conclusion?

1If credit conditions deteriorate first

In October, participation in A-rated corporate bond bookbuilding fell to 390%, down from 625% a year earlier.39 If that chill turns into a broader credit squeeze, the case for stopping outlined in Section 04 could strengthen before a deterioration appears in the real-economy data.

2If the Fed tightens harder

The next tests are the U.S. producer-price report on November 13 and the FOMC decision on December 10, Korea time. Fed funds futures favor a 25bp hike but still leave room for a 50bp move.8 If the Fed continues tightening beyond December, pressure on the won could increase, opening a conditional path to a 3.75% BOK terminal rate.33

3If export demand weakens

If semiconductor export growth slows or order cancellations emerge in the November 1–20 trade data due around November 21, that would strengthen the case iM Securities describes for a lower, 3.25% peak. Watch memory-chip contract prices as well.12

END-OF-LINE GUIDE

The stock-market crash alone may not stop a November hike.
The terminal rate is the real story.

The export data and growth outlook that matter to the BOK have not weakened, while inflation remains above its 2% target and core inflation pressures have not eased decisively. That is why a November hike remains the consensus despite the equity selloff. The real question is what comes next: does the cycle stop at 3.25% or 3.50%?

Bond markets have already put down an answer: 'almost over.' That makes predicting the terminal rate very different from profiting on bonds bought today. As November 11 showed, a single U.S. surprise can shift KTB yields by 9.6bp in a day. Three tests matter before November 26.

Glossary
Terminal rate
The peak policy rate at the end of the current tightening cycle; presented as the last stop on a route in this report.
Rate-path dot plot (conditional projections)
Each of the BOK Monetary Policy Board's seven members submits three dots for an appropriate policy rate six months ahead, reflecting a central scenario and upside/downside risks. One dot is not one vote. First published in February 2026, the projections appear in February, May, August and November.
Dissenting vote
A board member's vote against the majority decision. One member favored holding in August; there was no dissent in October.
Core services inflation
An iM Securities measure covering demand-sensitive service categories. It is not an official inflation series.
Core inflation
Korea publishes two series: excluding agricultural products and petroleum (October 2.8%; September 2.7%), and excluding food and energy (October 2.8%; September 2.8%).
Interest-rate swap (IRS)
An exchange of fixed- and floating-rate payments. The term structure of swap rates can be used to infer market expectations for future policy rates, subject to other premiums.
KTB futures
Contracts to buy or sell Korean government bonds at a predetermined future price. Heavy foreign buying can coincide with falling yields in the cash market.
Fair-value yield
A broker's estimate derived from its expected policy-rate path plus term and supply-demand premiums. iM uses the average short-term rate over three years plus 30–50bp.
CP⁠·CD
CP is commercial paper; CD is a negotiable certificate of deposit. Both provide benchmarks for short-term funding rates around three months.
Carry & duration
Carry is the income earned from holding a bond. Duration measures how sensitive a bond's price is to changes in yield.

Methodology
  • Implied average short-term rate: 3Y KTB yield of 3.729% (Nov 11) less iM's assumed 30–50bp premium = 3.23–3.43% (or 3.13–3.33% using Nov 10's 3.633%). This is a model-derived inference, not a direct observation of market expectations.
  • Bond price sensitivity: approximate percentage price change = −modified duration × change in yield. Assuming duration of 2.8, a move from 3.729% to 3.85% gives −2.8 × 0.121 percentage points ≈ −0.34%. Excludes coupon income, convexity (the error from a linear approximation for larger yield moves) and transaction costs.
  • Three-year KTB yield minus terminal-rate forecast: 3.729% − 3.50% ≈ 23bp; spread over the current base rate ≈ 73bp (Nov 10: 13bp and 63bp).
  • KOSPI drawdown: 5,059.69 / 9,114.55 − 1 = −44.5% (Nov 6); 5,018.85 = −44.9% (Nov 9, lowest close); 5,239.13 = −42.5% (Nov 10); 5,375.36 = −41.0% (Nov 11).
  • Dot-plot horizon: six months after the meeting. Aug 27 → approximately February 2027; Nov 26 → approximately May 2027.
