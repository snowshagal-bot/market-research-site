The KOSPI fell 21.6% from 7,080.92 on September 23 to 5,554.22 on October 15 (Snowshagal calculation).6 It closed at 5,768.01 on October 19.7 The Bank of Korea sets its base rate again on October 22. All four brokerage previews that explicitly address this meeting expect a hold at 3.00%. This note asks the harder question: could the stock-market selloff delay further hikes? We look at what the BOK did during a similar episode three months ago, what current market prices imply, the implications for bank earnings, and the counterargument offered by a strengthening won.

AT A GLANCE

  1. Every October-specific preview calls for a hold. The disagreements concern dissent (Hana sees a unanimous vote; Eugene considers a hike dissent unlikely; Kiwoom and Hanwha expect one) and the subsequent rate path. Hana and Hanwha forecast two more hikes, in November and February, taking the rate to 3.50%. The median of the BOK's August dot plot points to 3.25% six months out.
  2. The BOK has raised rates on a day when stocks were plunging. On July 16, the KOSPI fell sharply before the rate announcement, triggering a sell-side trading curb, and the BOK hiked that day. But the Board could not have considered the additional losses through July 30, which had yet to occur. By its next hike in August, equities had already rebounded substantially.
  3. Markets repriced the distant path more than the next move. From October 12 to 16, six-month OIS barely moved, while the one-year forward rate starting one year ahead fell 26 basis points. Hana interprets a roughly 20bp spread between the three-month Monetary Stabilization Bond yield and the base rate as pricing in an almost 80% chance of a November hike, but does not disclose its calculation. Turning a yield spread into a probability requires further assumptions.
  4. Third-quarter bank NIMs are expected to fall despite successive hikes. Banks have replaced low-cost deposits that flowed out with pricier time deposits and bank bonds. Even if the BOK holds in November, the repricing of loans following its July and August hikes should still feed through from the fourth quarter.
  5. The counterargument is the won. USD/KRW reached 1,330.3 on October 19, its lowest level since October 2024. Oil's contribution to inflation is also projected to decline toward year-end. Keeping policy restrictive and raising rates again are not the same decision.

PREVIEWS · SIX RESEARCH NOTES

One Hold Consensus, Different Fine Print

We compared six BOK-related publications from five brokerages dated October 16–19. The four that state an explicit October call all forecast a hold. The other two—annual outlooks from Hanwha and iM Securities—inform our comparison of the subsequent tightening path. The differences come down to three questions: Will anyone dissent in favor of a hike? Is the next hike in November? And where does the cycle end?

Most of the stated terminal-rate forecasts converge on 3.50%. Hana and Hanwha's 2027 outlook call for hikes in November and February; iM places them in Q4 and Q1 2027. At his August 27 press conference, the BOK governor explained the dot plot this way: 'The median is 3.25%, implying one further hike from here. But having already hiked twice, we need time to assess the effects.' He described the expected pace as 'gradual tightening.'14 Crucially, the dot plot is a conditional outlook: each member allocates three dots to reflect a probability distribution for future rates.15 It is not a pledge to hike at any particular meeting. Hana and Hanwha's annual outlook both project a move to 3.50% through November and February hikes—earlier than the dot plot's six-month horizon, and a larger cumulative increase than its 3.25% median.8, 12

PRECEDENT · JULY'S SELLOFF

What the BOK Did When Stocks Were in Free Fall

The market has been here before. On July 16, three months ago, the KOSPI plunged before the BOK's rate announcement. It opened at 6,960.50, down 4.45%, and a sell-side program-trading curb was triggered around 9:10 a.m.16 The BOK raised its base rate from 2.50% to 2.75% that day—the first hike since January 2023.2 The KOSPI closed at 6,820.60, down 6.37%.17 But the full 23.2% decline from July 15 through July 30, ending at 5,593.56, was not information available to the Board when it voted on July 16.18, 19 By the time the BOK hiked again to 3.00% on August 27, the index had recovered substantially, including a 17.9% one-day rebound on July 31.3, 20

Market prices need to be compared on a like-for-like basis. Six-month OIS rose 5.5bp during the July 15–30 rout. This time, it barely moved over October 12–16, while the 1y1y OIS forward rate fell about 26bp in the same week. The observation windows differ, but the pattern shows that near-term rate expectations and longer-dated pricing need not move together. Forward rates also reflect term premiums and positioning, not just the expected policy rate. Has the market abandoned a November hike—or kept the near-term hike on the table while marking down the path thereafter? Foreign investors bought a net 34,789 three-year KTB futures contracts on October 12, the largest single-day net purchase since 2005. Hanwha views the decline in forward rates as 'a price adjustment driven by the stock shock and market flows, not a change in the BOK's policy signal,' pointing to the governor's repeated commitment to restrictive policy during a parliamentary audit and an IMF discussion.11

'We should probably pay closer attention to semiconductor prices than to the share prices of semiconductor companies.'

BOK Governor Hyun Song Shin · July 16 press conference. At the same event, he said he did 'not fully agree' that higher interest rates were the reason stocks had fallen.21

By the measure the governor set out in July, semiconductor stock prices and chip-pricing prospects are now pointing in different directions. Hana expects Q3 DRAM prices to have held up better than anticipated and Q4 price increases to exceed its previous forecast. That is the opposite of the direction taken by Korean memory stocks, which have fallen back toward their late-June lows.22 Semiconductor exports reached $12.2bn in October 1–10, up 170.9% year on year. One important caveat: there were 5.5 working days, compared with 3.5 a year earlier—two additional days.23 Asked at an October 12 parliamentary audit whether the next hike would come in October or November, the governor replied that 'the most important factor is the inflation trend.'24

Three Channels Through Which a Stock Selloff Could Change the Rate Decision

Share prices are not themselves a BOK policy target. For an equity rout to alter the rate decision, it must spill over into financial stability, consumption, or the inflation outlook. Here is what the central bank and brokerages have said about those channels.

①FINANCIAL STABILITY

Governor, July 16: 'Unlike debt, equities have relatively few channels through which they create systemic risk.'25

Watch: margin calls and forced selling, credit stress, corporate bond and commercial-paper yields

②CONSUMPTION

Eugene Securities: the BOK has repeatedly argued that movements in Korean equities have a smaller effect on consumption than in other economies.9

Watch: consumer confidence and card-spending data

③INFLATION OUTLOOK

The two episodes are not directly comparable. By the August hike, the KOSPI had risen 23.6% from its July low to 6,912.37 (Snowshagal calculation). USD/KRW stood at 1,443.97 at the end of July—more than ₩110 above its current level.26 This time, equities remain close to their lows just before the meeting, and longer-dated rate expectations have also declined.

MARKET · PRICING THE PATH

How Much of a November Hike Is Priced In?

What if we look at prices instead of brokerage forecasts? Each instrument reflects a different part of the curve. The three-month Monetary Stabilization Bond and six-month OIS have not substantially erased expectations of a near-term hike. The 1y1y forward rate, by contrast, has repriced the path beyond it.

Hana wrote that the three-month MSB yield's roughly 20bp premium to the base rate 'reflects close to an 80% probability of a hold in October and a hike in November.' It did not provide the formula.8 Its own chart shows the spread above 25bp immediately before the July hike and around 10bp ahead of the August hike (values read from the chart).8 A higher yield spread does not translate one-for-one into an actual rate increase.

Converting that spread into a probability requires assumptions: how many days the higher policy rate would apply before the bond matures, the normal spread when no hike is expected, and the chances of changes at other meetings. A three-month yield also embeds maturity and liquidity premiums. The calculation below does not attempt to reconstruct Hana's methodology; it illustrates how different assumptions change the result.

Why a 20bp Spread Alone Cannot Establish the Probability of a November Hike · Snowshagal calculationAssumed normal spreads produce values ranging from 36% to above 100%

Suppose an MSB is bought on October 16 and matures 91 days later, on January 15, 2027. A 25bp hike on November 26 would affect only the last 50 of those 91 days, lifting the average base rate over the holding period by 25 × 50 ÷ 91 ≈ 13.7bp. Under this simplified model, the implied probability of a November hike is the observed 20bp spread less the normal spread (s), divided by 13.7bp.

The positioning data also need care. Hanwha estimates that foreign investors' net purchases of three-year KTB futures last week offset roughly 82% of their cumulative 78,000-contract net sales from the August 27 hike through October 8. Hanwha also notes that total open interest in those futures fell 5.4% from October 8, suggesting limited new long positioning.11 But open interest is a market-wide measure, while the 82% figure simply nets flows over a specified period. Neither establishes the outstanding size of foreigners' actual short positions. It would therefore be wrong to infer that only 14,000 contracts of buying capacity remain. Hanwha expects foreign buying to fade and yields to rebound if the BOK renews its tightening signal. The three-year KTB yield rose 2.3bp to 3.877% on October 19.27

BANKS · NET INTEREST MARGINS

Why Are Bank NIMs Falling During a Hiking Cycle?

Higher rates are usually considered good for banks. Yet many forecasts show net interest margins (NIMs) declining in the third quarter, despite two BOK hikes in July and August. All three brokerages surveyed expect a decline.

Ordinarily, margins improve first when rates rise. SK Securities reports that, as of July, floating-rate loans accounted for 70.5% of outstanding corporate loans and 56.7% of household loans. Loan rates typically reprice every three to six months, while rates on existing time deposits remain fixed until maturity and follow with a nine-to-twelve-month lag.28 That timing describes repricing under existing contracts. Even if a customer's time-deposit rate stays fixed to maturity, replacing departing low-cost deposits with newly issued time deposits or bank bonds immediately raises the average funding cost. The explanation for this cycle is that a shift in funding mix arrived before loan repricing could catch up.

The funding mix explains the Q3 squeeze. SK Securities argues that money moving into equities during Q2 depleted banks' core deposits, forcing them to replace that funding with time deposits and bank bonds. Banks issued roughly ₩12tn of bonds in July–August, while the yield on one-year AAA bank bonds rose from 3.743% at end-June to 4.071% on September 21.28 Hana estimates that September's monthly NIM slipped a further 2–3bp from August. It expects only Industrial Bank of Korea to report a Q3 NIM increase. Unlike commercial banks, IBK is exempt from the loan-to-deposit ratio requirement and has less reason to compete aggressively for replacement deposits through high-rate promotions.30

A new risk hangs over the hoped-for Q4 rebound. Following a series of cyberattacks on financial institutions in early October, demand deposits at the five largest banks fell by nearly ₩16tn in three business days. But the media reports summarized by Hana also note that demand deposits are typically volatile at the beginning of a month as payroll and corporate funds move, while money has also been shifting into equities and higher-yielding products. Not all the outflow can therefore be attributed to the attacks.30 Banks have lifted three-month deposit rates into the 3% range and launched promotional offers paying as much as 4%.31 These changes occurred after Q3 ended, so they cannot explain that quarter's NIM decline. They could, however, push Q4 funding costs higher.

That makes the projected Q4 rebound conditional. SK expects Q3 to mark the trough, with loan repricing lifting NIM from Q4 and, if there is one more hike in H1 next year, through at least that period. Yuanta expects a modest recovery as Q4 funding competition eases. Hana sees the question as whether the inflection comes in Q4 or Q1.28, 29, 31 As SK puts it, even at the same rate level in the 3% range, '3% on the way up and 3% on the way down can have very different implications for NIM.'28 Even if the next hike is delayed, the loan-repricing effect of July and August remains in the pipeline. The size and duration of any NIM improvement, however, depend on the subsequent rate path and competition for funding.

Bank stocks were telling a different story from the broader market. During the KOSPI's 14.5% loss over October 12–16, bank shares fell just 2.9%. Hana sees defensive appeal in that outperformance, but cautions that a prolonged market downturn could still compress bank valuation multiples.31 Looking at banks only as beneficiaries of higher rates misses half the picture. According to reports compiled by Hana, Korean banks had ₩165tn of outstanding mortgages due for interest-rate resets in Q4, ₩21tn more than in Q3.30 Higher rates also raise borrowers' debt-service burdens. Alongside NIM, investors need to monitor credit costs, capital ratios, and capacity for shareholder returns.

COUNTERARGUMENT · THE WON

Could a Stronger Won Push the Next Hike Back?

The strongest counterargument to the prevailing hold-in-October, hike-in-November view is the currency. A stronger won reduces import-price pressure and gives the BOK less reason to rush.

USD/KRW touched a new year-to-date low intraday on October 16 and stood at 1,331.4 at the 3:30 p.m. reference close. On October 19, onshore daytime trading ended at 1,330.3, the lowest since October 2, 2024.7, 32 According to iM Securities' calculations, the won depreciated 7.6% in the first half but appreciated 13.8% in the second half through October 12.33 The currency strengthened even as foreign investors sold Korean equities heavily last week. Hanwha speculates that some proceeds from equity sales may have shifted into won-denominated bonds.11

There are disinflationary forces on the price side, too. Assuming Dubai crude averages $80 a barrel, Hanwha estimates that petroleum products' contribution to headline CPI inflation will decline from 1.0 percentage point in October to 0.2 point in December, then run at 0.2–0.3 point in January–February 2027. It expects oil to exert upward pressure on inflation through Q1, then to contribute to disinflation through base effects.12 In September, headline CPI rose 2.9% year on year; petroleum products were up 14.8%.5 Won strength and the declining year-end contribution from fuel could justify delaying the next hike. Skipping November in favor of January or February would not necessarily conflict with the August dot plot's median of 3.25% six months ahead.

Two Distinctions That Matter in Assessing the Counterargument

GOVERNOR · IMF DISCUSSION, OCT. 13Welcoming Won Strength Is Not the Same as Calling for Another Hike

'There is room for the won to appreciate further, and further appreciation would be desirable.' At the same discussion, the governor said policy would remain restrictive until he was confident inflation would converge to target. He expected both headline and core inflation to stay above 2% for some time.34 Maintaining a restrictive stance can mean keeping the current rate unchanged. If won strength brings down import prices, it can reinforce earlier hikes and reduce the urgency of another increase.

HANWHA · 2027 OUTLOOK'No Reason to Cut' Is an Argument Against Easing, Not Proof of an Immediate Hike

Although Hanwha forecasts hikes in November and February, it argues that any negative oil contribution after Q1 would be 'entirely a base effect.' With inflation expectations in the mid-to-high 2% range, a positive output gap through 2027, apartment prices, and the Korea–U.S. rate differential, it sees 'no reason to cut rates whatsoever.'12 But not cutting and hiking at the next meeting are two different decisions.

The BOK operates its inflation-targeting framework over a medium-term horizon.35 If a decline in oil's contribution is reasonably foreseeable, the Board can incorporate it before the official data arrive. It may also choose to wait longer for the effects of earlier hikes without significantly revising its forecasts. Whether a temporary headline slowdown alone would justify that choice is another matter. In November, the issues will be whether core inflation and inflation expectations also ease, won strength persists, and the BOK can afford more time to assess its two recent hikes.

CHECKLIST · OCTOBER 22

Four Things to Watch Beyond the Hold

A hold is the shared forecast across the current brokerage previews. The market reaction may depend less on the decision itself than on how the implied path thereafter differs from expectations. On August 27, the BOK hiked, yet the three-year KTB yield closed 6.1bp lower at 3.755%. It had initially jumped roughly 8bp after the decision, then reversed as investors digested the statement and press conference.36 Markets trade not only the decision on the day, but also the size and pace of the tightening still ahead. Four signals will matter.

  1. How Many Members Dissent in Favor of a Hike?

    No hike dissents would match Hana's forecast (Eugene also sees the probability as low). One would align with Kiwoom and Hanwha. Kiwoom calls a dissent for a hike 'a policy signal foreshadowing the resumption of rate increases in November.' But if the market already expects one such dissent, it carries little new information on its own. In August, the dissent was in the opposite direction: Hwang Keon-il voted to hold.3, 10

  2. Does the Final Paragraph of the Policy Statement Change?

    The August statement said the BOK would 'determine the timing and pace of additional increases' in the base rate.3 Watch whether that formulation remains, and whether financial-market volatility or equity prices enter the list of risks the Board is monitoring.

  3. How Does the Governor Describe the Path Ahead?

    There is no new dot plot at the October meeting. The BOK publishes it alongside its economic projections in February, May, August, and November.15 At the press conference, listen for whether the governor retains August's 'one more hike' assessment, how he applies July's distinction between semiconductor stock prices and chip prices, what he says about the stronger won, and whether he signals a revision to November's inflation projections. Changes in the distribution of members' dots will become visible in November.14, 21, 34

  4. Which Part of the Curve Gets Repriced?

    Assess whether the statement and press conference raise or lower the expected path of further hikes relative to what was priced before the release. Then watch how that surprise is distributed across the three-month MSB spread (about 20bp), six-month OIS (3.30%), and 1y1y OIS forward rate (3.715%). Near-term and longer-term expectations can move independently. A hawkish tone does not require yields to rise at every maturity.8, 11

Delaying a November hike and lowering the ultimate level of rates have different implications for bonds and bank shares. If the timing alone slips, nearer-dated instruments such as six-month OIS may adjust first, while the loan-repricing effects of July and August remain intact. If the total tightening expected also falls, longer-dated instruments such as the 1y1y forward may decline further—and the magnitude and duration of bank NIM improvement may narrow. On October 22, the more revealing signal may be which part of the yield curve reprices after the announcement, rather than the words of the decision alone.

GLOSSARY · KEY TERMS
BOK DOT PLOT
A chart in which each of the seven Monetary Policy Board members assigns three dots to reflect their own probability distribution for the base rate six months ahead. First released in February 2026; published at the February, May, August, and November economic-projection meetings. These are conditional forecasts, not policy commitments.
DISSENTING VOTE
A Board member's position that differs from the majority decision. The policy statement identifies the dissenting member and their preferred rate.
MONETARY STABILIZATION BOND (MSB)
Debt issued by the Bank of Korea for liquidity management. Its three-month yield is sensitive to expected policy rates over the coming quarter.
OIS
A contract exchanging a floating overnight rate for a fixed rate over a set term. Six-month OIS broadly reflects the expected average short-term policy rate over that period.
ONE-YEAR FORWARD RATE ONE YEAR AHEAD (1Y1Y)
A one-year interest rate beginning one year from now, derived from the yield curve (the values in this article use Hanwha's OIS-based series). It contains both expectations of future policy rates and term premiums; it is not equivalent to a forecast of the terminal base rate.
SHORT COVERING
Closing a short position by buying back a previously sold contract. Aggregate net buying can include both short covering and new longs, which cannot be separately identified from net-flow figures alone.
NIM
Net interest margin: net interest income as a share of interest-earning assets.
REPRICING
The resetting of interest rates on existing loan or deposit contracts in response to market benchmarks, distinct from the cost of newly raised funding.
LOAN-TO-DEPOSIT RATIO RULE
A regulatory ceiling on loans relative to deposits. When deposits leave, banks may have to attract replacement deposits to maintain lending volumes.
SELL-SIDE PROGRAM-TRADING CURB ('SIDECAR')
A five-minute suspension of program-trading sell quotations triggered when KOSPI 200 futures decline at least 5% from the previous day for more than one minute.
BASE EFFECT
A mechanical change in the year-on-year growth rate caused by unusually high or low prices in the comparison period a year earlier.
OUTPUT GAP
The difference between actual and potential GDP, expressed as a percentage of potential output. A positive gap can indicate demand-driven inflation pressure.
