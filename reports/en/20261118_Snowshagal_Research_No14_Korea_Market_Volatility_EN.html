WHY IS KOREA'S
STOCK MARKET
SO VOLATILE?

An index dominated by two stocks.
Three layers of amplification.

Each selloff had a different trigger: war, earnings or regulation. This report examines what magnified those shocks. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix dominated the index. Above them sat three sources of potentially reinforcing flows: domestic leverage, offshore leverage and rules-based trading.

The three-layer framework is ours. Estimates of each layer's size and impact come from Daishin Securities and Yuanta Securities. A causal effect has not been established.

INTRODUCTION

Same good news.
Different market reactions.

Hover over underlined terms for definitions, or tap on mobile. Red dotted underlines mark key concepts. Use the + panels for additional analysis.

Nvidia beat market expectations in results released early on Nov 18 Korea time. Taiwan's TAIEX and Japan's Nikkei 225 each gained about 0.3% that day. The KOSPI rose 2.0%—roughly seven times as much.

  • Earnings Q3 revenue was $115.8bn, up 103% y/y; Q4 guidance was $130bn ±3%. Q4 includes an extra fiscal week and excludes China data-center revenue.3 Both beat consensus ($109.1bn for Q3; $125.3bn for Q4). Shares rose more than 4% after hours.4
  • Context The first results under an interim CEO after the founder stepped down on Nov 12.4
  • Flows Foreign investors sold a net ₩330bn of KOSPI stocks; institutions bought more than ₩500bn.5
  • Caveat This is a single session after two sharp KOSPI declines. Index composition also differs across markets. The data establish a larger Korean move, not its cause.

The pattern can reverse. On Nov 16, the KOSPI fell 3.6% while Taiwan's TAIEX lost 6.0%.1 On Nov 17, the KOSPI fell another 2.6%, closing below 5,000 for the first time in nine months.6, 7 Daily comparisons change with news and flows.

The full-year picture is different: KOSPI volatility was twice Taiwan's (Section 01). Moves above 10% occurred on March 4 (−12.06%), July 28 (−10.84%) and July 31 (+17.91%).8

What this report examines

Think of an audio amplifier. War, earnings and regulation supply the signal. The higher the gain, the louder the output. Several mechanisms could have amplified Korean market moves this year.

  • The base Two memory-chip stocks accounted for about half the index (Section 02).
  • Three layers Domestic leverage (03), offshore leverage (04) and systematic trading (05) can each generate extra trades, but at different times.
  • Limits and policy We examine the data gaps (06), policy responses (07) and indicators that might signal calmer markets (END).

01METER · THE SCALE

1.5× the 2008 level.
Volatility in numbers.

KOSPI swings this year exceeded those of the financial crisis—and most overseas markets.

  • 60.7% annualized volatility Calculated from daily KOSPI returns, the annualized volatility through Nov 6 was 3.7× the 2005–2025 median of 16.3%, and 1.5× the full-year 2008 figure of 39.2%.9 In daily terms, the standard deviation was about 3.8%, versus roughly 1% in a typical year and 2.5% in 2008. Moves of 3–4% became commonplace.
  • 40 days above 5% There were only 13 such days across the entire 2010–2025 period. July alone had ten this year.9
  • Twice Taiwan and Japan Measured over the same period, KOSPI volatility was twice the TAIEX and Nikkei 225, and even exceeded the U.S. Philadelphia Semiconductor Index.1
  • Research corroborates it The Korea Capital Market Institute measured first-half daily KOSPI volatility at 3.6%, up from 1.4% a year earlier. Across 36 major markets, the average barely moved, from 1.1% to 1.2%. This was not a global volatility shock of equal magnitude.10
Expand · The 40 extreme days, trading halts and drawdown speedTwo charts · Circuit breakers, sidecars, VKOSPI and Samsung volatility
  • Trading halts and the fear gauge Circuit breakers and sidecars temporarily interrupt trading during rapid moves. The options-implied VKOSPI measures expected future volatility. KOSPI sidecars were triggered three times in 2025 and twice in 2024.11
  • Speed The KOSPI fell 44.5% over 93 sessions from its June 22 peak to Nov 6—faster than the 1997 decline (109 sessions) or 2008 (240).14
  • Single-stock volatility Samsung Electronics moved more than 7% in a session twice in 2024, but 36 times through Nov 16 this year.15

Daily moves have three stages. This report focuses on the third.

02INPUT · THE BASE

Two stocks were half the index.
Volatility stayed high without them.

The index's composition is the starting point. Concentration is not the whole explanation.

Concentration rivalled Taiwan's

  • KOSPI Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix rose from 35.22% of index weight at the start of the year to 55.67% on June 22.22 They still accounted for about 46% on the morning of the Nov 6 circuit breaker.23
  • Taiwan The TAIEX's two biggest members, TSMC and MediaTek, represented 45.7% on Aug 31.24 Yet TAIEX volatility was only half the KOSPI's this year.

The difference lies in the industries

Memory chips are cyclical commodities whose prices move sharply with supply and demand. Memory makers' earnings and share prices are therefore more volatile than those of contract foundries.

  • The Korean pair Samsung and SK Hynix recorded their highest volatility since 2010.9
  • Not unique to Korea Memory makers worldwide saw large swings. TSMC, a foundry, recorded roughly one-third their volatility (Fig. 02-2).1
  • Korea's difference Its memory leaders were not uniquely volatile. Their high volatility simply accounted for half the index.

How much calmer without the two giants?

Index volatility reflects the weighted returns of its constituents. Independent moves offset; correlated moves compound. Samsung and SK Hynix moved closely together (2026 return correlation 0.82).10 ). Half the index therefore moved sharply in the same direction. What happens if both are excluded?

  • Fewer extreme days Stripping out their estimated index contribution reduces the number of days above 5% from 40 to 13–15.
  • Still unusually high The remaining stocks show estimated volatility around 44%. The KOSDAQ, which does not contain either company, recorded 54.3%.1
  • Research estimate The Korea Capital Market Institute roughly attributed one-third of the first-half rise in KOSPI 200 volatility to the two stocks.10
Concentration explains much, but not everything. The next question is what further amplified a memory-led move across the broader market.
03LAYER 1 · DOMESTIC

Buy more on the way up.
Sell more on the way down.

Domestic leverage is the first layer. Leveraged ETFs rebalance with the day's move near the close; margin and CFD accounts may sell when collateral runs short.

Why do leveraged ETFs trade near the close?

  • The rule A 2× ETF targets twice the underlying asset's daily return. After a rise, its exposure falls short of twice its higher NAV; after a decline, exposure exceeds the new target. The fund therefore buys into up days and sells into down days near the close. A 1% move implies rebalancing equal to 2% of NAV. This is known as leveraged-ETF rebalancing.9
  • Example A 2× ETF with ₩10tn in NAV must buy roughly ₩1tn more exposure near the close after a 5% gain in the underlying.
  • Caveat Actual orders differ. Funds can use futures or swaps, while inflows and redemptions change trading needs. Investors do not necessarily borrow to buy the ETF; the leverage is built into the fund's exposure.

How large did it get?

  • Assets NAV in domestic equity leveraged and inverse ETFs quadrupled in six months.9 Single-stock 2× ETFs launched on May 27 and peaked a month later.25
  • Rebalancing demand Daishin estimated demand equivalent to 5–8% of daily trading value in June–July. This uses NAV and daily returns—not actual executions.9
  • Why July was bigger Assets peaked in late June, but sharper index moves kept rebalancing demand higher in July.9

Did those trades move prices? Researchers disagree

Trading demand is not the same as price impact. Heavy buying at the close may have little effect if sellers are plentiful. The evidence supports different interpretations.

Amplification case · Daishin Securities
  • Daishin examined SK Hynix's closing call auction. During unrestricted trading in single-stock 2× ETFs, down-day declines deepened near the close, while up-day prices slipped in the auction.
  • Daishin argues this asymmetry could amplify downside moves, but acknowledges that the evidence is weak. Differences between periods were not statistically significant (t-statistics −1.3, −1.2).9
Inconclusive case · Research institutes and academics
  • The Korea Capital Market Institute said in July that 32 sessions since the launch of single-stock leveraged/inverse ETFs were too few for a verdict. It attributed much of the rise in volatility from May 27 to a clash of foreign/institutional selling against individual/broker buying.10
  • At a Nov 10 symposium, Seoul National University's Institute for Financial and Economic Research argued that volatility was already elevated before the funds launched and memory-stock shocks mattered more. Retail ETF buying and high-frequency trading (HFT) liquidity provision may have absorbed much of the down-day rebalancing pressure.26
Expand · Closing-auction evidence and caveatsOne figure · Up/down days, sample sizes and statistical limits

Using LSEG one-minute data, Daishin split SK Hynix closing-auction activity into three periods: before single-stock 2× ETFs, unrestricted trading, and post-restriction.9

  • Up days During unrestricted trading, shares gained another 0.18% in the last 30 minutes but fell 0.23% in the auction (t = −2.0); the auction's volume share was higher than before launch. Daishin suggests sellers absorbed the rebalancing purchases.
  • Down days Late-session losses were deeper than pre-launch, followed by a further 1.07% drop by the next open. That pattern disappeared after buying restrictions.
CaveatPrices also slipped in the auction before ETF launch on days up more than 2% intraday (Fig. 03-4). Period differences were not significant. Larger estimated rebalancing needs did not predict bigger auction declines. Each period had about 20 sessions, and the seller identity is unknown. Samsung, index leveraged ETFs and KOSPI 200 futures showed no clear period effects.9

The Korea Capital Market Institute viewed brokers' purchases during the flow standoff as largely ETF creations. Trading volume alone does not prove amplification; it recommended monitoring net flows, assets and intraday ETF premiums/discounts.10 The Seoul National University presentation favored policing spoof orders over restricting trades.26

Retail investors use two main forms of leverage: borrowing against shares through margin loans, or taking price exposure through contracts for difference (CFD). Both can trigger forced selling when collateral falls short.

  • Forced-selling mechanism When collateral is insufficient, brokers liquidate positions on a preset schedule. That is forced liquidation. If many accounts are sold on a plunge day, losses may deepen. In November the government temporarily waived maintenance margin requirements (Section 07).
  • Who trades CFDs? The Financial Services Commission reported that 96.5% of actual CFD investors were individuals in 2023.30 Total outstanding balances, including margin, reached a record ₩3.4177tn on April 23.31
Expand · Faster retail tradingDay trading, automated orders and revised cash-deposit requirements
  • Intraday churn Yuanta noted sharp simultaneous buying and selling by retail and foreign investors in individual stocks, alongside more same-day repayment of margin-financed purchases.21
  • Automated orders Trading through broker Open API interfaces also rose. At one broker, average daily Open API turnover jumped from ₩11bn in 2023 and ₩30.2bn in 2025 to ₩630bn in Aug 2026, Yuanta estimated from company disclosures.21

The mechanics are clear and potential volumes calculable. Their actual price impact remains disputed and statistically weak.
04LAYER 2 · OFFSHORE

Offshore leverage:
Losses can force broad deleveraging.

Some hedge funds use investment-bank derivatives for leveraged Korean equity exposure instead of owning stocks outright. This is the least observable of our three layers.

Equity exposure through contracts

  • The contract A hedge fund enters a total-return swap (TRS) with a prime broker. It receives share-price gains and pays losses without owning the underlying stocks. A relatively small margin deposit can support several times that exposure.
  • Whose name appears in the data? The prime broker may buy shares to hedge its exposure, although futures and other instruments are possible. Korean trading records then show the prime broker—not the hedge fund—as the foreign investor.21
  • Unknown share Not all offshore hedge-fund positions use TRS, and their share of Korean investment is not public.

RECAP · NO.32₩214tn sold by foreigners. Who was behind it?

Foreign investors sold ₩214tn of Korean listed equities in Jan–Sep (FSS, settlement basis). Accounts classified as 'U.K.' accounted for 46%. Yet IMF-reported Korean equity holdings of U.K. residents were only 0.53× the size of those accounts. Daishin interpreted the gap as possible hedge-fund trading routed through London prime brokers.9 The series have different reporting entities and coverage, so the gap does not prove the money came from non-U.K. investors. Monthly flows and methodology are detailed in Investment Insight No.32.

The math of 5× leverage

In the example cited by Yuanta from the U.K. Prudential Regulation Authority, a 20% margin supports 5× share exposure. A 12% stock decline consumes 60% of that initial margin.21

Forced selling can spread across a portfolio

TRS and CFD risks are often managed at the account or position level. A margin shortfall in one stock can force sales of other holdings. Yuanta says this can transmit a stock-specific shock to unrelated shares.21

Circumstantial evidence, not proof

  • Other stocks fell too Across the ten heaviest foreign-selling sessions from June 22 to Nov 6, almost all net selling targeted semiconductors (just 1.3% outside the sector). Yet 62% of non-chip stocks among the top 300 by market value declined. Daishin suggests margin calls on leveraged investors may have spread selling beyond chips.9
  • Oct 12–13 The KOSPI lost 3.7% and 5.5%. A Chinese DRAM product announcement was the reported catalyst, but power equipment, nuclear and robotics stocks also plunged. Yuanta judged it likelythat forced CFD/TRS liquidation contributed, without establishing causality.21
  • London turnover Many prime brokers operate through London entities. Trading value attributed to U.K. foreign accounts peaked in June, at more than 60% of all foreign turnover.21 Heavy activity does not prove that forced deleveraging drove the selloff.
Expand · London-linked trading and margin rulesOne figure · U.K.-linked turnover, HFT, dynamic margin and selloff data
  • Algorithmic orders Prime brokers offer TRS clients DMA and algorithmic execution. Five brokers earned ₩179.3bn in HFT commissions between Jan 2 and Aug 24; foreign-client revenue peaked in June.21 The counterargument—that HFT supplies liquidity—is covered in Section 03.
  • Flows shifting into Asia Yuanta suggests global quant trading may have migrated to Korea, Japan and Taiwan as China tightened DMA and HFT rules after a February 2024 quant-market slump.21
  • Margin requirements can jump faster Following the 2021 Archegos collapse, prime brokers began adjusting margin to underlying volatility and crowded positioning, known as dynamic margining. Yuanta expects margin calls to cluster on particular days, then selling pressure to fade quickly once positions are sharply reduced.21
  • Numbers from the selloff Yuanta cited foreign net sales of ₩2.02tn and ₩5.15tn on Oct 12 and 13, respectively.21
The size and pace of selling—and the sectors that fell together—are observable. No official statistics measure the forced liquidations on those dates.
05LAYER 3 · SYSTEMATIC

Sell as volatility rises.
The rules require it.

The third layer is rules-based trading. These funds need not borrow: their mandates can require them to cut equity exposure as volatility climbs.

  • The mechanism Volatility-targeting funds cut equity allocations as market volatility rises to keep portfolio risk near a target. A fund targeting 10% annualized volatility, for example, might halve its equity weight if volatility doubles. Unlike the first two layers, it can sell even on an up day.
  • Long-term funds can follow suit Pension and asset-allocation funds with risk budgets may also cut equities as volatility rises. Daishin suggests that short-term volatility forced longer-term funds to retreat, potentially deepening declines.9
  • Only U.S. market estimates are available Daishin attributed part of the Nov 5 U.S. selloff to mechanical selling by volatility-targeting funds and leveraged ETFs.14 All figures cited were U.S. market model estimates. Barclays projected that volatility-targeting funds might sell up to $135bn in U.S. equity futures over 24–48 hours.33 Nomura estimated roughly $47bn in leveraged-ETF selling, again for U.S. markets.14 Neither figure is confirmed execution volume. The subsequent selling in Korean equities is unknown.
  • Not all funds trade at once Lookback windows and rebalancing cycles differ by strategy.
  • It works in reverse too The same rules can trigger buying when volatility subsides. Systematic flows may become supportive in calmer markets.
Expand · Options-market signals and 'Why Korea?'One figure · Put/call skew, Yuanta's three conditions and their limits
  • What the options market says Yuanta reads the put/call skew as evidence that the market's dominant fear changed around July and has stayed that way.34
  • Why Korea? Yuanta described Korea as the only major market outside the U.S. combining open access to AI/semiconductor leaders, deep trading and single-stock futures/options for aggressive short exposure.34
  • A limitation Taiwan also lists TSMC single-stock futures and options. The 'only market' claim would require a comparison of liquidity and foreign access, which this report does not provide.
The mechanism is plausible, but this layer has the least Korean data. U.S. estimates cannot establish actual selling in Korea.
06MUTE · DATA GAPS

The label says 'foreign'.
The trader behind it is hidden.

Investor data identify who bought and sold. They do not show whether a trade was leveraged or whose capital ultimately stood behind it.

Four blind spots

A headline saying 'foreigners sold ₩1tn' cannot distinguish discretionary selling from forced liquidation. Four gaps make the three layers difficult to measure.

  • Was leverage involved? An offshore hedge fund's TRS trades can properly appear as foreign transactions. But the data do not disclose the leverage or whether a prime broker bought to hedge.
  • Retail-like capital After the July 31 deposit rule, trading by retail and foreign investors in single-stock leveraged ETFs both fell sharply. Yuanta sees the timing as evidence that some foreign flows may reflect retail-like short-term strategies.21
  • Country labels Official sources do not clarify how FSS 'U.K.' classifications relate to the account holder's nationality or the ultimate beneficial owner.
  • Market depth To demonstrate amplification, researchers would need order-book depth during shocks and the price impact of comparable-sized trades. This report lacks order-level and execution data.
Expand · How CFDs appear in investor statisticsBefore and after the September 2023 reform—and a discrepancy in broker data
  • Through Aug 2023 Even when the beneficial investor was retail, CFD trades routed via Korean brokers were recorded as 'institutional'; trades via foreign brokers as 'foreign'.30
  • From Sep 1, 2023 Equity trades arising from CFDs are recorded by the exchange under the underlying investor category (retail, institutional or foreign). Total CFD balances are published daily by KOFIA.35
  • Conflicting descriptions Yuanta wrote on Oct 14 that domestic CFDs routed through overseas prime-brokerage services (PBS) appear as foreign transactions.21 That differs from the September 2023 reform. This report follows the FSC notice. How offshore-routed CFDs are now classified requires separate clarification from the KRX or KOFIA.
Only domestic leverage is measured daily with reasonable precision. Our finding is therefore limited: plausible amplification mechanisms exist, and the circumstantial evidence is consistent with them.
07LIMITER · REGULATION

Korea targeted domestic leverage.

Researchers have also proposed reforms. The Korea Capital Market Institute recommended wider use of capped-weight indices, volatility interruptions calibrated to stock liquidity, block-trading platforms for institutions, and a deeper domestic long-term investor base. These are research proposals, not adopted policy.10

Short selling was not banned

Past temporary restrictions ran from Oct 2008 to May 2009, Aug–Nov 2011, and Mar 2020–Apr 2021. A comprehensive ban ran from Nov 2023 to Mar 2025.39 There was no ban in the latest selloff. Views are divided.

The case for a ban
  • Hana Securities viewed it as a practical way to prevent further declines while single-stock leveraged positions remained large.
  • Critics argue short selling adds pressure and increases volatility.
The case for caution
  • A ban could impede MSCI developed-market inclusion. MSCI downgraded Korea's short-selling assessment after the 2023 ban.
  • Whether bans reduce volatility is also disputed.
For context, KOSPI returns during previous bans were −3.6% in 2008–09, +5.89% in 2011 and +77.7% in 2020–21. Short selling accounted for about 7% of KOSPI turnover on May 26, 5% on June 25 and 6.4% on Aug 4.39

International approaches

Expand · India's SEBI order and the trading mechanism

First, a caveat: Daishin said public data cannot establish whether any specific manager pursued such strategies in Korea.9 India illustrates how derivatives can dominate cash-market exposure. It does not explain Korean losses.

The group bought ₹43.7bn net in underlying shares and futures in the morning, while adding ₹321.1bn in bearish options positions—7.3× the cash/futures purchases. Korea's response has focused on visible domestic leverage. It has not directly targeted offshore hedge-fund leverage or systematic strategies.

OUTRO · CONCLUSION

Amplification worked both ways.
Its size remains unproven.

Korean volatility was not confined to declines. U.S. megacap earnings sparked the July 31 rally, but estimated leveraged-ETF rebalancing demand also exceeded ₩8tn that day. The Nov 18 rebound likewise dwarfed neighbouring markets' response to the same news. The shocks came from outside, but the market itself had mechanisms capable of amplifying them. How much amplification they caused is still unknown.

Glossary
Leveraged-ETF rebalancing
Trading near the close to reset a 2× ETF's daily exposure. Buy after an up day, sell after a down day. Estimated amount = NAV × leverage × (leverage − 1) × daily return. Actual orders depend on futures, swaps and fund flows.
Prime broker
An investment-bank unit providing hedge funds with financing, stock lending, trading and settlement. Many operate through London entities.
TRS/CFD
Total-return swaps (TRS) and contracts for difference (CFDs) give price exposure without owning shares. Small margin deposits can support leveraged positions. Dealers often hedge by buying the underlying shares, but may use other instruments.
Margin calls and forced liquidation
Brokers sell collateral positions when margin runs short, often at a prescribed time. Portfolio-wide margining can spread selling from one stock to others.
Volatility-targeting fund
A rules-based strategy that adjusts equity exposure to keep portfolio volatility near a target: selling when volatility rises and buying when it falls.
Annualized volatility
Daily return standard deviation scaled to a year, usually daily standard deviation × √252.
Circuit breaker
A market-wide trading halt after an 8% or 15% decline (20 minutes), with the session ended after a 20% decline.
Sidecar
Pauses program-trading orders for five minutes if KOSPI 200 futures move 5% or KOSDAQ 150 futures 6% for at least one minute.
VKOSPI
An index of annualized 30-day expected volatility implied by KOSPI 200 options; often called Korea's fear gauge.
Closing call auction
Orders are collected from 3:20 to 3:30 p.m. and matched at one price, which sets the session close.
Correlation coefficient
A measure of how closely two return series move together: +1 means perfectly aligned, 0 means uncorrelated, −1 means perfectly opposite.
t-statistic
A measure used to assess whether a sample mean or difference could be due to chance. An absolute t near 2 is often a rough significance threshold.
Margin loan
Borrowing from a broker to buy shares, with the purchased stock serving as collateral.
Minimum account deposit
Cash required in the account before opening a position in certain high-risk products.
Open API
A broker-provided programming interface that allows investors to place orders automatically.
Dynamic margining
Margin requirements that vary with underlying volatility and position concentration rather than a fixed percentage; recalculated using market prices.
DMA
Direct Market Access (DMA) lets investors route orders to exchanges through a broker's infrastructure with minimal intervention.
High-frequency trading (HFT)
Rapid algorithmic order submission, modification and cancellation using dedicated trading systems.
Country classification in FSS statistics
The FSS publishes foreign portfolio flows by country. Positions classified as U.K. accounts are far larger than Korean equity holdings reported by U.K. residents, suggesting that the named account and ultimate risk-bearer can differ. Daishin interprets the classification as the location of settlement accounts, but that is its interpretation; official treatment and beneficial-owner identities remain unconfirmed.
Put/call skew
Difference between implied volatilities of put and call options. Relatively expensive puts suggest demand for downside protection; expensive calls may indicate demand to chase gains.
Liquidity-provider premium/discount limit
The gap between an ETF's market price and its NAV. Liquidity providers (LPs) are required to keep the discrepancy within a specified range.
Margin-loan collateral ratio
Collateral value divided by the loan amount. Brokers normally must maintain at least 140%; falling below that may trigger a collateral call or forced sale.
Sources, methodology and data differences

Definitions All dates are Korea time. Index returns are versus the prior close. 'Days above 5%' means absolute close-to-close changes of at least 5%. Annualized volatility = standard deviation of daily returns × √252.

Verification scope Broker figures are reproduced from their reports and have not all been independently recalculated. We did reproduce Daishin's 60.7% volatility and 40 extreme days from Yahoo Finance closes. Catalysts on major trading days follow contemporaneous reporting.

Cross-market comparison (Fig. 01-2) Yahoo Finance daily closes from the first trading day of 2026 to Nov 6, in local currencies and exchange calendars. This is not a full-year reading.

KOSPI excluding two stocks (Fig. 02-3) This is an approximation, not an official index. Starting with reported two-stock weights at the beginning of the year (35.22%) and June 22 (55.67%), we rolled the weights forward using daily stock and index prices and removed their estimated contribution to KOSPI returns. The resulting weights roughly match reports for July 15 (~52%) and Nov 6 (46–48%). Our estimated June 23 (−7.0%) and July 28 (−7.7%) ex-two-stock returns resemble Daishin's average declines for non-chip shares (−7.0%, −7.6%), but different methods prevent treating this as validation. Depending on starting weights, volatility ranges from 43.5% to 43.9% and the number of 5% days from 13 to 15. Preferred shares, new listings and capital raises were not fully adjusted for; actual error may exceed these ranges. Full-index 2008 KOSPI figures are not directly comparable.

Discrepancies between sources

  • Nvidia consensus Reuters quoted LSEG consensus of $109.1bn in Q3 revenue and $125.3bn for Q4. Other media/compilers put Q3 at $108.7–109.3bn. After-hours moves were reported as 4–5%, depending on the timestamp.
  • Nov 18 KOSPI flows Reports placed foreign net sales at ₩327.6–333.9bn and institutional net buying at ₩504.7–549.2bn, depending on outlet and time. The text rounds these figures.
  • CFD investor classification Yuanta (Oct 14) said domestic CFDs routed through overseas PBS appear as foreign trades. The FSC said that, from Sep 1, 2023, CFD trading would reflect the underlying investor's category. We follow the FSC statement.
  • Single-stock leveraged products Daishin's NAV series falls from ₩17.6tn on June 25 to ₩2.8tn on Nov 6 (ETFs; Hong Kong excluded). Saturday Economy reported market capitalization for 16 products falling from ~₩16.5tn on June 25 to ₩6–7tn in early October. Coverage and dates differ.
  • Margin loans Daishin's weekly report put balances 25.9% below peak on Nov 6; Yuanta measured ₩26.5tn on Nov 10 (−31.3%); news citing KOFIA reported ₩25.7728tn on Nov 12. The dates differ.
  • Peak CFD balance SBS Biz called ₩3.4662tn on Dec 30, 2025 an all-time high; Financial News called ₩3.4177tn (including margin) on April 23, 2026 a record. Treatment of deposits may explain the difference.31, 54
  • June 23 foreign net selling Korea Economic Daily cited roughly ₩6tn in KOSPI sales, versus ₩3.7tn in Daishin's table. Market coverage or settlement-versus-trade accounting may differ, so no amount appears in the body.
  • Two-stock volatility Daishin reported Samsung 82.4% and SK Hynix 97.6%; Yahoo Finance closes imply 83.4% and 98.4%. TSMC, Micron, Kioxia and SanDisk are based on Yahoo Finance closes in local currencies from the first 2026 session through Nov 6.
  • VKOSPI Daishin reported VKOSPI at 65.2 on Nov 6, Financial News at 65.17 at the close and SBS Biz at 64.74 intraday. The latter is not a closing value. Nov 13 (50.15) and Nov 16 (52.64) are KRED daily closes.13
  • Oct 12–13 foreign net selling Yuanta's −₩2.02tn and −₩5.15tn figures do not specify coverage or whether they are based on settlement or trade date.
  • TSMC weight StockFeel put it at 41.478% on Aug 31; Hana estimated about 43% on another date. Korean weights (June 22/Nov 6) and Taiwan's (Aug 31) are not same-date comparisons.
  • 20 ETF units versus 20 shares The FSC uses the ETF term 'units'; news reports say 'shares'. Both refer to the same rule.
  • 2008 volatility Daishin's 2008 figure is 39.2% versus 39.0% calculated from Yahoo Finance closes. Both give 60.7% for 2026 through Nov 6.
