When Should You Buy to Receive a Dividend?

Someone who bought Samsung on December 26 qualified for its year-end dividend.
An early-February buyer qualified for KB Financial's.
Both dividends related to 2025. Why the different deadlines?

01THE CASE

Different Dividend Recipients

First, a quiz. Every date in this example comes from an actual dividend timetable.

Investor A bought both Samsung Electronics and KB Financial on December 26, 2025, then sold both on January 5, 2026. Investor B bought both stocks on February 9, 2026, and sold them in early March. Who qualified for each company's 2025 year-end dividend?

Answer: Samsung: A; KB Financial: B

Dividend eligibility depends on whose name appears on the record dateThe date used to determine which shareholders are entitled to a dividend.in the company's shareholder registerThe company's official list of shareholders and their holdings.. Samsung's record date was December 31, 2025, so A's December 26 purchase qualified. KB Financial's record date was February 27, 2026. B qualified; A, having sold in January, did not. Selling after the record date does not forfeit the dividend.

2025 Year-End Dividends · Actual TimetablesTwo Companies, Opposite Sequences

Samsung set the record date first: shareholders entitled to the dividend were determined as of December 31, 2025. It announced the ordinary-share dividend of KRW 566 per share on January 29, 2026.

KB Financial announced the amount first. Its board approved KRW 1,605 per share on February 5, then set the record date roughly three weeks later, on February 27. Investors could see the payout before deciding whether to buy.

KRW 566 and KRW 1,605 refer to a single 2025 year-end dividend payment, not either company's total dividends for the year. Both companies also pay quarterly dividends.

Traditionally, most Korean companies identified year-end dividend recipients first and announced the amount only after the year had ended. A January 2023 reform proposal from the Financial Services Commission and the Ministry of Justice opened another route. A company can amend its articles of incorporationA company's governing rules. Shareholder approval is needed to amend them.to announce the dividend amount before setting a later record date.

Companies That Moved the Record Date · 2024 Year-End DividendsBrokerage Tally
Of KOSPI 200 constituents,98set their record dates in the first quarter of the following year.

Another analysis estimated that roughly two-thirds of KOSPI 200 companies had already amended their articles by the end of 2024.

So is the old advice, “Buy before the end of December
to collect your dividend,” now wrong?

It depends on the company. The advice still broadly applies when the record date remains December 31, as it does for Samsung. Even then, buying on December 29 or 30, 2025, would have been too late. It does not work for companies that moved their record dates: someone who bought in December and sold in January might receive nothing, while someone who bought the following January, February or March could qualify. The next section shows how to calculate the deadline.

Quarterly dividends can also use a later record date. Since January 2025, boards have been able to decide the amount before setting the record date.

IN A SENTENCE
What matters is whether you qualify as a shareholder on the record date, not simply when you bought.
One company's record date may be December 31; another's may fall the following February.

and Ex-Dividend Date

Knowing the record date is not enough. Buying on that date is already too late, because share ownership takes time to settle.

When a company retains a fixed record date, Step 03 comes first, and Step 02 usually does not require a separate notice. Its articles already specify December 31.

The Trade Is Immediate; Settlement Takes Two Business Days A stock trade executes when you buy, but the shares and money are exchanged two business days later. That issettlementThe exchange of shares and cash after a trade. Korean equities settle on T+2, two business days after the trade date. , or T+2 if the trade date is T. Only settled holders appear on the shareholder register. To qualify by the record date, you therefore need to buy at least two business days earlier. That cutoff is thelast cum-dividend trading dayThe last day on which buying shares will qualify for the dividend. Usually two business days before the record date; when the record date is a market holiday, count back from the preceding trading day.

Weekends and market holidays do not count. If the record date falls on a market holiday or weekend, start counting from the preceding trading day.

The timing works in reverse when selling. If you sell by the last cum-dividend trading day, settlement removes your name from the register by the record date. Sell on or after the ex-dividend date, and you remain entitled to that dividend.

Does the Share Price Drop on the Ex-Dividend Date?

The dividend entitlement has been detached. In theory, the stock price falls by the dividend amount.

But on cash-dividend ex-dates, the Korea Exchange does not mechanically reduce the stock's reference priceThe baseline price used to determine the day's permitted trading range, usually the previous close.in advance. Actual price movements depend on buyers and sellers that day.

Many forces besides dividends move the index. As more companies move their record dates, ex-dividend dates are spreading across the calendar rather than clustering at year-end.

Where Can I Find My Stock's Record Date?

Go to Korea's electronic disclosure system, DART, search for the company and open its "Decision on Cash/Stock Dividend" disclosure. Five fields matter. A company with a December 31 record date may file this after the record date—Samsung did so on January 29. If no dividend decision has been published, look for a record-date notice. Companies whose boards set a separate record date publish such a notice; KB Financial posted its "Year-End Dividend Record Date Notice" on February 5. Other companies retain December 31 as prescribed in their articles and issue no separate notice.

DEEPER DIVEHow Korea's Dividend Timetable Works

Dividend procedure reform · Announced by the Financial Services Commission and Ministry of Justice on January 31, 2023. It separated the voting-rights record date from the dividend record date and allowed companies to approve the dividend amount before selecting the record date. An amendment to the company's articles is required.

Advance notice of the record date · If the board sets the year-end dividend record date, it must announce that date at least two weeks in advance. No additional notice is necessary where the articles already prescribe the date.

Quarterly dividends · An amendment to Korea's Capital Markets Act, effective January 21, 2025, allowed boards to set a quarterly-dividend record date after approving the amount.

Year-end market closure · The Korean stock market is closed on December 31 (or the preceding weekday when December 31 falls on a weekend).

Reference price · The exchange adjusts the ex-dividend reference price for stock dividends, but not for cash dividends.

03CHECK & REVIEW

Reading Dividend Numbers
and Taxes

We've worked out when to buy. Now let's separate two percentages that often appear in dividend news—and look at Korea's dividend tax changes beginning in 2026.

Payout Ratio vs. Dividend Yield

Both are percentages, but the denominators differ. The dividend payout ratio measures dividends against company earnings, while dividend yield compares them with the share price.

①Dividend payout ratioRelative to earnings
Total cash dividends÷Net income

What share of profits is paid to shareholders? If a company earns KRW 10bn in net income and distributes KRW 4bn, the payout ratio is 40%.

KB Financial's full-year 2025 dividend payout ratio was 27%.
②Dividend yieldRelative to the stock price
Dividend per share÷Share price

How much do you receive relative to the price paid? If a KRW 10,000 stock pays KRW 500 over a year, its annual dividend yield is 5%.

Samsung's disclosed dividend-to-market-price ratio for the KRW 566 ordinary-share year-end dividend was 0.5%. That covers this one payment—not the full-year yield, which also includes its quarterly dividends.
If you're asking, “How much cash will I receive for what I invested?”, look at dividend yield . A company can have a high payout ratio but a low yield if its share price is high.

KRW 10bn/4bn and KRW 10,000/500 are hypothetical examples.

How Korea's Dividend Taxes Changed in 2026

Dividends ordinarily arrive after 15.4% withholding: 14% national income tax plus 1.4% local income tax. But if a year's interest and dividend income—your financial incomeIncome earned from interest and dividends.—exceeds KRW 20mn, the excess is combined with other income, such as wages, under Korea's comprehensive income tax rules. The top marginal rate, including local income tax, can reach 49.5%.

Beginning in 2026, dividends paid by qualifying high-dividend companiesListed companies that pay substantial dividends or increase payouts significantly, subject to the criteria below.may instead be taxed separately from other income. This optional regime is known as separate taxation of dividend income. It is temporary and covers dividends received from January 1, 2026, through distributions relating to fiscal 2028—including year-end dividends paid in 2029.

The KRW 20mn threshold in this table counts only eligible high-dividend-company payments. It is distinct from the KRW 20mn threshold for comprehensive taxation, which counts interest plus dividends. Distributions from funds and REITs, ETF distributions and foreign-stock dividends are excluded. This discussion concerns individuals resident in Korea for tax purposes.

To qualify, a listed company must first have maintained dividends at or above its fiscal-2024 level. It must also either have a dividend payout ratio of at least 40%, or a ratio of at least 25% while increasing dividends by at least 10% year on year. The company discloses its eligibility through the Korea Exchange's KINDby the day after the annual shareholders' meeting approves the dividend. Check that filing to see whether your stock qualifies. KB Financial was reported to have met the second test, with a 2025 payout ratio of 27% and a 32% increase in annual cash dividends.

The 14% national rate in the first KRW 20mn bracket is the same as ordinary withholding. This option is most relevant to investors whose larger dividends would otherwise face higher rates under comprehensive taxation. Use the following steps to assess it.

Four Checks When Reading Dividend News

01What is the record date?

Check the “Dividend Record Date” field in the dividend decision disclosure or a separate record-date notice. A company may keep December 31 or let its board choose another date.

02Has the amount already been announced?

Companies that changed their procedure may declare the dividend before the record date. Those using December 31 generally announce the amount after that date. Verify the actual order in disclosures.

03Payout ratio or dividend yield?

First ask whether the number is measured against profits or against the share price.

04Is it eligible for separate taxation—and did you elect it?

Use KRX KIND to check whether the company qualifies. Even for eligible dividends, the regime does not apply automatically. You must elect it when filing the following May.
COMMON MISTAKESPlausible, but wrong
MISCONCEPTION 01
“Buy by the end of December and you'll get the dividend.”
Only if the company's record date remains December 31—and even then, you must buy by the last qualifying trading day. KB Financial's 2025 year-end dividend had a February 27, 2026 record date. Someone who bought in December and sold in January did not qualify. Check the company's record date first.
MISCONCEPTION 02
“Buying on the ex-dividend date means getting the stock cheaper—and making money.”
As the illustration on page 2 shows, a KRW 10,000 stock paying KRW 500 would theoretically trade ex-dividend at KRW 9,500. A buyer at that price receives no KRW 500 dividend, so there is no free gain. The actual price may fall further or rise, depending on trading that day.
MISCONCEPTION 03
“Separate taxation means everyone's dividend tax just went down.”
Only dividends from qualifying companies are covered, and investors must elect the regime. The first KRW 20mn bracket still carries the familiar 14% national rate. Those most likely to benefit are investors whose substantial dividends previously faced high rates under comprehensive taxation.
EPISODE 10 · IN ONE SENTENCE

You receive a dividend only if your name is on the shareholder register on the record date. Korean equities settle in two business days, so buy early enough for settlement to finish by the record date .

“Buy in December for the dividend” applies only where the record date is December 31. Always check the company's actual date before buying.

A QUICK GLOSSARY
Dividend
A distribution of part of a company's profits to shareholders
Year-end dividend
A dividend relating to a completed financial year; a payment tied to a quarter is a quarterly dividend
Dividend record date
The date that determines eligibility. You must appear on the shareholder register as of that date
Shareholder register
The company's official record of shareholders and their holdings
Settlement (T+2)
The exchange of shares and cash two business days after a stock trade
Last cum-dividend trading day
The last day you can buy to qualify, generally two business days before the record date; count from the preceding trading day if the record date is a holiday
Ex-dividend date
The next trading day after the final qualifying purchase date. Buying from this date does not qualify; selling from this date does not forfeit the dividend
Reference price
The baseline used to determine that day's trading price limits. The KRX does not adjust it downward for cash dividends
Dividend-to-market-price ratio
A ratio published in a dividend disclosure: the dividend for that payment divided by a prescribed reference share price. Check the filing for the reference period
Articles of incorporation
The company's governing rules. Changing the record-date procedure requires an amendment
Dividend payout ratio
Cash dividends as a percentage of net income
Dividend yield
Dividends per share over a given period divided by the share price; the annual yield uses a full year's payments
Comprehensive taxation of financial income
When annual interest plus dividends exceed KRW 20mn, the excess is combined with other income for tax purposes
Separate taxation of dividend income
An elective, temporary 14–30% progressive tax regime for qualifying companies' dividends received from January 1, 2026, through distributions attributable to fiscal 2028
Qualifying high-dividend company
A listed company whose dividend has not fallen below its fiscal-2024 level and whose payout ratio is at least 40%, or at least 25% with dividends up 10% or more year on year. Eligibility is disclosed on KRX KIND
