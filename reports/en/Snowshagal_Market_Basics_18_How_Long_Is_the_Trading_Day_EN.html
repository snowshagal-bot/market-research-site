Short on time?Three key takeaways
  • The Korea Exchange (KRX) trades from 9:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. in its regular session. Include Nextrade's pre-market session and both venues' after-market sessions, and the window for trading extends from 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. But there are breaks along the way.
  • Prices are set differently across sessions. At the start and end of the KRX regular session, orders accumulate and execute together at one price. During continuous trading, eligible orders execute as soon as they can be matched.
  • Even though trading continues until 8:00 p.m., the day's official closing price is the final price from the 3:30 p.m. regular-session close.

How Long Is a
Stock Market Day?

The short answer
KRX's regular session lasts 6 hours and 30 minutes. Across the two venues, the overall trading window stretches from 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.—12 hours.
That does not mean continuous trading for all 12 hours. There are breaks, and Nextrade handles only about 600 eligible stocks. Price formation also changes by session. To see why, follow the day's timetable.
02Market structure and terminology

The Daily Trading Schedule

Korean stocks trade on two venues: the Korea Exchange (KRX) and NextradeSouth Korea's first alternative trading venue, launched in March 2025. It handles roughly 600 stocks also listed on KRX.(NXT), which launched in March 2025. Because the same shares can trade on either venue, the quoted prices may differ slightly at a given moment.

The Weekday Schedule at a Glance

On the KRX side, only the 30 minutes before the open and the final 10 minutes use the 'collect orders, then match' pattern. These are the periods in which the day's opening and closing prices are set.

Between 8:30 and 8:40 a.m., two order processes run side by side: pre-open, off-hours trading at the previous closing price (the small dotted segment in the chart), and collection of orders for the 9:00 a.m. opening auction. They are separate order-entry market segments.

Opening and Closing Prices Come From Auctions

KRX sets the first and last prices of its regular session by collecting orders before matching them.

Opening priceThe first execution price in the day's regular session.At the open8:30~9:00: orders accumulate and are matched at 9:00 a.m..
Closing priceThe final official price of the day's regular session, used to set the next day's daily price-limit band.At the close15:20~15:30: orders accumulate and are matched at 3:30 p.m..

In Korea, these windows are commonly called dongsi hoga (call-auction periods)The Korean term commonly used for the opening and closing call-auction windows: 8:30–9:00 a.m. and 3:20–3:30 p.m. Orders accumulate and then match at a single price.. The same single-price auction mechanism is used when trading resumes after a market-wide circuit breaker (seePart 17).

Why Use Auctions at the Start and Finish?

The first priceRight at the open, the order book may be thin. With continuous trading, just a few shares can determine the opening print—as happened in Nextrade's pre-market session on August 6.
The final priceThe closing price becomes the reference for the next day's daily price limitsThe permitted trading range for a stock during the day, generally ±30% of the previous close for ordinary Korean shares. and is used to value indices and funds. Collecting orders for 10 minutes helps prevent a last-second order from distorting that benchmark.

Pooling orders before matching makes it harder for an anomalous one- or two-share order to determine the opening price. Nextrade's pre-market still starts with continuous trading from the first incoming order. Compare the two mechanisms below.

Why Nextrade Takes a Break

The Nextrade timetable contains two breaks. NXT halts trading during the final 10 minutes before KRX fixes its opening and closing prices. Regulators say this is intended to prevent market manipulationIllegal trading activity designed to move prices artificially and mislead other investors. that exploits price differences between the two venues.

Trading Until 8 p.m.—but the Close Is at 3:30

After the regular session ends, the KRX schedule looks like this.

After-marketTrading after the regular session: 4:00–8:00 p.m. on KRX, and 3:40–8:00 p.m. on Nextrade.16:00~20:00The KRX after-market began on September 14. Orders are matched continuously, much like in the regular session.

With the after-market's arrival, the former off-hours single-price auction—which matched orders every 10 minutes from 4:00 to 6:00 p.m.—was abolished.

Three things matter in the after-market.

  • Price range · The same ±30% daily price limit as the regular session.
  • Order types · Only limit-order variants are accepted. Market ordersOrders to buy or sell immediately at the best available price, without specifying a limit price. are not accepted.
  • Eligible securities · Most KOSPI and KOSDAQ stocks are eligible. ETF·ETNETFs are funds designed to track an index or other benchmark; ETNs are securities issued by financial institutions that promise returns linked to an index. Both trade on exchanges like stocks. are excluded.

The official close ≠ the 8:00 p.m. priceEasy to confuse

The official closing price is set at the end of the regular session, at 3:30 p.m. That remains true after the launch of the after-market. KRX has warned listed companies that some price portals may display the last after-market trade as though it were the official close.

Going deeperCircuit breakers, illiquid stocks, overseas exchanges

A KRX circuit breaker also halts Nextrade · When a circuit breaker halts KRX, trading on Nextrade stops too. Since September 14, Nextrade has pooled orders for 30 seconds after KRX's reopening call-auction order period ends, before setting a reopening price.

Stocks that trade exclusively by periodic auction · KRX selects exceptionally illiquid stocks annually and matches orders every 30 minutes rather than continuously. Stocks with an average interval of more than 10 minutes between executions can qualify.

Overseas exchanges are extending trading hours too · In announcing its after-market, KRX pointed to intensifying competition over trading hours, including plans by US exchanges such as Nasdaq for 23-hour trading beginning in December.

Four Checks Before You Trust an Off-Hours Price

01Which venue?

The same stock can have different prices on KRX and Nextrade. Check the trading venue first.

02What time?

A pre-market or after-market print may reflect very little order flow. Compare it with the regular-session price .

03How many shares traded?

Off-hours prices can be set by trades involving just a few shares. Check the executed volume as well as the price.

04Is your order still active?

An unfilled KRX regular-session order does not carry over into the after-market, so you must submit a new order . Ordinary Nextrade orders remain valid through its after-market unless canceled.
Common MisconceptionsThey sound plausible—but they're wrong
Myth 01
“If a stock opens at limit down in pre-market trading, the regular session will open at limit down too.”
KRX separately determines its 9:00 a.m. opening price using orders collected from 8:30 a.m. The two venues set their opening prices independently.
Myth 02
“In an opening auction, the first order submitted sets the opening price.”
The price comes from the entire pool of orders. That's why, in the 'Set the Opening Price' exercise, the single-price auction outcome stayed the same regardless of when the one-share orders arrived .
Myth 03
“You can't place orders during a call-auction period.”
Orders are accepted; they simply accumulate instead of executing immediately. They match together at 9:00 a.m. (or 3:30 p.m. at the close) .
Part 18 · In one sentence

Korean shares can trade until 8:00 p.m., but not every price print carries the same weight.

An 11-share pre-market trade at limit down is not the same kind of price as the official close determined by an auction. The day's benchmark remains the 3:30 p.m. closing price.

Quick Glossary
Regular session
The main KRX trading session, 9:00 a.m.–3:30 p.m., with no lunch break
Call-auction period (dongsi hoga)
The commonly used Korean term for the opening and closing auctions: 8:30–9:00 a.m. and 3:20–3:30 p.m.; orders are pooled and matched at one price
Single-price auction
A method that collects orders for a set period and matches them at the single price that maximizes the volume executed
Continuous trading
Orders execute as soon as a compatible bid and offer can be matched
Opening / closing price
The first and last official prices of the regular session
Fixed-price off-hours trading
Trading only at the relevant closing price: the previous close from 8:30–8:40 a.m., or the current close from 3:40–4:00 p.m.
Former off-hours periodic auction
The discontinued session that pooled and matched orders every 10 minutes from 4:00–6:00 p.m.; abolished September 14, 2026
After-market
Trading after the regular session: 4:00–8:00 p.m. on KRX and 3:40–8:00 p.m. on NXT
Pre-market
Trading before the regular session; currently offered only by NXT, from 8:00–8:50 a.m.
Nextrade
South Korea's first alternative trading venue, launched in March 2025; roughly 600 eligible stocks (606 as of late August)
Main market
Nextrade's session overlapping the KRX regular trading day, from 9:00:30 a.m. to 3:20 p.m.
VI
A volatility-control mechanism that provides a two-minute cooling-off period when an individual stock's price moves sharply
Static VI
A mechanism that collects orders for a two-minute single-price auction when a trade would occur at least 10% from a reference price; introduced by Nextrade on September 14, 2026
Order book
The list of outstanding buy and sell orders for a stock, organized by price
