Investment Insight No. 26 · November 5, 2026

Hoban has become Hanjin KAL's largest shareholder. But under the amended Korean Commercial Act, the largest shareholder group's votes in audit committee elections are capped at 3%, and, based on the directors' term expirations, control of a board majority cannot change before March 2028 at the earliest.

Five key points

SUMMARY
  1. Hoban has overtaken Chairman Cho as Hanjin KAL's largest shareholder. As disclosed on Nov. 4, Hoban and related parties held 20.69%, versus 20.58% for Hanjin Group Chairman Walter Cho and related parties—a gap of just 0.11 percentage points.
  2. The largest shareholder group's audit committee votes are capped at 3%. The amended Commercial Act provision took effect in July 2026. If Hoban retains first place and other holdings are unchanged, the second-ranked Cho group could exercise votes representing 11.85–15.00% of outstanding shares.
  3. The arithmetic is different for ordinary board elections. Under Korea's Fair Trade Act, three public-interest foundations (holding 3.18%) may vote on specified matters only within a combined 15% ceiling alongside other related parties. As the Cho group's holdings excluding the foundations already total 17.40%, the foundations contribute no votes in ordinary director elections. The Cho group's 17.40% voting stake thus trails Hoban's 20.69% by about 3.3 percentage points.
  4. A change in the board majority cannot occur before March 2028 at the earliest. That timeline assumes directors leave at the end of their terms. The three seats expiring next year were filled in 2024 on nominations by Korea Development Bank (KDB).
  5. Hanjin KAL trades at 4.4 times the value of its stake in Korean Air. Eugene Investment & Securities estimates the average since 2024 at 2.8 times. The ratio also exceeded four times during the May 2025 spike; three months later, the stock was 20% below its peak.

Even as the KOSPI fell 5.28% on Nov. 5, Hanjin KAL rose 3.31% to close at ₩190,400—54% above its Oct. 30 close of ₩123,900. Its common-share market capitalization was roughly ₩12.7 trillion, exceeding the approximately ₩11.2 trillion market value of Korean Air as a whole, in which Hanjin KAL owns 26.13%1, 2.

The catalyst is a change in the ownership rankings. Reports emerged on Nov. 2 that Hoban had become the largest shareholder. The same day, Chairman Cho disclosed an agreement to purchase 325,000 shares3, 4. On Nov. 4, Hanjin KAL formally reported that its largest shareholder had changed from “Cho Won-tae and 18 others” to “Hoban Construction and three others”5.

01
Resolution 1 · Audit committee elections

Being No. 1
means a combined 3% voting cap

Under the amended Commercial Act, effective July 23, 2026, the largest shareholder must aggregate the holdings of related parties and may exercise no more than 3% of voting shares when electing or dismissing audit committee members. Other shareholders are subject to a separate 3% cap per holder. The restriction does not apply to ordinary director elections7, 8.

Key terminology

Audit committee member
A director serving on the committee that oversees financial reporting and management conduct. Hanjin KAL has three.
Largest shareholder
The shareholder with the greatest combined holdings when related parties are included; currently Hoban Construction and three related parties.
Related parties
Family members, affiliated companies and executives, controlled foundations and similar persons or entities whose stakes are aggregated with those of the largest shareholder.

What matters

  • Only the second-ranked group can vote above 3% in aggregate. Delta Air Lines (14.90%), KDB (10.58%) and the National Pension Service (5.11%) are each separately limited to 3% in audit committee elections. KDB is disclosed as a joint holder with the Cho group, but is not a related party and has no agreement delegating its voting rights, so its stake is not aggregated with Cho's (Hanwha Investment & Securities)8, 9.
  • Foundation votes depend on how the ceiling is applied when Cho ranks second. The Jungseok-Inha Educational Foundation, Jungseok Logistics Foundation and Ilwoo Foundation (combined stake: 3.18%) are generally barred from voting under the Fair Trade Act. They may vote on certain matters, such as director appointments, only if their combined voting rights with those of other related parties stay within 15%10, 11. If Hoban remains No. 1 on the record date, two results follow depending on the basis used to apply the 15% ceiling:
    • If the ceiling is tested against the other 16 holders' underlying 17.40% stake, they already exceed 15%; the foundations receive no votes → 11.85%.
    • If the ceiling is tested against the 11.85% of votes actually exercisable after the individual 3% caps, another 3.15 percentage points are available → 15.00%.
    At this March's AGM, when the Cho group ranked first, Hanjin KAL restricted all three foundations' votes on every resolution2. The company has not disclosed how it would treat them if the group ranked second.
  • The record-date shareholder register normally determines the ranking. The statute does not specify when the largest shareholder must be identified; in practice, the shareholder register as of the AGM record date is usually used (Hanwha Investment & Securities)8. Article 13(1) of Hanjin KAL's articles of incorporation grants voting rights at the AGM to shareholders registered on December 312. With the exchange closed on Dec. 31 and equity settlement taking two business days (T+2), on-exchange purchases must be executed by Dec. 28 to appear on that register.

Resolution 2 · Board terms

A board majority cannot change
before March 2028 at the earliest

Hanjin KAL has nine directors (two executive and seven independent), the maximum permitted by its articles of incorporation. Three directors' terms expire each year. To replace a majority—at least five seats—solely through scheduled expirations would therefore take two annual general meetings2, 8.

Next year's three seats were originally KDB nominations

  • KDB's 2024 nominations: The terms of independent directors Bae Seong-rye, Hong Dong-pyo and Song Baek-hun expire next March. All three were appointed in 2024 following nominations by KDB, according to Invest Chosun and The Economy14, 15.
  • Nomination rights and voting obligations: Under a 2020 investment agreement, KDB secured the right to nominate three outside directors, including one separately elected audit committee candidate. Chairman Cho is obliged to vote in favor of those nominees2, 14. The duration of the agreement has not been confirmed.
  • KDB may nominate the replacements as well: Invest Chosun reported that many analysts expect KDB to nominate the next three directors too, provided it remains a shareholder14. The actual nominees and nominating parties will be identified in the notice convening next year's AGM. Hoban would need to put forward candidates through shareholder proposals or a request for cumulative voting.

Key terminology

Separate election
Electing a director specifically to sit on the audit committee, with the 3% voting cap applying from the director-election stage.
Cumulative voting
Shareholders receive votes equal to their shares multiplied by the number of directors being elected, and may allocate all votes to one candidate. This can allow a minority shareholder to win a board seat. For a listed company's AGM, a request is due six weeks before the anniversary of the preceding year's AGM.
Shareholder proposal
A shareholder's right to submit AGM agenda items or director nominees. For an AGM, the deadline is six weeks before the anniversary of the prior year's meeting. Based on Hanjin KAL's March 26 AGM this year, the next deadline is Feb. 12, 2027.

What matters

  • Companies can no longer opt out of cumulative voting: The Commercial Act amendment effective Sept. 10, 2026 bars listed companies with assets of at least ₩2 trillion from excluding cumulative voting through their articles of incorporation16. Hanjin KAL also removed its opt-out clause at its March AGM2. A shareholder with at least 1% may request cumulative voting six weeks before the meeting, making it available from next March's AGM.
  • About 30% is needed to guarantee one seat: When two directors are elected cumulatively, a shareholder needs more than one-third of the votes represented at the meeting to guarantee a seat. At this March's turnout (94%), that equates to roughly 30% of outstanding shares (Hanwha's estimate is about 31%)2, 8. Neither group reaches that threshold on its own.
  • Midterm replacement requires a special resolution: Removing a sitting director before term expiry, or amending the articles to add seats, requires approval by at least two-thirds of voting rights present and at least one-third of all issued shares8, 17.

Eugene Investment & Securities sees scope for Hoban to gain some board representation next March if it shifts to actively pursuing management participation, but judges a board majority—and eventual control of Korean Air—to remain distant18. Hanwha Investment & Securities notes that a roughly 21% stake cannot guarantee a seat under cumulative voting; the outcome depends on how other shareholders allocate their votes8.

Resolution 3 · KDB's stake

Ordinary director elections
hinge on how Delta and KDB vote

The 3% cap does not apply to ordinary director elections. Delta Air Lines (14.90%), Korea Development Bank (10.58%), the National Pension Service and other shareholders can determine the outcome (Hanwha Investment & Securities)8. KDB can support or oppose particular nominees without selling a single share; a stake sale and an AGM vote are separate decisions.

The commitments and numbers behind KDB's stake

November 2020 investment agreement

  • Chairman Cho needs KDB's consent before selling Hanjin KAL shares, and his holdings are pledged as collateral to the bank. If he breaches the agreement, KDB has rights to dispose of the shares or require a tag-along sale.
  • KDB has no obligation to vote in concert with Chairman Cho.
  • Hanjin KAL must consult KDB and secure its prior approval on major management matters, according to iM Securities.

KDB's investment by the numbers

  • In December 2020, KDB subscribed to 7,062,146 newly issued shares at ₩70,800 each, investing ₩500 billion.
  • At the Nov. 5 close, the stake was worth approximately ₩1.345 trillion—2.7 times the original investment.
  • Merger schedule: Korean Air and Asiana's merger is set for Dec. 16; integration of the three low-cost carriers is targeted for March 2027.

How brokerages assess KDB and Delta

  • Hanwha Investment & Securities (Nov. 4): Hanwha calculates that if Delta backs Cho, even Hoban's purchase of all KDB shares at a 30% premium (about ₩1.61 trillion) would leave it behind, 31.6% to 35.96%. Hoban would need to buy another 4.3 percentage points or more—costing around ₩510 billion at the Nov. 3 close—to pull ahead. It therefore sees continued open-market buying as more likely than a premium purchase of the KDB block. The signals to revisit the scenario are a KDB stake-sale announcement and Delta's filings on investment purpose or shareholding changes19.
  • Hanwha Investment & Securities (Nov. 5): The brokerage argues that the underlying contest of votes—including Delta and KDB—does not change merely because the largest-shareholder ranking does. Even Hoban's 31.27% with KDB would be slightly below Hanwha's estimated 31.33% threshold for a guaranteed cumulative-voting seat at 94% turnout, requiring additional proxy support. It therefore expects the control premium to be determined more by negotiations over a block of Hoban shares (a privately negotiated large stake transfer) than by the eventual AGM ballot12.
  • DS Investment & Securities: DS reported that a KDB official said on Nov. 2 that completing the aviation restructuring was the priority. DS expects the bank to wait before selling and, if it exits, to favor an open competitive tender. A negotiated sale to one shareholder could prompt allegations that a state-owned policy bank had intervened to defend management control13.
  • iM Securities: iM treats Delta, KDB, LX Pantos (3.83%) and Japan Airlines as shares potentially friendly to Cho, and regards KDB's stake as “conditional” while the merger of the two airlines—its original policy objective—is being completed. Once the merger is done, the public-policy case for retaining the stake may diminish. With a substantial unrealized gain, KDB may consider an exit, although iM expects a cautious approach to timing and method21.

What matters

  • Nonvoting preferred shares rallied too: Hanjin KAL's nonvoting preferred shares climbed from ₩26,300 on Oct. 30 to ₩62,000 (the daily upper limit) on Nov. 5, a 2.4x gain1. Demand to accumulate votes cannot by itself explain that move; takeover speculation and trading flows are both likely playing a role.
  • The exchange issued an overheating warning: After the Nov. 4 close, the Korea Exchange gave advance notice that Hanjin KAL common and preferred shares might be designated as short-term overheated issues. If all price, turnover and volatility criteria are met within 10 trading days beginning Nov. 5, trading shifts to a single-price call auction every 30 minutes for three sessions. The preferred shares were placed on an investment-caution notice on Nov. 5 (advance notice of investment-warning designation) and were designated an investment-warning issue effective Nov. 6 after meeting conditions including a gain of at least 60% over five sessions. An investment-warning designation does not itself halt trading. A one-day suspension may follow if the price climbs another 40% or more over the two days after designation and remains above the pre-designation closing price23.
  • Each percentage point of ownership is becoming more expensive: A one-percentage-point stake (667,623 shares) is worth about ₩127.1 billion at the Nov. 5 close on a simple marked-to-market basis. iM's estimate at the Nov. 3 close was ₩117 billion21. Buying that many shares in the market could cost more as the price moves.
  • Trading activity surged: Turnover including NEXTRADE reached ₩423 billion on Nov. 4, 72 times the ₩5.9 billion recorded on Oct. 301.

What could sustain the control premium

  • The No. 1 ranking remains unsettled ahead of the Dec. 31 record date. Settlement of Cho's contracted purchase (beginning Dec. 3) and further buying by Hoban are still possible. Eugene expects the ownership contest to continue through year-end18.
  • DS believes Hoban has little reason to resolve the situation quickly, allowing market interpretations, rather than new confirmed facts, to drive the share price for some time. It also sees scope for this dynamic to persist until next year's first AGM subject to the revised cumulative-voting rules13.
  • iM points to an estimated effective free float of around 20%, which can support a sizeable premium to underlying asset value, although it regards the sharp short-term rally as a concern21.

What could erode the premium

  • Hanwha believes continued open-market purchases by Hoban could lift the stock further in the near term, but warns that the risk of a reversal grows once purchases stop8.
  • KDB said on Nov. 2 that completing the restructuring comes first, according to DS Investment & Securities13. Hanwha argues investors should discount the takeover premium until a KDB stake-sale disclosure appears8.
  • iM notes that the two-day surge has raised the cost of further share purchases for both sides21.
  • Hanwha notes that Hoban Construction's 2025 valuation gains (₩615.2 billion) were 4.5 times its operating profit (₩136.1 billion), while the value of holdings at year-end 2025 was an estimated 3.2 times their acquisition cost at the Nov. 3 close. Taking profits is therefore as plausible an option as buying more19.
  • Hanwha (Nov. 5) argues that in a negotiated block sale without a public tender offer, any control premium accrues to the parties to that transaction, not automatically to ordinary shareholders. It therefore recommends discounting the premium reflected in the public share price. It also recalls that after two limit-up sessions in May 2025, the stock traded roughly 21% below the second limit-up closing price three months later12.

Eugene's view on Korean Air Eugene Investment & Securities sees Korean Air as the economic asset at the heart of the dispute. It highlights the prospect of enhanced shareholder returns and retains Korean Air as its top pick in aviation, citing solid passenger demand, strong cargo operations and a weaker dollar against the won (Buy; ₩35,000 price target as of Nov. 4)18.

Key takeaways · What could change

Variables that could delay or overturn the conclusions

VARIABLE 1

Hoban's stated investment purpose

It has been “passive investment” since the initial 2022 filing8. A shift to “participation in management” would require a filing within five days and could be followed by shareholder proposals or requests for cumulative voting.

VARIABLE 2

How the foundation voting ceiling is calculated

If Hoban remains No. 1 and all other holdings stay unchanged, Cho's audit committee voting stake falls between 11.85% and 15.00% depending on how the 15% cap is calculated. The company would apply an interpretation first; a dispute could ultimately be decided in court.

VARIABLE 3

Delta's and KDB's decisions

Neither has announced its voting intentions. If Delta backs neither side, the balance in ordinary director elections changes (Figure 3). Separately from its shares, KDB could influence the nominees for next year's three board seats.

Variable 1 may change next year's AGM agenda; Variable 2 affects the calculation of Cho's audit committee votes; Variable 3 affects ordinary director elections. Track Variables 1 and 2 through ownership filings and AGM notices, and Variable 3 through KDB stake disclosures and the AGM outcome.

CONCLUSION

Hoban may be No. 1,
but control does not change hands overnight

The largest-shareholder ranking has changed; the board has not. Neither the audit committee voting rules, ordinary director elections nor the directors' staggered terms makes a move into first place equivalent to an immediate transfer of control. Year-end ownership rankings, votes actually cast at the AGM and KDB's choices will shape the outcome—but the share price is already moving ahead of those decisions.

No single disclosure can determine how long takeover speculation will support the share price. The year-end ranking, Delta's and KDB's decisions and the continuation—or halt—of Hoban's buying all matter.

  1. Who leads on the Dec. 31 record date?The largest group faces the aggregated 3% audit committee cap. Settlement of Cho's contracted purchase and additional buying by Hoban will determine the ranking.
  2. Which side will Delta and KDB support?If Delta supports Cho, the Cho camp remains ahead regardless of where KDB's votes go. If Delta supports neither camp, KDB's votes become decisive. KDB could also influence voting and nominations for next year's three seats without selling shares.
  3. Does Hoban keep buying—or stop?Hanwha warns that reversal risk grows once the record-date ranking is set and Hoban's purchases cease. DS argues that Hoban has little reason to resolve the situation quickly.

Calculation methodology
Share-count denominator

The denominator is 66,762,275 common shares per the Nov. 4 filing. The 530,297 preferred shares are excluded because they have no voting rights. Three percent equals 2,002,868 shares.

Audit committee votes (Hoban ranks first)
Cho group: Cho Won-tae 3,866,745 → 2,002,868; Cho Hyun-min 3,839,470 → 2,002,868; other 17 holders unchanged = 10,038,440 shares, or 15.04% (ignoring restrictions on foundation voting, following Hanwha's method). Excluding the three foundations (Jungseok-Inha 1,337,479; Jungseok Logistics 680,025; Ilwoo 107,461 = 2,124,965 shares or 3.18%) leaves 7,913,475 votes, or 11.85%. If foundation votes are allowed up to the combined 15% ceiling, 11.85% + 3.15pp = 15.00%. Hoban group: all four entities together hold 13,814,053 shares → 2,002,868 eligible votes = 3.00%.
Audit committee votes (Cho ranks first)
Hoban group: Hoban Construction 7,676,827 → 2,002,868; Hoban Hotels & Resorts 5,566,702 → 2,002,868; Hoban Industry 471,524; Hoban Co. 99,000 = 4,576,260 eligible votes, or 6.85%.
Ordinary director votes and KDB scenarios
Cho group (excluding foundations): 13,738,919 − 2,124,965 = 11,613,954 shares = 17.40%; adding Delta's 9,947,508 votes gives 32.30% (including foundations would yield a naive 35.48%, but their votes are excluded under the 15% ceiling). Hoban group plus KDB: 13,814,053 + 7,062,146 = 20,876,199 shares = 31.27%. After settlement of Cho's 325,000-share purchase, his group's stake rises to 14,063,919 shares = 21.07%, and the foundation-excluded group plus Delta reaches 32.78% (about 32.8%). Cho group (excluding foundations) + Delta + KDB = 28,623,608 shares, or 42.87%.
Threshold for one guaranteed cumulative-voting seat

When two seats are elected cumulatively, a shareholder needs more than one-third of voting rights represented at the meeting to guarantee a seat. Turnout at the March 2026 AGM was 94.2% (60,996,647 / 64,727,683 shares). Applying that turnout rate after excluding foundation votes: (66,762,275 − 2,124,965) × 94.2% ÷ 3 = approximately 30.4% of issued shares. Hanwha (Nov. 5) estimated about 31.33% at 94% turnout12.

Share-price valuation multiple

Hanjin KAL common-share market cap (common shares × closing price) ÷ (96,207,460 Korean Air shares × Korean Air closing price). Oct. 30: ₩8.27tn ÷ ₩2.55tn = 3.2x; Nov. 3: ₩11.70tn ÷ ₩2.80tn = 4.2x; Nov. 5: ₩12.71tn ÷ ₩2.92tn = 4.4x. Its 26.13% Korean Air stake is per the semiannual report. Summary of legal provisions
Commercial Act, Article 542-12(4) In elections and dismissals of audit committee members, voting rights attached to shares in excess of 3% cannot be exercised. The largest shareholder must aggregate holdings of related parties and specified others. The amendment extending the rule to all audit committee members, irrespective of whether they are outside directors (Act No. 20991, promulgated July 22, 2025), took effect July 23, 20267.
Commercial Act, Articles 542-12(2) and 542-7(3) The amendment requiring at least two separately elected audit committee members and prohibiting listed firms with assets of at least ₩2 trillion from opting out of cumulative voting (Act No. 21044, promulgated Sept. 9, 2025) took effect Sept. 10, 2026. Transitional provisions make it applicable from the first shareholders' meeting convened to elect directors after the effective date7, 16.
Commercial Act, Article 542-8(2)(5); Enforcement Decree, Article 38(1) The largest shareholder is the holder with the greatest stake after aggregation with related parties. For audit committee voting, aggregation additionally covers those holding shares for the account of the largest shareholder or related parties, and those who have delegated voting rights to them9, 33.
Monopoly Regulation and Fair Trade Act, Article 25(2) Public-interest foundations linked to the designated controller of a cross-shareholding-restricted business group are generally barred from voting the shares they own in affiliated companies. An exception permits voting on specified matters at listed affiliates, including director appointments or removals, amendments to the articles and mergers, but only if the total votes exercised by the foundations and other relevant related parties do not exceed 15% of issued voting shares.10.
Commercial Act, Articles 385 and 434 Removing directors and amending the articles of incorporation require a special resolution: at least two-thirds of the voting rights represented at the meeting and at least one-third of issued shares must approve17.
Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act, Article 147; Fair Trade Act, Article 11 Holders of at least 5% must report changes of one percentage point or more. Passive holders generally report by the 10th of the month following the month of change. A change in investment purpose must be disclosed within five days. A company acquiring at least 15% of a listed company and becoming its largest shareholder must file a business-combination notification; an on-exchange acquisition may be reported within 30 days after the transaction. The KFTC is to complete its review within 30 days of filing, extendable by up to 90 days if necessary8, 24, 31.

Glossary
Largest shareholder
The holder with the greatest stake after combining its shares with those of its related parties.
Related parties
Family members, affiliated companies and their executives, controlled corporations or foundations and similar parties aggregated with the largest shareholder.
Joint holder (disclosure category)
Parties who report holdings together because of an arrangement such as joint exercise of voting rights or consent to dispose of shares. KDB is reported as a joint holder with Cho under the 2020 investment agreement, but has no obligation to vote alongside him. Joint holders are not necessarily related parties.
3% voting cap
A restriction barring votes on shares above 3% in the election or dismissal of audit committee members. Only the largest shareholder must aggregate the holdings of related parties when applying the cap.
Separate election of an audit committee director
Electing a director specifically to serve on the audit committee, with the 3% cap applying from the director-election stage.
Cumulative voting
Shareholders receive as many votes as their shareholdings times the number of directors up for election, and may allocate them all to one nominee, allowing minority investors a path to board representation.
Restrictions on foundation voting rights
A Fair Trade Act restriction that generally prevents public-interest foundations linked to major conglomerates from voting the shares they hold in affiliated companies.
AGM record date
The date determining which shareholders may vote at the AGM. Hanjin KAL sets Dec. 31 as the record date under its articles, and holds its AGM the following March.
Business-combination notification
A filing with the Korea Fair Trade Commission when an acquisition or other transaction changes the control relationship, including by making the acquirer the largest shareholder.
Special resolution
A resolution requiring affirmative votes representing at least two-thirds of the votes present at the meeting and at least one-third of issued shares.
