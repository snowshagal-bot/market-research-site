About 7 minutes · 1 quiz · 1 interactive exercise

The essentialsIn three points
  • An index tracks the value of a basket of stocks using three inputs: which companies it includes, how many shares count, and their prices. The larger a company's market capitalization, the greater its influence on the index.
  • A fund that tracks an index does not normally need to trade just because share prices move. It trades when the basket's constituents or the number of shares counted in it change.
  • The November MSCI review added no stocks to the Korea Index, yet a higher share count for SK hynix could trigger an estimated KRW 580 billion in buying. Inclusion in an index does not guarantee a higher share price.

What goes into
a stock index?

To see why a fund may need to buy, we first need to understand how the index itself is built.

THE SHORT ANSWER
An index turns the changing value of a predetermined basket of stocks into a single number, relative to a starting point.
Three inputs matter: the stocks included, the share counts used, and the share prices. For SK hynix, it was the share count that changed.
02How it works

An index is
a basket's value

Start with the three ingredients of an index.

Multiply a company's share price by its number of shares to get its market capitalizationShare price × shares outstanding: the market value of all of a company's outstanding shares at the current price.. Add the market capitalizations of the basket's stocks to obtain its total value. The index compares that value with the basket's value at its base date.

How the KOSPI is calculatedKorea Exchange methodology
Base dateJan 4, 1980 = 100 FormulaCurrent value ÷ base value × 100 Nov 12 reading5,342.73 · roughly 53.4× the base

A KOSPI reading of 5,342.73 means the index is roughly 53.4 times its base level of 100 on January 4, 1980. It does not mean the market's actual capitalization has grown precisely 53-fold. New listings and additional share issuance can increase the basket's value. The Korea Exchange adjusts the divisor or base capitalization to prevent such changes from causing artificial jumps. That keeps the index focused on changes in share prices.

The largest stocks carry the most weight

Because the basket adds up market capitalizations, even a small move in a large company can move the whole index. Think of a basket of fruit.

Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are the KOSPI's watermelons. Together, they accounted for more than 46% of its market capitalization in reports dated November 6. Their combined share reached as high as 57.11% on June 25.

If both stocks rose 10% while every other constituent held steady, the KOSPI would climb roughly 4.6%. This approach, which gives larger companies more influence, is called market-capitalization weightingAn index construction method that weights each constituent by its market capitalization. The KOSPI, KOSPI 200 and MSCI equity indices use capitalization-based weighting, with different adjustments..

COMMON CONFUSIONA higher share price does not mean a bigger index weight
  • Even a stock priced above KRW 1 million has little influence if the company is small. What matters is the company's market value, not the price of one share.
  • Some indices—including the Dow Jones Industrial Average and Japan's Nikkei 225—are price-weighted rather than capitalization-weighted. Every index has its own construction rules.

MSCI adjusts for free float

Different indices can count a company's shares differently. MSCI first identifies the shares used in its calculation, then applies a free-float factorThe portion of shares considered available to public investors, excluding strategic or closely held stakes such as long-term holdings by controlling shareholders, governments or the company itself.. Shares tied up in strategic or long-term holdings, such as those held by controlling shareholders, governments or the company itself, are excluded from the investable share count.

The share count used for SK hynix rose by 17 million, while its free-float factor stayed at 77.5%. That increases the free-float-adjusted share count by roughly 13.18 million, raising its index weight (our calculation using 77.5%). Conversely, a company repurchasing its own stock (Part 12) can reduce its free float and, in turn, the number of shares reflected in the index.

03Applying what we learned

How to read
index-change news

Index-inclusion headlines often promise “billions in buying.” How should we read those estimates? First, find out when the index basket will change.

The basket is reviewed on a schedule

During scheduled reviews, stocks that meet size and other eligibility thresholds can be added; those that fall short can be removed. The share counts and free-float factors of existing constituents may also be updated.

  • MSCI indicesFeb · May · Aug · Nov | Four times a year
  • KOSPI 200Jun · Dec | Twice a year

LEARN MOREOther terms you will see beside an index

MSCI Korea vs. MSCI Emerging Markets · The MSCI Korea Index covers Korean large- and mid-cap stocks. Those companies are also included in the broader MSCI Emerging Markets IndexAn MSCI benchmark grouping stocks from emerging markets such as South Korea, Taiwan, India and China. Often abbreviated as the MSCI EM Index., which holds stocks from multiple countries. Korea has been included since 1992.

Rebalancing · Trading to bring a fund's holdings into line with revised index weights. Many tracking funds rebalance around the same effective date.

Index eligibility criteria · MSCI assesses both total market capitalization and free-float-adjusted market capitalization against minimum thresholds. These thresholds vary with market conditions and the review.

DELETEDFour stocksAverage −7.5%
  • Four stocks, including HLB and LG Display, were deleted

On the surface, additions rose and deletions fell. But everyone can see when index-tracking funds are expected to trade, and brokerages publish estimates of how much they may buy. That can prompt investors to buy ahead of the effective date.

Eugene Investment & Securities has observed that stocks entering an index often outperform ahead of inclusion and underperform afterward. Buying directly associated with the review usually subsides once the change takes effect. But earnings, industry conditions and other catalysts can also drive pre-inclusion gains, making it hard to separate an index effect from everything else.

Three questions to ask about SK hynix

Will KRW 580bn of new money enter the market?No

The figure is an estimate of net buying in one stock, not new funds flowing into Korea. As in the hypothetical fund, an index tracker may finance that purchase by selling other constituents.

Could it move the share price significantly?Uncertain

The estimated buying is around 10% of SK hynix's average daily trading valueThe total monetary value of shares traded over a given day.. Samsung Securities expects the price impact to be limited.

What about the next review?A factor to watch

Before the announcement, Samsung Securities estimated that a reduction in the free-float factor to 75% could trigger roughly KRW 260bn in selling. The factor remained at 77.5% this time, but the effect of ongoing share repurchases may show up in the next review.

Four checks for any index-inclusion story

01Which index?

The MSCI Korea Index, MSCI Korea Small Cap Index and KOSPI 200 attract different amounts of tracking capital. An addition does not carry the same weight in every benchmark.

02What actually changed?

Was a stock added or removed, or did an existing stock's share count or free-float factor change?

03When does it take effect?

There is often a two-to-three-week gap between announcement and implementation. Index-fund trading tends to cluster near the close on the effective date.

04How big relative to daily trading?

Compare estimated buying with the stock's average daily trading value. For less liquid stocks, the same amount of trading can have a much greater price impact.
COMMON MISREADINGSStatements that sound plausible—but aren't
MYTH 01
“An index addition guarantees a rise in the stock.”
Some stocks climb before the effective date, but results vary across companies and reviews. Inclusion is no promise of higher prices.
MYTH 02
“Index funds trade only when constituents change.”
That applies to a simplified, unleveraged fund with no subscriptions or redemptions. Real funds must also trade to handle investor inflows and withdrawals; leveraged and inverse ETFs may rebalance as markets move (Part 7). Trading rules differ by fund type.
MYTH 03
“SK hynix's 4.61% weight is within the Korea Index.”
It is SK hynix's weight in the MSCI Emerging Markets Index. Its weight in the Korea-only index is much higher. Always check which index a reported weight refers to.
PART 16 · THE TAKEAWAY

An index depends on which stocks it includes and how many shares count. When that composition changes, index-tracking funds may need to trade.

That is why even a headline reporting “zero additions” can lead to billions of won in trading.

A QUICK GLOSSARY
Stock index
A single number showing how a defined group of stocks has performed relative to a base date.
Market capitalization
Share price × shares outstanding; the total market value of a company's outstanding shares.
Market-cap weighting
An index method that weights constituents by their market capitalizations.
Base date
The starting reference date assigned a level such as 100. For the KOSPI: January 4, 1980.
Index share count
The number of shares MSCI uses in calculating an index, before applying the free-float factor.
Free-float factor
The share of stock considered available for trading, after excluding long-term strategic holdings.
MSCI
A U.S.-based company that produces global equity indices widely used as investment benchmarks.
MSCI Korea Index
MSCI's index covering large- and mid-cap Korean equities.
Emerging Markets Index
An MSCI benchmark grouping equities from Korea, Taiwan, India, China and other emerging markets; often called MSCI EM.
Small-cap index
An index representing smaller companies by market capitalization.
KOSPI
The benchmark index of companies listed on Korea's main stock market.
KOSPI 200
An index of 200 representative KOSPI-listed companies.
Index review
A scheduled reassessment of constituents and index share counts.
Additions / deletions
Stocks entering or leaving an index.
Effective date
The date when announced changes enter the index calculation.
Passive index fund
A fund designed to replicate a benchmark's holdings and weights.
ETF
An exchange-traded fund: a fund whose shares trade on an exchange like stocks.
Rebalancing
Trades that bring a fund's portfolio back into line with updated index weights.
ADR
An American depositary receipt representing shares in a non-U.S. company that trade on a U.S. exchange.
Trading value
The total monetary value of shares traded during a day.
