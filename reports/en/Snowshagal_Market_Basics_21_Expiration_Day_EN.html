The essentialsIn three points
  • KOSPI 200 futures expire in March, June, September and December; its standard monthly options expire on the second Thursday of each month. When index and single-stock futures and options expire on the same quarterly date, Korean markets call it “quadruple witching.”
  • On expiration day, the expiring futures contract stops trading at 3:20 p.m. KST. Its final settlement price is based on the KOSPI 200 index close at 3:30 p.m.
  • Stock baskets hedged with futures, along with index funds rebalancing their holdings, may all need to trade at that same closing price. That is why orders can pile up in the final 10-minute closing call auction.

For individual investorsThere is no need to rush to sell your stocks just because it is expiration day. If you trade near the close, check whether your order actually fills. And do not mistake a sudden closing-price move for bad news without looking further.

Answer: ②

Trading in the expiring futures contract ends at 3:20 p.m., but its final settlement is based on the KOSPI 200 closing level set 10 minutes later. The futures market closes first; the underlying stock index supplies the final settlement value.

So why can prices swing
in the final minutes?

THE SHORT ANSWER
At expiration, the stock market's closing level sets the final settlement value of futures and options. Orders that need to match that closing price converge in the final 10-minute call auction. If buying or selling dominates, the closing price can move sharply.
02THE MECHANICS

When the Closing Price
Sets the Settlement Value

The KOSPI and KOSPI 200 frequently appear together in headlines, but they are different indices. The KOSPI covers the main stock market; the KOSPI 200 tracks 200 leading constituents. They generally move in tandem, but KOSPI 200 futures and options settle against the KOSPI 200 closing level, not the broader KOSPI.

Futures Stop at 3:20; Settlement Is Set at 3:30

  • KOSPI 200 futures normally trade until 3:45 p.m. On expiration day, only the contract expiring that day stops trading at 3:20 p.m. Contracts with later expirations continue trading on the normal schedule.
  • Anyone holding an expiring contract to the end does not exchange shares. Instead, the contract is cash-settled for the difference between the agreed futures price and the 3:30 p.m. KOSPI 200 close. That closing index level is the final settlement priceThe reference price used to settle futures or options that remain open at expiration..

  • You can sell a futures contract—commit to a short position at 1,000—even without owning the underlying shares. If the index settles at 990, the seller effectively agreed to sell at 1,000 something now worth 990, and earns the difference.
  • Each one-point move in a standard KOSPI 200 futures contract is worth ₩250,000. A one-point difference in the closing index level therefore changes the settlement of one contract by ₩250,000. That is why the way the closing level is set matters.
  • KOSPI 200 options use the same closing index level for settlement. The cash payout depends on the gap between that level and the option's strike price. If exercising the right would not be favorable, the option expires worthless.

closing call auctionRather than matching orders continuously, the exchange collects buy and sell orders for a set period and executes them at a single clearing price. Here, that auction determines the day's official closing price. collects orders between 3:20 and 3:30 p.m., then matches them at a single price at 3:30 p.m. (see Part 18). Who Needs to Trade at the Close?

Some participants need to transact at precisely the closing price. One example is a cash-and-futures arbitrageA strategy that exploits a price gap between futures and their underlying cash market: buy the cheaper exposure and sell the more expensive one. trade, which pairs an index futures position with a “basket” of underlying stocks held in the same proportions as the index.

Why sell the stocks at the close? Because the futures settle against that closing level. Selling the stock basket at the same price makes a loss on one side offset a gain on the other. Sell the stocks too early, and a move in the index over the remaining 10 minutes leaves the futures exposure unhedged.

Which side dominates depends on the size and direction of outstanding paired trades and how many have been rolled forward. Expiration-day closing prices do not always fall—or always rise.

A 2007 Korean study found that expiration-day price effects were concentrated in large KOSPI 200 stocks late in the trading session, with a tendency to reverse the next day. The authors attributed the effect to temporary order imbalances rather than new information.

DEEPER DIVENov. 11, 2010: Inside Those 10 Minutes

What happened · Through Korea Deutsche Securities, sell orders totaling ₩2.4424 trillion were entered for 199 KOSPI 200 constituent stocks during the closing auction. Submitted in seven batches at prices 4.5%–10% below the preceding quotes, the orders accompanied a KOSPI close of 1,914.73, down 53.12 points (2.70%) for the day.

What followed · The Financial Services Commission referred the case for prosecution, alleging linked cash-and-derivatives market manipulation that generated roughly ₩44.8 billion in gains, including from KOSPI 200 put optionsAn option giving its holder the right to sell at a specified strike price. It generally gains value as the underlying asset falls. bought earlier. The Korea Exchange imposed its maximum member penalty of ₩1 billion. A criminal court convicted the defendants at first instance, but an appellate court reversed the ruling. In January 2024, South Korea's Supreme Court upheld the acquittals of Korea Deutsche Securities and its local executive. Proceedings involving overseas defendants who had not appeared in court remained unresolved.

The timing was different then · In 2010, the closing call auction ran from 2:50 to 3:00 p.m. When exchange hours were extended by 30 minutes in August 2016, the auction moved to today's 3:20–3:30 p.m. window.

The final minute · A 2016 study found that expiration-day closing auctions attracted more aggressively priced limit orders—sell limits far below and buy limits far above the indicative clearing price—and that such orders clustered in the final minute before the closing price was fixed.

Orders converging at the close can be grouped into four sources. Beyond cash-and-futures arbitrage unwinds and index-fund rebalancing, institutions with options or single-stock futures exposure may unwind stock hedges at the same closing price. Ordinary investor and institutional orders also enter the auction.

Four Checks Before You Trade

If you plan to buy or sell near the close—or hold a stock affected by the index changes—check these four points.

01Orders Entered After 3:20 p.m.

Orders accumulate in the closing call auction and may execute at a single price determined at 3:30 p.m.
  • Not every order is guaranteed to fill.
  • The indicative auction priceThe estimated price at which the auction would clear based on orders submitted so far. It can change as more orders arrive. displayed on your screen may change sharply before the auction ends.
Unless you specifically need to trade at the closing price, consider using a limit order to control your price rather than a market order. You may not get filled, but you will not buy above or sell below the limit you specified.

02Is Your Stock on the Index-Change List?

If a stock you own or want to buy is among the KOSPI 200 additions or deletions, it may attract large orders at the December 10 close.

03Don't Judge the Stock by the Close Alone

A closing-price move driven by expiration-day order pressure can reverse the next day. Check the next day's opening price and trading as well.

04Did You Place the Order on Nextrade?

Nextrade's regular session does not accept new orders between 3:20 and 3:30 p.m. If you need execution at the Korea Exchange's closing auction, confirm that your order is routed to the KRX (or through a smart order router, SOR). Nextrade separately offers a closing-price session after 3:30 p.m. that executes at that day's KRX closing price.

How to Read Expiration-Day Headlines

program tradingOrders that buy or sell multiple stocks together as a basket. Korean market data separates trades linked to futures arbitrage (“arbitrage”) from other basket trading (“non-arbitrage”). See Part 2.₩XXbn net sellingDistinguish arbitrage from non-arbitrage program flows. Closing a paired futures-and-stocks trade is usually recorded under arbitrage; index constituent or weighting changes are generally recorded under non-arbitrage. When semiconductor-index rebalancing coincided with the September 10 expiration, non-arbitrage program trading recorded roughly ₩3 trillion in net sales.
RolloverThe amount of futures exposure shifted to later expirations before the contract matures. A large roll does not automatically mean an equal reduction in expiration-day stock selling: some rolled futures positions have no paired stock basket.
open interestThe number of futures or options contracts still outstanding, rather than closed out or settled.A decline ahead of expiration signals that contracts are being closed. But open interest alone cannot distinguish arbitrage positions from other investors' trades.
COMMON MISREADINGSPlausible—but wrong
MYTH 01
“Stocks always fall near the close on expiration day.”
There is no predetermined direction. It depends on which orders dominate—selling paired stock baskets, buying them back, or index-fund rebalancing.
MYTH 02
“Expiration doesn't matter if I don't trade futures.”
Your closing-auction order, and the value of stocks held by your fund, can both be determined by that closing price.
MYTH 03
“If futures stop trading at 3:20, settlement is fixed at 3:20.”
No. Settlement uses the KOSPI 200 close 10 minutes later. Those 10 minutes after futures trading ends are central to understanding expiration day.
THE TAKEAWAY · PART 21

The late-day swings of expiration day are often less about a change in market judgment than about orders that must meet one final price arriving at once.

There is no guarantee that the resulting closing price will hold the following day.

Glossary: Terms Used in This Article
Expiration day
The day a futures or options contract reaches its last trading date.
Quadruple witching
In Korea, the second Thursday of March, June, September and December, when index futures and options plus single-stock futures and options expire together.
Simultaneous expiration
The quarterly expiration of index futures and options on the same day.
Final settlement price
The reference value for closing out contracts held through expiry. For KOSPI 200 futures and options, it is the KOSPI 200 closing level on expiration day.
Closing call auction
The auction that gathers orders from 3:20 to 3:30 p.m. and sets the closing price at one clearing price.
Cash-and-futures arbitrage
Buying one side and selling the other to exploit the price difference between an index futures contract and its underlying stock basket.
Rollover
Moving a futures position approaching expiry into a later-dated contract.
Open interest
The number of futures or options contracts that remain outstanding.
Weekly options
KOSPI 200 options that expire on Mondays or Thursdays each week.
Regular constituent review
The scheduled addition or removal of stocks from an index.
Rebalancing
Trading that adjusts a fund's holdings and weights to match a revised index.
Important Notes on the Numbers

3:20 p.m. · On the final trading day, the expiring KOSPI 200 futures contract trades from 8:45 a.m. to 3:20 p.m. KST. Later-dated contracts, and contracts on ordinary days, trade until 3:45 p.m.

Futures settlement · Futures gains and losses are actually marked to market and settled daily. At expiry, the final adjustment is from the prior day's settlement price to the final settlement price. Adding daily settlements over the life of the contract produces the total gain or loss illustrated above. Single-stock futures and options settle against the relevant stock's closing price, not the index.

2.5% versus 2.70% · On November 11, 2010, the KOSPI fell 48 points (2.5%) in the final 10 minutes, versus 53.12 points (2.70%) over the full day. The “one-quarter” comparison is the Financial Services Commission's estimate of final-10-minute selling relative to total daily trading.

The paired-trade example · The index at 1,000, futures at 1,005 and ₩100 million stock basket are hypothetical figures to illustrate the mechanics. A real basket cannot perfectly replicate an index; tracking error also affects returns.

Weekly single-stock options · Weekly options on Samsung Electronics, SK hynix, Hyundai Motor and LG Energy Solution were scheduled for listing on June 29, 2026, but the KRX postponed the launch on June 25. No revised listing date had been announced as of this article's cutoff date.

Academic studies · The 2007 and 2016 studies analyzed earlier periods. Their findings do not imply that the same effect persists today with equal magnitude.
