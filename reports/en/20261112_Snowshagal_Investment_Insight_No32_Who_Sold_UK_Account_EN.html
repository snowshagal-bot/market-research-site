U.S.-linked accounts trimmed their positions while the market climbed; U.K.-linked accounts sold aggressively after it turned. We trace who may have sold, why, and what would count as a genuine return.

The story in five points

  1. Foreign investors did not abandon Korea. Net sales of ₩214tn amounted to 16% of their holdings at the start of the year. The value of what they kept rose, lifting their share of the market from 30.8% to 34.8%.
  2. There were two very different kinds of selling. U.S.-linked accounts pared holdings in the rising markets of February and May; U.K.-linked accounts led selling during March's shock and the June–September slump.
  3. Single-stock concentration limits helped drive sales even as prices rose. As Samsung Electronics and SK hynix surged past some funds' 10% single-name limits, managers mechanically sold down their excess exposure.
  4. Hedge funds trading through London are the leading explanation for U.K.-linked selling. The postmark says London, but the sender may be based somewhere else entirely.
  5. It is too early to call this a return. Foreigners bought KOSPI stocks for three straight sessions on Nov. 10–12, but Nov. 11 buying was concentrated in two chipmakers and Nov. 12 KOSDAQ selling nearly offset KOSPI purchases. The test is whether buying spreads to other sectors.

Introduction

On Nov. 10, foreign investors became net buyers of KOSPI stocks for the first time in eight trading sessions. They remained net buyers through Nov. 12—a three-day streak.1, 2, 3 The KOSPI gained more than 7% across Nov. 10–11,4 then fell 0.6% on Nov. 12 as domestic institutions sold.

Before that, foreigners had net sold ₩214tn of Korean equities in January–September alone.5 That is why headlines spoke of foreigners fleeing Korea. But the picture changes when we examine who was selling each month.

  • “If they sold ₩214tn, doesn't that mean they left?”
  • “Who actually sold—and why?”
  • “Does three straight days of buying mean they're back?”
01 · Did they leave?

They sold ₩214tn—
yet foreign ownership rose

The ₩214tn sold was equivalent to 16% of foreign investors' holdings at the start of the year. Far more remained invested than was sold, and the value of those remaining shares climbed enough to increase foreigners' share of the Korean stock market.

  • Semiconductor shares, which foreigners hold heavily, outperformed the broader market.5, 6 Imagine holding ₩1 million of stock, selling ₩200,000 worth, and then watching the remaining shares double: the investment is now worth ₩1.6 million.
  • It is therefore misleading to equate ₩214tn in net selling with foreigners abandoning Korea. They reduced part of their exposure, and some sales were simply a response to rising portfolio weights.
02 · Who sold?

U.S.-linked accounts trimmed as stocks rose;
U.K.-linked accounts sold into the downturn

The leading sellers changed from month to month. Three distinct episodes account for roughly 98% of the ₩214tn total.

Why sell into a rally?
The 10% single-stock limit

Many overseas funds cannot invest more than 10% of assets in a single security. As Samsung Electronics and SK hynix rallied, funds whose holdings exceeded that limit had to sell the excess mechanically. Goldman Sachs estimated such selling at roughly US$69bn since late October 2025.7 Daishin Securities likewise interpreted May's sales as rebalancing after Korea's weight in portfolios had risen.8

Selling generated by rising prices is not the same as capital fleeing Korea.

  • The intensity of selling was different. Relative to their year-start holdings, January–September net sales amounted to 12% for U.S.-linked accounts (excluding April) but 68% for U.K.-linked accounts.9 U.S.-linked accounts trimmed at the margin; U.K.-linked accounts cut much more deeply.
  • Indeed, U.S.-linked accounts bought ₩17tn of Korean stocks during the July–August slump.10
03 · Who is behind the U.K. accounts?

Postmarked London—
but the sender may be elsewhere

A “U.K.-linked account” does not necessarily hold British investors' money. Trades placed by hedge funds around the world through London investment banks can be attributed to the United Kingdom. Those funds are the leading candidates behind the selling.

When you order something from overseas, the shipping label may show a distribution hub rather than the sender's address. Securities transactions can work much the same way. If a hedge fund in the United States or Hong Kong borrows through a London investment bank (a prime broker) to buy Korean stocks, the bank's U.K. entity may be the name that appears on the books.10 Daishin Securities argues that the FSS attributes trades of this kind to the United Kingdom. A single account in the name of an overseas broker can also aggregate orders from numerous foreign investors.11

Korean equities reported by U.K. residents amount to just over half the value attributed to U.K.-linked accounts. For the United States, the two measures are much closer.

Three reasons to suspect hedge funds

  • Banks tightened financing. On June 12, Citi, JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs raised financing charges and restricted transaction sizes for leveraged positions in the two chipmakers.12 The KOSPI peaked ten days later.
  • Hedge funds did reduce positions. Goldman Sachs client hedge funds unwound positions at a record pace in July, with Korea leading semiconductor selling across Asia.13
  • Past market shocks show a similar pattern. U.K.-linked accounts accounted for 79% of foreign selling during the August 2024 slump and 65% in April 2025.14, 15 By contrast, they were the largest buyers in the rising market of October 2025.16 These flows have been highly sensitive to risk.

Why did the selling last more than four months?

  • June–July: the cost of leverage. Financing was tightened just as the KOSPI lost 22% in July, forcing leveraged investors to reduce positions.17
  • July–September: volatility. Annualized KOSPI volatility this year was 1.5 times its 2008 level. Funds that scale equity exposure to volatility sold more as the market became more turbulent.10
  • Autumn: mounting doubts about AI. Questions grew over whether the pace of AI investment could be sustained. Taiwan also experienced record monthly foreign selling in October.18
Another theory: U.K. pension withdrawals

Korea Investment & Securities pointed to U.K. inheritance-tax changes affecting pensions. From April 2027, unused pension savings left at death will be brought into the inheritance-tax net, potentially encouraging earlier withdrawals.19 U.K. pension withdrawals did, in fact, rise 21.7% over one year.20

But the amounts do not line up well. At last year's withdrawal pace, four months of withdrawals across the entire U.K. pension system would total about ₩55tn (the available statistics run only through March). Even if withdrawals accelerated this year, virtually all the withdrawn money would have needed to come from selling Korean stocks to approach the ₩76tn sold through U.K.-linked accounts in June–September. Pension-related selling may have contributed, but it is unlikely to explain most of that ₩76tn.

What this report cannot establish

Public data do not identify who sold how much within U.K.-linked accounts. Hedge funds' exact share cannot be verified, and country-by-country figures from October onward are not yet available.

04 · Are they coming back?

Three straight days of buying—but still too narrow
The test is whether it broadens

U.S.-linked accounts sold the equivalent of 12% of their year-start holdings and then bought again in July and August. The money that needs to return is the capital sold during the crash. Actual purchases will tell us more quickly than country-of-account statistics whether it is returning.

Conclusion

They trimmed on the way up and sold hard on the way down—
actual buying will show whether they are back

The ₩214tn in foreign net sales reflects two distinct behaviors. U.S.-linked accounts reduced holdings during the rally; U.K.-linked accounts sold heavily during the crash. Hedge funds operating through London are the strongest explanation for the latter. Foreign investors reduced exposure by the equivalent of 16% of their starting holdings, while the value of what remained increased and their ownership share rose. That is not wholesale abandonment of Korea.

A slowdown in selling is not the same as a return to buying. Three tests matter now.

  1. Does foreign buying spread beyond semiconductors?Watch whether foreign net buying on KOSPI exceeds purchases of the two chipmakers combined, and whether investors also buy on KOSDAQ. Daily brokerage data can show this. Adjust for MSCI-related purchases on Nov. 30.
  2. Does VKOSPI keep falling?Less volatility would make it easier for both leveraged and longer-horizon investors to rebuild exposure.
  3. Have U.K.-linked accounts turned net buyers?The FSS November data, due in December, will provide a lagging check.

This is not a recommendation to buy or sell securities. The evidence and calculations appear in the “Deep dive” below.

Whose money sits behind U.K.-linked accounts? Weighing the evidence

Who was responsible for the U.K.-linked accounts that made up 76% of June–September selling? First, distinguish the entity in whose name a trade is recorded, the manager who makes the investment decision, and the ultimate owner of the capital. They need not be the same.

Two sell-side explanations

  • Daishin Securities: hedge-fund deleveraging. Daishin argues that hedge funds using leverage through prime brokers and instruments such as total return swaps (TRS) and contracts for difference (CFDs) reduced their borrowing and sold positions.10
  • Korea Investment & Securities: U.K. pension inheritance-tax reform. From April 6, 2027, unused pension funds remaining at death will be included in the U.K. inheritance-tax estate. The theory is not that current pension balances face an immediate tax, but that savers have an incentive to withdraw earlier. The firm points to the rise in selling from June, after implementation details were published, while acknowledging that the impact cannot be quantified precisely.9, 19

These explanations are not mutually exclusive: they could affect different investor groups at the same time. The table separates what each dataset does—and does not—establish.

A reality check on the amounts

The Korea Investment & Securities argument is that prospective inheritance taxes prompted early pension withdrawals. The size of those withdrawals is therefore the relevant benchmark. Total U.K. pension withdrawals of £91.2bn were worth about ₩166tn. That is a full year's withdrawals across investments in stocks, bonds and cash; four months at the same rate comes to roughly ₩55tn. Yet U.K.-linked accounts sold ₩76tn of Korean shares in June–September alone. Even assuming withdrawals accelerated this year, nearly all the withdrawn money would have had to be funded by Korean stock sales to approach that figure.

The conversion assumes about ₩1,820 per pound. There are also limits to the comparison: withdrawal data end in March 2026, not September, and do not include the trading of employer-managed defined-benefit pension schemes. The figures likewise cannot establish whether pension managers reduced Korean equities for reasons unrelated to withdrawals. The conclusion is therefore limited to: pension withdrawals are unlikely to have been the primary driver.

Assessment. Hedge-fund risk reduction is independently visible in banks' financing restrictions and prime-brokerage data. It is also consistent with the size, volatility and historical behavior of U.K.-linked flows, making it the most plausible explanation. The increase in U.K. pension withdrawals is real, but the scale makes it unlikely to have been the dominant factor. Public data nevertheless cannot establish hedge funds' share of U.K.-linked net sales.

Eight historical episodes of foreign selling

This pattern is not unique to 2026. Across eight episodes since 2024 for which figures are available, U.K.-linked accounts led during abrupt market shocks, while U.S.-linked accounts tended to lead during gradual, multi-month portfolio reductions.

  • U.K.-linked accounts also led the buying. In the rising market of October 2025, U.K.-linked accounts bought ₩2.4tn of the ₩4.2tn in total foreign net purchases—the largest share—while U.S.-linked accounts sold ₩1.0tn.16 The pattern is consistent with fast-moving, risk-sensitive capital: buying most aggressively on the way up and selling most heavily when shocks hit. This is the report's interpretation, not a verified identification of investor type.
  • The hedge-fund evidence lines up. Goldman Sachs prime-brokerage data show that hedge funds bought Asian equities at a record pace in the second week of February, concentrated in Korea, Taiwan and China;26 then made their biggest sales of Taiwanese, Korean and Indian stocks in a year during the mid-March war shock, primarily through short sales.27 In July they reduced positions at a record pace, with Korea driving Asian semiconductor selling.13 These are Goldman Sachs client figures; changes in positioning are not identical to net sales.
  • There are counterexamples. During the three-month September–November 2024 slump driven by semiconductor earnings concerns, U.S.-linked and Luxembourg-linked accounts were the two largest sellers, while U.K.-linked accounts were net buyers in November.28, 29, 30 U.K.-linked accounts do not always lead selling; the pattern is strongest during rapid, disorderly moves.

Shares are calculated against total foreign net sales for the relevant period. November 2025 uses nationality-based Korea Exchange trades for Nov. 1–24; the other periods use FSS settlement data. “Shock” versus “gradual reduction” is this report's classification.14, 15, 31

Why the selling continued for more than four months after the peak

The post-June selling was unusually large and prolonged. Tighter financing, volatility and AI-investment concerns overlapped rather than following neatly one after another; their relative importance shifted over time.

  1. 1June–July

    Banks tightened the cost of leverage

    Event
    On June 12, ten days before the market peak, Citi, JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs raised financing costs and capped the size of leveraged trades in SK hynix and Samsung Electronics.12 Swap financing spreads jumped from SOFR + 1–2 percentage points in early May to as much as roughly SOFR + 10 percentage points by mid-June.17, 32 The KOSPI fell 10.0% on June 23, the day after its peak.10
    Impact
    Maintaining or expanding leveraged positions became harder, and hedge-fund deleveraging was subsequently observed. Public figures do not show how much was forced liquidation versus voluntary reduction. Daishin Securities argues prime brokers cut positions in baskets heavily exposed to semiconductors. Domestic leveraged ETFsalso traded about ₩9.6tn around the June 23 close as they rebalanced, magnifying volatility.10
    Evidence
    Asian equity long/short hedge funds lost 18.6% in July through the 28th, approaching their worst monthly decline on record in Goldman Sachs data.13 U.K.-linked net sales in June: ₩32.2tn
    How it evolved
    By an Aug. 20 report, SK hynix swap-financing spreads had eased to SOFR + 1.5–3 percentage points, less than half their peak.17, 32 U.K.-linked net selling also slowed from ₩32.2tn in June to ₩6.9tn in September. Yet selling persisted into autumn after funding costs eased, so financing conditions alone cannot account for the entire episode.
  2. 2July–September

    Volatility pushed even longer-term capital out

    Event
    Annualized KOSPI volatility jumped from 21.8% in 2025 to 60.7%, or 1.5 times its 2008 level. The index moved more than 5% in a day on 40 occasions this year, against 13 such days in all 16 years from 2010 through 2025 combined.10
    Impact
    For investors whose allocations are scaled mechanically to volatility, maintaining the same risk budget would require a 64% reduction in equity exposure. Daishin Securities' interpretation is that turbulence amplified by short-term trading eventually forced longer-horizon investors to sell as well.10
  3. 3September–early November

    AI concerns deepened—and they were not confined to Korea

    Event
    Concerns about AI investment were already present during July's selloff, but intensified in autumn. The question around U.S. Big Tech capital spending shifted from “How much will they spend?” to “Can they keep spending?” Whenever AI cloud provider CoreWeave's CDScredit-default swap spread (CDS) widened, foreign investors sold the memory-chip-heavy KOSPI.33 After the Nov. 5 U.S. midterm elections, worries about tighter regulation of AI data centers added to the pressure.24
    Taiwan, too
    Foreigners net sold US$31.78bn (about ₩42.4tn) of Taiwanese equities in October, a monthly record.18 Korean net selling in the same month amounted to ₩19.5tn.10 Market sizes differ, so the amounts are not directly comparable. They nevertheless indicate a broader reduction in semiconductor- and AI-related risk beyond Korea.
    Evidence
    Foreign net sales in Korea totaled ₩30.1tn from Oct. 1 to Nov. 6, with the two chipmakers accounting for 75%. CoreWeave's CDS reached a peak of 1,264bp on Nov. 5.

The three forces overlapped rather than operating in discrete phases. When October's account-country figures are released, they can show whether U.K.-linked accounts also led the autumn selling.

Why the entire market fell when semiconductors bore the selling—and who bought

The two chipmakers made up 52% of the KOSPI's market capitalization as of July 15, so their losses dragged down the index;34 they also undermined the margin positions of other leveraged investors, prompting sales of non-semiconductor stocks as well (Daishin Securities).10

  • Retail investors absorbed the first wave, buying ₩32.4tn, but switched to net selling of ₩5.5tn in the second. Brokerage customer cash deposits fell 35%, from ₩139.7tn on June 4 to ₩90.3tn on Nov. 6.10
  • Corporations absorbed the second wave. Other corporationsThey bought a net ₩33.5tn of SK hynix and ₩11.6tn of Samsung Electronics, consistent with buyback activity by the two companies (Daishin Securities).10 Samsung Electronics completed a ₩15tn buyback on Oct. 6;35 SK hynix completed its first ₩40tn round on Oct. 21. Its second ₩20tn program (half earmarked for employee compensation)36is proceeding at about ₩0.35tn per day, roughly one-third the first round's pace.
  • Between Nov. 3 and 6, after Samsung's and SK hynix's first buybacks had ended and only the slower second round remained, foreigners sold ₩10.3tn over four sessions.10 The timing overlaps, but causation has not been established. For more on the buybacks, see NO.19⁠·NO.22 .

How far has the Nov. 10–12 rebound progressed?

  • Buying continued, but remained narrow. Foreigners were net buyers on all three days. On Nov. 11, however, purchases were concentrated in the two chipmakers, while the rest of the market saw net selling. On Nov. 12, KOSPI purchases were offset by KOSDAQ sales, leaving just about ₩18bn of combined net buying.3 That is not yet convincing evidence of a market-wide return.
  • Conditions are beginning to ease. The Nov. 10 rebound came after global investment banks argued that prospective AI data-center regulation would more likely delay projects than halt them, viewing AI fears as overdone. The U.S. semiconductor index had risen 6.9% the previous day.21 VKOSPI then fell 9.33% on Nov. 11.4 On Nov. 12, reports concerning Nvidia's CEO weighed on sentiment, while institutional selling erased the KOSPI's intraday gains and pushed it down 0.6%.3, 39
  • Some judgments should remain open. Foreigners net sold 3,805 KOSPI 200 futures contracts on Nov. 11 and continued selling futures on Nov. 12.3, 22 We cannot tell whether those futures sellers were the same investors buying cash equities. Glossary
Account-country classification (FSS foreign-investment statistics)
The country assigned to holdings and flows in FSS foreign-investor data. If an overseas broker's account aggregates orders from multiple investors, positions may be attributed to the broker's country rather than the ultimate investors' residence. Daishin Securities interprets the classification using the location of the settlement account.
prime broker
A division of a large investment bank that finances hedge funds, lends securities and handles trading and settlement. Many prime-brokerage desks operate out of London.
total return swaps (TRS) and contracts for difference (CFDs)
Derivatives that provide economic exposure to a stock's price changes without necessarily owning the shares. They can support large positions with relatively little margin. When the dealer hedges its exposure by trading shares, those trades may appear under the dealer's account.
Leveraged ETF rebalancing
Trading near the close by 2× or 3× leveraged ETFs to restore their target daily leverage. These funds generally buy more after up days and sell more after down days; their required trades can be estimated from daily returns.
Volatility-targeted allocation
A strategy that cuts equity exposure as volatility rises and adds exposure when volatility falls. Funds and pension investors use such rules to stay within a predetermined risk budget.
settlement-date basis
Figures recorded on the date cash and securities settle, rather than the trade date (typically two business days later). FSS monthly statistics use this basis.
Trade-date basis
Figures recorded on the day an order is executed. Korea Exchange daily investor-flow data use this basis, so they may differ from FSS monthly statistics.
IMF Coordinated Portfolio Investment Survey (CPIS)
The IMF survey in which countries report their residents' holdings of foreign portfolio equities and bonds (CPIS). The focus is the residence of the reporting investor.
non-residents
An investor who does not reside in the country in question—for example, a U.S. or Hong Kong hedge fund using a U.K. account.
leveraged
Taking a position larger than invested capital by borrowing or using derivatives. When prices fall, the investor may need to post additional margin or reduce the position.
VKOSPI
The 30-day forward-looking volatility implied by KOSPI 200 option prices. A higher figure signals expectations of larger market swings.
Other corporations
A company outside the financial sector. When a corporation repurchases its own shares, those transactions can fall under this category.
12-month forward P/E
Share price divided by expected earnings per share over the coming 12 months. A low multiple can suggest cheap shares, but can also reflect fears that earnings are near a cyclical peak.
U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)
The U.S. regulator of futures markets. It publishes weekly positioning reports by trader category.
open interest
The number of outstanding futures contracts that have not yet been closed or settled.
Passive index-tracking capital
Funds that seek to replicate an index such as MSCI's. When constituent weights change, they trade mechanically—often at the scheduled closing auction—to match the new weights.
CDS
A credit-default swap (CDS) spread, the market price of protection against a borrower's default. Higher spreads generally signal greater perceived credit risk.
