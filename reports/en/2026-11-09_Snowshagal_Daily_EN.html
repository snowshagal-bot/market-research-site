CATALYST / 06

U.S. Drug-Price Negotiations Treat the Subcutaneous Version as the Same Drug

The main catalyst behind Alteogen's plunge, plus a separate legal overhang
Alteogen, the KOSDAQ's largest company by market cap
-16.01%

From proposal to effective date

A new route of administration may no longer defer price negotiation; royalties tied to subcutaneous Keytruda face a test

WHAT: What did CMS decide?

The decisionThe Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) announced its final Medicare drug-price negotiation rule on Nov. 5 (U.S. time)

The key provisionWhen the same marketing authorization holder adds to an existing drug to create a new route of administration, the agency treats it as the same drug when determining negotiation eligibility

TERMS: Scope and timing
CMS final drug-price negotiation rule
Proposed ruleJune 12; retained in the final rule
Effective dateDec. 5
Applies toNegotiated prices taking effect in 2029 or later
Drugs selected annuallyUp to 20

The commercial linkMerck's subcutaneous Keytruda uses Alteogen technology. The product generated USD 830mn in Q3 sales, and Alteogen earns royalties tied to those sales

Company's position"The underlying demand for subcutaneous conversion and the competitiveness of the technology remain intact"

Next confirmationAnnouncement of the 2027 negotiation selections, followed by the negotiated price

Two Overlapping Risks for Alteogen

U.S.Drug-pricing rule
Pricing and royalties
EuropePatent dispute
Where it can be sold

The next European milestone is the first Italian injunction hearing on Dec. 2

SHAREHOLDERS: Why it matters

The core riskMerck expects Keytruda to be selected for price negotiation in 2027, with the negotiated price taking effect in January 2029. Under the new rule, the subcutaneous version would be treated as the same drug

The second riskIn October, a Dutch court ordered sales of subcutaneous Keytruda halted across eight European countries. Halozyme also sought an injunction in Italy in September

The company's responseThe company estimates that for the 10 drugs first subject to negotiated prices this year, the effect on net revenue, after rebates and discounts, has generally been within 20%. The quoted discounts of 38%-79% are measured against list prices. The negotiated price and royalty impact for subcutaneous Keytruda are not yet determined
