At a GlanceSUMMARY · Five Takeaways

  1. 01The October statement held rates steady but tilted toward December. The Fed upgraded its assessment of activity from “solid” to “strong,” while Governor Lisa Cook cast her first dissent since joining the Board in 2022—this time in favor of a hike.
  2. 02Markets have largely priced in a December hike, while trimming expectations for subsequent increases. On the meeting day, the two-year Treasury yield fell 3bp, while the 10-year rose 6bp.
  3. 03The path beyond December depends less on how high oil climbs than on how persistent its impact on core inflation becomes. Energy prices (+20.4%) drove September's headline inflation higher. Chair Kevin Warsh said the Fed's job was to prevent those price increases from spreading across the broader economy.
  4. 04If the Fed hikes in December, the Bank of Korea's November decision will determine the U.S.–Korea policy-rate differential. A BoK hold would widen the spread to 1.25 percentage points, back to its level at the start of the year; a BoK hike would keep it at the current 1.00 percentage point.
  5. 05One separate development: The Fed plans to dedicate one regular meeting in fall 2027 to monetary policy strategy, without a rate decision. The number of scheduled meetings—eight per year—would stay the same.

Markets spent the final week of October caught between the Federal Reserve and oil prices. On October 28 (early October 29 in Korea), the Fed kept its target range at 3.75–4.00%. Oil, meanwhile, swung between news of Iran negotiations and warnings of renewed U.S. strikes after the November 3 midterm elections. WTI settled at $104.12 on October 30, its highest level in six months. The hold itself was expected. Two questions remain for investors: Will the Fed actually raise rates again in December? And will it keep hiking afterward? Investment Insight No. 14 identified the scale and duration of the remaining tightening as the key issues for this meeting. The statement, the press conference and remarks from Fed officials two days later offered the first clues.4, 5

Toward December

What Stands Out

  • Cook's dissent was her first since joining the Board in 2022. She backed rate increases in 2022 and again this September. Economists quoted by the AP saw her dissent as evidence that support for further tightening remains broad.6, 7, 8, 9
  • There were already pro-hike dissents in July. Those came from three regional Fed presidents—Hammack, Kashkari and Logan—in a 9–3 vote. This time, a member of the Board of Governors joined them.10, 11
  • The September dot plot already pointed to “one more this year.” The median year-end 2026 projection of 4.125% is 25bp above the midpoint of the current range (3.875%). Sixteen of the 18 participants projected at least one additional hike.12

In September, the second sentence of the inflation paragraph emphasized the benefit of that day's rate increase. In October, it was replaced by language about gathering more information before deciding. The statement presented the pause as a decision to await more evidence, not as a response to economic weakness. iM Securities interpreted the wording change as a clear signal of a December hike; Eugene Investment & Securities called it a “pause.”13, 14

The statement does not list the ten members voting to hold. Their identities will be confirmed when the minutes are released on November 18 (early November 19 in Korea).

The Press Conference Sounded Less Hawkish,
and Markets Trimmed Bets on Hikes Beyond December

The two-year yield rose when the Fed hiked in September. When it held in October, the two-year fell while longer yields climbed.

Long Yields Rose on the Hold Day

Daily change in U.S. Treasury yields by maturity (bp) · U.S. Treasury par-yield data · Hatched bars: September 16 (hike) · Red bars: October 28 (hold)

What Warsh Said at the Press Conference

  • Financial conditions: “Financial and credit conditions have tightened for some businesses and households, much as we would expect.” He also said investors had become more discerning about how they allocate capital.17
  • December: “The period right in front of us is information rich.” Warsh said he would not prejudge the meeting and did not believe in forward guidance outside a crisis.18, 19
  • Market pricing: CME FedWatch showed a December hike probability of 84.2% on the meeting day, 79.6% on the morning of October 29 after the September PCE and third-quarter GDP releases, and roughly 75% by October 30—close to the 73.3% reading a week before the meeting.2, 20
  • Fed officials two days later: Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan, a 2026 voting member, said on October 30 that “at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases” were needed, followed by a pause to assess conditions. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem, a nonvoting member, also called for additional tightening “soon.”21, 22

In its post-meeting review, DS Investment & Securities argued that expectations for a December hike remained intact after the press conference, while expectations for hikes beyond December embedded in the two-year yield eased somewhat. Reuters likewise reported that markets still priced in roughly two additional hikes over the coming months. Kiwoom Securities saw limited scope for aggressive, back-to-back increases, given that high borrowing costs were already making AI-related funding more selective.7, 11, 23

The rise in long yields cannot be attributed to the Fed meeting alone. WTI also gained 2.9% that day, and Kiwoom Securities cited a higher term premium—the extra compensation investors demand to hold longer-dated bonds—reflecting fiscal and inflation risks. The 10-year yield subsequently fell to 4.94% by October 30 amid a tech-stock selloff following Alphabet's and Amazon's earnings.23, 24, 25, 26

Why Oil Is the Pivot

  • The Fed is watching whether inflation spreads. Chair Warsh said energy-related prices had risen again in recent weeks, and that the Fed's job was to prevent relative price changes in individual sectors from spreading throughout the economy.8
  • Oil prices kept climbing. WTI gained more than 15% in October. On October 30, reports emerged of a tanker being struck by a limpet mine in the Strait of Hormuz. It was the first successful attack of that kind since the war began in February.4
  • Wage growth has not yet reaccelerated. Wages and salaries in the third-quarter Employment Cost Index rose 0.7% from the prior quarter, down from 0.9% in Q2. Those figures, however, cover only the period through September. Richmond Fed President Thomas Barkin, who is not a 2026 voting member, said on October 30 that businesses faced mounting costs for diesel, semiconductors and skilled labor, and that companies in his district appeared willing to pass them through to customers. The October inflation data, due in November, will show how far that pass-through reaches core services prices.32, 33

Oil Has Catalysts in Both Directions

▲ Potential Upside Pressures

  • 10.22The New York Times reported a plan for three days of intensive strikes after the election; presidential approval remains uncertain34
  • 10.23President Trump: “We may do another little Epic Fury, or they'll agree.” Operation Epic Fury was the name of the February U.S. strikes on Iran35, 36, 37
  • 10.29U.S. officials warned allies to prepare for possible renewed military action after the election (AP)38

▼ Potential Downside Pressures

  • 10.29Oil fell more than $2 intraday on news of a letter from Vice President Vance and a new Qatari draft peace proposal. Losses narrowed after a senior Iranian official dismissed the draft as “unacceptable ideas that did not amount to an offer”39, 40
  • 10.30Qatari mediators held talks in Tehran for more than 12 hours; a diplomatic source reported “positive progress” (Axios). Talks were set to continue in Doha on October 31. Trump said, “We have time”41, 42, 43
  • 10.31China's manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3 in October from 50.1 in September, signaling contraction; new orders came in at 49.4. Softer demand could limit oil-price gains44

The obstacle is sequencing. Washington wants nuclear concessions before talks resume; Tehran first wants the naval blockade lifted, frozen funds returned and oil sales permitted. Reports that a U.S. delegation might join direct talks in Doha have not been confirmed by either government.42, 45

iM Securities argues that oil will determine whether the Fed keeps hiking after December. If military operations resume and damage to Gulf energy infrastructure persists, WTI could exceed $112.95, its high since the Iran war began (April 7). But if a brief operation provides an exit strategy, prices could stabilize quickly, easing inflation and rate-hike concerns alike.13, 46

In its October 6 outlook, the U.S. Energy Information Administration projected Brent crude averaging $105 in Q4 2026 before falling to $84 in 2027; the forecast predates the subsequent oil surge. Under that scenario, headline inflation could move closer to core inflation.47

What Stands Out

  • Most brokerages expect the BoK to raise its rate to 3.25% in November. Kyobo, Hanwha, Hyundai Motor Securities, Eugene and Hana forecast one hike in November and another in Q1 2027, bringing the rate to 3.50%. Eugene says the scope for further hikes will depend on next year's economic conditions.27, 28, 29, 51, 52
  • The rate differential has not dictated the won's direction. The Korean won strengthened even after the U.S. rate hike widened the gap in September. USD/KRW closed at 1,334.3 on October 30. Investment Insight No. 16 pointed to South Korea's large current-account surplus as an important reason.53, 54

Domestic inflation, growth and housing prices will all factor into the BoK's decision. Before its meeting, the October U.S. CPI release on November 10, the October PCE release on the evening before the meeting (KST), and oil prices could shift market confidence in the Fed's December path.55, 56

5COUNTERARGUMENTS · 05

Three Factors That Could Delay or Change the Conclusion

One

Even a December Hike Is Not a Done Deal

Chair Warsh said he would not prejudge the outcome. While he acknowledged tighter financial and credit conditions for some businesses and households, he stopped short of calling conditions restrictive overall. A roughly 75% hike probability also implies about a one-in-four chance of a hold.2, 13, 17, 19

Two

Falling Oil Would Not Necessarily End the Tightening Cycle

Even a diplomatic breakthrough and lower oil prices would not automatically end tightening. Annualized Q3 GDP growth was 3.4%, up from 2.2% in Q2. September core PCE inflation rose to 3.1% year over year from 3.0% in August, and both dissenting voters favored a hike. Yet core PCE inflation on an annualized quarter-over-quarter basis slowed to 2.5% in Q3 from 3.3% in Q2.1, 20, 31, 57

Three

Oil Alone Does Not Explain Long-Term Yields

WTI rose more than 15% in October, yet the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield fell 35bp, from 5.29% to 4.94%. The October 30 selloff in AI-related stocks prompted a flight to the safety of government bonds, according to the AP. Hana Securities found that the correlation between the 10-year yield and WTI shifted from roughly +0.6 to −0.3 after the September FOMC meeting. Warsh also noted that market yields had recently declined even as oil rose.4, 18, 25, 26, 29

The first factor puts December's decision itself in question; the second changes the path that follows. The third shows why the Fed's policy trajectory and the 10-year Treasury yield need not move together: A December hike would not automatically mean higher long-term yields.

6MEETINGS · A SEPARATE DEVELOPMENT

The Fed Plans to Turn One Fall 2027 Meeting
Into a Strategy Session Without a Rate Decision

That would mean one fewer scheduled rate-setting opportunity, while the chair avoids signaling the next decision in advance. The two-year yield turned lower when the change was announced during the press conference.

Seven Rate-Setting Meetings in 2027,
Possibly Six in 2028

Regular FOMC meetings · 2026 and 2027 dates as published by the Fed · Strategy-meeting plans based on Chair Warsh's press conference remarks

What Chair Warsh Said

  • Frequency: “Eight meetings per year is the right number,” Warsh said. In 2027, one meeting would address broad monetary policy strategy “with neither a vote on the federal funds rate nor a policy statement.” For 2028, he anticipated two strategy meetings and six policy-setting meetings.17, 59
  • Timing: The strategy meeting will be held in fall 2027. The exact date and what, if anything, will be released afterward have not been decided.59, 60
  • Rationale: The aim is to review near-term conditions regularly while setting aside time and space for longer-horizon strategic questions.59

DS Investment & Securities said investors viewed fewer rate-setting opportunities as a possible signal of slower tightening. It also suggested short-term yields could become more volatile on major data-release days than on meeting dates.11

This is a statement from the chair's press conference, not a formal Fed press release. It is still unclear whether the committee voted on the change or whether the 2028 schedule is final. Even with fewer scheduled rate decisions, the Fed can meet outside its regular calendar if needed, as it did for its emergency rate cut on March 15, 2020.61

The Bottom Line

Markets have already priced in much of a December hike. The next stage of the rate cycle will depend less on the oil-price increase itself than on how persistently it feeds through to core inflation. That is why investors should watch the direction of oil after the election, alongside the core readings in the November 10 CPI and November 25 PCE releases. The Bank of Korea meets first, on November 26. Its decision will determine whether the U.S.–Korea rate gap remains at 1.00 percentage point or widens to 1.25 after a December Fed hike.

Glossary · Key Terms
Basis point (bp)
0.01 percentage point; 25bp equals 0.25 percentage point.
Hawkish vs. dovish
Hawks emphasize containing inflation and favor tighter policy; doves place more weight on economic weakness and are more cautious about rate hikes.
FOMC
Federal Open Market Committee, the Fed body that sets the target range for the federal funds rate. It normally meets eight times a year.
Dot plot
A chart of FOMC participants' projections of the appropriate policy rate at year-end. Published with the Fed's economic projections in March, June, September and December.
Forward guidance
A central bank's communication about its expected future policy-rate path.
Headline vs. core inflation
Headline includes the full consumer basket; core excludes the more volatile food and energy components.
PCE price index
The Personal Consumption Expenditures price index, which the Fed uses to define its 2% inflation objective.
FedWatch
CME Group's estimate of probabilities for rate decisions at each Fed meeting, inferred from federal funds futures prices.
U.S.–Korea policy-rate differential
The upper bound of the U.S. federal funds target range minus the Bank of Korea base rate. Korean reports sometimes call a higher U.S. rate an “inverted” differential; this is not an inversion of the Treasury yield curve.
Employment Cost Index (ECI)
Measures quarterly changes in employer spending on wages and benefits. It adjusts for shifts in the mix of jobs and industries, making it useful for assessing wage pressures.
PMI
Purchasing Managers' Index, a survey-based measure of business activity. Readings above 50 indicate expansion; below 50 indicate contraction.
Annualized rate
A quarterly change expressed as the yearly rate that would result if the same pace persisted for four quarters. It is not the same as year-over-year growth.
FOMC voting rights
Twelve people vote on FOMC policy: the seven Fed governors and the New York Fed president vote every year, while four of the other eleven regional Fed presidents rotate annually.
Correlation coefficient
A measure of how closely two variables move together. Values near +1 indicate movement in the same direction; values near −1 indicate opposite directions.
