Executive SummaryFIVE KEY POINTS

  1. 01On Oct. 29, Samsung merely reaffirmed its planned total shareholder returns; the allocation of the remaining ₩55–75tn was left to the late-January board meeting, as announced in August. The previous day, SK hynix authorized a ₩20tn open-market buyback over three months, with purchases starting the following day.
  2. 02All ₩35tn of confirmed 2026 returns consists of cash dividends for Q1–Q3. The ₩15tn in shares repurchased between August and October is earmarked for employee compensation and excluded from shareholder returns.
  3. 03The remaining funds have three possible uses: a final or special dividend, cancellation of common shares, or cancellation of preferred shares. Canceling common shares would lift the combined holdings of Samsung Life Insurance and Samsung Fire & Marine Insurance above the 10% threshold under Korean financial-sector law. Analysts therefore lean toward preferred-share cancellations; Daishin Securities envisages roughly ₩25tn.
  4. 04For retail investors, the two dates to watch are the year-end dividend record date and the late-January board meeting. If the record date remains Dec. 31, shares would need to be purchased by Dec. 28. An all-dividend allocation would imply approximately ₩8,400–11,500 per share—an illustrative upper bound, not company guidance.
  5. 05The bigger question is what Samsung will do from 2027 onward. Daishin's ₩9.5tn estimate for 2027 includes only the existing regular dividend, while SK Securities calculates ₩193tn of potential funds if Samsung maintains a 50% free-cash-flow return policy. These measure different things and are not endpoints of a forecast range.

In the final week of October, Korea's two largest memory-chip companies reported third-quarter results one day apart. On Oct. 28, when SK hynix announced a ₩20tn open-market buyback over three months, the KOSPI jumped 6.63%. SK hynix climbed 10.89% and Samsung Electronics rose 9.01%. The next day, Samsung reported ₩107tn in quarterly operating profit and declared a dividend of ₩4,601 per share, totaling roughly ₩30tn. Yet the stock lost about 7% over the following two sessions, giving back most of the previous day's gain, before rebounding 1.78% on Nov. 2. Two questions dominated: Why did shares fall despite a plan to return up to ₩110tn? And when, and how much, will an individual shareholder actually receive? Both lead back to the ₩55–75tn whose allocation remains unresolved.4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10

What Matters

  • The remaining amount has not shrunk by ₩5tn. In August, the balance was described as ₩60–80tn because only the planned ₩30tn Q3 dividend had been deducted. In October, Samsung also counted ₩4.9tn of first-half dividends already paid, restating the split as ₩35tn confirmed plus ₩55–75tn remaining.2, 3
  • The ₩15tn buyback was not a shareholder payout. From Aug. 24, Samsung bought approximately 53.8 million shares, more than the roughly 53.28 million originally planned. Because they were acquired for employee compensation, the purchases do not count toward the ₩90–110tn return plan. The company's buying stopped when the program finished in early October.1, 12, 14
  • The April cancellations belong to a different return period. On April 2, Samsung canceled 73.36 million common shares and 13.60 million preferred shares. Those shares had been acquired in 2025, so they are not included in the 2026 ₩90–110tn program.15
  • The unusually large total reflects surging cash generation. Samsung's 2024–26 policy calls for returning 50% of cumulative three-year free cash flow (FCF). The company estimates total returns over that period at ₩120–140tn. Almost five times the previous annual high of ₩20.3tn in 2020 is now concentrated in 2026 alone.1

Samsung says the board will decide how to use the remaining funds in January. Under its current policy, the final three-year return amount depends on 50% of cumulative FCF from 2024 through 2026; it cannot be finalized until the 2026 results are confirmed. Meanwhile, there is no announced large-scale Samsung buyback to support the stock directly over the next three months.1, 6, 16

Why Samsung Fell: Three Overlapping Factors

  • Investors had been looking for immediate open-market buying. Samsung Securities argued that investors had hoped Samsung would begin repurchases sooner, but the announcement fell short of that expectation.13
  • Semiconductor profits were slightly below the more optimistic expectations. Ahead of the release, some investors expected operating profit in Samsung's Device Solutions (DS) division to approach ₩110tn. It came in at ₩106.5tn. Companywide operating profit nevertheless beat the consensus estimate by 1.7%.19
  • The second day of losses had other drivers. On Oct. 30, a broader tech sell-off linked to Alphabet's deteriorating cash flow sent the KOSPI down 3.48%. Samsung's preferred shares fell 5.33%, more than its common shares (−3.02%).9

What Buybacks Can—and Cannot—Do

  • Buybacks can cushion selling pressure, but cannot dictate market direction. According to Kiwoom Securities, Samsung and SK hynix bought ₩46.5tn of their own shares between August and October—1.7 times the ₩26.6tn in net foreign selling across the two stocks—while the KOSPI edged up 0.4%. Over the next ten trading sessions, as buybacks slowed to 60% of net foreign selling, the KOSPI fell 17.4%.20
  • Shares rose on Nov. 2 even without a new Samsung buyback. Institutions bought ₩1.2tn in net terms, and Samsung rebounded 1.78%. Kiwoom also identified the outlook for semiconductor earnings and foreign-investor flows as the keys to a sustained recovery.10, 20

After Samsung's employee-compensation purchases ended in early October, combined buybacks by the two chipmakers fell from a September daily average of ₩1.6tn to ₩0.79tn (Daishin Securities). SK hynix launched a separate ₩20tn program on Oct. 29. The 23.69 million SK hynix shares bought for ₩40tn between August and October—3.24% of shares outstanding—are scheduled to be canceled on Nov. 5. That is the retirement of shares already purchased, not a fresh wave of buy orders on the cancellation date.4, 14, 21

Why the Debate Is Tilting Toward Preferred Shares

  • Brokerage forecasts point in the same direction. Daishin Securities expects roughly ₩25tn in cancellations, focused on preferred shares, if Samsung proceeds. Daol Investment & Securities also expects the preferred-share proportion to increase.13, 25, 26
  • Activist investors are making the same case. Palliser Capital and Dalton Investments have publicly called for repurchases and cancellations of preferred shares, pointing to discounts of roughly 26–27% versus common shares. The three-year average discount is 18%.27
  • The scale would be material. ₩25tn equals about 18% of Samsung's preferred-share market capitalization of roughly ₩142tn as of Nov. 2. 404 · WHAT SHAREHOLDERS RECEIVE

For individual investors, the key dates are
the dividend record-date notice and the January board meeting

Eligibility for the Q3 dividend has already been determined. Any cash dividend paid from the remaining funds will go to holders eligible on a future record date. Repurchases and cancellations will follow their own execution timetable.

If I buy Samsung shares now, will I get the Q3 dividend of ₩4,601?

No. The record date was Sept. 30, and the dividend will be paid on Nov. 27 to the eligible shareholders of record.7, 11

When must I buy shares to qualify for the remaining dividend?

If the record date stays Dec. 31, as in previous years, the purchase deadline would be Monday, Dec. 28. The exchange is closed on Dec. 31 and Korean equity trades settle two business days after execution (T+2). That would make Dec. 29 the ex-dividend date. A special dividend could have a separate record date, so investors should check company notices again in December.11

Will common and preferred shareholders receive different dividends?

Not for Q3: both classes received ₩4,601 per share. With preferred shares trading 22.7% below common shares, the same cash dividend produces a higher dividend yield on the preferred class. A January decision to cancel preferred shares could create additional demand for them. But the discount also reflects a lack of voting rights and thinner liquidity; a lower price alone does not prove undervaluation.7, 9

What if Samsung pays all the remaining funds as dividends?

Using the Q3 payout ratio—₩30tn in total for ₩4,601 per share—a simple pro-rata calculation implies about ₩8,400 per share from ₩55tn and ₩11,500 from ₩75tn. These are mathematical upper bounds, not forecasts: Samsung has signaled a significant cancellation program, and whatever goes to buybacks reduces the amount available for dividends.

Is a cash dividend equivalent to a net investment gain?

No. Once shares trade ex-dividend, the market price may adjust downward to reflect the payment, and Korean dividend withholding tax is 15.4%. Cancellation reduces the share count rather than paying cash directly to holders; it does not guarantee an increase in the share price either.

What Matters

  • The potential funding looks substantial. Market estimates cited by Seoul Economic Daily put Samsung's 2027 and 2028 FCF at ₩376tn and ₩344tn, respectively. Applying the current 50% policy would imply about ₩360tn of potential return capacity over those two years.30

606 · WHAT COULD CHANGE THE OUTCOME

Three variables that could delay or change the outcome

ONE

What if January brings mostly dividends?

IBK Securities cut its price target to ₩350,000, arguing that the ₩30tn dividend disappointed expectations. Some analysts also expect a dividend-heavy program to be valued less favorably than cancellations.3, 25

TWO

Funding depends on semiconductor earnings

Because the policy is tied to FCF, the memory-chip cycle will shape the next payout. Samsung says it has signed long-term supply agreements with ten customers and is negotiating with ten more; talks over 2027 HBM supply volumes have reached its targeted pricing levels. Actual shipments and revenue will follow. At the same time, Samsung expects 2026 capital spending to reach ₩84.4tn, up ₩31.7tn year on year. Higher earnings do not automatically translate into more FCF if investment spending rises too.8, 16, 32, 33

THREE

The buyback gap before January

The employee-compensation buyback has ended, and Samsung has not announced a new repurchase schedule. Industry talk of another ₩30tn-plus employee-bonus buyback remains unconfirmed; even if it goes ahead, those shares would not count toward shareholder returns.14, 30

The first variable affects the quality of January's announcement; the second, the scale of returns from 2027 onward. The third means share prices could be more sensitive to investor flows than to earnings over the roughly three months until January.

Closing View

The ₩90–110tn figure was announced back in August. So far, just ₩35tn in dividends has been confirmed. What January will decide is how—and when—Samsung allocates the remaining ₩55–75tn, and whether the underlying approach carries over into the policy for 2027 and beyond. For the structure of the payout, watch the share of cancellations and the split between common and preferred shares. For timing, watch the repurchase schedule. Because of the 10% ownership threshold under Korean financial-sector law, the market currently expects preferred-share cancellations to lead.

Glossary
Shareholder returns
Capital returned to shareholders through cash dividends or share buybacks and cancellations.
Share repurchases and cancellations
A repurchase occurs when a company buys back its shares; cancellation retires those shares and reduces the outstanding share count. This increases each surviving share's proportionate claim on the company.
Preferred shares
Samsung's non-voting preferred equity, not a bond or a fixed-income claim. Economic rights, including dividends, are similar or equivalent to those of common shares. Samsung paid the same Q3 dividend on both classes.
Common–preferred discount
The price gap between Samsung's common and preferred shares, expressed as a percentage of the common-share price.
Dividend record date · ex-dividend date
Investors must be shareholders of record on the record date. Because Korean equity trades settle in two business days, purchases ordinarily must be made two business days earlier. The ex-dividend date is when shares begin trading without entitlement to the dividend. Korea does not mechanically adjust the reference price for a cash dividend.
Free cash flow (FCF)
Cash generated from operations after capital expenditures; the metric underpinning Samsung's 2024–26 shareholder-return policy.
Korean financial-sector ownership law
The Act on the Structural Improvement of the Financial Industry (commonly referred to in Korean as the Geumsan Act). It requires Financial Services Commission approval for certain holdings by financial affiliates above prescribed thresholds in another company's voting shares. Samsung Life and Samsung Fire & Marine together hold approximately 10% of Samsung Electronics common shares; moving above the threshold can require approval or a sale of excess holdings. The insurers sold shares ahead of the March cancellation.
Special dividend
A one-off or additional dividend paid separately from regular scheduled dividends.
