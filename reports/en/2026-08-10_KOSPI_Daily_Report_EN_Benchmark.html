Review Note

The direction was right, but the reason was different. All three internal-market signals cited for the upside persisted, yet the jobs report—excluded from the probability score—was what actually drove today's session. And the outcome of that report, which had been classified as a downside risk, worked in the opposite direction. The revised conditions introduced on Aug. 7 were applied for the first time today and worked as intended. Under the old conditions, the signal would have failed simply because both top market-cap stocks fell. But a decline of less than 0.5% cannot reasonably be called a sharp drop.

One-Line Glossary
sidecar
A circuit-breaker mechanism that suspends program-trading quotes for five minutes when index futures move sharply. The trigger conditions differ between KOSPI and KOSDAQ. For KOSPI, KOSPI200 futures must move at least ±5% for one minute. For KOSDAQ, KOSDAQ150 futures must move at least 6% and the cash index at least 3%. On Aug. 10, a KOSDAQ buy-side sidecar was triggered at 09:50; KOSPI did not trigger one.
Limit Up⁠· Limit Down
The maximum daily price move of ±30%. On Aug. 10, 11 KOSDAQ stocks hit limit-up; no KOSPI stocks hit limit-up, and neither market had a limit-down stock.
Employment Report
Monthly labor-market data released by the U.S. Department of Labor. Nonfarm payrolls and the unemployment rate are the key figures and are widely used as market benchmarks. The July report was released on the night of Aug. 7, with nonfarm payrolls down by 23,000.
Labor-Force Participation Rate
The share of the working-age population that is either employed or actively seeking work. If this rate falls at the same time, the unemployment rate alone becomes less informative about labor conditions.
CPI⁠· PPI
CPI is the Consumer Price Index and PPI the Producer Price Index. Both are released monthly by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, with markets typically focusing on CPI first. July data are due on the nights of Aug. 12 and Aug. 13.
Russell 2000
An index of 2,000 U.S. small- and mid-cap stocks. It is rate-sensitive and often outperforms large-cap indexes when rate pressure eases.
SOX
Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX), a key gauge of global semiconductor stocks
consensus⁠· guidance
Consensus is the average of brokerage forecasts; guidance is the company's own outlook for the next quarter. On earnings days, share prices often react more to guidance than to the reported quarter.
Short Selling
Selling borrowed shares to bet on a stock decline. KOSPI short selling of KRW1.36tn on Aug. 10 equaled 5.4% of combined KRX+NXT turnover. The KRW18.84tn KOSPI turnover figure in the body is KRX-only, so the denominators differ.
Program Trading
Automated orders that buy or sell multiple stocks at once, an important route through which indexes and investor flows move. Because program trades are already included in investor-level flow data, they are not added separately.
Arbitrage⁠· Non-arbitrage
Arbitrage program trading exploits price differences between cash and futures; non-arbitrage refers to other program trading such as index-tracking orders. Non-arbitrage is a major channel for foreign and institutional cash-equity orders.
Basis⁠· Cash-Futures Closing Basis
Basis is futures price minus the cash index. A positive value is contango, the normal structure in which futures trade above cash; a negative value is backwardation. But the cash market closes at 15:30 and KOSPI200 futures at 15:45, so simply subtracting the two closing prices does not produce a same-time basis. That is why this report calls it the closing spread and does not use it as a directional signal.
Onshore Daytime Close
The final USD/KRW transaction in the domestic FX daytime session (09:00–15:30).
Base Exchange Rate
The benchmark exchange rate published as a volume-weighted average of interbank transactions. Used here for JPY/KRW.
Sector⁠· Theme Change
Sector indexes are market-cap weighted, while theme returns are simple averages of constituent stocks. Because the methodologies differ, even identically named groups can show different returns, and themes with fewer constituents can produce larger moves. Where values differ, this report uses the sector figure as the reference.
Market Breadth
The share of stocks that advanced, rather than the index itself—a gauge of how broadly gains or losses are distributed.
Materials, Parts & Equipment
A Korean shorthand for materials, parts and equipment suppliers. In semiconductors, it generally refers to companies supplying materials and equipment to large memory-chip makers; many are listed on KOSDAQ.
