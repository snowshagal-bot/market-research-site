Short on time?Three-line summary
  • The won-dollar rate in the news is usually the 3:30 p.m. figure. The market stays open after that, so the trading day ends at 7 a.m. the next morning (6 a.m. in summer).
  • The official base rateThe reference rate set by Korea's Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulation. Seoul Money Brokerage publishes it every business day. It is not the “base rate” a bank sets for itself round by round. In Korean, 매매기준율., published by Seoul Money Brokerage, is the previous trading day's daytime trading averaged by volume. The “base rate” in a bank app starts the morning at this figure, then the bank resets it round by round. From January 2027 the official one becomes an average of the five minutes around 4 p.m.
  • Before reading “up” or “down,” check what it was compared with. The same day can come out up or down, depending on the comparison.

01WHERE

Same Friday,
Three Rates

On Friday, Nov. 20, the KOSPI fell more than 5%. That night in New York, the Nasdaq dropped more than 5%. Look up Friday's won-dollar rate on Monday morning and there is more than one number: 1,336.2 won in the news, 1,346.0 won in the “close” column of the Bank of Korea's statistics, and 1,337.3 won, dated Monday, on Seoul Money Brokerage's website. None of them is wrong.

The won-dollar rate is how many won it takes to buy one dollar. If it goes from 1,336 to 1,346, the dollar has become more expensive and the won has lost value.

Since July 6, 2026, the won-dollar pair has traded 24 hours on weekdays. Right now the market opens at 7 a.m. Monday and closes at 7 a.m. Saturday, an hour earlier during U.S. daylight saving timeClocks move forward an hour in summer. In New York it ran from March 8 to Nov. 1, 2026. During that period the Seoul won-dollar market ends its day an hour earlier, at 6 a.m.. So each figure cuts the day at a different point.

Read the small print first

Labels such as “15:30,” “close” (종가), “round” (회차) or “as of ○○ Bank” tell you when the figure was taken and who published it. If there is no label, find the source.

Why the base rate differs from the close

The official base rate is an average, not the last price, and it is weighted by volume. Here is how that works on a made-up day.

Friday's daytime average was 1,337.3 won. The nearly 10 won added overnight is not in that figure, posted on Monday. Tuesday's 1,346.6 won is the average of trades actually done during Monday's session.

Why the bank's “buy” price is higher

Banks post new rates several times a day. Each posted rateThe rate at which a bank says it will buy and sell foreign currency. Banks issue new ones round by round through the day. starts from the bank's own base rate. According to Seoul Money Brokerage, every bank opens the morning with the official base rate, then each resets its own round by round as the market moves. From there the bank prices what customers buy above it and what they sell below it. Buying dollar bills with won before a trip uses the “buy cash” rate; changing leftover dollars back into won afterward uses “sell cash.”

That day the official base rate was a single figure, 1,337.30 won, Friday's daytime average. The bank's base rate was reset as Monday's market moved, and the last round stood at 1,344.50 won, 7.2 won higher.

An NDFA won-dollar contract that fixes a future rate now and settles only the difference, in dollars, at maturity. Traded outside Korea. comes in several maturities; morning stories usually cite the one-month. A swap pointThe gap between the one-month forward rate and the spot rate. It reflects things like the interest-rate gap between the two countries; it is not a forecast of the future rate. is the gap between the forward price and the spot price. Since forward = spot + swap point, you subtract the swap point to get back to spot. Taking away a negative number made the figure bigger. 1,346.3 won is not a traded price but one converted for comparison; on this day it was almost exactly where Seoul closed that trading day (1,346.0 won).

Seoul used to shut at night, so the NDF was the main window on the won overnight. Now that Seoul trades through the night, the end-of-day close is watched as well. The NDF still appears in morning stories because it is a separate market where overseas participants trade.

03WATCH OUT

Changing Clocks,
Common Mistakes

The time behind each exchange-rate figure has moved over the years and with the seasons. It is about to move again.

  1. July 2024

    The close moved from 3:30 p.m. to 2 a.m. the next day (July 1).

  2. July 2026

    24-hour weekday trading (July 6). The day was cut at 6 a.m., in line with the New York close.

  3. Nov. 2026

    New York daylight saving time ended, so the cut moved to 7 a.m. from Nov. 2. It goes back to 6 a.m. when New York daylight saving returns on March 14, 2027.

  4. Jan. 2027

    The official base rate becomes “the previous business day's 4 p.m. time-weighted averagePrices sampled at regular intervals in the few minutes around a set time, averaged evenly regardless of volume. TWAP for short.” (Jan. 1).

The 3:30 p.m. rate stays through the first half of 2027; whether to keep it after that will be decided again toward the end of June. The authorities said they would publish statistics and press releases on that basis. The reason given is that 3:30 p.m. is when the stock market's regular session ends, which suits currency exchange for stock trades and some institutions' valuations. Records such as “the highest close in so many years” also chain together daytime closes. Until June 2024, that time was also when the market closed. Some say thin overnight trading makes prices jump, but the Bank of Korea found that volatility from 3:30 p.m. to 9 a.m. did not rise after the market went 24 hours.

In 2027 the base rate changes more than its clock time. A volume-weighted average of six and a half hours of daytime trading becomes an even average of the five minutes around 4 p.m., to match 24-hour trading and overseas practice. Anything calculated with the base rate, such as tax on overseas stock gains, follows. When you chain base rates across 2027, note that the name stays the same but the method changes.

HolidaysThe won-dollar pair trades on Korean public holidays. It is closed on weekends and Jan. 1. Settlement, when the money actually moves, happens on a day when banks in both Korea and the U.S. are open, and a new official base rate is posted only on business days
Your exchangeThe 24 hours refer to trading between banks. Each bank sets its own hours for currency exchange and transfers
First and last daysThe first business day of the year opens at 9 a.m.; the last closes at midnight
Other currenciesDirect won-yuan trading still runs only from 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Won rates for the yen or euro are worked out through the dollar (see More)
MISTAKE LOGFour common mistakes
MISTAKE 01
“The FX market closes at 3:30 p.m.”
The won-dollar pair trades 24 hours on weekdays. The 3:30 figure is one point in the day.
MISTAKE 02
“I can exchange money at the rate in the news.”
The news figure is the rate between banks. You get a posted rate the bank sets around its own base rate.
MISTAKE 03
“The base rate is the day's close.”
The official base rate is the average of the previous trading day's daytime trading. The overnight move is not in it, and it is dated the next business day.
MISTAKE 04
“If a portal's rate differs from the news, one of them is wrong.”
Check the bank name, round and time in the corner. It is that bank's rate at that time.

MOREHourly rates, the won-yen rate, overseas quote sites

Hourly rates · With 24-hour trading, Seoul Money Brokerage and Korea Money Brokerage publish a time-weighted average on the hour, from won-dollar trades in the 2 minutes 30 seconds either side. From 2027 the 4 p.m. figure becomes the base rate.

The won-yen rate · For currencies other than the dollar and the yuan, the base rate is worked out through the dollar (a cross rateAn exchange rate between two currencies worked out through a third, usually the dollar.). If a dollar is 1,350 won and 150 yen, then 100 yen is 1,350 ÷ 150 × 100 = 900 won. The Bank of Korea's won-per-100-yen series is Hana Bank's rate posted at 3:30 p.m.

Overseas quote sites · A “close” on an overseas site is not a figure from the Seoul FX market. Check which market and what time it uses.

PART 3 IN ONE LINE

An exchange rate only makes sense once you know when and how it was measured.

The news uses 3:30 p.m., the market's day ends at 7 a.m. the next morning (6 a.m. in summer), the official base rate is the previous trading day's daytime average (around 4 p.m. from 2027), and a bank app shows the bank's own figure. Before comparing rates, match their basis.

Quick Glossary
Won-dollar rate
The won needed to buy one dollar. When the number rises, the won has lost value
Seoul FX market
Where banks and other financial firms trade won against foreign currencies through brokers. Individuals exchange money through banks
Spot
A currency trade at today's price, usually settled two business days later
3:30 p.m. rate
The won-dollar rate at 3:30 p.m., also called the day-session close
End-of-day close
The rate when the trade date ends, at 7 a.m. the next day (6 a.m. in daylight saving time)
Open, high, low
The first, highest and lowest prices of the day, measured from 7 a.m. to 7 a.m.
Base rate
매매기준율. The reference rate set by the Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulation. For now, the previous trading day's daytime trades averaged by volume (market average rate, MAR). The “base rate” in a bank app starts the morning at this figure and is then reset by the bank round by round
TWAP
Time-weighted average price: prices sampled at regular intervals around a set time, averaged evenly
Posted rate
The rate a bank issues round by round, with separate buy and sell prices
Cash vs. wire
The rate for banknotes and the rate for transfers. The gap is wider on cash
Spread
In currency exchange, the gap between a bank's base rate and the prices at which customers buy and sell; an FX discount narrows it. In the market it can also mean the gap between bid and offer
NDF
A won-dollar contract that fixes a future rate now and settles only the difference, in dollars. Traded outside Korea. Morning stories usually cite the one-month
Swap point
The gap between the forward rate and the spot rate. Not a forecast of the future rate
Daylight saving time
Clocks move forward an hour in summer. Seoul's won-dollar day follows New York's
Cross rate
A rate between two currencies worked out through the dollar
Notes on the Numbers

Date checked · Figures and rules were checked on Nov. 25, 2026, against the Bank of Korea's ECOS database, the Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulation, guidance from Seoul Money Brokerage and the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and bank reports. Korean sources only. ECOS figures, the regulation text and the NDF story were checked again on Nov. 26.

End-of-day close · The finance ministry says the trade date changes when New York closes (5 p.m. New York time). ECOS has carried a “close” item since July 1, 2024. Its value for July 6, 2026, the first day of 24-hour trading (1,530.0 won), matches the figure reported as of 6 a.m. the next day, so we treat it as the rate at the end of the trade date.

Friday's figures · The 3:30 p.m. rate of 1,336.2 won, the close of 1,346.0 won and the open of 1,335.0 won are ECOS values. The high of 1,348.0 won, the low of 1,333.8 won and the NDF of 1,345.4 won come from Hana Bank's Nov. 23 FX report. The Nasdaq's 5.28% fall is from New York closing reports.

Monday's figures · The 3:30 p.m. rate of 1,345.9 won (up 9.7 won) and the open of 1,345.0 won match in ECOS and news reports. The close of 1,342.6 won and the 3.4-won fall were checked against ECOS and Hana Bank's Nov. 24 report.

Base rate · Through Dec. 31, 2026, it is the volume-weighted average of dollar spot trades from 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. on the latest trading day, done through licensed FX brokers and settled two business days later. From Jan. 1, 2027, it is the latest business day's 4 p.m. time-weighted average (Article 1-2 of the regulation; Ministry of Economy and Finance Notice No. 2026-88). A trading day is any day the won-dollar pair trades, Korean holidays included; a business day is a day banks are open. The regulation excerpt on Seoul Money Brokerage's site already shows the 2027 wording, but under the addenda the current method applies until the end of this year. The yuan keeps the current method. The volume-weighted example ($10 billion at 1,500 won, $1 billion at 1,510 won) is taken from a news report quoting the authorities.

Woori Bank example · The last round of Nov. 23, 2026, from Woori Bank's daily rate page. Buying cash was 1.75% above the bank's base rate and sending a wire about 0.97% above. The same table's Bank of Korea base rate, 1,337.30 won, matches the ECOS base rate for Nov. 23. That every bank's first round equals the official rate, and that it is posted around 8:30 a.m., comes from Seoul Money Brokerage's FAQ. The gaps and discounts vary by bank, currency and round. The discount example (1,346.85 won, about 2,117 won) leaves out fees and the bank's rounding.

Base-rate dates · ECOS and Seoul Money Brokerage list each base rate under the date it was posted, which is the business day after the trading day it comes from. The 1,330.8 won dated Nov. 20 comes from trading on the 19th (Thursday); the 1,337.3 won dated Nov. 23 comes from trading on the 20th (Friday).

Market holidays · The won-dollar market is closed on weekends and Jan. 1. It is open on Korean public holidays, and settlement falls on a day that is a business day both in Korea and in the other currency's country (the U.S. for won-dollar).

Why 3:30 p.m. · The point that thin overnight trading makes prices jump comes from a Newsis explainer (July 20). The finding that extended-hours volatility did not rise is from the Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Report (September 2026), and the reason for changing the base rate (24-hour trading and overseas practice) from reports on the market council's general meeting. Keeping the 3:30 p.m. rate through the first half of 2027 and revisiting it toward the end of June, the stock-market reason, and the 4 p.m. method (600 one-second samples, 300 from each of Seoul Money Brokerage and Korea Money Brokerage, simple average) come from a Yonhap Infomax report (Nov. 20) on the market council's Nov. 19 meeting.

Wording in the news · Korean media call the same figure the “day-session close,” the “3:30 p.m. reference rate” or simply the “close.” When a story says only “close,” check the text to see whether it means 3:30 p.m. or the end of the day.

Daylight saving in 2027 · New York daylight saving time starts on Sunday, March 14, 2027. After that, the day cut and the Monday open go back to 6 a.m. Check the Seoul market notice as well.
