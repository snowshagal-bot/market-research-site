ABOUT SNOWSHAGAL

Understanding Korea’s markets,
one report at a time.

Snowshagal is a personal research archive exploring Korean equities, industries and currencies in a global economic context.

About

Published under the name Snowshagal, the archive brings together market-close data, daily reviews, weekly outlooks, in-depth research, investment insights and market basics. It is for readers who want to understand Korean markets and their connections to the wider economy.

Reports draw on public materials, including company disclosures, official statistics and brokerage research. The aim is to make sources and data dates clear, and to distinguish reported facts from forecasts and interpretation.

Snowshagal is planned, written and published by a single editor under a pen name.

What we publish

Daily — After the Korean market closes: the indices, net buying and selling by investor type, sector moves, and what to watch in the next session.

Weekly — A look back at the week and the schedule and risks for the week ahead.

Research — Long-form reports on industries, policy and market structure.

Investment Insights — One question the market is arguing about, worked through with numbers.

Market Basics — Market terms and mechanics explained through real cases.

The date on each report is the date its data refers to, not the day it was posted. The English edition is the same report adapted for readers outside Korea.

Data sources

Market — Korean indices, investor flows, program trading, short selling and the largest companies by market value come from the Korea Exchange (KRX) regular-session close. If the regular-session close cannot be confirmed for a day, that day’s close record is not posted. Overseas indices, rates, currencies and commodities use public quotes, each shown with its own as-of time. The page draws on KRX, Naver Finance and Yahoo Finance.

Disclosures — From Korea’s Financial Supervisory Service electronic disclosure system (OpenDART).

Calendar — Official schedules from the U.S. Federal Reserve, the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) and the Bank of Korea, plus dates found in company filings.

Reports — Built on company filings, statistics and publications from governments, central banks and international organizations, and brokerage research.
